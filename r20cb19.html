20·cb19 · newsflash for 19f

The square’s moves as turns and flips

19f counted eight ways a square card fits its outline, by shuffling labels on its corners. It could not say what the moves were, only what they did to the labels. Now the moves are real. Four are turns about the centre. The other four, turning the card over in space, land every point exactly where a flip across a line in the plane sends it.

With the square centred at the origin: the turns by nothing, a quarter, a half and three quarters, and the flips across the floor, the upright axis and the two diagonals. Turns are even, flips are odd.

20·cb19.2Turning over is a flip

Stands on 19f3 (turning a card over) and 20c3 (flips).

Turning a flat card over about a line in its own plane lands every point where the flip across that line sends it. A point on the card goes round the line in a half circle, at right angles to it, and comes down on the far side, as far from the line as it started.

\text{about the floor: } (x, y) \mapsto (x, -y) @@ the point swings over the floor, the same distance on the far side
\text{about } y = x:\ (x, y) \mapsto (y, x) @@ the flip across y = x (20c3)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a half turn in space} = \text{a flip in the plane}} @@ the card's face does not care how it got there

Seen from above the table, the flip reverses which way round (20g). Seen from space, it is a turn. Which way round a figure goes depends on which side you look from.

20·cb19.319f’s facts, as facts about moves

Stands on 20e (two flips), 20g4 (odd and even flips) and 19d (part-groups).

Every fact 19f found by shuffling labels is now a fact about moves of the plane. Two flips across lines through the centre an eighth of a turn apart make a quarter turn (20e2). The turns are the even moves, a part-group of 4 inside 8 (19d). The flips are the odd moves, and no flip is a turn (20g4).

The accepted names

The square's eight moves are the dihedral group of order 8, often written D₄ or D₈. The turns form the cyclic part-group of order 4.

19·comp · computing

How many ways can a puzzle cube be scrambled?

How many ways can a puzzle cube be scrambled? The puzzle is a cube cut into three layers each way, with a colour on each face. Any face can be twisted a quarter turn, and a few twists scramble the colours. Getting them back takes most people hours, or forever. People asked how many scrambles there are, whether every scramble can be solved, and how many twists it takes. What makes it hard is that the twists do not turn about: twist the right face and then the top, and you get something different from the top and then the right. And the count is too large to list: a computer trying a billion scrambles a second would need over a thousand years. This page reads each twist as a shuffle of the stickers, so 19’s moves combine and undo. It counts the ways to put the pieces back by hand. It finds three things that no string of twists can change, so most of those ways can never be reached. Then it shows the small change made by doing one twist, then another, then undoing the first, then undoing the second, which is what solvers build on.

A twist moves some stickers and leaves the rest, so it is a shuffle, and twists combine and undo as 19 says. Pull the cube apart and put it back any way, and there are about five hundred million million million ways. But every twist keeps three things: the shuffles of the corners and of the edges are both even or both odd; the corners’ twists add to whole turns; and an even number of edges sit the wrong way round. So only one way in twelve can be reached by twisting. Two strings of twists that share one moved piece, done and undone in turn, change at most three pieces.

19comp1A twist is a shuffle of the stickers

Stands on 19a (shuffles; groups) and 19b (order).

The cube has 54 stickers, nine on each face. Its pieces are of three kinds. Each of the 8 corners carries 3 stickers, each of the 12 edges carries 2, and each of the 6 centres carries 1. The centres only turn in place, so they fix which colour belongs on which face. Name the faces by where they sit: Up, Down, Front, Back, Left, Right.

From here on, on this page, R is a quarter turn of the right face, clockwise as you look at that face; likewise U, D, F, B and L. R′ is R undone, a quarter turn the other way. A string of twists is read in the order done: R U means R first, then U. As a product in 19a’s sense, that string is UR.

A twist sends each sticker to one place and no two to the same place, so a twist is a shuffle of the 54 stickers. Strings of twists are shuffles too (shuffles-compose), every one can be undone, and doing nothing is the empty string. So the strings of twists form a group, a part of the shuffles of the 54 stickers.

R:\ \ 5 \text{ loops of } 4 \text{ stickers} @@ the 8 non-centre stickers of the face in two loops; 12 side stickers in three
R^4 = e,\ \ R^2 \ne e @@ each loop of 4 comes back after 4 steps, not before
\text{order of } R = 4,\ \ R' = R^3 @@ order (19b); one-answer (19b) with R⁴ = e
\text{the undo of } R\ U \text{ is } U'\ R' @@ socks and shoes (19c): the last twist done is the first undone
\htmlClass{new}{R\,U \text{ has order } 105} \quad \blacksquare @@ found by following the stickers: loops of 15, 7, 7 and 3; 105 is the least number that 15, 7 and 3 all divide

So a string of twists done over and over comes back to the start, as 19b’s order-exists says it must in a finite group. R U takes 105 rounds. Some strings take 1260.

19comp2Putting the pieces back

Stands on 19·comp.1 and 4j (counting).

Pull the cube apart, and put it back together any way at all. Count the ways in stages (4j). Each corner piece can go in any corner place, and then sit three ways round. Each edge piece can go in any edge place, and then sit two ways round. The centres stay put.

8! = 40\,320 @@ the corners into the corner places
3^8 = 6561 @@ each corner, three ways round
12! = 479\,001\,600 @@ the edges into the edge places
2^{12} = 4096 @@ each edge, two ways round
\htmlClass{new}{8! \cdot 3^8 \cdot 12! \cdot 2^{12} = 519\,024\,039\,293\,878\,272\,000} @@ stages multiply: about 5.2 × 10²⁰

Take-apart count: a cube pulled apart can be put back in 8! · 3⁸ · 12! · 2¹² ways, about 5.2 × 10²⁰. Every twisted cube is one of these. The question is how many of them twisting can reach.

19comp3Three things no twist changes

Stands on 19·comp.2, 19e (parity) and 19d (copies).

Each twist changes the cube a great deal. Three things it never changes.

First, parity. A quarter turn moves 4 corner pieces round a loop of 4 places and 4 edge pieces round a loop of 4. A loop of 4 is 3 swaps, so each is odd (19e).

\text{each quarter turn: an odd shuffle of corners, an odd one of edges} @@ a loop of 4 is 3 swaps (19e)
\text{each quarter turn changes both parities at once} @@ parity (19e): odd after even is odd, odd after odd is even
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the corner shuffle and the edge shuffle are always both even or both odd}} \quad \blacksquare @@ induction on the number of quarter turns (4b); at the start both are e

Second, the corners’ twists. Every corner has one sticker of the Up or Down colour. Count a corner’s twist in thirds of a turn: 0 if that sticker faces Up or Down, 1 if it sits a third of a turn clockwise from there, 2 if two thirds. Then add the twists of all 8 corners.

U,\ D:\ \ \text{every Up or Down sticker stays facing Up or Down} @@ the total is unchanged
R,\ L,\ F,\ B:\ \ \text{the four corners moved gain } 1, 2, 1, 2 \text{ thirds} @@ followed sticker by sticker
1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6 \text{ thirds} = 2 \text{ whole turns} @@ the total gains a whole number of turns
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the total twist is always a whole number of turns}} \quad \blacksquare @@ induction on the number of quarter turns; at the start it is 0

Third, the edges’ ways round. Give each edge place a first face: Up or Down if it touches one, otherwise Front or Back. Give each edge piece a first sticker in the same way, from its home place. An edge sits the right way round when its first sticker is on its place’s first face.

U,\ D,\ R,\ L:\ \ \text{every first sticker lands on a first face} @@ R sends Up to Back, Back to Down, Down to Front, Front to Up
F,\ B:\ \ \text{all four edges moved change their way round} @@ F sends Up to Right: a first face to one that is not
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the number of edges the wrong way round is always even}} \quad \blacksquare @@ each twist changes it by 0 or 4; at the start it is 0

Now count what is left. The positions reached by twisting form a part-group G of all the ways of putting the cube back. Doing a pull-apart change a after the twists gives the copy aG, and copies-same-size and copies-never-overlap (19d) split all the ways into copies of G, each of one size. The three rules can take 3 · 2 · 2 = 12 different sets of values, and pull-apart changes reach every one. Ways with different values never lie in one copy, since twisting keeps the values. So there are at least 12 copies.

\text{at least } 12 \text{ copies of } G,\ \text{all of size } |G| @@ the three rules: 3 twist totals, 2 parities, 2 counts of wrong-way edges
|G| \le \tfrac{519\,024\,039\,293\,878\,272\,000}{12} @@ copies-never-overlap: the copies share nothing
\htmlClass{new}{|G| \le 43\,252\,003\,274\,489\,856\,000} @@ about 4.3 × 10¹⁹

Reach count: no string of twists reaches more than one way in twelve of putting the cube back. Solvers’ methods show that every way meeting the three rules can be reached, so the count is exactly 43 252 003 274 489 856 000. That half is held in trust here: it needs the tools of the next footing, used with care on every case.

19comp4Do, do, undo, undo

Stands on 19·comp.1, 19c (socks and shoes; turn-about-undo) and 19a (shuffles).

Do a string x, then a string y, then undo x, then undo y. In time order it is x, y, x⁻¹, y⁻¹. As a product it is C = y⁻¹x⁻¹yx.

C = y^{-1}x^{-1}\,y\,x = (xy)^{-1}(yx) @@ socks and shoes (19c): (xy)⁻¹ = y⁻¹x⁻¹
C = e \iff yx = xy @@ one-answer (19b): (xy)⁻¹(yx) = e exactly when yx = xy
\htmlClass{new}{C \text{ undoes nothing extra exactly when } x \text{ and } y \text{ turn about}} \quad \blacksquare @@ so C measures how far they fail to

Small-change technique: if x and y each move some places, and exactly one place p is moved by both, then C moves at most three places: p, x⁻¹(p) and y⁻¹(p). Here a place is where a piece sits, and moving a piece includes turning it.

q \text{ moved by neither}:\ \ C(q) = q @@ each of x, y, x⁻¹, y⁻¹ leaves q
q \text{ moved by } x,\ q \ne p,\ x(q) \ne p:\ \ y \text{ leaves } x(q),\ x^{-1} \text{ brings it to } q,\ y^{-1} \text{ leaves } q @@ x moves only its own places, and p is the only one y shares
q \text{ moved by } y,\ q \ne p,\ y(q) \ne p:\ \ x \text{ leaves } q,\ y \text{ sends it to } y(q),\ x^{-1} \text{ leaves it},\ y^{-1} \text{ brings it back} @@ the same, with x and y exchanged
\htmlClass{new}{\text{only } p,\ x^{-1}(p),\ y^{-1}(p) \text{ can move}} \quad \blacksquare @@ x(q) = p means q = x⁻¹(p); y(q) = p means q = y⁻¹(p)
  
x = R'\ D'\ R,\ \ y = U @@ x moves many pieces, but only one of the Up layer: the Up-Front-Right corner
x,\ y,\ x^{-1},\ y^{-1}:\ \ R'\ D'\ R\ \ U\ \ R'\ D\ R\ \ U' @@ eight quarter turns
\htmlClass{new}{\text{three corners go round a loop; nothing else moves}} @@ small-change, with p the Up-Front-Right place
x = R'\ D'\ R\ D\ R'\ D'\ R\ D,\ \ y = U @@ x turns the Up-Front-Right corner in its place, and of the Up layer moves nothing else
\htmlClass{new}{\text{two corners turned in place, the opposite ways; nothing else moves}} @@ here two of the three places coincide

Solvers build on these. Put the pieces of the first layers in by hand, where there is room to spare. In the last layer every twist would wreck what is done, so use do, do, undo, undo: a loop of three pieces, or two corners turned in place, and nothing else touched. With enough such tools every way meeting the three rules can be solved.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

The puzzle is the 3 × 3 × 3 twisting puzzle cube; its pieces are cubies, and the twists in letters are Singmaster notation. Do, do, undo, undo is a commutator, written [x, y]; a string of the form x y x⁻¹ is a conjugate. The positions reached by twisting form the cube group, a part-group of index 12 in the group of all reassemblies. A loop of three pieces is a 3-cycle; a corner turned in place is a twisted corner, and an edge the wrong way round is a misoriented edge. The fewest turns that solve every scramble is called God’s number.

Who said it first

The puzzle was made in 1974 by a professor of architecture in Budapest, to show his students how the pieces of a solid could move. David Singmaster wrote its letters, R, U, F and the rest, in 1979, and the first solving methods by commutators. In 19·comp.6.5, the team is Tomas Rokicki, Herbert Kociemba, Morley Davidson and John Dethridge (2010). A computer proof first split mathematicians in 1976, when Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken showed that four colours suffice for every map by checking nearly two thousand cases on a machine.

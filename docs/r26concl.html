Growth and Turning Are One Function

The exponential, carried into ℂ by its endless sum, turns: e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ. Every number but 0 has endlessly many logarithms. A function with a complex slope totals zero round a loop with no hole, is rebuilt inside a circle from its rim, and round a hole totals 2πi times its residue.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 26h residues is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 26 asked whether growth and turning are one function. For every real θ, e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ, and a function with a complex slope is fixed inside a loop by its values on the loop. The price is the logarithm: every number but 0 has endlessly many.

26a carried e^z into the plane and found it turns. 26b kept adding into multiplying. 26c found the many logarithms. 26d defined a complex slope. 26e and 26f totalled functions round loops. 26g rebuilt the inside from the rim, and 26h counted what holes leave behind. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

The sum Σ zᵏ/k! settles for every z in ℂ; its value e^z obeys e^(z + w) = e^z e^w and e^(a + bi) = e^a(cos b + i sin b), so growth and turning are one function.

e^z = 1 exactly when z = 2πik, so every z ≠ 0 has the logarithms ln|z| + i(θ + 2πk), one for each whole number k.

If f has a complex slope on an open disc, then round every loop in the disc f totals 0, and inside every circle in the disc f is rebuilt from its values on the circle, as a power series.

If f has a complex slope everywhere and is bounded, it is constant. Round a circle that encloses poles, f totals 2πi times the sum of their residues.

So the rung’s claim holds: one function grows along the floor and turns along the upright. And a complex slope, asked of a function at every point, is a far stronger demand than a slope on the line.

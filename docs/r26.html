Rung 26 · theory

Growing and turning, together

Rung 17 built growth: a function that multiplies by the same factor over equal steps. The sine and cosine built turning: they go round and round. They looked like two different things. One grows without end, the others never leave a band. This rung feeds an imaginary number to the growth function, through the endless sum that rung 23 found for it, and the growth function turns. Growth and turning are one function, seen along two directions of the plane. Then it asks what a slope means in the plane, and finds that a function with a complex slope is rigid: what it does round a loop fixes everything inside.

Growth and turning looked like strangers. Seeing them as one is not something anyone can tell you into. You have to stand where you now stand.

The exponential, carried into ℂ by its endless sum, turns: e to the iθ is cos θ + i sin θ. A function with a complex slope is fixed inside a loop by its values on the loop.

The class · read it through, then go to 26a

What it means to say it

To say growth and turning are one function is to say there is a single rule, e to the z, which grows along the floor of the plane and turns along the upright. Along the floor it is rung 17’s growth. Along the upright it is the turning of the sine and cosine. Every value of it is a growth times a turn.

For every real θ, e to the iθ is cos θ + i sin θ, so growth and turning are one function. The rung proves it from the endless sum, then follows what a complex slope forces.

Where it comes from

Roger Cotes found a form of the turning formula in 1714. Leonhard Euler wrote e to the ix as cos x + i sin x in 1748, and with it the many logarithms of a negative number. Caspar Wessel drew the numbers of the plane in 1799. Augustin-Louis Cauchy proved that the total round a loop is zero in 1825, and gave the integral formula in 1831 and residues around the same years. Bernhard Riemann made the complex slope the centre of the subject in 1851. Édouard Goursat removed a needless assumption from Cauchy’s proof in 1900.

The principles, in plain words

The rung proves five things, each from the ones before.

First, the endless sum for e to the z settles for every complex z, and at z = iθ it splits into the sums for cos θ and sin θ.

Second, e to the z still turns adding into multiplying, so e to the a + bi is a growth by e to the a and a turn through b. It is never zero, and it repeats every 2πi.

Third, because it repeats, every number but 0 has endlessly many logarithms, one for each whole turn. A main one can be chosen, at a cost.

Fourth, a slope in the plane must be the same from every direction. Polynomials and e to the z have one, the flip has none. Round a loop with no hole inside, a function with a complex slope totals zero.

Fifth, so the values round a circle rebuild the function inside it, as a power series. A bounded function with a complex slope everywhere is constant, and that proves rung 25’s root technique again in two lines. Where the function has a hole, the loop’s total is 2πi times what the hole leaves behind.

Anexact, yet rigorous. Beside the proofs, the annexes carry boxes of the second kind of care, the kind that measures how wrong it might be instead of proving. 26·then carries the cloud, read as one value: a gate reads a whole band of voltages as true and another as false, with a stated margin between them. 26·phys carries the cloud, tuned: a tuned circuit hears a band, not a single pace, and its width is chosen and stated.

What follows

With those five, the techniques compute. The sum formulas of rung 18 fall out of e to the i(a + b) in one line. The radius of a real power series is the distance to the nearest complex trouble, which is why 1/(1 + x²) stops at radius 1 though nothing goes wrong on the line. Real integrals that resist every rung 16 technique fall to one residue. The factorial is carried past the counting numbers. The annexes take the turning exponential into a braked spring, a switch that says true or false, and a radio that hears one station.

What you can do because you know it

You can write any swing as the floor part of a turning exponential, and add swings by adding numbers of the plane. You can find the logarithm and the powers of any number but zero, and say which one you chose. You can total a function round a loop by looking only at its holes, and use that to compute real integrals.

Every rung from here on that touches waves, circuits or chance leans on this one.

What it costs

Nothing new is laid down. The rung spends rung 23’s endless sums and radii, rung 22’s settling, rung 17’s growth, rung 18’s turns and traced curves, rung 13’s slope rules, rung 16’s integrals and rung 25’s sizes and roots. At the end of the rung you will know what it means to say growth and turning are one function, when it is true, and what you can do because you know it.

What Is the Faint Glow Left From the Beginning?

What is the faint glow left from the beginning? In 1964 two radio engineers in New Jersey could not rid their antenna of a faint hiss coming equally from every direction. They had found light released when the universe was 380 000 years old, stretched by the growing ruler of 33·cb29 until it arrives as microwaves. That glow is the best-measured picture of the young universe. Its temperature, its tiny ripples and the way they are spaced say what the universe is made of, and they disagree, by a small but stubborn amount, with the growth measured from nearby galaxies. This page stretches light with the ruler, cools the early fire to today’s glow, reads the ripples, and lays out the disagreement.

As the ruler R grows, every wavelength grows with it: 1 + z = R now / R then. A hot glow keeps its shape when stretched and its temperature falls as 1/R. The universe turned clear at about 3000 K; stretched 1100 times, that is 2.7 K today. Ripples of one part in a hundred thousand are the seeds of galaxies, and their spacing weighs the universe: about 5% ordinary matter, 27% unseen matter, 68% the push that speeds the growth.

33phys1Light Stretched by the Ruler

A wave travelling between galaxies is laid out along the ruler of space. As the ruler grows, so does every crest-to-crest distance.

\lambda \propto R @@ the wave grows with the ruler
1 + z = \dfrac{\lambda_{\text{now}}}{\lambda_{\text{then}}} = \dfrac{R_{\text{now}}}{R_{\text{then}}} @@ z, the stretch less one
\text{a galaxy at } z = 1 \text{ sent its light when the ruler was half today’s} @@ distances were halved
\htmlClass{new}{\text{each photon’s energy } hc/\lambda \text{ falls as } 1/R} @@ lumps of hf (17·phys), f = c/λ

From here on, on this page, z is the stretch: how much longer the light arrives than it left, less one.

33phys2From Fire to Hiss

\text{early universe: hot enough to strip atoms, opaque} @@ light scattered off free electrons
\text{at about } 3000 \text{ K, atoms form; light flies free} @@ 380 000 years after the start
T \propto \dfrac1R:\ T_{\text{now}} = \dfrac{3000}{1 + z} = \dfrac{3000}{1100} \approx 2.7 \text{ K} @@ stretched 1100 times
\htmlClass{new}{\text{measured: } 2.725 \text{ K, the same in every direction}} @@ peaking near a millimetre: microwaves

Afterglow technique: a glow that fills a growing universe keeps its shape, with its temperature falling as one over the ruler; so the clear-sky moment at 3000 K arrives today at 2.7 K. A satellite in 1990 found its spectrum to be the shape of a perfect glowing body to better than a part in ten thousand.

33phys3Ripples of One Part in a Hundred Thousand

\text{temperature differs across the sky by about } 1 \text{ part in } 10^5 @@ found by COBE, 1992
\text{denser spots pulled in more matter: the seeds of galaxies} @@ gravity grows them
\text{the spots have a favoured size, about a degree across} @@ sound waves in the early fire
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the favoured size says space is flat, to within a fraction of a per cent}} @@ a known ruler seen at a known distance

The glow’s ripples are a snapshot of sound waves in the young universe; their size and spacing measure its shape and contents. Their discoverers shared the 2006 Nobel Prize in Physics.

33phys4What the Universe Is Made Of

\text{ordinary matter: about } 5\% @@ atoms of every kind
\text{unseen matter: about } 27\% @@ pulls, but gives no light

Read through the growing ruler, the ripples say that ordinary matter is a twentieth of the universe. The unseen matter is also needed by the spin of galaxies; what it is, and what the push is, are unknown.

33phys5A Stubborn Disagreement

\text{from the glow, through the theory: } H \approx 67.4 \text{ km/s per Mpc} @@ Planck satellite, 2018
\text{from nearby stars and exploding stars: } H \approx 73.0 @@ ladders of distance, 2022
\text{each claims about } 1\% \text{ to } 1.5\% \text{ precision} @@ the gap is about five times that
\htmlClass{new}{\text{an error somewhere, or something new in the theory}} @@ not yet settled

The growth rate read from the young universe and from the nearby one disagree by more than either’s error. Either one measurement hides a mistake no one has found, or the theory joining them is missing a piece. New telescopes are aimed at the question now.

33phys7Temperature From the Peak, and an Age From the Spreading

The early universe cooled as it stretched (33phys1). Its leftover glow is a perfect glow curve, and the place of the curve’s peak gives the temperature. Its spreading rate, run backwards, gives roughly how long ago everything was together.

\lambda_{\text{peak}} T = 2.898 \text{ mm·K} @@ the peak technique (17phys5)
T = 2.725 \text{ K}:\ \lambda_{\text{peak}} \approx 1.06 \text{ mm} @@ microwaves
\text{spreading rate } 70 \text{ km/s per Mpc}:\ \dfrac{1}{H} = \dfrac{3.086 \times 10^{19}}{70} \text{ s} \approx 14 \times 10^9 \text{ years} @@ distance over speed, the same for every galaxy
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a rough age; the careful sum, with the changing rate, gives } 13.8 \times 10^9} @@

Peak-and-age technique: a glow’s temperature is 2.898 mm·K over its peak wavelength, and one over the spreading rate is a first estimate of the universe’s age. Shown from a satellite: the faint glow’s measured brightness at each wavelength lies on a perfect glow curve for 2.725 K, the points sitting on the line within the width of the line drawn.

The cellar · there if you want it

Accepted names

The glow is the cosmic microwave background (CMB); the stretch z is the redshift; the clear-sky moment is recombination and decoupling. The ripples are anisotropies, their favoured size the acoustic peak from baryon acoustic oscillations. Unseen matter is dark matter, the push dark energy; the model is ΛCDM; the disagreement is the Hubble tension.

Who said it first

Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson found the hiss in 1964 and shared the 1978 Nobel Prize; Ralph Alpher and Robert Herman had predicted a glow of about 5 K in 1948. John Mather and George Smoot led the COBE measurements of 1990 and 1992 and shared the 2006 Nobel Prize.

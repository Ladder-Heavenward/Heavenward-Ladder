33g · proof

Round drums

Rung 24 met the rule of a beaten drum without saying where it came from, and found that the drum’s pitches are not whole multiples of each other. This step derives the rule, from the string’s rule spread over a round sheet. First the sum of the two second slopes has to be rewritten for a round shape, in reach and turn instead of east and north. Then the drum separates into a turn, a reach and a time, and the reach obeys the rule of rung 24, now with a whole family of members, one for each number of times the shape winds round. Then the claim that a drum’s tones are not harmonics, which rung 24 read off a computer’s numbers, is proved, with bounds that the alternating sums of rung 22 give exactly. Last, the same work is done for a ball, where the round rule turns out to be the rod’s rule in disguise. A ball the size of the earth, cooling as a whole, would need far longer than the earth has existed.

In reach and turn, the sum of second slopes picks up a term from the reach. A round drum separates into whole windings, the drum functions of rung 24, and a swing, and its pitches are the zeros of those functions, which are provably not whole multiples. A ball that depends only on distance from its centre obeys the rod’s rule for r times u.

33g1The round sum of slopes

A drumhead is a sheet, and its rule is the string’s rule spread over the plane: u_tt = c²Δu, with Δu = u_xx + u_yy, by the books of 33c1 kept on a small square instead of a short piece. A round drum asks for reach r and turn θ, with x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ (21r).

Round-rate technique: for r > 0, Δu = u_rr + u_r/r + u_θθ/r².

u_r = u_x\cos\theta + u_y\sin\theta @@ roads add (28c): x_r = cos θ, y_r = sin θ
u_{rr} = u_{xx}\cos^2\theta + 2u_{xy}\sin\theta\cos\theta + u_{yy}\sin^2\theta @@ roads add again on u_x and u_y; swap knobs (28d)
u_\theta = -u_x\,r\sin\theta + u_y\,r\cos\theta @@ x_θ = −r sin θ, y_θ = r cos θ
u_{\theta\theta} = r^2\big(u_{xx}\sin^2\theta - 2u_{xy}\sin\theta\cos\theta + u_{yy}\cos^2\theta\big) - r\,u_r @@ the product rule; the last term is −r(u_x cos θ + u_y sin θ)
u_{rr} + \tfrac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta} = u_{xx} + u_{yy} - \tfrac{1}{r}u_r @@ add, with sin² + cos² = 1 (21c)
\htmlClass{new}{\Delta u = u_{rr} + \tfrac1r u_r + \tfrac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}} \quad \blacksquare @@ move the last term across

The middle term is new. Along a reach, the circles grow as r grows, so a slope along the reach is spread over more and more room. That term is why a drum is not a string.

33g2The drum’s shapes

Tie a drumhead of radius R round its rim: u = 0 at r = R. Look for shapes that keep their form: u = P(r) Θ(θ) T(t).

Drum-shape technique: the drum’s answers of this form are J_m(kr) cos(mθ − θ₀) times a swing cos(ckt − t₀), for a whole number m ≥ 0 and a k with J_m(kR) = 0, where J_m(x) = Σ (−1)ʲ (x/2)^(2j + m) / (j! (j + m)!).

\tfrac{T^{\prime\prime}}{c^2T} = \tfrac{P^{\prime\prime} + P^{\prime}/r}{P} + \tfrac{1}{r^2}\tfrac{\Theta^{\prime\prime}}{\Theta} = -k^2 @@ put PΘT into u_tt = c²Δu with the round-rate technique; the left side moves only with t
\tfrac{\Theta^{\prime\prime}}{\Theta} = -m^2,\ \ \Theta = \cos(m\theta - \theta_0) @@ multiply by r²: the turn part moves only with θ; the steady swing (24h)
m \text{ is a whole number} @@ θ and θ + 2π are the same point of the drum, and a swing with m repeats every 2π/m
r^2P^{\prime\prime} + rP^{\prime} + (k^2r^2 - m^2)P = 0 @@ what is left, multiplied by r²P
x^2y^{\prime\prime} + xy^{\prime} + (x^2 - m^2)y = 0,\ \ x = kr @@ the drum’s rule of 24l3, with a term for the winding
s^2 = m^2,\ \ u_j = -\tfrac{u_{j-2}}{j(j + 2m)} @@ the starting-power technique (24l); only s = m stays finite at the centre
\htmlClass{new}{P = J_m(kr),\ \ J_m(kR) = 0} \quad \blacksquare @@ the recipe, from u₀ = 1/(2ᵐm!); the rim is tied

So each shape is fixed by m, the number of times it winds, and by which zero of J_m sits at the rim. A shape with m = 1 has a line across the drum that never moves. Its frequency is c k/(2π) = c j/(2πR), where j is the zero. Rung 24’s drum was the shape with m = 0.

33g3Tones that are not harmonics

24l3 read the first zeros of J₀ off its sum, as 2.405 and 5.520, and took their ratio. A drum’s tones are not whole multiples. Is that a fact, or a rounding?

Unharmonic-drum technique: the first two round tones of a drum, with m = 0, have a ratio between 2.28 and 2.31, so the second is not a whole multiple of the first.

J_0(x) = 1 - \tfrac{x^2}{4} + \tfrac{x^4}{64} - \tfrac{x^6}{2304} + \cdots @@ the recipe with m = 0
\text{for } x \le 6, \text{ the terms shrink in size from } j = 3 \text{ on} @@ the jth term is the one before times −(x/2)²/j², and 9/j² < 1 once j ≥ 4
J_0(2.40) \in [0.00249,\ 0.00293],\ \ J_0(2.41) \in [-0.00998,\ -0.00225] @@ two running totals in a row bracket the total (22g), worked in exact fractions
J_0(5.51) \in [-0.00962,\ -0.00284],\ \ J_0(5.53) \in [0.00332,\ 0.00401] @@ the same, with more terms
\text{a zero in } (2.40, 2.41) \text{ and one in } (5.51, 5.53) @@ no gaps (12e): J₀ is continuous and changes sign
\htmlClass{new}{2.28 < \tfrac{5.51}{2.41} < \tfrac{j_2}{j_1} < \tfrac{5.53}{2.40} < 2.31} \quad \blacksquare @@ and no whole number lies between 2.28 and 2.31

That the two zeros found are the first two takes one more look: J₀ is positive from 0 to 2.40 and has one sign between them. The step leaves that check to its standing questions. The same bounds for J₁ put its first zero between 3.83 and 3.84. So a drum of radius R sounds, against its lowest tone, at about 1.59, 2.14, 2.30, 2.65 and 2.92 times it. A string sounds at 2, 3, 4. A snare drum 36 cm across, with c = 100 m/s in its head, has its lowest tone at 100 × 2.405 / (2π × 0.178) ≈ 215 swings a second, and the rest sit at no whole multiple of it.

33g4A ball, by the rod’s rule

In space, Δu = u_xx + u_yy + u_zz. Let u depend only on the distance r from the centre: u = U(r), with r = √(x² + y² + z²).

Round-ball technique: if u depends only on the distance r from the centre, then Δu = (1/r)(rU)″ for r > 0.

u_x = U^{\prime}(r)\,\tfrac{x}{r} @@ the chain rule: r_x = x/r
u_{xx} = U^{\prime\prime}\tfrac{x^2}{r^2} + U^{\prime}\big(\tfrac1r - \tfrac{x^2}{r^3}\big) @@ the product rule
\Delta u = U^{\prime\prime} + U^{\prime}\big(\tfrac3r - \tfrac1r\big) = U^{\prime\prime} + \tfrac2r U^{\prime} @@ add the three, with x² + y² + z² = r²
\htmlClass{new}{\Delta u = \tfrac1r\,(rU)^{\prime\prime}} \quad \blacksquare @@ (rU)″ = rU″ + 2U′

So for a ball, w = rU obeys the rod’s rule. A ball of radius R, started evenly warm and held at 0 on its skin, cools in the shapes sin(nπr/R)/r, each fading like e^(−κn²π²t/R²) by 33a2’s lines for w, with w = 0 at the centre and at the skin. The slowest loses a factor of e every R²/(κπ²). A steel ball 2 cm across, with κ = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s, takes under a second. A ball of rock the size of the earth, with κ about 10⁻⁶ m²/s, would take some 130 billion years, about ten times the age of everything. A body that large has cooled only in its outer skin.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

u_tt = c²Δu on a disc is the wave equation for a circular membrane; Δu in reach and turn is the Laplacian in polar coordinates. J_m is the Bessel function of the first kind of order m, x²y″ + xy′ + (x² − m²)y = 0 is Bessel’s equation, and its zeros are written j_{m,n}. The shapes are the normal modes of the membrane, and the lines that never move are nodal lines. The number of the reroute is the Rayleigh quotient, and the fact that it is never below the lowest number is the Rayleigh principle.

Who said it first

Leonhard Euler studied a round drum in 1764 and found the drum’s rule. Daniel Bernoulli had met J₀ in a hanging chain in 1732. Friedrich Bessel studied the functions in 1824 for the motion of planets. Siméon Denis Poisson cooled a ball by the rod’s rule for rU in the 1820s. William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) estimated the earth’s age from its cooling skin in 1862, and Lord Rayleigh’s Theory of Sound (1877) set out the drum’s tones and his quotient. Mark Kac asked in 1966 whether the tones of a drum fix its shape; Carolyn Gordon, David Webb and Scott Wolpert found two different drums with the same tones in 1992.

A proof that is not allowed

“The drum’s rule has two solutions, so each shape is a mix of J₀ and the second one, and the rim fixes the mix.” Not allowed: the second solution passes every bound at the centre (24l3), and a drumhead is finite at its centre, so the mix has none of it. The rim then fixes k, not the mix.

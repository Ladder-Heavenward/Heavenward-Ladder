The Bracket of a Group

A skew grid X is an arrow at I (29k). Follow it, and the path is exp(tX), the solution of Q′ = XQ from 27·cb24: a steady turn. So arrows at I become turns, and two arrows can be bracketed as two fields were in 29j. For grids the bracket is short: [X, Y] = XY − YX. Turning a little about one axis, then another, then back, misses by a turn about the third. This step turns arrows into turns, brackets them, and finds the cross product inside the turns of space.

If Xᵀ = −X then exp(tX) is a turn for every t. The bracket of the group is [X, Y] = XY − YX; it is again skew. For turns of space, the arrows of w and u bracket to the arrow of w × u: a loop about x and y turns about z.

Exponential technique: for every arrow X at I, the path exp(tX) stays in the group, has arrow X at t = 0, and satisfies exp(sX) exp(tX) = exp((s + t)X); it is the flow of 29j for the field Q ↦ XQ.

29l2The Bracket of Two Grids

e^{tX}e^{tY}e^{-tX}e^{-tY} = I + t^2(XY - YX) + \ldots @@ expand each to second order (28l)
[X, Y] = XY - YX @@ the bracket of the group
[X, Y]^{\mathsf T} = Y^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T} - X^{\mathsf T}Y^{\mathsf T} = YX - XY = -[X, Y] @@ skew again
\htmlClass{new}{[X, Y] = 0 \iff XY = YX} @@ the group turns about only where the bracket is 0

Group bracket technique: a short loop exp(tX) exp(tY) exp(−tX) exp(−tY) misses I by t²[X, Y] plus smaller terms, with [X, Y] = XY − YX, which is again an arrow at I; it is 29j’s bracket for the fields Q ↦ XQ and Q ↦ YQ, up to sign.

29l3The Cross Product Inside the Turns

L_x v = e_1 \times v,\ L_y v = e_2 \times v,\ L_z v = e_3 \times v @@ the skew grids of 29k3
[L_x, L_y] = L_z,\ [L_y, L_z] = L_x,\ [L_z, L_x] = L_y @@ multiply the grids out
[L_w, L_u] = L_{w \times u} @@ the bracket is the cross product
\htmlClass{new}{\text{tip about } x,\ \text{about } y,\ \text{back, back: a small turn about } z} @@ turns do not turn about (27o)

In the turns of space the bracket is the cross product: a loop of small tips about two axes turns about the third, by the product of the two angles. A pilot who banks and pitches in a short loop finds the plane has turned in heading.

Accepted names

exp(tX) is the exponential map; [X, Y] = XY − YX is the commutator, the Lie bracket of the Lie algebra 𝔰𝔬(3), which is ℝ³ with the cross product. The loop is the group commutator; the full formula for exp(X) exp(Y) is the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula.

Who said it first

Sophus Lie found that a continuous group is fixed near I by its bracket, in the 1870s and 1880s; Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan classified the simple ones between 1888 and 1894.

43i · technique

Primes in Every Hour of a Clock

On the clock of 4, the hours [1] and [3] each hold endlessly many primes, by two separate tricks (43g). For the clock of 10 or 101 no trick of that kind is known for every hour. Dirichlet found one method for all clocks: give each hour a sign, build a ζ with signs, and show it is not 0 at s = 1. Then every hour that shares no factor with the clock holds endlessly many primes, and they share the primes equally. This step does it on the clock of 4, where the signed ζ at 1 is π/4.

χ(n) = 0, 1, 0, −1 as n ≡ 0, 1, 2, 3 (mod 4) keeps products: χ(mn) = χ(m)χ(n). Then L(s) = Σ χ(n)/nˢ = Π 1/(1 − χ(p)p^(−s)), and L(1) = 1 − ⅓ + ⅕ − ⋯ = π/4 ≠ 0. So Σ χ(p)/p stays bounded while Σ 1/p does not, and each hour gets half of the divergence.

43i1Signs That Keep Products

\chi(n) = \begin{cases} 1 & n \equiv 1 \\ -1 & n \equiv 3 \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases} @@ an odd number’s hour, as a sign
\chi(mn) = \chi(m)\chi(n) @@ 3 · 3 ≡ 1, 1 · 3 ≡ 3
L(s) = \textstyle\sum_n \dfrac{\chi(n)}{n^s} = \prod_p \dfrac{1}{1 - \chi(p)\,p^{-s}} @@ the prime-product technique, with signs (43g)
\htmlClass{new}{L(1) = 1 - \tfrac13 + \tfrac15 - \tfrac17 + \cdots = \tfrac{\pi}{4}} @@ the arctangent series at 1

Signed-product technique: if a sign χ on the hours keeps products, Σ χ(n)/nˢ is the product over primes of 1/(1 − χ(p)p^(−s)), for s > 1.

43i2Not Zero at One

\ln L(s) = \textstyle\sum_p \chi(p)\,p^{-s} + \text{a bounded part} @@ the higher powers of primes add up to little
L(1) = \pi/4 \ne 0 \Rightarrow \textstyle\sum_p \chi(p)\,p^{-s} \text{ stays bounded} @@ ln L stays finite
\textstyle\sum_p p^{-s} \to \infty @@ thin but endless (43g1)
\htmlClass{new}{\textstyle\sum_{p \equiv 1} p^{-s} = \tfrac12\big(\sum p^{-s} + \sum \chi(p)\,p^{-s}\big) + \ldots \to \infty} @@ and the same for p ≡ 3 with a minus

Not-zero technique: because the signed sum is not 0 at s = 1, the sums of 1/p over each hour both grow without bound; so each hour holds endlessly many primes, and they split the growth in half.

43i3Every Clock

c(m) \text{ hours share no factor with } m @@ the units (43e)
c(m) \text{ signs keep products; together they pick out each hour} @@ as 1 and χ picked [1] and [3]
\text{each signed sum, other than all-ones, is not } 0 \text{ at } 1 @@ the hard part, Dirichlet’s
\htmlClass{new}{\text{each such hour holds endlessly many primes, a share } 1/c(m)} @@ e.g. 1, 3, 7, 9 on the clock of 10, a quarter each

On every clock, each hour sharing no factor with the clock holds endlessly many primes, and the primes share themselves equally among those hours. Primes up to a million on the clock of 10 end in 1, 3, 7, 9 nearly a quarter each, with a slight lead for 3 and 7.

The cellar · there if you want it

Accepted names

The signs are Dirichlet characters, the signed sums Dirichlet L-functions; the theorem is Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, with equal shares from the prime number theorem for progressions. The lead of 3 over 1 is Chebyshev’s bias.

Who said it first

Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet proved it in 1837; Chebyshev noticed the bias in 1853.

44i · proof

What Compass and Straightedge Can Build

Rung 18 showed that the compass and the straightedge reach exactly the numbers built from one by the four operations and square roots, and it reported without proof that the side of a cube of twice the volume and the third of the angle of an equilateral triangle are out of reach. The field tools of this floor prove it. A number the tools reach lies at the top of a tower of fields that doubles at each floor, so the degree of its field is a power of two. The side of the doubled cube has degree three, and so does the cosine of twenty degrees, and so does the cosine that builds the regular seven-sided figure. None of them can be reached. Then the other way: when the group of an equation has a power of two members, its field comes down to the rationals in square-root steps, and every number in it can be built. The circle can be cut into seventeen equal arcs this way, and the step builds the cosine that does it.

A constructible number lies in a tower of steps of degree two, so its degree over the rationals is a power of two. The side of the doubled cube, the cosine of twenty degrees and the cosine that builds the seven-sided figure have degree three, so those constructions are impossible. A field of roots whose group has a power of two members comes down in square-root steps, so the seventeen-sided figure can be built.

44i1A Tower of Square Roots

Call a real number constructible when the tools of 18j, started from (0, 0) and (1, 0), reach a point with it as a coordinate. 18j5’s root-and-ratio technique says each new point has coordinates p + q√k with p, q, k in the field K of the coordinates before; its converse, the square-root technique, builds √k for every k > 0 reached.

Power-of-two technique: a constructible number α has [ℚ(α) : ℚ] a power of 2.

\mathbb Q = K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq K_s,\ \ K_{i+1} = K_i(\sqrt{k_i}),\ k_i \in K_i @@ one floor for each point marked, by root-and-ratio (18j5)
[K_{i+1} : K_i] = 1 \text{ or } 2 @@ √kᵢ is a root of x² − kᵢ
[K_s : \mathbb Q] = 2^t @@ degrees multiply up the tower (43f)
\alpha \in K_s:\ \ [K_s : \mathbb Q] = [K_s : \mathbb Q(\alpha)]\,[\mathbb Q(\alpha) : \mathbb Q] @@ the tower ℚ ⊆ ℚ(α) ⊆ K_s
\htmlClass{new}{[\mathbb Q(\alpha) : \mathbb Q] \text{ divides } 2^t,\ \text{so it is a power of } 2} \quad \blacksquare @@ 4j’s one prime list

44i3Groups of Two to a Power Come Down in Square Roots

Two-group technique: if K is a splitting field over ℚ and Aut(K/ℚ) has 2ᵏ members, then every member of K is reached from ℚ by square roots.

\{e\} = H_0 \subset H_1 \subset \cdots \subset H_k = G,\ \ |H_i| = 2^i @@ the prime-power-chain technique (44h2)
K = K^{H_0} \supset K^{H_1} \supset \cdots \supset K^{H_k} = \mathbb Q,\ \ \text{each step of degree } 2 @@ the middle-field technique (44d3)
E \subset E(\beta),\ [E(\beta) : E] = 2:\ \ \beta^2 + b\beta + c = 0,\ b, c \in E @@ β’s polynomial over E
\htmlClass{new}{E(\beta) = E(\sqrt{b^2 - 4c})} \quad \blacksquare @@ the quadratic formula (8i); each floor adds one square root

Square roots of non-real numbers are reached too, through their real and imaginary parts, which are square roots of reals by 25’s polar form. So every member of K is constructible as a point of the plane.

44i4Seventeen Equal Arcs

Let ζ = cos(2π/17) + i sin(2π/17). Its polynomial is Φ = 1 + x + ⋯ + x¹⁶, irreducible by 43d’s test applied to Φ(x + 1). So [ℚ(ζ) : ℚ] = 16. A symmetry sends ζ to ζᵏ for some k from 1 to 16, so Aut(ℚ(ζ)/ℚ) is the clock group (ℤ/17)ˣ of 16 members, and 3 reaches every hour of it (42e): 3, 9, 10, 13, 5, 15, 11, 16, 14, 8, 7, 4, 12, 2, 6, 1.

Seventeen-arcs technique: cos(2π/17) is constructible, and it is reached by three square-root steps after ℚ(√17).

16 = 2^4 \Rightarrow \zeta \text{ and } 2\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{17} = \zeta + \zeta^{16} \text{ are constructible} @@ the two-group technique
\eta_0 = \textstyle\sum_j \zeta^{3^{2j}},\ \eta_1 = \sum_j \zeta^{3^{2j+1}}:\ \ \eta_0 + \eta_1 = -1,\ \eta_0\eta_1 = -4 @@ the even and odd powers of 3: fixed by the part-group of 8; their sum is ζ + ⋯ + ζ¹⁶
\eta_0, \eta_1 = \tfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{17}}{2} \approx 1.5616,\ -2.5616 @@ 8i, on x² + x − 4

\beta_0 + \beta_4 = \alpha_0,\ \ \beta_0\beta_4 = \alpha_3:\ \ \beta_0 = \zeta + \zeta^{16} \approx 1.8649 @@ the sums of two, fixed by {e, ζ ↦ ζ¹⁶}
\htmlClass{new}{\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{17} = \tfrac{\beta_0}{2} \approx 0.93247} \quad \blacksquare @@ three quadratics after ℚ(√17), each by 8i

The same count settles every regular figure: an n-gon is constructible exactly when the clock group of n has a power of two members, which happens exactly when n is a power of 2 times different primes of the form 2^(2^j) + 1. The known ones are 3, 5, 17, 257 and 65 537. 44l works the list by hand.

The cellar · there if you want it

The accepted names

Constructible numbers are those reached by ruler and compass. The power-of-two technique is Wantzel’s theorem. The three old problems are doubling the cube, trisecting the angle, and the regular heptagon; the fourth, squaring the circle, needs π to be transcendental. The sums ηᵢ, αᵢ, βᵢ are Gaussian periods, and the primes 2^(2^j) + 1 are Fermat primes. The test for which n-gons can be built is the Gauss–Wantzel theorem. Φ is the 17th cyclotomic polynomial.

Who said it first

Carl Friedrich Gauss found the construction of the 17-gon on 30 March 1796, at eighteen, by the periods of this step, and published it in the Disquisitiones Arithmeticae (1801), with the sufficient condition for every n. Pierre Wantzel proved in 1837 that the doubled cube and the trisection are impossible, and completed the condition for n. Ferdinand von Lindemann proved π transcendental in 1882. Friedrich Julius Richelot published a construction of the 257-gon in 1832, and Johann Gustav Hermes spent ten years on the 65 537-gon; his manuscript is kept at Göttingen.

A proof that is not allowed

“cos 20° is a root of 8x³ − 6x − 1, a cubic, so it cannot be constructed.” Not allowed as it stands: a cubic can have constructible roots: x³ − 2x² − x + 2 = (x − 2)(x² − 1) has the roots 2, 1 and −1. The proof needs the cubic to be irreducible over ℚ, which is the line checking that ±1, and so every rational, is not a root of y³ − 3y − 1.

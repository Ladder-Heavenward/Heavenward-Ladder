45·∎ · conclusion

Many Machines, One Answer

A machine knows only what it has been told. From that, prove what can be agreed, what cannot, and what chance and counting buy back.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 45l is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 45 promised to say what machines that only talk can agree on. 45a set the model, and 45b proved the generals’ plight. 45c built handshakes, 45d ordered events, and 45e committed together. 45f and 45g agreed despite crashes and liars, and 45h by lottery. 45i spread news, 45j found keys, 45k merged edits, and 45l split jobs. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

A machine’s move is a function of its view, so a crashed machine and a slow one look the same when delay is unbounded. No finite plan over a lossy link meets the generals’ three demands. Resending until acknowledged delivers every message with chance 1, and sequence numbers compare on a clock of 2³² while fewer than 2³¹ are in flight. If e → f then L(e) < L(f), and e → f exactly when V(e) is below V(f) in every place and differs. Two-phase commit never finishes two ways and commits only on every yes, but may wait when its coordinator dies.

Any two sets of more than n/2 machines meet. With no bound on delay and one possible crash, no fixed method meets agreement, validity and termination in every run. With forgeable messages, f liars need more than 3f machines, and quorums of 2f + 1 among 3f + 1 share f + 1. An attacker z blocks behind catches up with chance (q/p)ᶻ. In pulling gossip, a fraction u not knowing becomes u² in expectation. On a ring, a join or leave moves only the keys just before it. Merges that are associative, commutative and idempotent make copies with the same updates equal. An associative combine gives one answer for every split, and a part s in a row caps the speedup below 1/s.

u \mapsto u^2 @@ the squaring-pull technique (45i3)
\text{only the keys just before move} @@ the ring-move technique (45j1)

So the rung’s claim holds: what many machines can agree on is fixed by what each can see, and counting, chance and algebra buy back what certainty cannot.

Shapes Are Read in Their Rings

Polynomials cut out shapes, and shapes give back ideals. Ideals in finitely many letters are finitely made; over ℂ no zero means 1, points are largest ideals, and shapes match the root ideals. Whole shapes are primes, maps run backwards, dimension counts chains, good makers decide membership, and two curves of degrees m and n share mn points.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 45 asked what the ring of a shape knows about the shape. Shapes are read in their rings: every question about a shape cut out by polynomials is a question about an ideal, and over the turning numbers the answer is exact.

45a made ideals finitely made, 45b added letters, 45c cut out shapes, and 45d matched shapes with root ideals over ℂ. 45e split shapes into whole pieces, 45f read a shape in its ring, 45g measured dimension, 45h divided with several letters, 45i added the points at infinity, 45j counted shared points, and 45k found where a curve crosses itself. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

Every ideal of F[x₁, …, xₙ] is finitely made, so every system of polynomial equations has the answers of finitely many of them. V(fg) = V(f) ∪ V(g) and V(I + J) = V(I) ∩ V(J). Over ℂ, V(J) is empty exactly when 1 ∈ J; the largest ideals of ℂ[x₁, …, xₙ] are the (x₁ − a₁, …, xₙ − aₙ); and I(V(J)) = √J.

A shape is whole exactly when its ideal is prime, and every shape is a union of finitely many whole shapes, in one way. Polynomial maps X → Y match ring maps F[Y] → F[X]; the parabola is a line, and y² = x³ is not. dim ℂⁿ = n, and one equation cuts a whole shape’s dimension by one.

A good set of makers gives one remainder, 0 exactly for members, and with letters ordered first removes them: x = t², y = t³ gives y² = x³. In the projective plane any two lines meet once, every unsplit conic is one curve, and curves of degrees m and n with no shared piece meet in mn points; two circles share the circular points at infinity. A point where f, f_x and f_y vanish has its tangents in the lowest terms; a crossing cubic is traced by one letter, and a smooth curve of degree d has (d − 1)(d − 2)/2 handles.

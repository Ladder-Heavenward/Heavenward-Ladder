Mathematics floor

Rungs 31 to 37. You arrive with spaces and chance, and with tools you have tried on every kind of question. Here the tools become the things you study: functions, shapes, infinities and proofs, picked up and turned over like stones on a beach. The guesses grow bolder, and so does what you can check.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

On this floor, the things you have been working with become the things you work on. Functions become points in a space with endlessly many directions. Shapes are studied for what survives stretching. Infinities are compared. In the end, proofs themselves become objects.

Rungs 31 to 33 give functions lengths and angles, break a repeating function into turnings, and use rates in space and time to describe heat and waves. Rung 34 keeps nearness and throws away distance, and finds that a loop can count holes. Rung 35 compares the sizes of endless sets. Rung 36 asks what a colour is, and the answer is a blindness: an eye keeps three numbers out of endlessly many, and bends them onto a circle so that the ends meet.

Rung 37 does something the ladder has been preparing since rung 0. It writes down what a statement, a structure and a proof are, as mathematical objects, and asks what arithmetic can say about itself.

On this floor, mathematics turns its tools on itself, and finds both its reach and its limits.

The people who have climbed this far will be the first to tell you how much is still ahead of them. They are right, and so is the climb that brought you here.

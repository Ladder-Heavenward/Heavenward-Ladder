Mathematics floor

Rungs 31 to 36. You arrive with spaces and chance, and with tools you have tried on every kind of question. Here the tools become the things you study: functions, shapes, infinities and proofs, picked up and turned over like stones on a beach. The guesses grow bolder, and so does what you can check.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

On this floor, the things you have been working with become the things you work on. Functions become points in a space with endlessly many directions. Shapes are studied for what survives stretching. Infinities are compared. In the end, proofs themselves become objects.

Rungs 31 to 33 give functions lengths and angles, break a repeating function into turnings, and use rates in space and time to describe heat and waves. Rung 34 keeps nearness and throws away distance, and finds that a loop can count holes. Rung 35 compares the sizes of endless sets.

Rung 36 does something the ladder has been preparing since rung 0. It writes down what a statement, a structure and a proof are, as mathematical objects, and asks what arithmetic can say about itself.

On this floor, mathematics turns its tools on itself, and finds both its reach and its limits.

The people who have climbed this far will be the first to tell you how much is still ahead of them. They are right, and so is the climb that brought you here.

The rungs of this floor

  • 31 · functions are arrows with lengths and angles
  • 32 · every periodic function is a sum of turnings
  • 33 · rates in space and time govern heat and waves
  • 34 · nearness survives without distance
  • 35 · infinities come in sizes
  • 36 · arithmetic describes its own proofs

At the top of the floor is its conclusion, and beside it the floor’s certification: one fresh question a rung, about an hour in all. It covers the floor’s last rung too, so that rung’s last step needs no certification of its own. Passing it gives you the title mathematician, and the Advanced floor opens above. Like every title on the ladder, it is a word for what you have begun, not for what you have finished.

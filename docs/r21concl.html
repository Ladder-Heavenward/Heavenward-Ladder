21·∎ · conclusion

An Angle Is a Power of i

An angle is a statement that names a distance walked around the circle of radius 1, and cosine and sine are where the walk ends. Turning adds angles, flipping reverses them, and each coordinate changes at the rate of the other.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 21g the slopes of sin and cos is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 21 asked how far round a turn goes, and promised that the answer would be a length. An angle is a statement that names a distance walked around the circle of radius 1, and cosine and sine are where the walk ends. Everything else on the rung is that sentence, worked out.

21a measured the arc and named π. 21b defined cos and sin as coordinates. 21c kept the point on the circle. 21d added arcs by turning. 21e flipped them. 21f compared a small arc with its height. 21g found the slopes. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

Measured as arc length, angles add when turns are combined, and the coordinates they reach change at rates given by each other.

Let L(x) = ∫ₓ¹ dt/√(1 − t²) and π = L(−1).

Let (cos θ, sin θ) be the point of x² + y² = 1 at arc θ from (1, 0), anticlockwise, for every real θ.

Then around ÷ across = π for every circle.

cos² θ + sin² θ = 1, and both repeat every 2π.

cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B, and sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B.

cos(−θ) = cos θ, sin(−θ) = −sin θ, and cos(π/2 − θ) = sin θ.

sin h / h → 1 as h → 0.

sin′ = cos and cos′ = −sin, and every f with f″ = −f is f(0) cos + f′(0) sin.

Measure an angle in quarter turns and it becomes an exponent: the call back to 20·cb8, when you reach it, shows the point at t quarter turns is the quarter turn raised to the power t. That is what it means to say an angle is a power of i.

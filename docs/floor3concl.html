What the Derivation Floor Shows

Change at an instant and total over a stretch are both limits, and each one undoes the other.

The floor conclusion · its principles, in the words and marks it built

This page opens once every step of the Derivation floor is ticked. Its principles are proved on the rungs below, so it waits for them.

What the floor set out to show

The Derivation floor took the limit and asked two questions with it. How fast is a function changing at one input? How much does it gather over a stretch? It promised to answer both with proof, and to find out how the answers are related.

Here are the floor’s five principles again, one to a rung, each in the words and the marks the floor built. Each links to the rung conclusion that proves it.

The principles, rung by rung

12 · skipping nothing on the way

A function continuous on a closed interval skips no value, and reaches a highest and a lowest one.

13 · measuring change in an instant

The slope at a point is the limit of the chord slopes.

14 · reading a shape from its slopes

The change between two heights is a slope the function really has between them, times the step.

15 · measuring what is under a curve

Exactly one number sits between every too-little and every too-much, and it is the area.

16 · running change backwards

Area and slope undo each other.

What the floor shows

The floor ran on two kinds of limit. The slope is the limit of chord slopes as the step shrinks. The area is the one number squeezed between boxes under the graph and boxes over it. Rung 12 made both safe: a continuous curve on a closed interval skips nothing and runs off nowhere.

On the Derivation floor, change at an instant and total over a stretch both became limits, and rung 16 proved that each one undoes the other.

Rung 14 was the hinge. It turned what a slope says at each input into what the heights do between two inputs. With that, the slope of the area so far is the height, and the area under a slope is the change from end to end.

So the floor demonstrates that two questions people asked apart for centuries are one question asked in two directions. The kit now holds every technique for slopes and areas proved from a single limit each. It is a small kit still, and the ladder has only begun to use it.

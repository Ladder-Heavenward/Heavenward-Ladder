What the Mathematics floor shows

Every tool the ladder built is itself a thing to measure, and a proof is a number arithmetic can talk about.

The floor conclusion · its principles, in the words and marks it built

This page opens once every step of the Mathematics floor is ticked. Its principles are proved on the rungs below, so it waits for them.

What the floor set out to show

The Statistics floor made the endless settle and made chance a size. The Mathematics floor picked up the tools themselves and turned them over: functions as arrows, repeating functions as sums of turnings, rates that run through space and time, nearness with no distance, endless sets of different sizes, the colours an eye makes of a spectrum, and at the top, proofs as objects. It promised to make each of them exact, and to prove every answer.

Here are the floor’s seven principles again, one to a rung, each in the words and the marks the floor built. Each links to the rung conclusion that proves it.

The principles, rung by rung

31 · one product gives functions lengths, angles and closest points

One product, straight, indifferent to order and positive, gives every arrow a size and every two arrows an angle, and in a space with no holes every closed flat piece has a closest point, the shadow, whose miss stands at right angles.

32 · a periodic function is the sum of its shadows

The turnings are a right-angled list that leaves no direction out, so every periodic function with finite energy is the sum of its shadows on them, and its energy is the sum of the squares of their sizes.

33 · heat forgets, waves keep, still water averages

Heat smooths and forgets, waves carry and keep, and still water is the average of its surroundings.

34 · stretching keeps what open sets decide

Continuity needs only which sets are open, so being in one piece, being finitely covered and keeping points apart are decided by the open sets too, and the loops of a space, slid without breaking, count its holes.

35 · infinities come in sizes

Two sets are the same size when they can be matched; the counting numbers cannot be matched with the line, and every set is smaller than its set of parts.

36 · colour is a blindness coloured in

A colour is three numbers an eye keeps out of endlessly many, bent onto a circle so that the ends meet, and the spectrum behind it is still there.

37 · arithmetic describes its own proofs

What is reached from a collection of axioms is exactly what is true in every world where they hold; and arithmetic, describing its own proofs, has a true statement it does not reach, cannot reach its own freedom from clash, and cannot define its own truth.

What the floor shows

The floor began by giving functions a length and an angle. Rung 31 found that one product does it, and that closest points are shadows. Rung 32 broke every repeating function into turnings at right angles, and rung 33 let rates in space and time decide how heat forgets and waves keep. Rung 34 kept nearness and threw away distance, and still counted holes. Rung 35 matched endless sets and found that they come in sizes. Rung 36 asked what a colour is, and found a blindness: three numbers kept out of endlessly many, bent into a circle.

On the Mathematics floor, the tools of the ladder became things to study, and a proof itself became an object that arithmetic can describe.

Rung 37 turned the floor’s tools on the ladder’s own first steps. A statement, a world and a proof are mathematical objects. What is reached from axioms is what is true in every world where they hold, and arithmetic, written about itself, has a true statement it cannot reach. The guesswork that rung 0 began with words has become a thing with edges, and the edges are now known.

So the floor demonstrates that mathematics can study its own machinery and say exactly where that machinery stops. You have the tools now, and the next floor sends them out to the edge of what is known.

The floor’s certification

Beside this page on the ladder is the floor’s certification: one question a rung, about an hour, of fresh questions. Sit it under a proctor and it becomes a green check. Sit it on your own and it becomes a gold star. Passing it gives you the title mathematician, and the Advanced floor opens above. Like every title on the ladder, it is a word for what you have begun, not for what you have finished.

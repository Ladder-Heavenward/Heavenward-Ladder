39·∎ · conclusion

A machine made of switches

True is 1 and false is 0. Switches with gain build every table from not-and, an odd ring of nots is a clock and an even ring a memory. Gates add, and a machine follows a list of numbers in its own memory. Its turns are counted, and their family survives a change of machine.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on r39o is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 39 asked how the tables of true and false can be built in matter, and what a machine built of them can do in how many turns. Every table of n inputs is built from not-and gates alone, made of switches worked by switches whose slope above 1 keeps every level clean; an odd ring of nots is a clock and an even ring a memory; with them a machine runs any list of instructions stored as numbers, and the turns it takes grow in a family that a change of machine does not change.

39a wrote true as 1 and false as 0. 39b read a wire as the sign of a turning, against a threshold, wrong at a known rate, and 39c told power from message. 39d found what valves cannot do, and 39e the switch that can. 39f built every table from not-and. 39g built the clock and 39h the memory; 39i added; 39j built the machine that follows a list. 39k counted its turns, 39l searched and sorted, 39m solved counts in pieces, 39n kept data findable, and 39o kept answers. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

On {0, 1}, both is xy, either is the greater of x and y, not is 1 − x, and exactly one is the remainder of x + y by 2. A level with margin d under bell noise of spread σ is misread with the chance that the bell lies past d/σ spreads; a wire passing frequencies up to B carries at most 2B log₂ M bits a second with M levels, and a known wave carries none. Networks of diodes and resistors never give a not and never shrink a rise; a pair inverter with |g′(m)| > 1 restores its levels, and noise along a chain stays below η/(1 − k). Every table of n inputs is built from at most 5 · 2ⁿ⁻¹ − 4 not-ands, and some table needs more than g of them when (n + 2 + g)^(2g + 1) < 2^(2ⁿ).

A ring of an odd number n of nots, each with delay τ, has no rest that holds and runs with period 2nτ; an even ring holds one of two patterns, and two latches catch a bit at the rise of a clock. A row of n places adds with a carry found in about log₂ n levels, and subtracts by adding 2ⁿ − y. A machine whose state is a list of numbers, with instructions stored as numbers among its data, follows any list placed in its memory. A count O(f(n)) on one machine is O(f(n)) on a machine that does each turn in c turns; search by yes-or-no questions needs log₂(n + 1) questions; the random-pivot sort and the random search tree make 2(n + 1)Hₙ − 4n comparisons on average; T(n) = T(αn) + T(βn) + cn with α + β < 1 grows in proportion to n; a heap has height ⌊log₂ n⌋; a recursion on sizes below n, with answers kept, costs the number of sizes times one step.

So the rung’s claim holds: the tables of true and false are built of switches, kept clean by gain, made into clocks, memories, adders and a machine that follows a list, whose turns are counted and ranked. Held in trust: that every reasonable machine mimics every other within a fixed factor or a power, until rung 40, and the capacity of a noisy wire, from 30·comp.

That is a machine made of switches. It follows any list it is given, as fast as its switches flip, in a count of turns that can be ranked. Rung 40 asks what no list can do.

Quantifying Over Sets

Rung four did not say that induction holds for every formula. It said something stronger: the numbers are what is in every inductive set, so any set that holds zero and is closed under next holds every number. Rung eight did not say that every bounded formula has a least upper bound. It said every bounded set does. Both principles speak of every set, and the language of this rung speaks only of members. That is why its worlds can be strange, with numbers past every numeral and lines with holes. So let the language speak of sets too. Add placeholders for parts of the world, and let every and some run over all of them. Then induction is one statement, and its worlds are all copies of the numbers: the strange worlds are gone. The price is high. The new language has no calculus that reaches all its truths, the finite-parts technique fails for it, and what “every part” means is handed back to the theory of sets, with all its open questions.

With placeholders for sets, induction and the least upper bound are single statements, and their worlds are all matched with the numbers and the line. The price is that no recipe can list what is true in every such world, and the meaning of “every set” comes from the theory of sets.

38l1Placeholders for Sets

Add to a language a second kind of placeholder, X₀, X₁, …, and the formulas X(t): “t is in X”. Let every and some bind these too. In a world M, ∀X φ is true when φ is true for every part of |M|, every member of 2g’s set of all subsets. Its accepted name is second-order logic; the logic of 38a is first-order.

Then 4b’s principle is one statement, and so is 8c’s:

\forall X\,\big((X(0) \land \forall x\,(X(x) \Rightarrow X(S(x)))) \Rightarrow \forall x\,X(x)\big) @@ induction: every part that holds 0 and is closed under next holds everything (4b)
\htmlClass{new}{\forall X\,\big((\exists x\,X(x) \land \exists u\,\forall x\,(X(x) \Rightarrow x \le u)) \Rightarrow \exists v\,(v \text{ is a least upper bound of } X)\big)} \quad \blacksquare @@ the least upper bound: every bounded part that has a member (8c)

Call PA₂ the two next lines of PA with second-order induction in place of the endless list of first-order inductions.

38l2One World, up to Matching

Only-the-numbers technique: any two worlds of PA₂ are matched by a matching that carries 0 and next.

M, M' \models PA_2 @@ given
h = \text{the smallest set of pairs holding } (0^M, 0^{M'}) \text{ and closed under } (x, y) \mapsto (S(x), S'(y)) @@ the smallest-closed-set technique (4d), in |M| × |M′|
\{x : \text{exactly one } y \text{ has } (x, y) \in h\} \text{ holds } 0^M \text{ and is closed under } S @@ 0 is nobody’s next and next is one-to-one, in M′ and in M; where-each-pair-comes-from (4d)
h \text{ is a function on all of } |M| @@ second-order induction in M, on that part
\text{the outputs of } h \text{ hold } 0^{M'} \text{ and are closed under } S' @@ h is closed
h \text{ is onto } |M'| @@ second-order induction in M′, on the outputs
\{x : h(x) = h(x') \Rightarrow x = x' \text{ for every } x'\} \text{ holds } 0^M \text{ and is closed under } S @@ next one-to-one, 0 nobody’s next, both worlds
\htmlClass{new}{h \text{ is a matching that carries } 0 \text{ and next}} \quad \blacksquare @@ second-order induction in M, once more

Adding and multiplying are fixed by their lines, by induction, so h carries them too. So every world of PA₂ is a copy of N. The worlds of 38f, with members past every numeral, are not worlds of PA₂. The same can be done for the line: the field techniques, the order, and the single statement of the least upper bound have only R as a world, up to matching. 38l.6.3 asks for the outline.

38l3Why the First-Order List Is Not Enough

38f built a world M where every first-order induction is true and some member is past every numeral. Why is second-order induction false there? Because the part of |M| holding the values of the numerals holds 0, is closed under next, and is not all of |M|. 38f3 showed no formula picks out that part. First-order induction speaks only of the parts some formula picks out.

\text{the formulas of arithmetic can be listed} @@ the proof-listing technique’s listing of strings (38b3)
\text{each formula, with members for its other placeholders, picks out one part} @@ in a listed world, countably many parts in all
\text{the parts of an endless listed world cannot be listed} @@ more parts than members (35c)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{first-order induction covers few parts; second-order covers all}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the numerals’ part in 38f’s world is one it misses

38l4The Price

No-full-calculus technique: no recipe lists exactly the second-order statements true in every world.

\varphi \text{ a first-order statement of arithmetic} @@ given
N \models \varphi \iff (PA_2 \Rightarrow \varphi) \text{ is true in every world} @@ every world of PA₂ is matched with N (38l2), and matched worlds share statements (38a4)
\text{if a recipe listed the statements true in every world, it would list the truths of } N @@ list them, and keep those of the form PA₂ ⇒ φ
\text{the truths of } N \text{ cannot be listed by a recipe} @@ 38i3
\htmlClass{new}{\text{no such recipe}} \quad \blacksquare @@ refute

So second-order logic has no calculus of 38b’s kind that reaches all its truths: the way back of 38d fails. The finite-parts technique fails too. Every finite part of PA₂ together with all the demands n̄ < c has a world, N with c large; the whole has none, since every world of PA₂ is N.

And “every part” must come from somewhere. It means every member of 𝒫(|M|), as the theory of sets supplies it. Whether 35f’s in-between size exists can be written as a question about which second-order statements are true in every world, and 35f reported that the axioms of sets cannot settle it. Categoricity was bought by handing the hard questions to the theory of sets.

The cellar · there if you want it

The accepted names

Set placeholders are second-order variables, and reading ∀X over all subsets is the full, or standard, semantics of second-order logic; reading it over a chosen family of parts is Henkin semantics, under which completeness and compactness return. A theory with only one model up to isomorphism is categorical, and the only-the-numbers technique is Dedekind’s categoricity theorem for second-order Peano arithmetic; the line’s is the categoricity of the complete ordered field. The no-full-calculus technique says second-order validity is not recursively enumerable.

Who said it first

Richard Dedekind proved categoricity for the numbers in “What are numbers and what should they be?” (1888). Gottlob Frege’s logic of 1879 was already second-order. Thoralf Skolem argued from 1922 that first-order logic is the right frame for set theory, and Ernst Zermelo argued against him in 1929 and 1930; Leon Henkin gave second-order logic its general semantics in 1950. Stewart Shapiro’s Foundations without Foundationalism (1991) is the modern case for the full semantics.

A proof that is not allowed

“PA₂ has one world. A collection with one world is complete. So the calculus of 38b, with second-order moves added, reaches every truth of PA₂.” Not allowed: completeness on this rung is the reached-if-true-everywhere technique, and it was proved for first-order languages only. For second-order statements, the no-full-calculus technique shows no recipe can do it.

42l · technique

Counting Up to Turning

A necklace of four beads, each black or white, can be strung in 16 ways, but turning the necklace makes some of them the same. Counting what is left by listing is easy for four beads and hopeless for forty. The symmetries form a group (19f). Count, for each symmetry, the colourings it leaves alone, and average: the average is the number of different necklaces. This step proves the averaging count, counts necklaces, and counts the ways to paint a cube.

Let a group G act on a set of colourings. Each family of colourings that turn into one another counts once. Then the number of families is (1/|G|) Σ_g (colourings g leaves alone). Four-bead necklaces: (16 + 2 + 4 + 2)/4 = 6. Cubes with faces black or white: 240/24 = 10.

42l1Reach Times Keepers

\text{keepers of } x:\ K_x = \{g : gx = x\},\ \text{a part-group} @@ closed under products and undoing
gx = hx \iff h^{-1}g \in K_x @@ same place, same coset
|\text{places } x \text{ reaches}| = \text{number of cosets of } K_x @@ one place per coset (19d)
\htmlClass{new}{|\text{reach of } x| \cdot |K_x| = |G|} @@ place-then-count, for any action (19f)

Reach-keepers technique: for every x, the number of places the group sends x to, times the number of symmetries that keep x, is the size of the group.

42l2Average What Each Symmetry Keeps

\textstyle\sum_g |\{x : gx = x\}| = \sum_x |K_x| @@ the same pairs, counted two ways (4k)
= \textstyle\sum_x \dfrac{|G|}{|\text{reach of } x|} @@ reach-keepers
\text{each family adds } |G| \text{ in all} @@ its members share one reach
\htmlClass{new}{\text{families} = \dfrac{1}{|G|}\textstyle\sum_g |\{x : gx = x\}|} @@ the averaging count

Averaging technique: the number of families of colourings is the average, over the symmetries, of the number of colourings each symmetry leaves alone.

42l3Necklaces and Cubes

\text{4 beads, turns by } 0, 1, 2, 3:\ 16, 2, 4, 2 @@ a turn by 1 keeps only all-black and all-white
\tfrac14(16 + 2 + 4 + 2) = 6 @@ 0000, 0001, 0011, 0101, 0111, 1111
\text{cube, 24 turns: } 64 + 6\cdot 8 + 3\cdot 16 + 8\cdot 4 + 6\cdot 8 = 240 @@ by the axes: none, faces 90°, faces 180°, corners, edges
\htmlClass{new}{240/24 = 10 \text{ different cubes}} @@ from 64 paintings

Counting up to symmetry is an average of what each symmetry keeps; a symmetry keeps a colouring when it is one colour along each cycle the symmetry moves round. Chemists count molecules this way, the different ways to place atoms on a frame.

Accepted names

Families are orbits, keepers the stabiliser; reach times keepers is the orbit-stabiliser theorem; the averaging count is Burnside’s lemma, also the Cauchy-Frobenius lemma. Counting by cycles is Pólya enumeration.

Who said it first

Augustin Cauchy (1845) and Georg Frobenius (1887) knew the averaging count; William Burnside printed it in 1897. George Pólya built counting by cycles in 1937, for chemistry.

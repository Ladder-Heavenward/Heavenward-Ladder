20·cb9 · newsflash for 9d

The inverse’s pairs, flipped

9d ran a function backwards by swapping the two entries of every pair. 9e drew the swapped pairs and said they looked like the graph seen in a mirror along the diagonal, and left open whether swapping really is a mirror. It is. Swapping the entries is the flip across the diagonal where the two coordinates agree, so the graph of the backwards function is the graph of the function, flipped.

The flip across that diagonal swaps the two coordinates. So the graph of the backwards function is the flip of the graph, and its equation comes from swapping the two letters.

What this calls back to, and what it stands on

Calls back to 9d (running backwards, swapped pairs) and 9e (graphs, the column test). Stands on 20c (flips) and 20i (moving graphs).

20·cb9.1Swapping is a flip

Stands on 20c3 (the flip formula) and 9d (swapped pairs).

Swapping the two entries of every point is the flip across the line y = x. Take u = (1/√2, 1/√2), along that line, in 20c’s flip formula.

F(x, y) = 2\big((x, y) \cdot u\big)u - (x, y) @@ 20c3
= 2 \cdot \tfrac{x + y}{\sqrt 2} \cdot \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(1, 1) - (x, y) @@ u = (1, 1)/√2
= (x + y,\ x + y) - (x, y) = (y,\ x) @@ share out
\htmlClass{new}{\text{graph of } f^{-1} = F(\text{graph of } f)} \quad \blacksquare @@ 9d: (u, v) ∈ f exactly when (v, u) ∈ f⁻¹

So 9e’s mirror was a real one. Every distance on the graph of f is a distance on the graph of f⁻¹.

20·cb9.2The equation, swapped

Stands on 20·cb9.1 and 20i1 (the equation moves the other way).

The equation of the flipped graph is the old equation with x and y swapped. The flip undoes itself, so by 20i1 it goes into the equation as it is. Solving the swapped equation for y gives the formula of f⁻¹.

y = x^3:\ \ x = y^3 \Rightarrow y = \sqrt[3]{x} @@ 8f's odd roots; one answer for every x
y = x^2,\ x \ge 0:\ \ x = y^2,\ y \ge 0 \Rightarrow y = \sqrt{x} @@ the part with x ≥ 0 is one-to-one
\htmlClass{new}{y = f(x) \leadsto x = f(y)} @@ then solve for y

20·cb9.3The column test, flipped

Stands on 20·cb9.1 and 9e (the column test).

A function has a backwards function exactly when every level line meets its graph at most once. The flip sends columns to rows. So the flipped graph passes the column test exactly when the graph passes the same test along rows: that is one-to-one, seen.

F(\{x = c\}) = \{y = c\} @@ the flip sends the column at c to the row at c
\text{flipped graph meets each column once} \iff \text{graph meets each row once} @@ F keeps how many points meet
\htmlClass{new}{f \text{ one-to-one} \iff \text{each row meets the graph at most once}} \quad \blacksquare

The accepted names

The graph of an inverse function is the reflection of the graph in the line y = x. The row test is the horizontal line test. A function that is its own inverse is an involution.

What Are the Arrows Between Arrows Between Arrows?

What are the arrows between arrows between arrows? Categories have maps between them, and rung 47 found natural maps between those maps. That is three levels: things, arrows, and arrows between arrows. Arrows between arrows run in a row two ways, along and across, and one condition ties the two ways together. When two ways of combining share a do-nothing and obey that condition, they turn out to be the same way, and it turns about. So the second loop group of any space turns about, though rung 34’s first loop group need not. The levels go on without end, and ∞Ε’s pentagon was the first of their conditions. A full theory of the tower exists, a second was built from its conditions alone in 2022, and in 2023 its arrows-into technique was checked by machine.

Natural maps α : F ⇒ G, α′ : G ⇒ G″ between F, G, G″ : C → D, and β : H ⇒ K, β′ : K ⇒ K″ between maps D → E. Along: α′ ∘ α. Across: (β ∗ α)_X = β_{GX} ∘ H(α_X). Interchange: (β′ ∘ β) ∗ (α′ ∘ α) = (β′ ∗ α′) ∘ (β ∗ α). Two operations with one unit e and (a ∗ b) ∘ (c ∗ d) = (a ∘ c) ∗ (b ∘ d) are equal and turn about. So loop groups from the second level on turn about.

inf26.1Along and Across

47c1 ran two natural maps one after the other at each thing: that is along. There is a second way. If α : F ⇒ G is a natural map between maps C → D, and β : H ⇒ K one between maps D → E, then going through both levels at once gives a natural map β ∗ α from H after F to K after G, across: at X it is β at GX after H of α at X.

Interchange technique: for α, α′ along on one side and β, β′ along on the other, (β′ ∘ β) ∗ (α′ ∘ α) = (β′ ∗ α′) ∘ (β ∗ α). Four arrows in a two-by-two grid can be combined rows first or columns first.

\text{left side at } X = \beta'_{G''X}\, \beta_{G''X}\, H(\alpha'_X)\, H(\alpha_X) @@ across, after doing each side along
\text{right side at } X = \beta'_{G''X}\, K(\alpha'_X)\, \beta_{GX}\, H(\alpha_X) @@ along, after doing each pair across
K(\alpha'_X)\, \beta_{GX} = \beta_{G''X}\, H(\alpha'_X) @@ β is natural, read at the arrow α′_X : GX → G″X
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the two sides agree at every } X} \quad \blacksquare @@ put the third line into the second

inf26.2Two Ways That Are One

Take a set with two ways of combining, ∘ and ∗, that share one do-nothing e and obey the interchange condition (a ∗ b) ∘ (c ∗ d) = (a ∘ c) ∗ (b ∘ d). Nothing else is assumed: not that either way has free brackets, not that either turns about.

Two-become-one technique: then a ∘ b = a ∗ b, and both turn about.

a \circ b = (a \ast e) \circ (e \ast b) @@ e does nothing for ∗
= (a \circ e) \ast (e \circ b) = a \ast b @@ interchange, then e does nothing for ∘
a \circ b = (e \ast a) \circ (b \ast e) @@ e does nothing for ∗, the other way
= (e \circ b) \ast (a \circ e) = b \ast a @@ interchange, then e
\htmlClass{new}{a \circ b = a \ast b = b \ast a = b \circ a} \quad \blacksquare @@ the second line and the fourth

Adding and multiplying numbers share no do-nothing and fail the condition: (1 · 2) + (3 · 4) is 14 and (1 + 3) · (2 + 4) is 24. The technique needs both demands, and when it has them it leaves no room for two different ways.

inf26.3Second Loops Turn About

34g2 made the loops at a point into a group, and that group need not turn about: on a figure eight, going round the left ring then the right is not the same as the other order. Now take squares instead of loops: maps from a square into the space that send the whole rim to the base point, up to sliding. Two such squares can be set side by side, or one above the other.

Second-loops technique: the squares, with side by side and one above the other, form a group that turns about.

\text{side by side} = \ast,\ \ \text{one above the other} = \circ @@ two ways of combining squares
\text{the square sent wholly to the base point does nothing for both} @@ one shared do-nothing
\text{four squares in a two-by-two grid: rows first or columns first} @@ the same map on the big square: interchange
\htmlClass{new}{\ast = \circ,\ \text{and it turns about}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the two-become-one technique

The same holds for cubes and every higher level: from the second level up, every loop group turns about. In a space, the ways of sliding one loop to another are arrows between arrows, and this footing is the first place the tower changes the answer.

inf26.4Levels Without End

∞Ε.4 found that regrouping three things is an arrow, not an equation, and that the arrows for four things close a pentagon. ∞Ε.5 found that once the pentagon holds, every face of the solid for five things closes. Each level’s condition is an arrow one level up. 47·now.1 built ways of being the same and ways between those ways, with no top.

Every-level technique: in a tower of arrows between arrows, at every level from the second up, combining in any two directions obeys interchange with a shared do-nothing, so it turns about.

\text{level } k \ge 2\text{: at least two directions to combine in} @@ a k-dimensional cube has k directions
\text{any two directions: interchange, by the grid of four} @@ as for squares
\text{the cube sent wholly to the point does nothing in every direction} @@ shared do-nothing
\htmlClass{new}{\text{every level from the second turns about}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the two-become-one technique, once per pair

What the tower needs at the top of each level is a condition that the ways of regrouping agree, one level up: the pentagon at the third level, the solid of five things at the fourth, and so on, with no last condition. A definition of the whole tower that a beginner can use is still being sought.

How we got here

In 2022 two mathematicians published a book building the theory of the tower from its conditions alone, so that every technique proved in it holds in each of the models that had been used before.

In 2023 three mathematicians checked by machine the arrows-into technique of 47g for the tower, the first such check of a technique of this theory, in a new checker built on 47·now’s calculus of ways of being the same.

The accepted names

Natural maps between maps of categories are 2-morphisms in the 2-category Cat; along is vertical composition, across is horizontal composition or whiskering, and the condition is the interchange law. The two-become-one technique is the Eckmann–Hilton argument; the squares form the second homotopy group π₂, abelian. The tower is an ∞-category or (∞, 1)-category; its conditions at each level are coherences; the arrows-into technique is the ∞-categorical Yoneda lemma. The 2022 book is model-independent ∞-cosmos theory; the 2023 checker is Rzk, for the simplicial extension of homotopy type theory.

Who said it first

26i · technique

Maps That Keep Angles

A function with a complex slope does something to the plane that no other map does as cheaply: near each point where its slope is not 0 it acts as a turn and a stretch, the same in every direction. So it keeps the angle at which any two curves cross. This step proves that from 26d’s complex slope, finds where the keeping fails, and uses angle-keeping maps to carry a hard shape onto an easy one, which is how the flow of air past a wing was first computed.

Near z₀, f(z₀ + w) ≈ f(z₀) + f′(z₀)w, and multiplying by f′(z₀) = re^(iφ) stretches by r and turns by φ. Two curves through z₀ are both turned by φ, so their angle is kept wherever f′ ≠ 0. Where f′ = 0, as for z² at 0, angles are multiplied.

26i1A Turn and a Stretch Up Close

Angle-keeping technique: if f has a complex slope at z₀ and f′(z₀) ≠ 0, then any two curves crossing at z₀ at an angle θ are carried to curves crossing at f(z₀) at the same angle θ, turned the same way.

\gamma(t),\ \gamma(0) = z_0:\ (f \circ \gamma)^{\prime}(0) = f^{\prime}(z_0)\,\gamma^{\prime}(0) @@ the chain technique for complex slopes
f^{\prime}(z_0) = re^{i\varphi}:\ \text{every arrow at } z_0 \text{ is stretched by } r, \text{ turned by } \varphi @@ multiplying (26a)
\arg\big((f\circ\gamma_2)^{\prime}\big) - \arg\big((f\circ\gamma_1)^{\prime}\big) = \arg \gamma_2^{\prime} - \arg \gamma_1^{\prime} @@ φ cancels
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the crossing angle, and its sense, are kept}} \quad \blacksquare @@ f′(z₀) ≠ 0 so the arrows are not crushed to 0

From here on, an angle-keeping map is a function with a complex slope that is never 0 on the region considered.

26i2Where Keeping Fails

f(z) = z^2,\ f^{\prime}(0) = 0 @@ the slope vanishes
re^{i\theta} \mapsto r^2 e^{2i\theta} @@ angles from 0 are doubled
\text{two rays at } 90^\circ \mapsto \text{rays at } 180^\circ @@ a corner opened flat
\htmlClass{new}{\text{elsewhere, } f^{\prime}(z) = 2z \ne 0: \text{ angles kept}} @@ the doubling only at 0

At a point where the slope is 0 and the first nonzero term of the series is the k-th power, angles are multiplied by k. That flaw is a tool: z² opens a quarter plane into a half plane.

26i3Hard Shapes Onto Easy Ones

z \mapsto z^2:\ \text{quarter plane} \to \text{half plane} @@ angles at 0 doubled
z \mapsto \dfrac{z - i}{z + i}:\ \text{upper half plane} \to \text{the unit disc} @@ distance to i over distance to −i is below 1
z \mapsto \tfrac12\big(z + \tfrac1z\big):\ \text{a slightly shifted circle} \to \text{a wing’s outline} @@ Joukowski, 1910
\htmlClass{new}{\text{solve on the easy shape, carry the answer back}} @@ the easy answer comes back along the map

A still flow of air solved round a circle, carried back by Joukowski’s map, gives the flow round a wing. Every region of the plane without holes, short of the whole plane, can be carried onto the unit disc by an angle-keeping map; that theorem, Riemann’s, is held in trust here, and the three maps above are cases of it that can be checked by hand.

26i4Moves to Watch Out For

Each line below is a move that looks right and is not. The tag says what kind of mistake it is; the words say which footing catches it.

z^2 \text{ keeps angles everywhere}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-c">outside the collect</span> at 0 its slope is 0 and angles double (26i2)
\text{an angle-keeping map keeps lengths}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-l">legal, with a cost</span> it stretches by |f′|, which changes from point to point (26i1)
\bar z \text{ keeps angles}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-a">out of arrangement</span> it keeps their size but reverses their sense; it has no complex slope (26i1)

Accepted names

An angle-keeping map is conformal; (z − i)/(z + i) is a Möbius or Cayley transform; ½(z + 1/z) is the Joukowski transform; the theorem held in trust is the Riemann mapping theorem. The impossibility for the whole plane is Liouville’s theorem.

Who said it first

Bernhard Riemann stated the mapping theorem in his 1851 thesis; its first full proof came from Constantin Carathéodory and Paul Koebe around 1912. Nikolai Joukowski used his map for wing sections in 1910.

24·∎ · conclusion

A tame rate fixes the future

A tame rule on rates, with a start, has exactly one solution near the start, built by feeding guesses back into an area. Nearby starts drift apart no faster than a growth function. Without tameness, one start can have many futures.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 24e when the future is not fixed is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 24 asked whether a rule on a function’s rates, with a start, picks out one function. When the rule is tame, exactly one solution passes through the start, and it is the settled end of repeated integration. Without tameness, a start can have many futures.

24a said what a solution is and solved growth at its own rate. 24b turned the rule into an area and fed guesses back in. 24c measured gaps between functions and proved a shrinking map has one fixed point. 24d showed the area map shrinks for a tame rule, and bounded the drift. 24e found the rules that break the claim. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

A tame rule on rates, with a start, has exactly one solution near the start; nearby starts drift apart no faster than e^(L|x − x₀|); and without tameness the future need not be fixed.

Let F be continuous on R = [x₀ − α, x₀ + α] × [y₀ − β, y₀ + β], with |F| ≤ M there and |F(x, y) − F(x, z)| ≤ L|y − z|. Choose h ≤ a with hM ≤ b and hL < 1. Then y′ = F(x, y), y(x₀) = y₀ has exactly one solution on [x₀ − h, x₀ + h], and the guesses yₙ₊₁(x) = y₀ + ∫ₓ₀ˣ F(t, yₙ(t)) dt settle everywhere at once to it.

Two solutions of the same tame rule satisfy |y(x) − z(x)| ≤ |y(x₀) − z(x₀)|·e^(L|x − x₀|).

y′ = √|y|, y(0) = 0 has a different solution for every c ≥ 0. y′ = y², y(0) = 1 has y = 1/(1 − x), which no solution carries past x = 1.

The proof

Each part below is a technique already proved on a step of this rung. The proof cites them by name and joins them.

y' = ky,\ y(x_0) = A \iff y = Ae^{k(x-x_0)} @@ the own-rate technique (24a)
y' = F(x,y),\ y(x_0)=y_0 \iff y = y_0 + \int_{x_0}^{x} F(t,y(t))\,dt @@ the area-form technique (24b)
\htmlClass{new}{y = Ty} \quad \blacksquare @@ a solution is what the area map T leaves unchanged (24b)
  
\|Tf - Tg\| \le hL\,\|f - g\|,\ \ hL < 1 @@ tameness and the largest gap (24c, 24d)
\text{crowding functions settle to a continuous } f @@ the function-crowd technique (24c)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{exactly one solution on } [x_0-h,\,x_0+h]} \quad \blacksquare @@ the shrinking-map and existence techniques (24c, 24d)
  
|y(x) - z(x)| \le |y(x_0)-z(x_0)| + L\left|\int_{x_0}^{x} |y-z|\,dt\right| @@ the area form and tameness (24d)
\htmlClass{new}{|y(x) - z(x)| \le |y(x_0)-z(x_0)|\,e^{L|x-x_0|}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the drift technique (24d)
  
\sqrt{|y|} \text{ is not tame at } 0 @@ its slope in y passes every ceiling (24e)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{one start, endlessly many futures}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the many-futures technique (24e)
\htmlClass{new}{y = \tfrac{1}{1-x} \text{ ends at } x = 1} \quad \blacksquare @@ the run-off technique (24e)

So the survey’s title holds for every tame rule, near its start. The first counterexample shows tameness cannot be dropped. The second shows that “near its start” cannot be dropped either.

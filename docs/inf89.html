Can a Crystal Be Designed Before It Is Made?

Can a crystal be designed before it is made? A crystal is one small arrangement repeated without end. Some crystals are built as a frame: metal joints held apart by rods of carbon, with empty rooms between, and the rooms can be made to hold a gas. To design one is to choose which joint is tied to which before choosing any length or angle. That choice is a graph with endlessly many dots, and it fits in a small drawing: a few dots, a few links, and on each link a label that says how many repeats the link steps across. A walk in the small drawing is a walk in the crystal. Its label sum says where the walk comes out, whether it closes into a ring, and whether the crystal is one net or several woven through each other.

Small map: dots, one-way links, a label of whole numbers on each link. Net: dots (v, x), x a cell; a link with label ℓ joins (u, x) to (w, x + ℓ). A walk of the small map from a cell has one walk over it, ending at the cell plus the label sum; against a link’s direction the label counts minus. It closes exactly when the sum is 0. Pieces of the net: the classes of cells under the sums of closed walks. Open share of the toy frame: 1 − 3s² + 2s³.

inf89.1A Crystal From a Small Map

Tile a floor with squares and put a dot at every corner. Every dot looks like every other: four links, to the dots east, west, north and south. Name each dot by its cell, a pair of whole numbers (x₁, x₂), and the whole floor is said in one line: the dot of cell x is linked to the dots of cells x + (1, 0) and x + (0, 1).

From here on, on this page, a small map is a graph with a few dots and one-way links, where each link carries a label, a list of whole numbers of one fixed length: two numbers for a pattern on a floor, three for a crystal. Two dots may have several links between them, and a link may run from a dot to itself. The net of a small map has a dot (v, x) for each dot v of the small map and each cell x. For each link from u to w with label ℓ, and each cell x, the net has a link between (u, x) and (w, x + ℓ). The direction of a link is there to fix the sign of its label. A walk may take a link either way, and against its direction the label counts minus.

The floor of squares is the net of one dot with two links to itself, labelled (1, 0) and (0, 1). The honeycomb is the net of two dots A and B with three links from A to B, labelled (0, 0), (1, 0) and (0, 1): three links at every dot. In three numbers, one dot with links to itself labelled (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1) gives the cubic net, six links at every dot. Two dots A and B with four links from A to B labelled (0, 0, 0), (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1) give the net of diamond, four links at every dot.

Net-lift technique: a walk of the small map that starts at u, and a cell x, give exactly one walk of the net from (u, x) that lies over it, link for link. It ends in the cell x + s, where s is the sum of the labels along the walk.

So each dot of the net has as many links as its dot of the small map has link-ends, a link to itself counting two. A joint with six arms wants a dot with six link-ends. A joint with four arms wants four.

inf89.2Rings

A ring in a crystal is a cycle of its net (42a). In the small map it shows as a closed walk. But not every closed walk of the small map is a ring.

Ring technique: the walk of the net over a closed walk of the small map is closed exactly when the labels along it sum to 0.

\text{a }\allowbreak\text{closed }\allowbreak\text{walk }\allowbreak\text{at } u \text{ }\allowbreak\text{with }\allowbreak\text{label }\allowbreak\text{sum } s\text{, }\allowbreak\text{lifted }\allowbreak\text{from } (u, x)\text{, }\allowbreak\text{ends }\allowbreak\text{at } (u, x + s) @@ the net-lift technique
\htmlClass{new}{(u, x + s) = (u, x) \iff s = 0} \quad \blacksquare @@ two dots of the net are one when dot and cell agree

On the floor of squares, take the link labelled (1, 0) once. In the small map that is a closed walk. Over it the net walks one cell east, and taken again and again it runs east for ever. Take (1, 0), then (0, 1), then each backwards: the sum is (1, 0) + (0, 1) − (1, 0) − (0, 1) = 0, a ring of four, the square.

In the honeycomb call the links e₀, e₁, e₂, with labels (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1). A closed walk at A goes out along one link and back along another, in turn. Out e₀, back e₁, out e₂, back e₀, out e₁, back e₂ has label sum 0 − (1, 0) + (0, 1) − 0 + (1, 0) − (0, 1) = 0: a ring of six. No shorter walk that never turns straight back has sum 0, so six is the smallest ring, and the small map said so without a picture of the honeycomb. The same count on the small map of diamond gives six again.

inf89.3One Net or Several

Take one dot with two links to itself, labelled (1, 1) and (1, −1). The small map is in one piece. Is its net? From the cell (0, 0) every step changes x₁ + x₂ by 2, 0 or −2. So no walk reaches (1, 0). The net falls in two pieces, each a floor of squares set cornerwise, and each lies in the gaps of the other.

Fix a dot v₀ of the small map, and let L be the set of label sums of the closed walks at v₀. A closed walk followed by a closed walk is one, and so is a closed walk taken backwards, so L holds the sum and the difference of any two of its members.

Piece-count technique: for a small map in one piece, (v₀, x) and (v₀, y) are joined by a walk of the net exactly when y − x is in L. So the pieces of the net match the classes of cells, two cells being in one class when their difference is in L, and the net is in one piece exactly when L is every cell.

In the example L is the set of cells (a + b, a − b), the cells with x₁ + x₂ even. There are two classes, even and odd, and two pieces. In three numbers take one dot with links to itself labelled (1, 1, 0), (1, 0, 1) and (0, 1, 1). L is again the cells with an even sum, so the net is two nets. Each has six links at every dot and rings of four, as the cubic net has, and each runs through the rooms of the other.

inf89.4Read It With Arrows

The small map gives a category (47a1). Its things are its dots, its arrows are its walks, links taken either way, and the walk of no links is the do-nothing arrow. The label sum sends each walk to a cell. A walk followed by a walk goes to the sum of their cells, and the walk of no links goes to the cell of all 0s. So the label sum is a map of categories, from the walks to the cells under +, as ∞z’s tally was. A walk taken backwards goes to the cell with every sign turned.

Walks-are-dots technique: in the piece of the net that holds (v₀, 0), the dots match, one to one, the walks of the small map from v₀, when two walks count as one that end at the same dot with the same label sum.

\text{each }\allowbreak\text{dot }\allowbreak\text{of }\allowbreak\text{the }\allowbreak\text{piece }\allowbreak\text{ends }\allowbreak\text{a }\allowbreak\text{net }\allowbreak\text{walk }\allowbreak\text{from } (v_0, 0)\text{, }\allowbreak\text{lying }\allowbreak\text{over }\allowbreak\text{some } p @@ what a piece is (42a)
\htmlClass{new}{p \text{ }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{and } p' \text{ }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{give }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{one }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{dot} \iff \text{same }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{end }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{and } s(p) = s(p')} \quad \blacksquare @@ dot and cell agree

So the crystal is not a second thing beside its plan. Each joint of the crystal is a way of walking the small map, kept up to where the walk ends and what its labels sum to. The three techniques before are each one line about the map s. Net-lift: s says where an arrow comes out. Ring: an arrow from a dot to itself is a ring when s sends it to 0. Piece-count: the cells that s reaches from closed walks at one dot decide how many nets there are.

See what the arrows let hide. No length, no angle and no place in space has been named. The endless crystal is out of sight as well, with all its cells. What is left is the plan: which joint is tied to which, and across how many repeats. Lengths and angles come last, and many choices of them fit one plan.

inf89.5The Same Net, Longer Rods

A frame is built from two parts: a joint, a cluster of metal atoms with arms, and a rod, a stiff carbon molecule that ties two arms. Put joints at the dots of a net and rods along its links. The net is given by the small map alone, so a longer rod between the same joints builds the same net with larger rooms. How much larger? Take a toy: the cubic net, the repeat a cube of side a, every link a square bar of side t.

Open-share technique: in the toy frame the share of space left open is 1 − 3s² + 2s³, where s = t/a.

\text{in }\allowbreak\text{one }\allowbreak\text{repeat, }\allowbreak\text{three }\allowbreak\text{bars }\allowbreak\text{meet }\allowbreak\text{at }\allowbreak\text{a }\allowbreak\text{corner, }\allowbreak\text{one }\allowbreak\text{along }\allowbreak\text{each }\allowbreak\text{direction} @@ the cubic net: three links to a cell
\text{each }\allowbreak\text{bar }\allowbreak\text{fills } t^2 a\text{; }\allowbreak\text{the }\allowbreak\text{corner }\allowbreak\text{cube } t^3 \text{ }\allowbreak\text{lies }\allowbreak\text{in }\allowbreak\text{all }\allowbreak\text{three} @@ volumes of boxes
\text{filled} = 3t^2 a - 2t^3 @@ the corner was counted three times; count it once
\htmlClass{new}{\text{open }}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{\text{share} = 1 - \frac{3t^2 a - 2t^3}{a^3}}\allowbreak\htmlClass{new}{{} = 1 - 3s^2 + 2s^3} \quad \blacksquare @@ divide by a³; s = t/a

At s = ½ half the space is open. Double the repeat with the same bar, s = ¼, and the open share is 27/32, about 84%. Double again, s = ⅛, and it is 245/256, about 96%. A real frame is not made of square bars, and the toy says nothing of what holds it up. It says how fast rooms grow when only the rod is changed: in the toy, a share of 91% open needs s under one fifth.

  • In 1984 a paper set out how a net that repeats in three directions is written as a small graph with a label of three whole numbers on each link, and how nets are made from such graphs.
  • In 1989 copper ions were tied by a molecule with four arms into a crystal built on the net of diamond, with large empty rooms. It was not stable and fell in easily.
  • In 1995 such crystals were given their name, in 1997 one was shown to take a gas in and give it back, and in 1999 one was made that kept its frame when emptied and heated to 300 °C.
  • In 2002 sixteen crystals were reported on one net, joints with six arms and rods of different lengths: rooms from 3.8 to 28.8 Å across (1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m), and up to 91.1% of the crystal open.
  • The Nobel prize in chemistry of 2025 was given for the development of these frames.

A short history · told from more than one side

How we got here

A crystal is most often described by where its atoms sit. Its net, which atom is tied to which, is a second description, and it is the one a designer starts from.

In 1984 the net was given its own short writing, a small graph with labels, and nets could be listed and made from graphs before any crystal was at hand. In 1989 a crystal was built on the net of diamond, from copper and a molecule with four arms.

Through the 1990s the frames were made to last: to take a gas in and give it back, and to stand empty. In 2002 one net was built sixteen times over with rods of different lengths. The Nobel prize in chemistry of 2025 went to three of the people named in the cellar.

The accepted names

A frame of metal joints and carbon rods is a metal–organic framework, MOF. The joint is a node or secondary building unit, the rod a linker or strut. The small map is the labelled quotient graph of the net, its labels voltages, and the net is its derived graph or periodic net; a cell is a lattice vector in whole numbers, and the repeat is the unit cell. Net-lift is unique path lifting, and the net is a covering graph of the small map. The set L is a sublattice, and the count of its classes is its index. Nets woven through each other are interpenetrated. Crystals on one net are isoreticular, and designing from the net is reticular chemistry. The cubic net is pcu, the net of diamond dia, the honeycomb hcb. The open share is the void fraction or porosity. The category of walks taken either way is the free groupoid on the graph.

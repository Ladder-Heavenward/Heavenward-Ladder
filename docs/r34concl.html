34·∎ · conclusion

Stretching keeps what open sets decide

Continuity is a statement about open sets, and so are being in one piece, being finitely covered and keeping points apart. A stretching that matches open sets keeps them all. Surfaces fall into one list, told apart by a count. Loops, slid without breaking, form a group that counts holes, and so a map of the disc to itself keeps a point.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 34 asked what is left of nearness when distance is taken away. Continuity needs only which sets are open; being in one piece, being finitely covered and keeping points apart are decided by the open sets too, so a stretching that matches open sets with open sets keeps them all; and the loops of a space, slid without breaking, count its holes. The price is a choice spent on products of finitely covered spaces, and several steps held in trust.

34a built spaces with a distance and their open sets. 34b kept the open sets and threw the distance away. 34c found what being in one piece means, and 34d what finitely many suffice means. 34e matched spaces of the same shape, and 34f listed every closed surface. 34g counted holes by loops, 34h found a point that stays put, and 34i named the loops of glued spaces. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

A map between spaces with a distance is continuous exactly when it pulls open sets back to open sets, so continuity, and every notion built from it, is a statement about families of open sets. Being in one piece, being joined by paths, being finitely covered and keeping points apart are statements of that kind, and a same-shape map keeps each of them.

A part of the line is in one piece exactly when it is a stretch, and finitely covered exactly when it is closed and bounded; continuous maps carry pieces to pieces and finitely covered spaces to finitely covered spaces, and so a continuous function on a closed stretch takes every value between and has a highest value. Products of two finitely covered spaces are finitely covered; that every product is, is as strong as choice.

Every closed surface in one piece, cut into triangles, is the same shape as exactly one of the sphere, a sum of g tori and a sum of k cross-caps, told apart by its characteristic and its sides. The loops of a space at a point, up to sliding, form a group, kept by same-shape maps; the circle’s is the whole numbers, counted by winding; and so no continuous map of the disc into itself moves every point. The loops of a glued space are named by the letters and relations of its pieces.

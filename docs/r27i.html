27i · technique

Pictures of Two Changing Quantities

Two quantities that change together, each at a rate set by both, make a straight rate statement x′ = Ax with a 2 × 2 matrix A. Its solutions are paths in the plane, and their picture is fixed by A’s eigenvalues: paths fall into the origin, flee it, swirl round it, or pass it by like a saddle. This step solves x′ = Ax by 27f’s eigenvectors, sorts every picture by two numbers, the trace and the determinant, and reads 24h’s swinging spring as a swirl.

If Av = λv, then x = e^(λt)v solves x′ = Ax. With two eigenvectors, every solution is c₁e^(λ₁t)v₁ + c₂e^(λ₂t)v₂. λ₁λ₂ = det A and λ₁ + λ₂ = tr A: det < 0 gives a saddle; det > 0 and tr < 0, every path falls in; tr² < 4 det, the paths swirl.

27i1Straight-Line Solutions

Eigen-path technique: if A has eigenvectors v₁, v₂ with different eigenvalues λ₁, λ₂, every solution of x′ = Ax is x = c₁e^(λ₁t)v₁ + c₂e^(λ₂t)v₂, and c₁, c₂ are fixed by x(0).

(e^{\lambda t}v)^{\prime} = \lambda e^{\lambda t} v = e^{\lambda t} A v = A(e^{\lambda t}v) @@ one eigenvector, one solution
\text{sums of solutions solve: } A \text{ is a straight map (27c)} @@ add them
v_1, v_2 \text{ independent (27f), so } x(0) = c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2 \text{ in one way} @@ a basis of the plane
\htmlClass{new}{\text{one solution for each start (24d), so these are all}} \quad \blacksquare @@ tame statements fix the future
  
x_1 = x,\ x_2 = x^{\prime}:\ A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2 & -3 \end{pmatrix} @@ position and velocity
\lambda^2 + 3\lambda + 2 = 0:\ \lambda = -1, -2 @@ its eigenvalues
v_1 = (1, -1),\ v_2 = (1, -2) @@ from (A − λ)v = 0
\htmlClass{new}{\text{every path falls into the origin}} @@ both eigenvalues below 0

27i2Trace and Determinant Sort the Pictures

\lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr} A)\lambda + \det A = 0 @@ the eigenvalue equation (27e)
\det A < 0:\ \lambda_1 < 0 < \lambda_2 @@ a saddle: in along one line, out along the other
\det A > 0,\ \operatorname{tr} A < 0,\ \operatorname{tr}^2 \ge 4\det:\ \text{both below } 0 @@ a sink: every path falls in
\htmlClass{new}{\operatorname{tr}^2 < 4\det:\ \lambda = \alpha \pm i\beta,\ \text{paths swirl, in if } \alpha < 0} @@ e^(αt) times a turn, by 26a

Picture technique: for x′ = Ax in the plane, det A < 0 gives a saddle; det A > 0 with tr A < 0 makes every path fall in, and with tr A > 0 flee; and tr² < 4 det makes the paths swirl. 24h’s spring x″ = −x has tr 0, det 1: a swirl that neither falls in nor flees, a circle.

27i3Rabbits and Foxes Near Balance

u = \text{rabbits above balance},\ w = \text{foxes above balance} @@ small departures
u^{\prime} = -w,\ \ w^{\prime} = u @@ more foxes eat rabbits; more rabbits feed foxes
\operatorname{tr} = 0,\ \det = 1 @@ a swirl
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the two rise and fall a quarter turn apart, forever}} @@ foxes peak after rabbits

Near a balance, many pairs of quantities follow a straight statement, and its trace and determinant tell whether the balance holds, breaks or swings. Lynx and hare pelts traded at Hudson’s Bay for a century show such swings, about ten years long.

Accepted names

The picture is a phase portrait in the phase plane; the cases are a saddle, a stable or unstable node, a spiral (focus) and a centre. The rabbits and foxes are the Lotka-Volterra equations near their balance.

Who said it first

Henri Poincaré drew such pictures in the 1880s; Alfred Lotka (1925) and Vito Volterra (1926) wrote the predator and prey equations. The Hudson’s Bay Company lynx and hare records were gathered by Charles Elton in 1942.

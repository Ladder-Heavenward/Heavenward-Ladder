Can a Light Bulb Work Out an And?

This page grows from one line of 1e2: each joining word is a machine that takes T or F for each part and hands back 1 or 0.

A battery, a bulb and some buttons. The wiring does the work of the joining word, and the bulb shows the answer. A student can build every footing by hand.

The 1 and 0 are marks, as in 1e. Nothing is added or multiplied.

1phys1A Bulb for a Mark, a Button for a Letter

Take a small battery, a small bulb and some wire. Run wire from one end of the battery to the bulb, and from the bulb back to the other end of the battery. The bulb lights. Cut the wire anywhere and the bulb goes dark. That is a given from observation (1a5): the bulb is lit when there is an unbroken path from the battery, through the bulb, and back, and it is dark when there is not.

Use one or two small batteries, the kind that go in a flashlight. Never use a wall socket. Never join the two ends of a battery with wire alone, with no bulb on the path: the wire gets hot.

A button is a gap in the wire that a finger can close. Hold the button down and the gap is closed. Let go and it is open again.

Now make two agreements, the way 1e1 agreed on its marks.

A button stands for a letter. Let P be “button p is held down”. P is a statement: so or not so.

The bulb stands for the answer. Lit is 1. Dark is 0.

With one button on the path, the bulb's column is P's own:

Pbulb
T1
F0

Each way of holding the buttons is one row of a table (1e6). To fill in a row, hold the buttons that way and look at the bulb. The wiring fills in its own table.

1phys2Two Buttons in a Line

Put two buttons on the path, one after the other.

The path has two gaps. It is unbroken only when both are closed. Let Q be “button q is held down”, and try every row:

PQbulb
TT1
TF0
FT0
FF0

The column reads 1 0 0 0. That is the column of P ∧ Q: 1 only in the row TT (1e2). Two buttons in a line are an and.

Swap the two buttons round, q first and then p. The path still has the same two gaps, and the column is the same. That is 1c3 in wire: an and turns about.

1phys3Two Buttons Side by Side

Now split the wire into two branches, put one button on each branch, and join the branches again.

There are two ways through. The path is unbroken when either gap is closed, or both.

TT1
TF1
FT1
FF0

The column reads 1 1 1 0. That is the column of P ∨ Q: 0 only in the row FF. Two buttons side by side are an or, and it is the generous or of 1c5. With both buttons held down the bulb is lit, and it is content.

1phys4A Button That Breaks the Wire

There is a second kind of button. It is closed while nobody touches it, and holding it down opens the gap. A refrigerator has one in the door frame. The shut door holds the button down, and the light inside is off. Open the door, the button is let go, and the light comes on.

Put one of these on the path, and let P be “the button is held down”, as before.

Pbulb
T0
F1

The column is P's column swapped. That is the not machine of 1e2: T gives 0, F gives 1. The bulb shows ¬P.

So the three machines that 1e16 used to reach every column each have a wiring: a line for and, side by side for or, and the breaking button for not.

1phys5The Stairway Light

A light over a staircase has a switch at the bottom and a switch at the top. Flip either one, from either end, and the light changes. Neither an and nor an or can do that. In the and's column, start from the row FF and change P alone: the row TF still gives 0, and the light has not changed.

The stairway uses a switch with a lever and two wires out. The lever joins the wire coming in to the upper wire or to the lower wire, never to both and never to neither. Two of these face each other.

The path is unbroken exactly when both levers sit on the same wire. Let P be “lever p is up” and Q be “lever q is up”.

PQbulb
TT1
TF0
FT0
FF1

The column reads 1 0 0 1. That is P ⇔ Q, exactly when: 1 where the letters match (1d5, 1e2).

Now cross the two middle wires over, so that p's upper wire arrives at q's lower place. The path is unbroken exactly when the levers differ. The column reads 0 1 1 0, which is P ⊕ Q, or but not both (1c11).

Either wiring does the stairway's job. Look at either column and change one letter alone, from any row: the answer always changes. That is the whole of what a stairway light needs.

1phys6Two Wirings, One Column

1e14 said that two statements with the same column are the same statement by the table's lights. A bulb agrees. It shows a column, and it cannot show how the column was wired.

Take three buttons. Wiring one puts p in a line with a side-by-side pair, q and r. It is P ∧ (Q ∨ R).

Wiring two has two branches. One is p in a line with q. The other is p in a line with r. It is (P ∧ Q) ∨ (P ∧ R). Button p now has to close two gaps at once, one on each branch, so it needs two contacts under one finger.

The two columns match in all eight rows. Wiring one has three gaps to close and wiring two has four. Someone who looks only at the bulb can never tell which one is in the box. Someone who has to pay for the parts builds wiring one.

1phys8A Wiring Read as a Table, and Three Doors

Current flows only round a closed loop. So a wiring of buttons is a statement: the lamp is lit exactly when some path from the battery through the lamp and back is closed. Read any wiring by listing every way its buttons can be set.

Wiring-table technique: list every setting of the buttons, and for each ask whether some path is closed; the column of lit and dark is the wiring’s statement. Shown on a board: a lamp wired through two buttons in a line lights only in the one row where both are down, and a hall lamp with three switches changes at every flip of any one of them.

Accepted names

The loop of battery, wire and bulb is a circuit. Parts in a line are in series, and parts side by side are in parallel. A button that closes when held is normally open; one that opens when held is normally closed. The lever switch with two wires out is a two-way switch in Britain and a three-way switch in the United States; both names are for the same part. Two contacts moved by one finger are ganged.

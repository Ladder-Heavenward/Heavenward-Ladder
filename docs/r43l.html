Rational Points on a Cubic

A line through two fraction points of y² = x³ − 2 meets the curve a third time, and that point has fractions too: the three crossings come from a cubic whose zeros add to a fraction. So two answers make a third, and an answer makes another by its tangent. From (3, 5) the tangent gives (129/100, −383/1000). The rule, with one far point added, turns the fraction points into a group. This step finds the third crossing, adds points, and reads an old question about right triangles off a curve.

Line y = λx + ν through P and Q meets y² = x³ + ax + b where x³ − λ²x² + ⋯ = 0. The three zeros add to λ² (9g), so the third has x = λ² − x_P − x_Q, a fraction. Reflect it: P + Q. With a far point as 0, the fraction points form a group, built from finitely many.

43l1The Third Crossing

(\lambda x + \nu)^2 = x^3 + ax + b @@ where the line meets the curve
x^3 - \lambda^2 x^2 + \cdots = 0,\ \ x_P + x_Q + x_R = \lambda^2 @@ sum of zeros (9g)
x_R = \lambda^2 - x_P - x_Q,\ \ y_R = \lambda x_R + \nu @@ fractions in, fractions out
\htmlClass{new}{P + Q = (x_R,\ -y_R)} @@ reflect the third crossing

Chord technique: the line through two fraction points of y² = x³ + ax + b meets it at a third fraction point; with λ = (3x² + a)/(2y), the tangent at one point does the same.

43l2A Group of Points

P = (3, 5):\ 25 = 27 - 2 @@ a whole point
\lambda = \dfrac{3\cdot 9}{2\cdot 5} = \dfrac{27}{10},\ \ x = \dfrac{729}{100} - 6 = \dfrac{129}{100} @@ the tangent
2P = \Big(\dfrac{129}{100},\ -\dfrac{383}{1000}\Big) @@ check: (383/1000)² = (129/100)³ − 2
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a far point } O \text{ as } 0:\ P + O = P,\ \ (x, y) + (x, -y) = O} @@ vertical lines meet O

Point-group technique: the fraction points of y² = x³ + ax + b, with the far point O as 0 and reflection as undoing, form a group that turns about; and every such group is built from finitely many points.

43l3Triangles With Square Areas

3, 4, 5:\ \text{area } 6 @@ n = 6 is such an area
\text{area } n \iff y^2 = x^3 - n^2x \text{ has a point with } y \ne 0 @@ a matching of the two
n = 6:\ (-3, 9):\ 81 = -27 + 108 @@ the 3, 4, 5 triangle’s point
\htmlClass{new}{n = 1:\ \text{only } y = 0 \text{ points, by Fermat’s descent}} @@ no right triangle has area a square (43k)

A whole number n is the area of a right triangle with fraction sides exactly when the curve y² = x³ − n²x has a fraction point off the x-axis; deciding which n are areas leads to one of the great open questions about such curves.

Accepted names

These are elliptic curves; the chord rule is the chord-and-tangent law; the far point is the point at infinity; built from finitely many is Mordell’s theorem. Areas of rational right triangles are congruent numbers; the open question is the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

Who said it first

Diophantus used chords about 250; Fermat proved 1 is not a congruent number about 1640; Louis Mordell proved the finite building in 1922.

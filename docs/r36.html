Rung 36 · theory

Colouring blindness

Since 31 a function has had a length and angles, and since 32 a periodic function has been a sum of turnings with endlessly many coefficients; a spectrum is such a function on an interval. This rung asks what a colour is, and the answer is a blindness: an eye keeps three of those coefficients and throws the rest away, and the three it keeps, mixed, make colours that no part of the spectrum has. Red light and blue light together make a purple that matches no wavelength, and the purples close the open line of the spectrum into a circle. Everything after that is mathematics on the circle, and on the cylinder the circle sweeps out when brightness is added: hue as an angle, saturation as a radius, brightness as a height. The rung builds that space, does its arithmetic, says what a colour is as a name, lets colours make functions of their own, and ends where it began, with the fact that the spectrum behind a colour is still there, however many of its dimensions the eye dropped.

The gesture: a colour is three numbers an eye keeps out of endlessly many, bent onto a circle so that the ends meet. Everything you do to a colour you do to the numbers, and then you look.

The class · read it through, then go to 36a

What it means to say it

A colour, as everyone meets it, is a thing seen. The claim of this rung is that the thing seen is three numbers, that the three come from a function with endlessly many, and that the mathematics of colour is the mathematics of what three numbers can do once they are bent into a circle. The bending is the eye’s, not the world’s: no wavelength is purple, and the circle closes only because red and blue, mixed, look like something. So the rung’s space is a human space, built on human blindness, and it is exact anyway, because three numbers are three numbers.

Where it comes from

Sunlight split with a prism was found to be a spread of wavelengths, and the first colour circle was drawn by bending the spread round and joining the ends; the joining was a person’s, not the prism’s. It was proposed that the eye has three kinds of receiver, and the idea was carried through; and it was shown by matching that three lights suffice to match any colour, with some matches needing a light moved to the other side. The horseshoe region of everything the eye can see was drawn from matching experiments, and its one straight edge is the line of purples. The accepted names: the three receivers are cones, three lights are primaries, and the whole is colorimetry.

The principles, in plain words

The rung proves six things, each from the ones before.

First, a spectrum is a function on an interval, and a colour is three coefficients of it, taken against three fixed curves. Two spectra with the same three coefficients are one colour, and there are endlessly many of them for every colour.

Second, mixing lights adds spectra, and so adds coefficients. The mix of red light and blue light has coefficients that no single wavelength has, so the mixes close the open spectrum into a circle, and the closing belongs to the eye.

Third, at full saturation a colour is an angle on that circle, and the functions on the circle are 20’s moves and 21’s arcs: shifting a hue is adding to the angle, the opposite hue is a half turn away, and mixing two hues is a point on the chord between them.

Fourth, brightness is a height and saturation a radius, and the colours fill a cylinder whose axis is grey, whose ends are black and white, and on whose axis the angle has no answer: 5e’s forbidden division, seen in colour.

Fifth, three chosen lights reach a triangle inside the eye’s region and not all of it, and what they miss lies in a known direction.

Sixth, a colour is a name in both of 0a’s meanings, a set of spectra named by its triple, and an inductive object as 7 and a pigment are: statements about it are 4a’s columns, facts about it are 30w’s recurrence as evidence, and the chemistry of the pigment is the mathematics behind the facts. The functions colours make for themselves, dodge, burn, screen and the rest, are functions in 9a’s sense and nothing more, and at the corners they are 1e’s tables.

Anexact, yet rigorous. Three boxes ride on this rung. 36c carries the cloud: a colour is a crowd of spectra, and the crowd is exact in number and uncountable in size. 36f carries laws with a known exception rate: three primaries reach most of what the eye sees, and the rest lies in a known direction. 36i carries two sizes of truth: a picture is a crowd of colours side by side, and the mix is what the crowd looks like from far enough away.

What follows

The blends a painting program offers are each a line of arithmetic (36h and 36·comp). A pigment in a solvent is a case in an induction, and the formula that runs from its bonds to its colour carries a rate (36g3, 36·phys). The dye spreading in a glass of water is 33’s heat equation with colour for temperature (36j3). The gamut of a screen and the horseshoe of the eye are two of 34’s regions, and the difference between them is a tabulated failure (36f). A colour is a class of uncountably many spectra, 35’s size of the line (36j6). And the strip of greys is a world of 7a’s kind, in which the either-way principle fails by calculation (36h5).

What you can do because you know it

You can say what a colour is and why no wavelength is purple. You can turn a hue by an angle, find the hue opposite it, and mix two hues on the circle. You can write any colour as an angle, a radius and a height, and go between that and three coefficients. You can say which colours three lights can and cannot make, and in which direction the failure lies. You can prove things about colours by components and learn things about pigments by cases with a rate, and say which size of truth each line lives at. You can say why “red beside green” and “yellow” are different objects. And you can say, with the proof in hand, that the spectrum behind a colour is still there.

What it costs

One definition is laid down, the three curves of 36b3, and they are laid down as the eye’s, measured, not derived. Everything else is 31’s lengths and angles, 32’s coefficients, 20’s moves, 21’s arcs, 34’s regions and 35’s sizes, spent on three numbers. At the end of the rung you will know what it means to say that colour is a blindness coloured in, and what you can do because you know it.

The basement · there if you want it

Who said it first

Newton, in 1666 and in the Opticks of 1704, split sunlight with a prism and found it a spread of wavelengths, and drew the first colour circle by bending the spread round and joining the ends; the joining was his, not the prism’s. Thomas Young in 1802 proposed that the eye has three kinds of receiver, and Hermann von Helmholtz carried the idea through in the 1850s and 60s; James Clerk Maxwell in 1860 showed by matching that three lights suffice to match any colour, with some matches needing a light moved to the other side. The horseshoe region of everything the eye can see was drawn by an international commission in 1931 from matching experiments, and its one straight edge is the line of purples.

The tables of 36h at the corners are the algebra of two values that George Boole set out in 1847 and 1854.

The accepted names

The three receivers are cones, and the three-receiver account is the Young–Helmholtz trichromatic theory. Three lights are primaries. The whole is colorimetry. The commission of 1931 is the International Commission on Illumination (CIE), and its horseshoe is the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram.

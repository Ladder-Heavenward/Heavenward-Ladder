A Geometry Is What Its Moves Keep

The moves that keep distances form a group. Each is at most three flips, fixed by three points, and the count of flips decides which way round. A geometry is a group of moves, and its facts are what those moves keep.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 20h sorting geometries by the moves they keep is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 20 asked what a geometry is, if the old geometry’s “lay one figure on another” is taken at its word. The moves came first, and the facts second. A geometry is a group of moves, and its facts are exactly what those moves keep.

20a named the moves that keep distances and those that keep shape. 20b showed each family is a group. 20c wrote down slides, turns and flips. 20d proved they keep distances and defined congruence. 20e built turns and slides from two flips. 20f built every isometry from at most three, and earned the congruence tests. 20g counted the flips by which way round. 20h sorted the geometries. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

The moves of the plane that keep distances are a group, each is at most three flips, and the count of flips decides which way round it leaves a figure.

Let an isometry be a function f from the plane to itself with d(f(P), f(Q)) = d(P, Q) for all P and Q.

Then the isometries form a group, inside the group of similarities.

Every isometry is a string of at most three flips.

Every isometry is fixed by where it sends three points not on one line.

Two triangles are congruent when their three sides agree in pairs, or two sides and the angle between them, or two angles and the side between them. They are similar when two angles agree.

An isometry made of an even number of flips keeps D(A, B, C) for every A, B, C. One made of an odd number changes its sign.

If one group of moves lies inside another, every fact the bigger one keeps, the smaller keeps too.

The old geometry laid one triangle on another and trusted the move. Every move used here was built and checked. That is what it means to say a geometry is what its moves keep.

What the rest of the rung adds

Each step and annex after the proof steps adds a line to the claim. A line appears here once you have finished its page.

  • 20i moving graphs: sliding, flipping and stretching a graph is done by changing its equation: replace x by x − h to slide it right by h, and y by −y to flip it over the floor. Each move of the plane moves every graph, and its equation moves the other way.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20j turns in coordinates: a turn by (a, b) is the pair of formulas (ax − by, bx + ay). Turns combine by a rule for pairs, (a, b) then (c, d) is (ac − bd, ad + bc), and the pairs whose squares add to 1 are exactly the turns.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·cb19 the square’s moves as turns and flips: the eight moves of 19f’s square card are four turns about its centre and four flips across its lines, and every fact of 19f is a fact about isometries.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·cb9 the inverse’s pairs, flipped: the graph of an undoing function is the graph of the function flipped across y = x: swapping each pair is that flip.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·cb8 numbers that turn: the pair (0, 1) is the quarter turn, and done twice it is the half turn, (x, y) ↦ (−x, −y). So a pair whose square is −1 is a move that exists, not an answer assumed.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·phys does light take the shortest path?: A flip of the target makes the shortest bounce a straight line, two mirrors at right angles make a half turn that sends light back, and the quickest path across a change of speed bends so that run over length, over speed, agrees on both sides.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·crypt can a guessed word break the machine?: A guessed word rules out places by no-self; round a loop of its pairs the board cancels; and every wrong setting refutes itself by a clash, leaving about 2.7 × 10⁷ checks of 1.6 × 10²⁰.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·chem can a molecule be left-handed?: It shows handedness at work in space: a carbon with four different neighbours has a way round that no turn changes and a mirror reverses, so no turn carries it onto its mirror image.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 20·nav which way is the ship facing after its turns?: It reads a voyage by composing moves of the plane: the turn part of a composed move is the product of the turns, whatever the runs, and a turn and a slide never turn about unless one of them does nothing.its line appears when you finish it.

That is the geometry of moves. Every figure you have drawn sits in one of its sorts. You have only just begun to see what space allows.

Who said it first

The old geometry is Euclid’s. His Elements proved equal triangles by laying one figure on another and trusting the move.

Accepted names

The congruence tests are often called Euclid’s tests.

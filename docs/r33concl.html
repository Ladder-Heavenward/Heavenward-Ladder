33·∎ · conclusion

Heat forgets, waves keep, still water averages

The heat rule fixes one future from a start, makes no new extremes, smooths by a spreading bell and fades. The string’s rule carries shapes at one speed and keeps its energy. A still state is the average round every circle, and its edge fixes it. Riding paths break into jumps that keep the books, and symmetric rules have perpendicular shapes that build every answer.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 33g round drums is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 33 asked what a rule tying rates in space to rates in time can say about heat, about waves and about still water. Heat smooths and forgets, waves carry and keep, and still water is the average of its surroundings. The three are one kind of rule, and they behave in three different ways.

33a wrote the heat rule from the books of heat and solved it by fading sines. 33b showed that it makes no new hottest point, fixes one future, spreads like a bell and loses its past. 33c wrote the string’s rule, split every answer into two running shapes, found the tones of a tied string and kept its energy. 33d found the still states in the real parts of rung 26 and proved that each point is the average round every circle. 33e rode along first-slope rules, broke them, and kept the books at the jump. 33f showed that the shapes are perpendicular because the rules are symmetric, and built every load from one kick. 33g took the shapes into a round drum and a ball. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

If heat runs down its slope and is kept, u_t = κΔu. On a rod with its ends held at 0, the answer from a start whose sine coefficients have a settling total is the fading series; it is the only answer, it never passes the greatest value of its start and ends, it fades at least like e^(−κπ²t/L²), and on the line it is the start smoothed by a bell of spread 2κt.

If a string’s swings are small, y_tt = c²y_xx. On a long string every answer is F(x − ct) + G(x + ct), and the height at x uses the start only within ct of x. A string tied at both ends sounds at whole multiples of c/(2L), and its energy is kept.

A balanced function, with Δu = 0, is the average of itself round every circle inside its domain; its greatest and least values are on the edge, the edge fixes it, and the real part of every function with a complex slope is one. A first-slope rule is constant along its riding paths; when the speed is the answer, a start breaks at t = 1/max(−f′), and a jump keeps the books by moving at [q]/[u]. A second-slope rule with its ends at 0 is symmetric, so its shapes are perpendicular, and every load’s answer is a total of kicks. A round drum sounds at zeros of the drum functions, the first two in a ratio between 2.28 and 2.31.

So the rung’s claim holds. The heat rule forgets: its answers are fixed by the past and fade toward the still state, losing the detail first. The string keeps: its energy and its shapes run on unchanged, forward or back. The still state averages: every point is the mean of its neighbours, and the edge decides it all. Two measured laws are held in trust, as laws: the flow of heat down its slope, and the motion of a string under its pull.

7g · support

Yes or No Is Data

Rung 7 fixed truth: every statement is true or false. This step says what that buys for deduction, and what it does not claim about the world.

True or false makes deduction easy. It is more data for a better guess, not a verdict from on high.

7g1Two Values Make Deduction Easy

With the either-way principle, every statement has a row: 1 or 0, true or false. Proof by cases on any statement, not-not out and proof by contradiction all follow (7e, 7f). Deduction gets much easier, because there are only two ways anything can come out.

7g2The World Keeps Its Middle

The world often has a middle. Drizzle is somewhere between raining and not. A glass can be half full. The principle does not deny this.

It speaks of statements, not of weather. “It is raining” is made true or false by deciding what the words claim. The middle is still there in the world, and a sharper statement can say where.

7g3More Data for Better Guesses

Yes or no, so or not so, true or false, 1 or 0: each is more data for a better guess. None of them is a verdict handed down from on high. A true line says that, as worded, the claim holds. The guess behind the words is still a guess.

This is why the ladder keeps showing its guesswork. A verdict that hides its guess loses what the guess knew.

Anexact, yet rigorous · the verdict that is data

True or false settles the statement, not the world. It is exact about the words, and honest that the words are a guess.

Accepted names

The either-way principle is the law of excluded middle; logics that keep a middle value are many-valued or fuzzy logics.

20d · proof

All Three Keep Distances

20c wrote down three kinds of move. This step proves that each keeps every distance, so each is an isometry, and so is anything built from them. Then “congruent” gets the meaning the old geometry needed and could not quite say: two figures are congruent when a move that keeps distances carries one onto the other.

A slide keeps every displacement. A turn and a flip about the origin send every vector to a vector of the same length. So all three are isometries, and two shapes are congruent when some isometry sends one onto the other.

20d1Slides

Slide-keeps technique: a slide keeps every distance. S(Q) − S(P) = (Q + (h, k)) − (P + (h, k)) = Q − P: the displacement is the same, so its length is.

20d2Turns

The turn-keeps technique of 18f2 said that a turn keeps every distance and every opening. Here it is read for distances alone, about the origin: a turn sends the displacement Q − P to R(Q − P), and the cross terms cancel.

R(Q) - R(P) = R(Q - P) @@ R(x, y) = (ax − by, bx + ay): differences go through
v = (x, y):\ \ |R(v)|^2 = (ax - by)^2 + (bx + ay)^2 @@ ﹀ |R(v)| (18a2)
= a^2x^2 - 2abxy + b^2y^2 + b^2x^2 + 2abxy + a^2y^2 @@ square-of-a-sum, twice
= (a^2 + b^2)(x^2 + y^2) = |v|^2 @@ the cross terms cancel; a² + b² = 1
\htmlClass{new}{d(RP, RQ) = d(P, Q)} \quad \blacksquare @@ roots of equals

A turn about any other point is a slide, a turn and a slide back (20c4), so by 20b it keeps distances too.

20d3Flips

Flip-keeps technique: a flip keeps every distance.

F(Q) - F(P) = F(Q - P) @@ F(P) = 2(P · u)u − P: differences go through (the dot technique shares out)
|F(v)|^2 = 4(v \cdot u)^2(u \cdot u) - 4(v \cdot u)^2 + |v|^2 @@ the dot technique on (2(v · u)u − v) · (2(v · u)u − v)
= |v|^2 @@ u · u = 1
\htmlClass{new}{d(FP, FQ) = d(P, Q)} \quad \blacksquare @@ and for a line not through O, slide, flip, slide back (20b)

20d4Congruent, Defined

From here on, two sets of points S and T are congruent, written S ≅ T, when some isometry f has f(S) = T.

Congruent-is-sorting technique: congruence is an equivalence relation. S ≅ S by doing nothing. If f(S) = T then f⁻¹(T) = S, an isometry by 20b. If f(S) = T and g(T) = U then g(f(S)) = U.

Congruent figures agree in every length. If f(S) = T, each distance between two points of S is the distance between their images in T. This is what the old geometry meant by figures “equal in all respects”, and what 18f measured by hand.

The accepted names

Congruence by an isometry is sometimes called direct congruence when no flip is needed, and opposite congruence when one is (20g).

Who said it first

The old geometry is Euclid’s Elements, and “equal in all respects” is its phrase. In 20d.4.5, the philosopher is Arthur Schopenhauer, The World as Will and Representation (1819), §15, on Euclid's “mousetrap proofs”.

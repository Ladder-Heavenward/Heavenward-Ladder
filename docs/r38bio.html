38·bio · biology

Is a neuron a gate?

Is a neuron a gate? A nerve cell either fires or does not, and each spike is all or none, so it is tempting to read a brain as a circuit of the gates of this rung. The first picture of a neuron as a gate did exactly that: a unit adds its inputs, each with a weight, and fires when the sum reaches a threshold. Such units build both, either and not, and so every table. The complex rung’s newsflash then gave a nerve cell as a circuit of its own: a store of charge that leaks, filled by its inputs, firing when it climbs a set height. That cell does more than a gate. It has a rest after each spike, so it has a top speed. It adds only inputs that come close together in time, so it is a both-gate with a window. And cells that silence one another, in a ring of an odd number, are this rung’s liar box: a clock, whose period the drive sets. So a neuron is a gate, and also a timer, and the brain’s rhythms are its clocks.

A unit that fires when its weighted sum reaches a threshold builds both, either and not, so every table. A leaking cell with a rest after each spike has a top rate of one over the rest. It adds two kicks only if they come within a window, so it is a both-gate in time. An odd ring of cells that silence each other runs as a clock.

38bio1A unit with a threshold

Give a unit inputs x₁, x₂, … that are 0 or 1, a weight wᵢ for each, and a threshold θ. It gives 1 when w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + ⋯ ≥ θ, and 0 otherwise. A weight above 0 is an input that urges it on; a weight below 0 one that holds it back.

Weighted-threshold technique: with weights 1, 1 and threshold 2 a unit is both; with threshold 1, either; with weight −1 and threshold 0, not; with weights −1, −1 and threshold −1, not-and. So networks of such units build every table (38f1).

x_1 + x_2 \ge 2 \iff x_1 = x_2 = 1 @@ both
x_1 + x_2 \ge 1 \iff x_1 = 1 \text{ or } x_2 = 1 @@ either
-x \ge 0 \iff x = 0 @@ not
-x_1 - x_2 \ge -1 \iff \text{not both} @@ not-and: the sum is −2 only on row 11
\htmlClass{new}{\text{every table, from units with thresholds}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the nand-alone technique (38f1)

One unit draws one straight cut through the rows, so 28·now2’s either-not-both technique says no single unit gives either-but-not-both. Two layers do: either, and not-and, joined by both. With three inputs of weight 1 and threshold 2, one unit gives the majority of three, which takes several gates of 38f: a threshold is a stronger gate than a not-and.

38bio2A top speed

26·then6’s fire technique gave the time from rest to a spike, T = τ ln(RI/(RI − d)), for a leaking cell of time τ driven at RI toward a threshold d above rest. A real cell, once it fires, cannot fire again for a rest t_r of a millisecond or two: its sodium gates are shut and must reopen.

Rate-ceiling technique: a leaking cell with a rest t_r after each spike fires at rate 1/(t_r + τ ln(RI/(RI − d))) when RI > d; the rate starts from 0 at RI = d, rises with the drive, and never reaches 1/t_r.

\text{period} = t_r + \tau\ln\tfrac{RI}{RI - d} @@ the rest, then the climb from rest (26·then6)
\tau = 10\text{ ms},\ d = 15\text{ mV},\ t_r = 2\text{ ms} @@ a typical cell
RI = 20:\ 13.9 + 2 \Rightarrow 63\text{ a second};\ \ RI = 60:\ 2.88 + 2 \Rightarrow 205\text{ a second} @@ checked by machine
RI \to d:\ \ \ln\tfrac{RI}{RI - d} \to \infty @@ the climb never ends
\htmlClass{new}{\text{rate from } 0 \text{ up toward } 500 \text{ a second}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the log heads to 0 as the drive grows, leaving t_r

So the cell turns a drive into a count of spikes a second, a number and not only a 1 or 0: a gate reads a level, and a cell also reads how much. The ceiling is why no nerve sends more than a few hundred spikes a second, and why loud sounds and bright lights are coded by more cells firing, not by any one firing faster.

38bio3A both-gate in time

A kick from one input lifts the cell by k at once, and then the lift leaks away as k e^(−t/τ), by 24·then2’s target technique with the drive gone. Take a threshold d with k < d ≤ 2k: one kick alone never fires the cell.

Coincidence-window technique: a cell with threshold d, kicked by k at time 0 and again by k at time Δ, with k < d ≤ 2k, fires exactly when Δ ≤ τ ln(k/(d − k)).

V(\Delta) - E = k\,e^{-\Delta/\tau} + k @@ the first kick, leaked for Δ, and the second
k\,e^{-\Delta/\tau} + k \ge d \iff e^{-\Delta/\tau} \ge \tfrac{d - k}{k} @@ fire when the lift reaches d
\iff \Delta \le \tau \ln\tfrac{k}{d - k} @@ take logs (17)
\tau = 10\text{ ms},\ k = 10\text{ mV},\ d = 15\text{ mV}:\ \ \Delta \le 10 \ln 2 \approx 6.9\text{ ms} @@ a window of about 7 ms
\htmlClass{new}{\text{both inputs, within } 6.9 \text{ ms}} \quad \blacksquare @@ a both-gate whose rows include time

Owls find a mouse in the dark this way: cells in the brainstem get a spike from each ear and fire only when the two arrive together, and the lengths of the fibres from the two ears set which cell gets them together for which direction. The windows there are tens of millionths of a second, set by cells with a very short τ.

38bio4A ring of cells as a clock

Some cells silence the next cell they reach: while a cell fires, its target is held at rest. Each such cell, driven steadily, is a not: it fires when its holder is quiet. 38g3’s ring-period technique counted a ring of an odd number of nots, each with one delay. A cell has two delays: the climb from rest, τ ln(RI/(RI − d)), when it is let go, and a short δ for its silencing to reach the next when it fires.

Inhibitory-ring technique: a ring of an odd number n of steadily driven cells, each silenced by the one before, runs as a clock of period n(δ + τ ln(RI/(RI − d))); a stronger drive runs it faster.

\text{one change goes round the ring twice in a period} @@ an odd ring: after one round the change comes back turned over (38g2)
\text{each cell passes it once as let go, once as silenced} @@ two passes, two kinds of change
\text{period} = n\,(t_\text{let go} + t_\text{silence}) = n\big(\tau\ln\tfrac{RI}{RI - d} + \delta\big) @@ the ring-period count, with unequal delays
n = 3,\ \tau = 10\text{ ms},\ RI = 20,\ d = 15,\ \delta = 1\text{ ms}:\ \ 3 \times 14.9 \approx 44.6\text{ ms} @@ checked by machine
\htmlClass{new}{\text{about } 22 \text{ cycles a second}} \quad \blacksquare @@ with equal delays this is 38g3’s 2nτ

Rings like this, of cells that hold one another back, set the beat of walking in the spinal cord of a lamprey and of a cat, the rhythm of breathing in the brainstem, and the fast waves of 30 to 80 a second seen on a scalp, where the delays are a few milliseconds. Two cells that silence each other are an even ring: they hold one state, a memory of which one fired first (38h), unless each tires and lets the other go.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

The unit with a threshold is the McCulloch–Pitts neuron, or a linear threshold unit; a network of them with weights learned is a perceptron (28·now). The leaking cell is the leaky integrate-and-fire model, its rest the absolute refractory period, and the rate against drive its f–I curve. The both-gate in time is coincidence detection, and its use in the owl the Jeffress model of sound localisation. A ring of cells that silence each other is a central pattern generator built from reciprocal inhibition; the pair that tires is a half-centre oscillator. The crosswalk items here are the integrate-and-fire and rate models of a first course in computational neuroscience, and neural networks as threshold units.

Who said it first

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts gave the threshold unit in 1943, in which a single holding-back input vetoed firing outright. Louis Lapicque modelled the cell as a leaking store of charge in 1907. Lloyd Jeffress proposed the owl’s, and the ear’s, coincidence scheme in 1948, and Masakazu Konishi and Catherine Carr found it in the barn owl in the 1980s. Thomas Graham Brown proposed the half-centre for walking in 1911.

A proof that is not allowed

“Each spike is all or none, so a nerve cell is a gate with a 1 or 0 out, and the brain is a circuit of 38f.” Not allowed as it stands: the cell’s output is a train of spikes whose rate and timing carry the message, and its inputs add only within a window. A gate is the small case of the cell, with the times left out; the cell is a gate and a timer at once.

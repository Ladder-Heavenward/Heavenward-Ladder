37g · proof

A Sound Can Be a Name

One letter of the alphabet, said aloud, already names four things. There is the letter itself, a mark on the page. There is the sound it stands for, a vowel. There is the note musicians call by that letter, and there is the chord built on that note. Rung 36 found that a colour is a name, a set and an inductive object at once, and this step finds the same of a sound, each resting on the one before. A sound is a name in rung 0’s sense, the sound itself and not only its letters. A vowel is a set, every sound the ear files under it. A note is an inductive object, built from one frequency by doubling and halving, so that induction runs over it as it runs over the counting numbers. A chord is a set of notes, and order does not change it. And statements about sounds are statements like any others: some are proved, and some are facts with a rate.

The letter, the vowel it stands for, the note and the chord are four names spelled with one mark. A vowel is the set of every sound heard as it. A note is the set of one frequency and all its doublings and halvings, built so that induction runs over it. A chord is a set of notes, in any order.

37g1The Letter, the Sound, the Note

0a2 gave a letter for a thing. The ladder keeps the first letter of the alphabet out of its prose as a name, because in English it is already a word; this footing shows it is already more than that. Spelled with that one mark, there are at least four names: the letter on the page; the vowel it stands for in “father”, a sound; the note musicians write with it, A; and the chord they build on that note.

From here on, a sound name is any of these, or a recording, or a sound made in the air and pointed at. As 36g1 did for colours, the sameness between two sound names is 0b’s: they name one thing exactly when the matter at hand makes them. On the matter of pitch, the note A and the sound of a tuning fork at 440 Hz are one; on the matter of speech, they are not, and the vowel is another thing again.

Use against mention (0a9) holds here as for letters. “Tune to A” uses the note name. “A is the first letter” mentions the letter. “Say ah” mentions the vowel by its sound. The moves 0b withheld, swapping inside a mention, are withheld here too.

Sound-name technique: two sound names spelled alike may name different things, and which thing is set by the matter at hand, never by the spelling. Proof: 0b’s sameness is relative to the matter at hand, and the four names above differ on the matters of pitch, speech and writing.

37g2A Sound as a Set

The vowel “ah” is not one sound. A thousand people saying it make a thousand different pressures, at different pitches, loudnesses and lengths, and the ear files all of them under one name. By 37f3, what they share is that their first two formants lie in one region of the (F1, F2) plane.

From here on, a vowel is the set of every sound whose first two formants lie in its region, as 36g2 made a colour the set of every spectrum on one triple. So a vowel is a set of sounds, and a sound belongs to the vowel when its formants lie in the region. A recording of one person saying “ah” is a member of the vowel, and is not the vowel (2d12).

Vowel-set technique: two vowels are the same exactly when their regions are, and a vowel has more members than the counting numbers. Proof: same members (2d6) means the same formant pairs, which means the same region. And every sound s in the vowel stays in it when its pressure is scaled by any real k from 1 to 2, since scaling changes no formant; so the vowel holds a copy of an interval of reals, and 35b says those cannot be listed.

So the ear is a function from sounds to vowels, many to one, as 36c1’s col was from spectra to triples. Its blindness is of the same kind: two sounds in one vowel are two sounds, told apart by the air, and filed together by the ear.

37g3The Note as an Inductive Object

Play A at 440 Hz, at 880, at 220, at 110: every musician calls each of them A. The note is not one frequency but a family, and the family is built the way 4b9 built ℕ: start with one member, and close up under a move.

From here on, a set of frequencies is closed under octaves when it holds 2f and f/2 whenever it holds f. The note A is the smallest set of frequencies that holds 440 and is closed under octaves: the common part of all such sets, as 4b9 took the common part of every inductive set.

Octave-induction technique: to prove that every frequency in the note A has a property, prove it for 440, and prove that doubling and halving keep it. Proof: the frequencies with the property form a set that holds 440 and is closed under octaves; the note A is the smallest such set, so it is part of this one (4c2’s set form, with two moves in place of one).

P(f):\ f = 440 \cdot 2^k \text{ for a whole number } k \text{ (positive, 0 or negative)} @@ the property to prove
P(440):\ k = 0;\ \ P(f) \Rightarrow P(2f), P(f/2):\ k \to k \pm 1 @@ the base and the two steps
\text{so every frequency in A is } 440 \cdot 2^k @@ the octave-induction technique
440 \cdot 2^k = 660 \cdot 2^j \Rightarrow 2^{k - j} = 3/2 @@ the pure E above, 660 Hz, and its octaves
\htmlClass{new}{\text{impossible: } 2^{k-j+1} = 3 \text{ has no answer}} \quad \blacksquare @@ 4j18, one prime list

So induction applies to sounds as it applies to numbers, because a note is built as ℕ was. A tuning is a choice of one frequency for each note name: equal tuning puts the note n semitones above A at 440 · 2^(n/12 + k), and the technique above shows why no pure fifth is ever an octave of A: rung 6’s puzzle, proved again in one line by the same prime list.

37g4A Chord Is a Set of Notes

The chord musicians call A major is the notes A, C♯ and E sounded together. Each is a note in 37g3’s sense, a set of frequencies, so the chord is a set whose members are sets (2d5).

From here on, a chord is a set of notes, and a voicing of it is a choice of one or more frequencies from each of its notes, played together. Strum A major on a guitar and you play one voicing; play it on a piano with C♯ at the bottom and you play another.

Voicing technique: two voicings that use the same notes are the same chord, whatever order and octaves they use, and may still be different sounds. Proof: the chord is the set of notes, and a new order or a repeat names the same set (2d8); the two voicings hold different frequencies, so their recipes differ, and by the recipe technique (37d1) they are different sounds.

37g5Sounds Under the Arrangement

Statements about sounds are statements like any other, and this works just as 36g4 did for colours. A statement about notes, chords or recipes is a statement about sets and sums of turnings, and a column proves it line by line. Every technique on this rung was proved that way.

Some statements about sounds have no answer in the arrangement at all. “This sound is an A” is true or false for a sound with a pitch; for a hiss it has none, since a hiss has no pitch (37b1). And some lines enter a column only with a rate, as facts by cases (36g3): the hearing window, the bands of 37d3, the vowel regions of 37f3. A proof about what a listener hears carries those rates forward to its end.

Rate-forward technique: a conclusion about what an ear hears is exact about the sound and carries the rates of the laid-down lines it used. Proof: 36g3’s cases technique, applied with the lines of 37b2, 37c4, 37d3, 37d4 and 37f3 as the facts by cases.

The accepted names

The note as a set of frequencies is a pitch class, and closing under octaves is octave equivalence. A voicing that puts a note other than the root at the bottom is an inversion. The (F1, F2) plane is the vowel chart, or formant chart. “Tune to A” and “A is a letter” are use and mention.

Who said it first

Pitch classes as sets, and chords as sets of them, were made a working mathematics by Milton Babbitt in the 1940s and Allen Forte in The Structure of Atonal Music (1973).

A proof that is not allowed

“A is 440 Hz. 880 Hz is not 440 Hz. So 880 Hz is not an A.” Not allowed: the first line uses “A” for one member and the conclusion uses it for the set; by 37g3, A is the set {440 · 2ᵏ}, and 880 is in it.

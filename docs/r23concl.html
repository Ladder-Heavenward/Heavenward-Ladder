23·∎ · conclusion

A power sum is rebuilt from one point

Inside its reach, a power sum is rebuilt from its centre: its coefficients are its slopes there over factorials, and its slopes and areas come term by term. The growth function, the waves and the logarithm are such sums. A smooth function need not be.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 23 asked how much one point can tell about a smooth function. Inside its reach, a power sum is rebuilt from its centre: its coefficients are its slopes there over factorials, and it may be taken apart term by term. Many functions are such sums. Not every smooth function is one.

23a built the one polynomial that matches each count of slopes. 23b measured its error. 23c found the reach of an endless power sum. 23d found the settling that keeps curves whole. 23e took power sums apart term by term. 23f found the smooth function that refuses. 23g used the sums to show e and π are not fractions. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

A power sum is rebuilt from its centre inside its reach; e, sin, cos and ln(1 + x) are such sums; and smooth alone is not enough.

If f has n slopes at w, Tₙ(x) = Σₖ₌₀ⁿ f⁽ᵏ⁾(w)(x − w)ᵏ/k! is the only polynomial of degree at most n matching them, and f(x) − Tₙ(x) = f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(c)(x − w)ⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1)! for some c between w and x.

eˣ = Σ xᵏ/k!, sin x = Σ (−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k + 1)! and cos x = Σ (−1)ᵏx²ᵏ/(2k)! for every real x; ln(1 + x) = Σ (−1)ᵏ⁺¹xᵏ/k for −1 < x ≤ 1; arctan x = Σ (−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k + 1) for |x| ≤ 1.

Every power sum Σ uₖ(x − w)ᵏ has a reach R: it settles in size where |x − w| < R and wanders where |x − w| > R. If |uₖ₊₁/uₖ| → L, then R = 1/L.

Inside the reach it settles everywhere at once on each [w − r, w + r] with r < R, its total f is smooth, f′ and ∫f are found term by term, and uₖ = f⁽ᵏ⁾(w)/k!.

e^(−1/x²), with 0 at 0, is smooth and has every slope 0 at 0, so it is not rebuilt from 0.

e and π are not fractions.

The proof

Each part below is a technique already proved on a step of this rung. The proof cites them by name and joins them.

p^{(k)}(w) = k!\,u_k @@ the slopes of a power of (x − w) (23a)
T_n \text{ is the only match} @@ the match technique (23a)
\htmlClass{new}{f - T_n = \tfrac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-w)^{n+1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the match-error technique (23b)
  
|R_n(x)| \le \tfrac{K|x-a|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!} \to 0 @@ the error-ceiling technique (23b) and 22h2
e^x,\ \sin,\ \cos \text{ are their sums everywhere} @@ 23b2
\htmlClass{new}{\ln(1+x),\ \arctan x \text{ from the finite geometric sum}} \quad \blacksquare @@ 23e4
  
\text{settles at } x_0 \Rightarrow \text{settles in size nearer } w @@ the reach technique (23c)
R = \sup\{|x - w| : \text{settles}\},\ \ R = 1/L @@ the reach-of-a-sum and reach-by-ratio techniques (23c)
\text{settles everywhere at once inside } R;\ \text{continuous} @@ the at-once comparison and kept-continuity techniques (23d)
\htmlClass{new}{f' = \textstyle\sum k u_k (x-w)^{k-1},\ \ u_k = \tfrac{f^{(k)}(w)}{k!}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the same-reach, term-by-term and smooth-sum techniques (23e)
  
f^{(n)}(0) = 0 \text{ for every } n,\ f(x) = e^{-1/x^2} > 0 \text{ for } x \ne 0 @@ the flat-point technique (23f)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{smooth} \not\Rightarrow \text{rebuilt}} \quad \blacksquare @@ its matching sum is 0 (23f)
\htmlClass{new}{e \ne \tfrac pq,\ \ \pi \ne \tfrac ab} \quad \blacksquare @@ the irrationality techniques (23g)

So the survey’s title holds for every function given by a power sum, and for the functions this ladder has met so far. It fails for e^(−1/x²). A smooth function is rebuilt from one point exactly when it is a power sum near that point.

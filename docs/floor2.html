Analysis floor

Rungs 8 to 11. You arrive with the counting numbers and the fractions, and find the line between them full of holes. Here the guesswork turns to closeness: how near is near enough, and who gets to say. You leave able to prove what “as close as you like” means, which for a very long time nobody could.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

The Logic floor built numbers from nothing and gave you the techs to reason about them. This floor asks a question that sounds too simple to need asking: what is a point on a line? The fractions seem to fill the line, and yet they leave holes. Rung 8 finds the holes and fills them.

With the line whole, functions come next. A function is a rule that gives one output for each input, and rung 9 makes that exact. You will draw functions as graphs, run them backwards, and build new ones from old.

Then comes the idea that took the longest to get right. People used “gets as close as you like” for two thousand years before anyone could say exactly what it meant. Rung 10 turns it into a game between a challenger and you, played with every and some. Rung 11 uses the game to say what it means to draw a curve without lifting the pen.

This floor is where the line, the function and the limit become things you can prove, not pictures you trust. Most of what comes after stands on it.

Expect this floor to feel slower than the last one. The proofs are longer, and the statements have more every and some in them. That is not a sign you are lost. It is the sign of a floor that is doing its job, and you have only seen the start of what these tools can do.

The rungs of this floor

  • 8 · drawing lines
  • 9 · making functions
  • 10 · pushing the limit as close as you can imagine
  • 11 · drawing without lifting the pen

At the top of the floor are its conclusion and its measuring stick. Passing it gives you the title analyst, and the Derivation floor opens above.

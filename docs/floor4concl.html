What the Geometry Floor Shows

A shape is a statement about pairs of numbers, and a geometry is whatever its moves leave unchanged.

The floor conclusion · its principles, in the words and marks it built

This page opens once every step of the Geometry floor is ticked. Its principles are proved on the rungs below, so it waits for them.

What the floor set out to show

Until this floor the plane was only a place to draw graphs. The Geometry floor asked what a shape is, what a move is, and what a geometry is. It promised to build distance, shape and turning from what the ladder already had, and to prove every answer.

Here are the floor’s five principles again, one to a rung, each in the words and the marks the floor built. Each links to the rung conclusion that proves it.

The principles, rung by rung

17 · turning multiplying into adding

The area under 1/t from 1 to x, named ln x, turns multiplying into adding.

18 · drawing with numbers

A point is a pair of numbers, a distance is a square root, and every fact about lines, circles and conics is a calculation.

19 · undoing the moves

Moves that combine, undo, and include doing nothing force the rest: one way to do nothing, one undo for each move, and undoing runs backwards.

20 · moving the plane

A geometry is a group of moves, and its facts are exactly what those moves keep.

21 · rounding an arc

An angle is a distance walked around the circle of radius 1, and cosine and sine are where the walk ends.

What the floor shows

The floor built its shapes from two things: numbers in pairs, and moves. Rung 17 finished the kit of functions first. ln is an area that turns multiplying into adding, and run backwards it gives a function that is its own slope, and powers for every real exponent. Rung 18 made a point a pair of numbers and a distance a square root, so a drawing became an equation.

On the Geometry floor, a shape became a statement about pairs of numbers, and a geometry became a group of moves and whatever those moves keep.

Rung 19 asked only how moves combine, and found how much that one question forces. Rung 20 put the moves on the plane. Every move that keeps distances is at most three flips, and the old tests for congruent triangles came out as techniques about those moves. Rung 21 measured turning as a distance walked round the circle. Where the walk ends gave cosine and sine, and each turned out to be the slope of the other.

So the floor demonstrates that the old geometry of compass and straightedge was right, and shows why: its facts are what its moves keep. Change the moves and the geometry changes with them. There are more geometries than the one you were taught, and the ladder has only met the first.

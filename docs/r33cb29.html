33·cb29 · newsflash for 29

Gravity as Curvature

Rung 29 found that space can be curved and that its curve can be read from its ruler alone. It left open what curves it. This page brings the news. Everything falls the same way: a feather and a hammer, dropped together where there is no air, land together, and the best measurements find no difference to one part in a million billion. So in a room that falls freely, gravity cannot be felt at all. That suggests gravity is not a pull on things but a shape of the ruler that things follow. Rung 33 supplies the other half. The pull of gravity has a potential, and the potential obeys the still-state statement with a source. Put the potential into the ruler of space and time, and the straightest paths of that ruler are the paths of falling things. The statement that fixes the ruler is a rate statement in space and time of exactly the rung’s kind, and in a slow field it is the potential’s statement. From the ruler come the field outside a star, the bending of starlight by the sun, measured at an eclipse in 1919, and a ruler for the whole universe that grows.

Everything falls alike, so gravity can be read as a shape of the ruler that falling things follow straightest. In a slow field the ruler’s time part holds the potential of 33j, and its straightest paths are the laws of falling. Outside a star the ruler takes one round form. Light crossing it bends by twice what falling alone would give, and a ruler that grows in time gives a universe whose galaxies spread apart.

33·cb29.1A Room That Falls

A measured law: every body, whatever it is made of, falls with the same acceleration in the same place. Pendulums of gold and of wood, balances of platinum and of water, and two masses of titanium and platinum carried round the earth in a satellite in 2022 agree to about one part in 10¹⁵.

Falling-room technique: in a room that falls freely through a field that is the same everywhere in the room, every body’s motion relative to the room is the motion it would have with no field at all.

x_k^{\prime\prime} = g + \tfrac{F_k}{m_k} @@ each body k, with the field g and any other force Fₖ: the law of motion with one acceleration for every body
X^{\prime\prime} = g @@ the room itself falls freely
(x_k - X)^{\prime\prime} = \tfrac{F_k}{m_k} @@ subtract
\htmlClass{new}{\text{inside the room, } g \text{ has gone}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the same law of motion as with no field

The step from that to geometry is a guess, and a bold one. Since nothing in the room can feel the field, the field is not a force on the bodies; it is the shape of the ruler that the room and the bodies follow, straightest. Where the field differs from place to place, rooms falling side by side drift apart or together, and that drift is a curving of the ruler that no choice of room removes. A falling room is the flat chart of 29h, good only near its middle.

33·cb29.2The Ruler of a Slow Field

29·phys gave the ruler of space and time with no gravity: ds² = c²dt² − dx² − dy² − dz². A clock at rest reads the time τ with c dτ = ds. 29·now found that a clock where the potential is φ runs fast by φ/c² of each second. Put both into one ruler for a slow field: Here c is the speed of light again (29·phys1), not rung 33's wave speed.

Slow-ruler technique: in this ruler, the paths of slow bodies that make the time on their own clocks greatest obey x″ = −∇φ: the law of falling, with φ the potential of 33j.

c\,d\tau = \sqrt{(1 + 2\varphi/c^2)c^2 - (1 - 2\varphi/c^2)v^2}\;dt @@ along a path with speed v
d\tau \approx \big(1 + \tfrac{\varphi}{c^2} - \tfrac{v^2}{2c^2}\big)dt @@ φ/c² and v/c small: √(1 + ε) ≈ 1 + ε/2, the matching line of 23a; terms of size φv²/c⁴ dropped
\textstyle\int d\tau = \int dt - \tfrac{1}{c^2}\int\big(\tfrac12v^2 - \varphi\big)dt @@ collect
\text{greatest } \textstyle\int d\tau \iff \text{least } \int\big(\tfrac12v^2 - \varphi\big)dt @@ the first total is the same for every path between the same two events
\htmlClass{new}{x^{\prime\prime} = -\nabla\varphi} \quad \blacksquare @@ the action-to-force technique (29·then), the best-path technique (29g) with potential energy φ per kilogram

So in the slow ruler, falling is following the straightest path in time. And the potential obeys Δφ = 4πGρ (33j1). Gravity’s full statement is a rate statement in space and time of the kind on this rung: it ties certain second slopes of the ruler’s entries, the curving that 29e read from a ruler, to the density of energy in space. It has the heat statement’s and the string’s kind of slopes, ten entries at once, and in a slow field it comes down to 33j’s statement for φ. The full statement is held in trust here; its accepted name is in the cellar.

33·cb29.3The Field Outside a Star

Outside a round star of mass M nothing is there, and gravity’s full statement asks a certain curving of the ruler to be 0. In 1916 the round answer was found, a ruler that depends only on the distance r from the star’s centre. It is held in trust here:

Star-ruler technique: far from the star, the time part of this ruler is the slow ruler’s, with φ = −GM/r, the potential of a point mass outside a round body.

1 - \tfrac{r_s}{r} = 1 + \tfrac{2\varphi}{c^2} @@ match the time parts
\varphi = -\tfrac{c^2r_s}{2r} = -\tfrac{GM}{r} @@ r_s = 2GM/c²
-\nabla\varphi = -\tfrac{GM}{r^2}\,\tfrac{x}{r} @@ the slope along the reach
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the pull } GM/r^2 \text{ toward the centre}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the shell-flux technique’s pull outside a round body (33j1)

So far from the star nothing new happens. Near it, the factor 1 − r_s/r matters. At r = r_s the time part vanishes and the space part passes every bound. For the sun, r_s = 2 × 1.327 × 10²⁰ / (2.998 × 10⁸)² ≈ 2.95 km, deep inside it, where the ruler outside no longer applies. For the earth, r_s ≈ 8.9 mm. A star squeezed inside its own r_s is the dark star of the accepted name in the cellar.

33·cb29.4Starlight Bent by the Sun

Light keeps ds = 0. In the slow ruler that gives its speed in x, y, z as c√((1 + 2φ/c²)/(1 − 2φ/c²)) ≈ c(1 + 2φ/c²). So the slow field acts on light like glass whose index is n = 1 − 2φ/c², and 29·then’s straightest-light technique says light follows the straightest paths of n²(dx² + dy²).

Light-bend technique: a ray passing a mass M at closest distance b, slowly enough bent, turns through 4GM/(c²b).

n = 1 + \tfrac{2GM}{c^2\sqrt{y^2 + z^2}} @@ φ = −GM/r, the ray travelling along z at height y near b
y^{\prime\prime} \approx \tfrac{\partial n}{\partial y} @@ the best-path technique (29g) on ∫ n √(1 + y′²) dz, with y′ small and n near 1
\tfrac{\partial n}{\partial y}\Big|_{y = b} = -\tfrac{2GM\,b}{c^2(b^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} @@ toward the mass
\textstyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tfrac{b\,dz}{(b^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} = \tfrac{2}{b} @@ put z = b tan u (21n)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{turn} = \textstyle\int y^{\prime\prime}\,dz = \tfrac{4GM}{c^2b}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the change in the slope y′ along the whole path

For a ray grazing the sun, b = 6.957 × 10⁸ m, the turn is 4 × 1.327 × 10²⁰ / ((2.998 × 10⁸)² × 6.957 × 10⁸) ≈ 8.49 × 10⁻⁶ of a radian: 1.75 seconds of arc. Half of it comes from the time part of the ruler, the part a falling room would give. The other half comes from the space part, which no falling room sees. Stars near the sun can only be seen when the moon covers it. At the eclipse of 29 May 1919, two teams photographed them and found shifts near 1.6 and 2.0 seconds: the whole, not the half.

33·cb29.5A Ruler That Grows

Take a ruler for the whole universe that is the same at every place and in every direction, with its space part growing in time: ds² = c²dt² − R(t)²(dx² + dy² + dz²). Two galaxies at fixed x, y, z drift apart as R grows, at a speed in proportion to their distance: v = HD, with H = R′/R. Galaxies are measured to recede so, with H now about 70 km/s for every megaparsec, 3.09 × 10¹⁹ km.

Expanding-ball technique: in a universe of even density ρ, with gravity slow on the scale of a ball, (R′/R)² = (8πG/3)ρ − k/R² for a constant k.

D = R(t)\,x_0 @@ a galaxy on the edge of a ball about us, of radius D
D^{\prime\prime} = -\tfrac{G\,M}{D^2},\ \ M = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho D^3 \text{ fixed} @@ the shell-flux technique (33j1): only the mass within pulls, and the ball keeps its galaxies
\tfrac12D^{\prime 2} - \tfrac{GM}{D} = \text{constant} @@ multiply by D′ and take areas (16c)
\big(\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{R}\big)^2 = \tfrac{2GM}{D^3} - \tfrac{k}{R^2} @@ divide by ½D² = ½R²x₀²; k collects the constant
\htmlClass{new}{H^2 = \tfrac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \tfrac{k}{R^2}} \quad \blacksquare @@ 2GM/D³ = (8πG/3)ρ

With k = 0 the density is 3H²/(8πG) ≈ 9.2 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³, about five protons in every cubic metre. Then ρ falls as 1/R³, and R grows as t^(2/3), so the universe’s age is 2/(3H) ≈ 9.3 billion years. The oldest stars are measured older than that, near 12 or 13 billion years. One of the two is wrong, or something else is in the universe besides mass. The measurement of 1998 found the growth speeding up, and the something else has a name in the cellar and no explanation yet.

The accepted names

The falling-room technique is the equivalence principle, and the measured law it stands on is the universality of free fall, the equality of inertial and gravitational mass. Gravity’s full statement is Einstein’s field equation, G_μν = (8πG/c⁴)T_μν, in which the Einstein tensor G_μν is built from second slopes of the metric g_μν and T_μν holds the density and flow of energy and momentum. The slow ruler is the weak-field (Newtonian) limit. The star’s ruler is the Schwarzschild metric and r_s the Schwarzschild radius; a star inside it is a black hole. The growing ruler is the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric, R the scale factor, H the Hubble constant, v = HD Hubble’s law (the Hubble–Lemaître law), and the expanding-ball technique Friedmann’s equation. The density with k = 0 is the critical density. The something else is dark energy.

Who said it first

Galileo Galilei argued that bodies fall alike; Isaac Newton tested it with pendulums; Loránd Eötvös with torsion balances (1889 to 1908); the satellite MICROSCOPE reported its final result in 2022. Albert Einstein had the falling-room thought in 1907, called it the happiest of his life, and gave the field equation in November 1915, as David Hilbert reached the same equation by a best-path argument. Karl Schwarzschild found the star’s ruler in 1916, writing from the Russian front, months before he died. Arthur Eddington and Frank Dyson sent the teams of 1919 to Sobral in Brazil and Príncipe off Africa. Alexander Friedmann found the growing universes in 1922, Georges Lemaître in 1927, and Edwin Hubble measured the recession in 1929. Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt and Adam Riess found the growth speeding up in 1998.

Wrong moves, worked

“Light has no mass, so gravity cannot pull it, so starlight is not bent.” Not allowed: in the slow ruler, nothing is pulled; everything follows straightest paths, and light’s path is a straightest path too. Newton’s own pull, applied to a particle of light, gives half the bending, and the full ruler gives twice that.

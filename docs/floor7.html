Advanced floor

Rungs 37 to 42. You arrive having seen the near edge of the field, which is all anyone has seen. Here the roads run out into country still being mapped, and the guesses are the kind the people at the front are making now. You leave with the questions the field is still asking, and a few of your own.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

This floor follows several roads at once, each to the edge of what is known. Rung 37 asks what a machine can decide, and finds questions that no machine can answer. Rung 38 shows that counting alone can say how things connect.

Rungs 39 to 41 take plus and times on their own: on a clock, as rules standing alone, and as the key to an old question about formulas for roots. Rung 42 steps back and asks what mathematics is about, and answers with arrows.

On this floor, the ladder hands you the tools the field uses to ask its own open questions.

Many of the annexes here point at work still under way. Some of it was settled only recently, and some of it is not settled at all. You are close to the front now, and the front is always a beginning.

The rungs of this floor

  • 37 · some questions no machine can answer
  • 38 · counting alone says how things connect
  • 39 · arithmetic works on a clock
  • 40 · the rules of plus and times stand on their own
  • 41 · past degree four, no formula
  • 42 · arrows matter more than things

At the top of the floor is its conclusion, and beside it the floor’s certification: one fresh question a rung, about an hour in all. It covers the floor’s last rung too, so that rung’s last step needs no certification of its own. It gives no new title. Passing it opens the Endless floor, where the titles stop because the ladder has no top.

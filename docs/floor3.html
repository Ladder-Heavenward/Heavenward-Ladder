F3 · floor

Derivation floor

Rungs 12 to 16. You arrive with limits and unbroken curves. Here you guess at things no ruler can reach, the speed of a single instant and the area under a bend, and then trap each guess from both sides until it has nowhere left to go. You leave able to measure change in an instant and to add up what lies under a curve.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

The Analysis floor proved what a limit is. This floor spends it. Rung 12 shows that an unbroken curve skips no value on its way, and finds the one place in each proof where the line with no holes is needed.

Rung 13 asks how fast something changes at a single instant, when an instant has no length. The answer is a limit of slopes, and from it come rules for products, powers and chains, each proved once. Rung 14 reads a whole shape from its slopes: where it rises, where it falls, where it turns.

Rung 15 asks a different question: how much is under a curve? The answer is squeezed between estimates from below and above. Then rung 16 shows the two questions are one question asked twice, once forwards and once backwards.

On this floor, slope and area turn out to undo each other. That one fact is the heart of the study whose accepted name is calculus.

You will finish this floor able to do what took the best minds of two centuries to settle. That is a real height. It is also the first few steps of a much longer climb, and the ladder has barely begun to show you what you can do.

The rungs of this floor

  • 12 · skipping nothing on the way
  • 13 · measuring change in an instant
  • 14 · reading a shape from its slopes
  • 15 · measuring what is under a curve
  • 16 · running change backwards

At the top of the floor is its conclusion, and beside it the floor’s certification: one fresh question a rung, about an hour in all. It covers the floor’s last rung too, so that rung’s last step needs no certification of its own. Passing it gives you the title deriver, and the Geometry floor opens above. Like every title on the ladder, it is a word for what you have begun, not for what you have finished.

Can a Shape Be Turned Into a Number by Cutting It Up?

Can a shape be turned into a number by cutting it up? Send each circle to a space of vectors, and each surface with rim circles to a straight map from the space of its incoming rims to the space of its outgoing ones, so that gluing surfaces along rims is running maps in a row. A closed surface has no rims at all, and goes to a number. Every surface cuts into four kinds of piece, so the whole rule is fixed by what it does to a disc and a pair of trousers, each read both ways round, and those must obey one picture rule. With a small choice of space the number for a surface with g holes is 2ᵍ, and the number for the doughnut is the dimension of the space, as ∞א‎’s loop of wire was. Since 2009 it has been claimed that in every dimension such a rule is fixed by its value on a single point; in 2020 it was proved that the simplest such rules in four dimensions cannot tell apart the smooth shapes that rung 34 left open.

Things: lists of circles. Arrows: surfaces with in-rims and out-rims, glued as colimits (47e3); χ adds along circles. Pieces: disc in (η), disc out (ε), trousers two-to-one (m), one-to-two (Δ). Then A = Z(circle) has a product that turns about, a unit, and ε with the picture rule. Example A = ℂ², e₁, e₂, ε(eᵢ) = ½: Δ(eᵢ) = 2eᵢ ⊗ eᵢ, handle H = mΔ(1) = (2, 2), Z(surface with g holes) = ε(Hᵍ) = 2ᵍ. Z(doughnut) = dim A.

inf25.1Surfaces as Arrows

Make a category. Its things are finite lists of circles. An arrow from one list to another is a surface whose rim is those circles, the first list marked incoming and the second outgoing. Two arrows run in a row by gluing the outgoing rims of the first to the incoming rims of the second, a colimit of 47e3. The arrow that does nothing is a tube, a circle times a stretch.

Glued-count technique: gluing two surfaces along whole circles adds their counts: χ(S glued to T) = χ(S) + χ(T).

\chi(S \cup T) = \chi(S) + \chi(T) - \chi(S \cap T) @@ cut both into triangles that agree on the rims; corners, edges and faces add, the shared ones counted twice
\chi(\text{circle}) = n - n = 0 @@ a circle cut into n corners and n edges
\chi(\text{disc}) = 1,\ \ \chi(\text{trousers}) = -1 @@ <a href="r34f.html#c23405">34f3</a>’s count, or by cutting
\htmlClass{new}{\text{trousers with two legs capped: } -1 + 1 + 1 = 1 = \chi(\text{disc})} \quad \blacksquare @@ capped trousers are a disc

inf25.2Four Pieces

Four surfaces make all the rest. A disc read as starting a circle, from no circles to one; a disc read as ending one; trousers with two legs in and a waist out; and trousers with a waist in and two legs out. By 34f4’s surface list, a closed surface in one piece with two sides is a sphere with g handles.

Four-pieces technique: a sphere with g handles is a disc in, then g times trousers out-then-in, then a disc out: 2g + 2 pieces.

\text{disc in} + g \times (\text{trousers } 1 \to 2,\ \text{trousers } 2 \to 1) + \text{disc out} @@ each pair of trousers, split then rejoined, is a handle
\chi = 1 + g(-1 - 1) + 1 @@ the glued-count technique
= 2 - 2g @@ add up
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the count of a sphere with } g \text{ handles}} \quad \blacksquare @@ as 34f4 found; g = 0 gives the sphere, two discs

inf25.3What the Pieces Must Go To

A rule Z sends the circle to a space A, a list of circles to A ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ A, and no circles to the numbers. The disc in goes to a vector 1 in A, the trousers to a product m : A ⊗ A → A, the disc out to a reading ε : A → numbers, and the other trousers to Δ : A → A ⊗ A. Shapes that are the same must go to the same map.

Product-rules technique: the product m has 1 as a unit, turns about, and has brackets that do not matter.

\text{trousers with one leg capped by a disc in} = \text{tube} \Rightarrow m(1, a) = a @@ capping a leg is the same shape as a tube
\text{turn the trousers over, swapping the legs} \Rightarrow m(a, b) = m(b, a) @@ the same shape
\text{two trousers, three legs in, either way} \Rightarrow m(m(a, b), c) = m(a, m(b, c)) @@ both are a sphere with three holes in and one out
\htmlClass{new}{(m, 1) \text{ is a product that turns about, with a unit}} \quad \blacksquare @@ and the picture rule below ties m to Δ

The picture rule: splitting then joining with a leg passed across is the same shape as joining then splitting, so (m ⊗ I)(I ⊗ Δ) = Δ m. With ε, the pairing ε(m(a, b)) and the copairing Δ(1) yank straight as ∞א‎.3’s cup and cap do. These are all the demands: a space with such a product, unit and reading gives a rule, and every rule comes from one.

inf25.4The Number for g Holes

inf25.5The Doughnut Is the Dimension

∞א‎.2 found that a loop of wire, a cup then a cap, is the number n, the dimension. That was the same kind of rule one dimension down: points go to a space, bent wires to the cup and cap, and a closed loop to a number. The doughnut is a circle times a loop.

Doughnut technique: for every rule of this kind, the doughnut goes to dim A.

\text{doughnut} = \text{tube with its ends glued together} @@ a circle carried once round a loop
\text{gluing ends} = \text{copairing, then the tube, then pairing} @@ a cup and a cap of A, as in ∞א‎
= \textstyle\sum_i \langle e^i, e_i \rangle = \text{trace of } I_A @@ the tube is the map that does nothing
\htmlClass{new}{Z(\text{doughnut}) = \dim A} \quad \blacksquare @@ for the rule above, 2 = dim ℂ²

The 2020 result shows that the simplest kind, built from pieces the way this page builds them from a space, cannot. Whether some richer rule can is open.
A short history · told from more than one side

How we got here

In 2009 a mathematician gave a detailed sketch of the claim that in every dimension a rule of this kind, made suitably layered, is fixed by what it sends a single point to.

In 2020 a mathematician proved that the rules in four dimensions built from a finite stock of pieces can see only coarse features of a shape, and so cannot tell apart smooth shapes that are the same when bending is allowed.

The accepted names

The category of surfaces with rims is the cobordism category 2Cob; a rule Z is a two-dimensional topological quantum field theory, a symmetric monoidal functor 2Cob → Vect. The space A with its product, unit and reading is a commutative Frobenius algebra; the picture rule is the Frobenius relation; the count χ is the Euler characteristic; a sphere with g handles has genus g. The one-dimension-down case is the snake or zigzag identity. The 2009 claim is the cobordism hypothesis; the 2020 rules are semisimple TQFTs, and the shapes they cannot tell apart are exotic smooth structures.

Who said it first

Jacob Lurie, “On the classification of topological field theories” (arXiv 2009); an earlier form, John Baez and James Dolan (1995; date not yet revisited). David Reutter, “Semisimple 4-dimensional topological field theories cannot detect exotic smooth structure” (arXiv 2020).

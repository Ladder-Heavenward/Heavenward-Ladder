22·∎ · conclusion

A Total Is Where the Running Totals Settle

An endless sum has a total when its running totals settle, and on the line with no holes they settle exactly when they crowd together. Tests by comparison, by areas and by alternating signs decide which sums do. When a sum settles only because its signs take turns, the order of its pieces is part of the total.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 22g signs that alternate, and sums that can be rearranged is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 22 asked when an endless sum can have a total. A total is where the running totals settle, and on a line with no holes they settle exactly when they crowd together. Everything else on the rung is that sentence, worked out.

22a settled a climb under a ceiling. 22b found a settling thread in every bounded list. 22c settled lists by crowding, and showed the fractions fail. 22d made a sum’s total the limit of its running totals. 22e compared sums with geometric ones. 22f laid sums beside areas. 22g let the signs take turns, and found that order can matter. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

On the line with no holes, a list settles exactly when it crowds, and a sum has a total exactly when its running totals crowd.

Let s₁, s₂, s₃, … be a list of reals, and Σ uₙ have running totals sₙ = u₁ + ⋯ + uₙ.

If sₙ climbs and has a ceiling, sₙ settles at its lowest ceiling. Every list between a floor and a ceiling has a settling thread.

sₙ settles exactly when, for every ε > 0, some N makes |sₘ − sₙ| < ε for all m, n ≥ N. Over the fractions alone, this fails.

If Σ uₙ settles, uₙ → 0; but Σ 1/n wanders.

For pieces never negative: below a sum that settles, Σ uₙ settles; if uₙ₊₁/uₙ or the nth root of uₙ heads below 1, Σ uₙ settles; if uₙ = f(n) with f falling, Σ uₙ settles exactly when ∫₁^∞ f does; Σ 1/nᵖ settles exactly when p > 1.

If vₙ falls to 0, Σ (−1)ⁿ⁺¹vₙ settles, within vₙ₊₁ of sₙ.

If Σ |uₙ| settles, so does Σ uₙ. If Σ uₙ settles and Σ |uₙ| does not, some order of its pieces settles at any T you choose.

The Proof

Each part below is a technique already proved on a step of this rung. The proof cites them by name and joins them.

s_n \uparrow,\ s_n \le C \Rightarrow s_n \to \sup s_n @@ the climb technique (22a)
|s_n| \le C \Rightarrow \text{a thread } s_{n_k} \text{ settles} @@ the thread and halving techniques (22b)
\text{crowds} \Rightarrow \text{a settling thread} \Rightarrow \text{settles} @@ the settle-crowd and crowd techniques (22c)
\htmlClass{new}{s_n \text{ settles} \Leftrightarrow s_n \text{ crowds, on the line with no holes}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the √2 decimals show the fractions fail (22c)
  
\textstyle\sum u_n = \lim s_n @@ the partial-sum technique (22d)
\textstyle\sum u_n \text{ settles} \Rightarrow u_n \to 0;\ \ \sum \tfrac1n \text{ wanders} @@ the shrink and grouping techniques (22d)
\textstyle\sum u_n \text{ settles} \Leftrightarrow s_n \text{ crowds} @@ the crowd technique for sums (22d)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a total is where the running totals settle}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the lines above
  
0 \le u_n \le v_n,\ \textstyle\sum v_n \text{ settles} \Rightarrow \sum u_n \text{ settles} @@ the comparison technique (22e)
\tfrac{u_{n+1}}{u_n} \to r < 1 \text{ or } \sqrt[n]{u_n} \to r < 1 \Rightarrow \text{settles} @@ the ratio and root tests (22e)
\textstyle\sum f(n) \sim \int_1^\infty f;\ \ \sum n^{-p} \text{ settles} \Leftrightarrow p > 1 @@ the area technique for sums and the p-sums (22f)
\htmlClass{new}{(-1)^{n+1}v_n,\ v_n \downarrow 0 \Rightarrow \text{settles, within } v_{n+1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the bracket technique (22g)
  
\textstyle\sum |u_n| \text{ settles} \Rightarrow \sum u_n \text{ settles} @@ the settles-in-size technique (22e)
\text{settles only by taking turns} \Rightarrow \text{gains and losses each pass every ceiling} @@ 22g2
\htmlClass{new}{\text{some order settles at any } T} \quad \blacksquare @@ the reorder technique (22g)

Read the last block twice. An endless sum is not one number handed over. It is a list of pieces and an order to take them in. When it settles in size, the order does not matter, and the sum behaves like the finite sums of rung 4. When it settles only by taking turns, the order is part of the answer.

That is the endless sum, tamed. Every total you will meet from here, of chances, of areas and of waves, is a place some running totals settle. You have only just begun to see how much an endless list can hold.

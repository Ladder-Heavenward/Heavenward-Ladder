45i · technique

Points at Infinity

Two lines in the plane meet once, unless they are parallel. Add one point for each direction, where the parallel lines of that direction meet, and every two lines meet exactly once. The plane with its points at infinity is the projective plane: lines through the centre of 3-space, written [x : y : z]. In it the ellipse, the parabola and the hyperbola of 18 are one curve, seen against the line at infinity in three ways. This step builds the plane, meets parallels, and unites the conics.

A point is a line through 0 in 3-space: [x : y : z] = [λx : λy : λz]. z ≠ 0 gives the ordinary plane (x/z, y/z); z = 0 is the line at infinity. An equation of degree d becomes one with every term of degree d. y = mx + b and y = mx + c meet at [1 : m : 0]. A conic meets the line at infinity in 0, 1 or 2 real points: ellipse, parabola, hyperbola.

45i1Lines Through the Centre

[x : y : z] = [\lambda x : \lambda y : \lambda z],\ \lambda \ne 0 @@ one point per line through 0
z \ne 0:\ [x : y : z] = [x/z : y/z : 1] @@ the ordinary plane
z = 0:\ [x : y : 0] @@ a line of points at infinity, one per direction
\htmlClass{new}{f(x, y) \text{ of degree } d \mapsto z^d f(x/z, y/z)} @@ every term raised to degree d

The projective plane is the set of lines through the centre of 3-space; its points with z ≠ 0 are the ordinary plane, and those with z = 0 form one more line, at infinity.

45i2Parallel Lines Meet

y = mx + bz,\ \ y = mx + cz @@ raised to degree 1
(b - c)z = 0 \Rightarrow z = 0,\ y = mx @@ they meet only at infinity
[1 : m : 0] @@ the point of direction m

Meeting technique: in the projective plane any two different lines meet in exactly one point; parallel lines meet at the point at infinity of their common direction.

45i3Three Conics Are One

x^2 + y^2 = z^2:\ z = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 0 @@ no real point at infinity: an ellipse
yz = x^2:\ z = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0,\ [0 : 1 : 0] @@ one point, touching: a parabola
x^2 - y^2 = z^2:\ z = 0 \Rightarrow y = \pm x @@ two points, the asymptote directions: a hyperbola
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a change of coordinates of 3-space turns each into each}} @@ one curve, three views (18h)

In the projective plane every conic that does not split is the same curve; ellipse, parabola and hyperbola differ only in meeting the line at infinity in 0, 1 or 2 real points. A painter’s horizon is the line at infinity of the ground.

Accepted names

The plane is the projective plane ℙ²; [x : y : z] are homogeneous coordinates; raising to degree d is homogenization. The two turning-number points on every circle, [1 : i : 0] and [1 : −i : 0], are the circular points at infinity.

Who said it first

Girard Desargues added points at infinity in 1639, from the painters’ perspective; August Möbius and Julius Plücker gave the coordinates in 1827 and 1830; Jean-Victor Poncelet found the circular points in 1822.

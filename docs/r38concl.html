38·∎ · conclusion

Arithmetic Describes Its Own Proofs

Proofs are finite, checked by a recipe and listed. What is reached is true in every world, and what is true in every world is reached. Endless worlds have countable ones, and arithmetic has numbers past every numeral. Coded as numbers, arithmetic has a true statement it does not reach, cannot reach its own freedom from clash, and cannot define its truth.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 38k truth cannot be defined inside is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 38 asked what the techniques of reasoning look like from outside. What is reached from a collection of axioms is exactly what is true in every world where they hold; and arithmetic, describing its own proofs, has a true statement it does not reach, cannot reach its own freedom from clash, and cannot define its own truth. The price is that the axioms of arithmetic, and of the line, have worlds that are not the intended ones.

38a wrote the language and said what holds in a world. 38b made proofs objects, checked by a recipe and listed. 38c showed that what is reached is true everywhere, and 38d the other way. 38e found countable and bigger worlds, and 38f numbers past every numeral. 38g coded strings as numbers and laid out the axioms of arithmetic; 38h built the statement about itself; 38i showed it true and unreached; 38j showed arithmetic cannot certify itself; 38k showed its truth cannot be said inside. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

Every formula is built in exactly one way, and whether a list of lines is a proof from a finite collection is settled by a recipe that always stops. For every collection G in a listed language and every statement φ, G reaches φ exactly when φ is true in every world of G; G is clash-free exactly when it has a world; and G has a world when each finite part of it does. A collection with an endless world has a countable world and worlds as big as any set, so the true statements of the line have a countable world, and the true statements of arithmetic have a world with a number past every numeral.

Statements are coded as numbers, and there is a statement U such that PA reaches U ⇔ ¬Prov(⌜U⌝). If PA is clash-free, U is true in ℕ and PA reaches neither U nor ¬U; no collection holding PA, settled by a recipe and true in ℕ, reaches every truth of arithmetic; PA does not reach Con; and no formula of arithmetic is true of exactly the codes of the true statements.

29k · technique

Groups That Are Smooth Spaces

The turns of space (27o) can be composed, undone, and varied a little at a time: tip a globe slightly more and it is still a turn. So they are at once a group and a smooth space, and the two views help each other. One equation, QᵀQ = I, cuts them out of the nine entries of a grid. The equation fixes six entries in terms of three, by 28f, so the turns form a space of three directions: two to aim the axis, one for the angle. This step counts the directions of such groups and finds the arrows tangent to them at the grid that does nothing.

A matrix group is a set of grids closed under products and undoing, cut out by smooth equations. For turns, QᵀQ = I is six equations on nine numbers, so three directions remain. Its arrows at I are the X with Xᵀ = −X: the slope of QᵀQ = I along a path through I.

29k1Groups Cut Out by Equations

\text{all undoable } n \times n \text{ grids: } \det A \neq 0 @@ the determinant (27e)
\det A = 1 @@ keep volume and handedness
Q^{\mathsf T}Q = I @@ keep lengths (27o1)
\htmlClass{new}{Q^{\mathsf T}Q = I,\ \det Q = 1: \text{ the turns}} @@ keep lengths and handedness (27o)

Each set is closed under products and undoing: if AᵀA = I and BᵀB = I, then (AB)ᵀ(AB) = BᵀAᵀAB = I. The products are smooth in the entries, so each is a group and a smooth space at once.

29k2Counting the Directions

Q^{\mathsf T}Q = I \text{ is symmetric: entry } (j,k) = \text{entry } (k,j) @@ only the entries on and above the middle count
3 + 2 + 1 = 6 \text{ equations on } 9 \text{ entries} @@ n(n + 1)/2 of n²
\text{their slopes are independent at every } Q @@ so the fixing technique applies (28f)
\htmlClass{new}{9 - 6 = 3 \text{ directions}} @@ two to aim the axis, one for the angle (27o)

Count technique: the grids with QᵀQ = I in n directions form a smooth space of n(n − 1)/2 directions; one direction for turns of the plane, three for turns of space, six for four directions.

29k3The Arrows at the Grid That Does Nothing

Q(t)^{\mathsf T}Q(t) = I,\ Q(0) = I @@ a path of turns
Q^{\prime}(0)^{\mathsf T} + Q^{\prime}(0) = 0 @@ the product technique, at t = 0
X^{\mathsf T} = -X @@ X = Q′(0) is skew
\htmlClass{new}{\text{skew } 3 \times 3: \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -c & b \\ c & 0 & -a \\ -b & a & 0 \end{pmatrix},\ Xv = (a,b,c)\times v} @@ three numbers, as the count says

Skew technique: the arrows tangent to the turns at I are the skew grids, Xᵀ = −X; in space each one is a cross product with a fixed vector w = (a, b, c), and turning about w at speed |w|. They are the tangent arrows of 29i, at one special point.

Accepted names

A group that is a smooth space is a Lie group; the grids with QᵀQ = I are the orthogonal group O(n), the turns the special orthogonal group SO(n), all undoable grids GL(n), det 1 the special linear group SL(n). The tangent arrows at I form the Lie algebra, here 𝔰𝔬(n), the skew-symmetric matrices.

Who said it first

Sophus Lie studied continuous groups of motions from the 1870s; the matrix view was set out by John von Neumann in 1929 and Claude Chevalley in 1946.

How Does Moving Water Turn a Turbine?

How does moving water turn a turbine? At the foot of a mountain dam, a jet of water as thick as an arm strikes a wheel of cups and spins a generator that lights a town. The water leaves the wheel almost still. All its push went into the turning. This page measures turning by force times arm (12·phys), finds the spin water carries about a shaft, gets the turbine’s torque from the spin the wheel takes out, draws the water’s speed at the blade as triangles of arrows (21·phys), and finds the one wheel speed that takes out everything, with 14h.

Water going round a shaft carries spin: its distance out times its speed around. The torque on the wheel is the spin that enters each second minus the spin that leaves. Times the turning rate, that is the power. For a jet into cups, the water leaves with speed 2u − V around, so the power per kilogram is 2u(V − u), greatest at u = V/2.

25eng21Torque Is Force Times Arm

Mike runs a small hydro plant. Its wheel turns on a shaft. A force pushing sideways at a distance r from the shaft turns it with torque force times r (12·phys).

\text{torque} = F\,r @@ force times arm (12·phys)
\text{power} = \text{torque} \times \omega @@ ω the turning rate in radians a second (23·eng)
\htmlClass{new}{u = \omega r} @@ the blade’s own speed at radius r

25eng22Water Carries Spin About the Shaft

Split the water’s speed into a part going round the shaft and the rest. From here on, on this page, the swirl w is the speed round the shaft, and a kilogram of water at radius r carries spin r·w about the shaft.

\text{push} = \text{change of momentum each second} @@ 14·phys
\text{torque on the water} = \dot m\,(r_2 w_2 - r_1 w_1) @@ ṁ kilograms a second; in at 1, out at 2
\htmlClass{new}{\text{torque on the wheel} = \dot m\,(r_1 w_1 - r_2 w_2)} @@ the wheel feels the opposite

25eng23Torque From the Spin Taken Out

\text{power} = \omega\,\dot m\,(r_1 w_1 - r_2 w_2) @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{\text{power per kilogram} = u_1 w_1 - u_2 w_2} @@ blade speed times swirl, in minus out

Swirl technique: a turbine’s power per kilogram of water is the blade speed times the swirl where the water comes in, minus the same where it leaves. Nothing about the blade’s shape enters except through what swirl it leaves behind. The best turbine sends the water out with no swirl at all.

25eng24Triangles of Speed at the Blade

The cups move. The water’s speed seen from a cup is its speed over the ground minus the cup’s (21·phys). A jet of speed V hits a cup moving at u; seen from the cup, the water arrives at V − u, runs round the cup, and leaves backwards at V − u.

\text{in: } w_1 = V @@ the jet is all swirl at the cup
\text{out, seen from the cup: } -(V - u);\ \ \text{over the ground: } w_2 = u - (V - u) = 2u - V @@ arrows add (21·phys)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{power per kilogram} = u\,(V - (2u - V)) = 2u\,(V - u)} @@ u₁ = u₂ = u for a cup

25eng25Why a Turbine Runs Best at One Speed

Too slow a wheel takes little, too fast and the jet barely catches it. Find the best u (14h).

\dfrac{d}{du}\,2u(V - u) = 2V - 4u = 0 @@ 14h
u = \tfrac12 V:\ \ \text{power per kg} = \tfrac12 V^2 @@ all the jet’s energy of motion
w_2 = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 V - V = 0 @@ the water leaves with no swirl, dropping off the cups
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a 400 m fall: } V = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 400} \approx 89 \text{ m/s},\ u \approx 44 \text{ m/s}} @@ about 420 turns a minute at 1 m radius

A cup wheel takes out all the jet’s energy when its cups move at half the jet’s speed; then the water leaves with no swirl. Mike runs a test wheel on a brake at many speeds; its power rises, peaks at half the jet speed, and falls, as the curve 2u(V − u) says. A plant designer picks the wheel’s size so that, at the generator’s fixed speed, the cups run at half the jet.

Accepted names

The swirl technique is Euler’s turbine equation; swirl is the tangential velocity and the triangles are velocity triangles. A cup wheel is a Pelton wheel, an impulse turbine; turbines that run full of water are reaction turbines, such as the Francis and Kaplan.

Who said it first

Leonhard Euler set out the turbine equation in the 1750s; Lester Pelton patented his split cup wheel in 1880 (not yet revisited for the dates).

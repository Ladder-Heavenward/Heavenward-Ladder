How Much Can One Term Move a GPA?

How much can one term move a GPA? A grade point average weighs every course by its credits. A hard term early on can drag it down a long way; the same term in the last year barely shows. This page builds a term’s GPA as a weighted average with 5j’s ratios, writes the running GPA as an update that moves by a share of the surprise, finds why that share shrinks as credits pile up, and finds the bound on how far the GPA can still move before graduation, a bound that closes in on the GPA itself as the credits run out, in rung 10’s sense.

New GPA = old GPA + (this term’s credits ÷ all credits so far) × (this term’s GPA − old GPA). The share in front is the term’s weight; it falls as the credits grow. With R credits left of T, the final GPA lies within 4R/T of where it would be with only zeros or only top grades still to come.

10stat21A Term’s GPA

Joy’s college grades each course on a 4-point scale: an A is 4, a B is 3, a C is 2. A course is worth a number of credits, about the hours it takes a week. Her first term:

coursecreditsgradepoints × credits
calculus4A, 416
chemistry4B, 312
writing3A, 412
history3B, 39
total1449

From here on, on this page, a term’s GPA is the total of grade points times credits, over the total credits: each grade weighted by its credits.

\text{GPA} = \dfrac{\sum \text{grade} \times \text{credits}}{\sum \text{credits}} @@ a weighted average (5j)
\htmlClass{new}{= \tfrac{49}{14} = 3.5} @@

10stat22The Running GPA

The cumulative GPA treats every course taken so far as one long term: all the grade points times credits, over all the credits. It can be kept up to date term by term without listing every course again.

\text{after } c \text{ credits at GPA } g:\ \text{grade points so far} = g \cdot c @@ undo the division
\text{add a term: } t \text{ credits at GPA } s @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{g_{\text{new}} = \dfrac{g\,c + s\,t}{c + t}} @@ all the points over all the credits

Only two numbers have to be kept between terms: the GPA so far and the credits so far.

10stat23The Update: A Share of the Surprise

Rewrite the running GPA to show how far it moves. Call s − g the surprise: how far this term landed from the GPA so far.

g_{\text{new}} = \dfrac{g\,c + s\,t}{c + t} = \dfrac{g\,(c + t) + (s - g)\,t}{c + t} @@ add and take away g·t
\htmlClass{new}{g_{\text{new}} = g + \dfrac{t}{c + t}\,(s - g)} @@ old, plus a share of the surprise

Running-update technique: the new GPA is the old GPA plus this term’s share of all the credits so far, times how far this term’s GPA landed from the old. A term that matches the old GPA changes nothing; a term above it pulls the GPA up, by a share of the gap and never all of it.

10stat24The Share Shrinks

Take one hard term: 15 credits at a GPA of 2.0. Compare it after one year (30 credits at 3.6) and in the final year (105 credits at 3.6).

\text{first year: share } \tfrac{15}{30 + 15} = \tfrac{1}{3} @@ 
g_{\text{new}} = 3.6 + \tfrac{1}{3}(2.0 - 3.6) \approx 3.07 @@ down more than half a point
\text{final year: share } \tfrac{15}{105 + 15} = \tfrac{1}{8} @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{g_{\text{new}} = 3.6 + \tfrac{1}{8}(2.0 - 3.6) = 3.4} @@ down a fifth of a point

The share t/(c + t) falls as credits pile up, so later terms move the GPA less. An adviser reading a transcript knows this: a first-year GPA says little, and a fourth-year GPA is hard to move either way.

10stat25How Far It Can Still Move

Joy’s degree takes 120 credits. With c done and R = 120 − c left, her final GPA is fixed except for the R credits to come, each worth between 0 and 4 points.

\text{lowest final: } \dfrac{g\,c + 0 \cdot R}{120},\ \ \text{highest: } \dfrac{g\,c + 4R}{120} @@ all zeros, or all top grades, to come
\text{width of the range} = \dfrac{4R}{120} @@ highest minus lowest
c = 105,\ g = 3.4:\ \text{range } 2.98 \text{ to } 3.48 @@ R = 15: width 0.5
\htmlClass{new}{R \to 0:\ \dfrac{4R}{120} \to 0} @@ the range closes in on one number (10a)

With R credits left of T, the final GPA lies in a range of width 4R/T, and the range closes in on the final GPA as R runs out. Pick any closeness, ε: once R is below εT/4, the final GPA is known to within ε. That is rung 10’s challenge and answer, met by a transcript. A registrar can tell a student in their last term exactly which GPAs are still within reach.

Accepted names

The cumulative GPA is a running weighted mean, and writing it as old plus a share of the surprise is the incremental or online update. The width of the reachable range closing to 0 is a limit in rung 10’s sense.

Who said it first

The chess rating of Arpad Elo, adopted by the international chess federation in 1970, uses the same update, new = old + K × (result − expected), with a fixed share K instead of one that shrinks.

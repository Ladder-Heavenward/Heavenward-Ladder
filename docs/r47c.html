47c · proof

Maps Between Maps

Rung twenty seven found that a space of arrows of finite dimension and its space of readings have the same dimension, so they can be matched. But the matching needed a basis, and a different basis gives a different matching. The space and its readings of readings can also be matched, and that matching needs nothing: a vector is read by each reading, and that is all. Mathematicians called the second kind natural for a long time before anyone could say what the word meant. In 1945 the meaning was written down. Two maps of categories go from the same place to the same place. A map between them is a family of arrows, one at each thing, that agrees with every arrow of the category: go along an arrow and then across, or across and then along, and land in the same place. The readings of readings pass that test. The basis matching fails it, and the step shows the failure in one line of numbers. Families that pass it can be run in a row and undone, as arrows can, so maps of categories are themselves the things of a category.

A map between two maps of categories is one arrow at each thing, from the first map’s value to the second’s, such that along then across equals across then along, for every arrow of the category. A space of arrows of finite dimension is matched naturally with its readings of readings, and not naturally with its readings. A natural family of matchings has a natural family of undoings.

47c1Along, Then Across

Let F and G be maps of categories from C to D. A natural map α from F to G, written α : F ⇒ G, gives each thing A of C an arrow α_A : F(A) → G(A) of D, such that for every arrow f : A → B of C,

Draw F(A) top left, F(B) top right, G(A) bottom left and G(B) bottom right. The rule says the square closes: across the top by F(f) and down by α_B lands where down by α_A and across the bottom by G(f) lands. The mark ⇒ here joins two maps of categories, never two statements; it keeps its 1d meaning everywhere else. Its accepted name is a natural transformation.

Square-in-a-row technique: two natural maps run one after the other, at each thing, make a natural map.

\alpha : F \Rightarrow G,\ \beta : G \Rightarrow H;\ \ (\beta\alpha)_A = \beta_A \circ \alpha_A @@ run the two arrows in a row at A
H(f) \circ \beta_A \circ \alpha_A = \beta_B \circ G(f) \circ \alpha_A @@ β’s square, with brackets free (9b3)
= \beta_B \circ \alpha_B \circ F(f) @@ α’s square
\htmlClass{new}{H(f) \circ (\beta\alpha)_A = (\beta\alpha)_B \circ F(f)} \quad \blacksquare @@ the square for βα

With id_F(A) at each A as the family that does nothing, the maps of categories from C to D, with the natural maps between them, are a category. Arrows between arrows between things: that is how high the step climbs.

47c2Readings of Readings

Fix a field k, and let Vect be its spaces of finite dimension with linear maps. 47b3 turned arrows round once with *. Done twice, V ↦ V** and f ↦ f** is a map of categories Vect → Vect that does not turn arrows round. For v in V, let ev_V(v) be the reading of readings r ↦ r(v): read v by each reading.

Double-reading technique: reading a vector by every reading matches each space of finite dimension with its readings of readings, and the family of these matchings is natural.

f : V \to W,\ \ s \in W^* @@ a linear map, and a reading of W
f^{**}(\mathrm{ev}_V(v))(s) = \mathrm{ev}_V(v)(f^*(s)) = \mathrm{ev}_V(v)(s \circ f) = s(f(v)) @@ the definitions, read off one at a time
\mathrm{ev}_W(f(v))(s) = s(f(v)) @@ the definition of ev at W
f^{**} \circ \mathrm{ev}_V = \mathrm{ev}_W \circ f @@ the square closes, for every f: natural
v \ne 0 \Rightarrow \text{some reading has } r(v) \ne 0 @@ grow v to a basis (27b), and take the reading that is 1 at v (27h1)
\dim V^{**} = \dim V^* = \dim V @@ the reading-basis technique, twice
\htmlClass{new}{\mathrm{ev}_V \text{ is a matching}} \quad \blacksquare @@ it loses nothing, so lost plus reached (27c2) makes it onto

Nothing in ev was chosen. No basis was named. The same vector, read by the same readings, gives the same reading of readings in every space, and that is what the square says.

47c3A Matching That Needs a Choice

27h1 matched V with V*: send a basis to its reading basis. A different basis gives a different matching. Could some rule pick one matching α_V : V → V* in each space, with no choice, so that every matching f : V → W carries one to the other? Carrying means: reading v by α at v is the same as reading f(v) by α at f(v) after f, α_V(v)(u) = α_W(f(v))(f(u)) for all v, u. That is the most a family between a space and its readings, which turn arrows round, could be asked.

No-chosen-reading technique: over a field with more than three members, no family of matchings of each space of finite dimension with its readings is carried along by every matching.

t \in k,\ \ t \ne 0,\ \ t^2 \ne 1 @@ t² = 1 has at most two answers (9g), and k has more than three members
V = k,\ \ c = \alpha_k(1)(1) @@ a reading of the line is fixed by its value at 1
c \ne 0 @@ α_k is a matching, so α_k(1) is not the reading 0
f(x) = t\,x \text{ is a matching of } k @@ undone by x ↦ x/t
c = \alpha_k(f(1))(f(1)) = \alpha_k(t)(t) = t^2 c @@ carried along by f; α_k and the reading are both linear
(t^2 - 1)\,c = 0 @@ subtract
\htmlClass{new}{c = 0,\ \text{against the third line}} \quad \blacksquare @@ t² − 1 is not 0, and a field has no zero products (5e)

Over ℝ, ℚ or ℂ take t = 2: the line alone breaks every such family. The fields with two and three members need a space of more dimensions, and a standing question finds which.

So V and V* are matched, and not naturally. The readings of readings are matched naturally. In 1945 this pair of facts was the first example of what natural meant, and it still is. A natural map is a map made without choices; a matching that needs a basis, which rung 6’s choice may be needed to find in a space without one in hand, is not.

47c4Naturally Matched

A natural map α : F ⇒ G with every α_A undoable is a natural matching, and F ≅ G says so.

Natural-undo technique: if every arrow of a natural map can be undone, the undoings form a natural map back.

G(f) \circ \alpha_A = \alpha_B \circ F(f) @@ α’s square
\alpha_B^{-1} \circ G(f) \circ \alpha_A \circ \alpha_A^{-1} = \alpha_B^{-1} \circ \alpha_B \circ F(f) \circ \alpha_A^{-1} @@ run α_B⁻¹ before and α_A⁻¹ after, on both sides
\htmlClass{new}{\alpha_B^{-1} \circ G(f) = F(f) \circ \alpha_A^{-1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the undoings cancel (47a3): the square for the family α⁻¹ from G to F

One more natural map, worked with numbers. For a ring R that is turn-about (44a), let M(R) be the 2 by 2 matrices over R, and det_R(A) = ps − qr for A with rows (p, q) and (r, s), as 27e. A ring map φ : R → S, applied entry by entry, sends M(R) to M(S). Since det is a sum of products of entries, φ(ps − qr) = φ(p)φ(s) − φ(q)φ(r): the square closes, and det is natural. Over ℤ, A with rows (3, 5) and (1, 4) has det 7. Its image on the clock of 5 (43) has rows (3, 0) and (1, 4), with det 12, which is 2 on that clock, and 7 is 2 there too.

The accepted names

A natural map is a natural transformation, and its square is the naturality square; a natural matching is a natural isomorphism, or natural equivalence. The category of functors from C to D with natural transformations is the functor category, written D^C or Fun(C, D). ev_V is the canonical embedding into the double dual. The no-chosen-reading technique is the standard example that V ≅ V* is not natural; the family it rules out would be a dinatural or invariant isomorphism, and the usual statement is that no natural isomorphism exists between the identity functor and the dual, which cannot even be written since one turns arrows round. det is a natural transformation from GL₂ to the units functor.

Who said it first

Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders Mac Lane, “General theory of natural equivalences”, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society, 1945. Their first example is the double dual. Their earlier paper of 1942 on group extensions already needed the word, and the 1945 paper was written to define it.

A proof that is not allowed

“Pick a basis of V. Then V ≅ V* by the reading basis, and since every space has a basis, V ≅ V* naturally.” Not allowed: natural is a statement about every arrow f at once, and the matching depends on the basis picked. A rule that picks a basis in each space is still a choice; the square is what has to be checked, and 47c3 shows it fails.

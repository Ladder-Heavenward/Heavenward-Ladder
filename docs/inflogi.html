How Is a Best Plan Checked?

How is a best plan checked? Three logistics annexes, from rung 6 to rung 42, each found a best plan by searching: the corners of a bakery’s day, the routes of a shipping plan, the ways to hand jobs to drivers. This page reads them again with rung forty-seven’s arrows. An order is a picture with at most one arrow between two things, and “at most” is the arrow. When every plan has an arrow to every bound, a plan and a bound that meet prove each other best, and the search hides. The bakery’s worths, the river cut, the port prices and a group of jobs short of drivers are four such bounds.

a ≤ b for every a in A and b in B, and a₀ = b₀: a₀ is the largest of A, b₀ the least of B. Bakery: 3x + 2y = (2x + y) + (x + y) ≤ 700 = g(100, 200). Shipping: cost ≥ Σ p · want − Σ q · supply, met at 212 000. Jobs: served ≤ n − (|K| − |N(K)|) for every group K.

inflogi.1A Bound That Is Met

18·logi2 and 18·logi3 found the bakery’s best day by walking its corners, and 18·logi4 found what one more unit of each limit is worth. The worths do more than price the limits. They prove the corner best, in one line.

Met-bound technique: if a ≤ b for every a in A and every b in B, and some a₀ in A equals some b₀ in B, then a₀ is the largest of A and b₀ is the least of B.

a \le b_0 = a_0 \text{ for every } a \text{ in } A @@ every a has an arrow to b₀
b \ge a_0 = b_0 \text{ for every } b \text{ in } B @@ a₀ has an arrow to every b
\htmlClass{new}{a_0 = \max A,\qquad b_0 = \min B} \quad \blacksquare @@ each is a proof of the other
  
3x + 2y = 1 \cdot (2x + y) + 1 \cdot (x + y) @@ the worths of 18·logi4: $1 for each unit of 2x + y, which is $4 a kilogram of flour, and $1 an oven place
\le 1 \cdot 400 + 1 \cdot 300 = 700 @@ the limits, for every allowed plan: the price-bound technique (42g)
g(100, 200) = 300 + 400 = 700 @@ one plan meets the bound
\htmlClass{new}{(100, 200) \text{ is best, and no cheaper worths cover the loaves}} @@ the met-bound technique; no corner was walked

What hides is the arrangement: the corners and the order of walking them. The hands have room to spare and a worth of 0, so they are not in the line at all.

inflogi.2Ask for the Other Side

42·logi.2 and 42·logi.4 each set two kinds against each other: flows and cuts, shipping plans and prices. Each is the met-bound technique, with the arrows supplied by a technique of rung 42.

\text{flow} \le \text{cut}:\quad 14\,000 = (3 + 4) \cdot 2000 @@ the flow-under-cut technique (42f); met at the river (42·logi.2)
\text{cost} \ge \textstyle\sum p \cdot \text{want} - \sum q \cdot \text{supply}:\quad 212\,000 = 212\,000 @@ the route-price technique (42·logi.4); met by the plan of 42·logi.3
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the largest flow, the smallest cut, the cheapest plan, the dearest prices}} @@ four bests, and no search among flows, cuts, plans or prices
  
\text{a climber reads}:\quad 2 \text{ ports},\ 3 \text{ cities}:\ \text{at most } \tbinom{6}{4} = 15 \text{ sets of routes in use} @@ a corner uses at most 2 + 3 − 1 routes (the tree-corner technique, 42·logi.3)
\text{a machine reads}:\quad 4 \text{ ports},\ 5 \text{ cities}:\ \tbinom{20}{8} = 125\,970 @@ it could try them all
\htmlClass{new}{\text{neither reads}:\quad 20 \text{ ports},\ 30 \text{ cities}:\ \tbinom{600}{49} \approx 3 \times 10^{72}} @@ the machine walks from corner to corner and never lists them

So a best plan for 600 routes comes back from a machine, and nobody can retrace how. The check is the other side. Ask for the prices with the plan: 600 lines say that no route is underpriced, one line each for the routes in use says they are priced exactly, and two totals meet. What hides is the arrangement, the particular roads, and the sequence, the corners the machine walked.

inflogi.3A Group Short of Drivers

6·logi3 proved that if some k jobs have fewer than k drivers between them, no fair assignment exists. A fair assignment is an arrow from jobs to drivers with an undo on one side: from each driver used, back to the job. Count how far short a group is, and the blocking-group technique becomes a bound.

Short-by technique: let a group K of the n jobs have the drivers N(K) between them. A fair assignment of some of the jobs serves at most n − (|K| − |N(K)|) of them.

\text{served jobs in } K \le |N(K)| @@ they are matched with a part of N(K): the no-room technique (6·logi3)
\text{served jobs outside } K \le n - |K| @@ there are no more
\htmlClass{new}{\text{served} \le n - |K| + |N(K)|} \quad \blacksquare @@ add the two
  
n = 5:\quad g, s, f \text{ from Sarah, Randy};\quad t \text{ from Terry};\quad v \text{ from Sarah, Randy, Terry, Claire} @@ the board of 6·logi3
K = \{g, s, f\}:\quad |K| - |N(K)| = 3 - 2 = 1,\quad \text{served} \le 4 @@ short by 1
g \mapsto \text{Sarah},\ s \mapsto \text{Randy},\ t \mapsto \text{Terry},\ v \mapsto \text{Claire}:\quad 4 \text{ served} @@ fair: no driver twice
\htmlClass{new}{4 = 4:\ \text{no board serves more, and no group is shorter}} @@ the met-bound technique

Such a pair always exists (42e). Finding it may take the swaps of 6·logi4, and on a board of forty jobs and forty drivers there can be 40!, about 8 × 10⁴⁷, fair assignments to look through. Checking it is two counts. What hides is the arrangement: the names of the drivers.

The accepted names

An order read as a picture with at most one arrow between two things is a thin category, or a preorder. That every plan is at most every bound is weak duality; a bound that is met is a certificate of optimality, and that one exists for programs with fractions allowed is strong duality (the equal-prices technique, 42g). The worths are shadow prices, or dual variables; that a limit with room to spare has worth 0, and a route in use is priced exactly, is complementary slackness. Flow against cut is the max-flow min-cut theorem. The shipping program is the transportation problem, its corners are basic feasible solutions, and the walk from corner to corner is the simplex method. A fair assignment is an injection, its undo on one side a left inverse, and a fair assignment of some jobs a matching. The number |K| − |N(K)| is the deficiency of K; the short-by bound met is the defect form of Hall’s marriage theorem, or Kőnig’s theorem (42e). The gap between the best whole plan and the least bound is the integrality gap.

What the arrows let hide

All three footings hide a search: the corners of the bakery, the corners of the shipping plans, the ways to fill the board. What is left is a pair, a plan and a bound, and one line that says they meet. A search is the machine’s work and may be past retracing. The pair is the climber’s to ask for and to check: that every plan has an arrow to the bound, and that this plan reaches it. No dated claim is made on this page.

What Does a Free Choice Cost the One Who Keeps a Secret?

Two friends say their meeting places out loud and keep back which one they picked. A listener looks for the place where their free moves have to be paid for.

6crypt1The Choice Is the Secret

What does a free choice cost the one who keeps a secret?

Evita and Randy meet three times this week. Evita says the plan out loud in the hall, where Mike can hear. “Monday, the gym or the library. Wednesday, the library, the bus stop or the field. Friday, the gym.”

She has told Mike every place they might meet. She has not told him where they will meet. Randy knows. Mike does not.

Put it in rung 6's words. Each day has a set of places. Each of the three sets has a member, so a pick can be made from each (6b2). The three sets together are a collection (6b3).

DayIts set of places
Monday{gym, library}
Wednesday{library, bus stop, field}
Friday{gym}

The friends' plan takes one place from each set. That is a choosing function for the collection (6b4): each set is paired with its pick, and each pick is in its set.

From here on, in this step, the boxes are the sets that are said out loud, and the pick is the choosing function that is kept.

The boxes can be heard by anyone. The secret is the pick.

Nothing Evita said was untrue, and nothing was in a code. She said the boxes and stopped.

6crypt2Hiding With a Free Option

Suppose Evita wants to say Monday's place out loud and still keep it. How can she?

She holds the line “we meet at the library”. 6a2 gives her a free move: from x, write x ∨ y, for any y at all. So she says, “We meet at the library or the gym.”

The or is true, because one of its options is. It cost her nothing in truth. But 6a2 named a price for this move: the news of which. The or no longer says that the library is the place. Nothing in it can get the library back out without cases.

In 6a that price was a loss. Here it is the whole point. The news of which is the one thing Evita does not want Mike to have.

A free option hides by saying less. The price of the move is what the listener is left without.

So every box in 6crypt1 can be read as a line with free options written into it. Monday's box is “the gym or the library”. Friday's box is “the gym”, with no option added, and it hides nothing.

6crypt3The Friend Needs the News Back

The news of which is gone from the sentence. Then how does Randy get to the right place?

Not from the sentence. Randy hears the same or that Mike hears. If the sentence were all he had, he would stand where Mike stands.

Randy held the pick before Evita spoke. The two of them settled it on Sunday, away from the hall. The free option took the news out of the sentence, and the news had to be somewhere else already.

The news of which is not sent. It has to be held before the sentence is said.

Now Mike. He holds an or, and 6a2 said what an or leaves you: cases. For Monday he has two cases, the gym and the library. He can go to one and hope, or he can find a way to watch both.

For the whole week he needs a whole pick. 6c5 counts the picks of a finite collection: one factor for each box.

Six picks fit what Mike heard. Each option Evita adds to a box makes that box's factor one more.

Look at Friday. Its factor is 1. A box with one member leaves one case, and Mike will be at the gym on Friday.

6crypt4The Other Way Round

So far the boxes were said and the pick was kept. Can it be done the other way round?

It can. This week Evita and Randy settle the boxes on Sunday, away from the hall, and tell them to nobody. On Monday, in the hall, Evita says how they pick. “Each day, the place that comes first in the alphabet.”

That is a rule that picks: the least member of each box, as in 6b7. Mike now holds the choice, whole. He knows how every pick of the week is made.

He still cannot walk to the right place. A rule that picks takes a box in and gives a member out. Mike has nothing to put in. First in the alphabet, out of what?

A rule that picks, with no box to pick from, gives no place. The listener who holds the choice still needs the boxes.

This way has a price too, and it is paid a little at a time. A pick is a member of its box (6b4). So each time Mike sees where the friends went, he has one member of that day's box.

With the rule he has more than that. Suppose he follows them on Monday and finds them at the library. The library came first in the alphabet in Monday's box. So nothing in that box comes before it: not the bus stop, not the field, not the gym.

Each pick that is seen gives away a member of its box. With the rule in hand, it says something about every other member.

One more thing can go wrong on the friends' side. The rule promises a pick from every box. A box with no member has no first place (6b2), and 6c5 counted the picks of a collection with such a box: none. If one day's box is empty, the plan as a whole cannot be carried out, and saying the rule out loud did not show that.

6crypt5The Price Is an Opening

Hiding was paid for with a free move. Do the other free moves have a price a listener can use?

Here is a small lock, built to show one thing. On Friday Randy keeps the door of the clubroom. He and Evita want a check with no word to overhear. They settle on this: a visitor says two numbers, n and a, and Randy opens the door when

Their thinking was 6a5's. The number they are really asking for is 1, and 1 can be written as n/n. A visitor who knows to say 1 for a gets in with any n. For an n that is not 0, no other a works: do the same to both sides, dividing by n (5g), and a = 1.

Now the price. 6a5 said the free 1 is good only for x ≠ 0. At 0 it is 0/0, and 5e5 found that 0/0 has every answer.

Mike says, “0 and 7.”

The check passes. It passes for 0 and anything at all. Mike did not find the number they were asking for. He found the place where the free move is not free.

A lock that leans on a free move takes on the condition of that move. Where the condition fails, the lock opens to anyone.

6a6 said the cost of free is your attention. Evita and Randy made the move and did not attend to its condition. The fix is one more line in the check: n is not 0. A condition written on the line is a price paid. A condition left off the line is a price still owed, and a listener may be the one to collect it.

The clubroom lock is a toy. The opening is not. Here is a lock nearer to ones that are built.

Randy and Evita share a locker, and they want a fresh number on it each week without ever saying the number. On a Sunday, away from the hall, they settle one kept number, k. Each Monday they take n, the first number anyone says in the hall after the bell. The locker number for the week is

Anyone can hear n. Only the two of them hold k, so only they can work out k · n.

Their thinking was this. Two different kept numbers give two different locker numbers: if k · n = j · n, divide both sides by n (5g), and k = j. So a listener who is short of k is short of the locker number. That dividing is the free 1 of 6a5 again, n/n, and it is good only for n ≠ 0.

One Monday the first number said after the bell is Mike's. “0.”

The locker number is 0 for every k. Mike holds the week's number, and he has not learned k. The fix is the same one line: n is not 0. On a Monday when the first number said is 0, the friends wait for the next one.

The same opening is in the small lock and in the sturdy one. It comes with the free move, whatever is built on top of it.

The sturdy lock has a second weakness, and this one is not at 0. Suppose Mike learns one week's locker number. He heard that week's n in the hall. He divides the locker number by n (5g) and holds k, and with k he holds every week after.

That is no reason to give the method up. The friends can keep each week's number better out of sight, or settle a new k once a number has been seen. Each fix is one more thing hidden, and each will have a price of its own to look for.

Every weakness in the method is another reason to keep going deeper: to make the secret more secure by finding more ways to hide the information.

6crypt6Who Does the Picking

In 6crypt5, who chose the number n?

The visitor did. That was the second thing the friends gave away, and it cost them more than the first.

6a7 said that picking is a free move: when a set has a member, a proof may name one. 6b1 said what such a pick is worth. It names one thing, and says nothing about which one, so the work that follows has to hold whichever it is.

When Evita picks Monday's place, the pick is hers. She needs one good member, and she looks until she has it. That is a some (2b).

When Randy lets the visitor pick n, the pick is the visitor's. Randy's check now has to be right for whichever n comes. That is an every (2b). One member that breaks it is enough, and the one who picks gets to look for it.

A choice you keep asks for one good member. A choice you hand over asks you to be ready for every member.

Mike did not need to be lucky. The set of numbers he could pick from was said out loud, 0 was in it, and nobody had checked 0.

6crypt7Promising Nothing and Coming Through

Can a line be true and promise nothing at all?

Go back to the week of 6crypt4, when the boxes are kept. The field is in none of them. In the hall Evita says, “If we meet at the field, I'll bring the ball.”

She will not be caught out. 6a3 gives this if for free: from ¬x, write x ⇒ y, for any y at all. They do not meet at the field, so the line is true whatever she does about the ball. 6a3 called it an empty truth and named its price: its condition can never be met, so it can never be carried through.

An empty if promises nothing, and the one who says it comes through all the same.

For Evita the price is small. The line tells Randy nothing, and he needed nothing from it. Mike is the one who may pay. If he takes the line as news that the field is in a box, he has read into it what it does not say.

Now turn it round, as in 6crypt6, and let the listener be the one who comes through. Randy keeps the door and asks three questions. His check is three ifs: for each question, if the visitor answers it, the answer is right.

Mike answers none of them.

Each if has a condition that is not met, so each one is true, emptily. The check is passed. It promised nothing about a visitor who stays silent, and Mike came through it by doing nothing.

A check made of ifs is passed by the visitor who meets none of its conditions.

The fix is to write an and where the if was: the visitor answers each question, and the answer is right. A silent visitor does not pass that.

6crypt8Picks Without End

Evita and Randy want to go on meeting, week after week, with no last week. Can they hold a pick for all of it?

For one week, yes. 6c showed that finitely many picks are paid for one at a time. Evita makes each pick, writes the pairs down, and hands Randy the list on Sunday.

For a run of weeks with no last one, the list is never finished. A column of calls runs out before the boxes do (6b8). There are two ways to go on.

The first is a rule that picks. “Always the place that comes first in the alphabet.” That is a least member of each box, as in 6b7. A rule is short. It can be settled once, on one Sunday, and it gives a pick from every box there will ever be.

Its price is the same shortness. Mike does not have to find out a pick for each week. If he learns the rule once, he holds every pick.

The second way is to have no rule. Then for boxes without end the picks have to be laid down, and that is the choosing axiom (6b5). It says a choosing function exists. Call it c. 6b8 said what can be said of it: c's pick from a box is a member of that box. That is all. Not which member, for any box.

So the axiom is no help to the friends. Evita knows that a pick exists. Randy knows that a pick exists. Neither can say Monday's place from it, so they cannot both walk to the same door.

The choosing axiom says that a pick exists. It hands the pick to no one, so two people cannot share it.

Accepted names

A choosing function is a choice function, and the choosing axiom is the axiom of choice. Adding an option is disjunction introduction. The set of options a secret is hidden among is its anonymity set. A value the other side is allowed to pick is a chosen input, and one picked in order to break the check is an adversarial input. Writing a move's condition into the check is input validation; the input that was not thought of is an edge case. A short rule that gives a long run of picks is a generator, and the short thing it starts from is a seed.

29·∎ · conclusion

Flat is one geometry among many

The parallel axiom is independent of the other axioms: a sphere and a disc give geometries where it fails. A surface’s ruler gives every length, angle and area, its bend can be read from the ruler alone, shortest paths obey one equation, and the total bend counts holes.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 29h spaces flat up close is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 29 asked whether the fifth postulate of the old geometry follows from the other axioms, and how a creature inside a surface could tell which geometry it lives in. The parallel axiom can be neither proved nor refuted from the other axioms, and the bend of a surface, which tells the geometries apart, can be read from its ruler alone. The price is that geometry is no longer one thing: which one the world obeys must be measured.

29a took the numbers away and put the fifth postulate on trial. 29b drew triangles on a sphere and 29c built the disc plane. 29d wrote the ruler, 29e read the bend from it, and 29f totalled the bend. 29g found the shortest paths and paid back 29f’s trust, and 29h covered a surface with pages. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

Without the parallel axiom, every triangle has angle sum at most π. On a sphere of radius R a triangle has area (α + β + γ − π)R². The disc plane obeys the other axioms and not the parallel axiom, so the parallel axiom is independent of them.

On a surface with ruler ds² = E du² + 2F du dv + G dv², lengths, angles and areas come from E, F, G, and change of coordinates sends the ruler grid g to Jᵀ g J. The surface bend K = (LN − M²)/(EG − F²) is the product of the main bends and is built from E, F, G and their slopes alone.

A curve that makes ∫L(x, y, y′) dx least obeys d/dx Lp = Ly. A triangle of straightest paths has ∬K dA = α + β + γ − π, and a closed surface with g holes has ∬K dA = 2π(V − E + F) = 4π(1 − g). A surface is covered by charts whose changes are undoable, and every chart agrees on length, angle, area and bend.

Who said it first

The fifth postulate is Euclid’s, stated in his Elements around 300 BCE.

Accepted names: the fifth postulate is Euclid’s fifth postulate, and the geometry in which it holds is Euclidean geometry.

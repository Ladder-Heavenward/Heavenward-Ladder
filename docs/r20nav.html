Which way is the ship facing after its turns?

Which way is the ship facing after its turns? A ship’s officer keeps a log: the heading, each change of course, and the time run at each speed. Hours later, after a dozen changes, the ship must still know which way it faces and where it is. What makes it hard is that some orders can be done in either order and some cannot. Two runs on fixed headings can be swapped, and the ship ends in the same place. A turn and a run cannot: turning first sends the run off another way. This page shows, with the moves of rung 20, that a heading only adds up the turns, starting again past a whole turn, that the ship’s place and facing together are one move of the plane, and that a voyage is those moves composed, in an order that matters.

Turns add, and a whole turn is the same as none, so the heading is the sum of the turns, starting again past a whole turn. The ship’s place and facing are one move of the plane: the turn and then the slide that carry the ship from where it started. Each new order is done from where the ship is, facing the way it faces, so a voyage is its orders composed. Runs can be swapped; a turn and a run cannot, unless one of them does nothing.

20nav1Turns add, starting again past 360°

Stands on 18f (degrees; angle addition), 20j (pair-product) and 4i (starting again past).

A heading is the direction the ship faces, in degrees, turning from north toward east: 000 is north, 090 east, 180 south, 270 west. A turn to starboard, the ship’s right, adds to it. A turn to port, its left, takes away.

\text{two turns in a row} = \text{the turn by their product} @@ pair-product (20j)
\text{the sizes of two turns in a row add} @@ angle addition (18f’s size technique)
4 \times 90^\circ = 360^\circ:\ \ (0, -1)^4 = (1, 0) @@ four quarter turns to starboard: doing nothing
\htmlClass{new}{\text{heading} = \text{start} + \text{starboard} - \text{port},\ \text{starting again past } 360} \quad \blacksquare @@ like 4i’s clock

Heading-sum technique: the heading after any turns is the first heading, plus the turns to starboard, less the turns to port, starting again past 360.

350^\circ + 105^\circ = 455^\circ @@ roughly north up the Washington coast, then a turn to starboard into the Strait of Juan de Fuca
455 - 360 = 095^\circ @@ starting again past 360: a little south of east, roughly the strait’s line
\htmlClass{new}{095^\circ + 60^\circ = 155^\circ} @@ off Point Wilson, 60° to starboard, roughly down Admiralty Inlet toward Seattle

20nav2Place and facing as one move

Stands on 20c (slides and turns), 20c4 (turns about other points) and 20b (the isometries form a group).

Draw the ship as an outline at the start: at the origin, facing north, with east along x and north along y. Wherever the ship is later, one move of the plane carries that outline onto it: turn it about the origin to face the ship’s way, then slide it to the ship’s place.

From here on, on this page, the pose of the ship is the move g(P) = R(P) + s, where R is the turn of its facing and s is its place. R is its turn part and s its slide part. A quarter turn to starboard is R₍₀,₋₁₎(x, y) = (y, −x).

g(P) = R_{(0,-1)}(P) + (2,\ 0) @@ facing east, 2 miles east of the start
c = R_{(0,-1)}(c) + (2,\ 0) @@ a point that g keeps
(c_1,\ c_2) = (c_2 + 2,\ -c_1) @@ R₍₀,₋₁₎(x, y) = (y, −x)
c = (1,\ -1) @@ c₂ = −c₁, so c₁ = −c₁ + 2
\htmlClass{new}{g = \text{the quarter turn to starboard about } (1, -1)} \quad \blacksquare @@ c + R(P − c) = R(P) + c − R(c), and c − R(c) = (2, 0) (20c4)

Pose technique: the ship’s place and facing are one move of the plane, a turn and then a slide, and unless the turn does nothing, that move is a turn about one point. The ship could have reached its pose by swinging once round (1, −1), as a boat swings round a mooring buoy.

20nav3A voyage is moves composed

Stands on 20·nav.2, 20b (compose-keeps) and 9b (regroup for composing).

An order on the bridge is given in the ship’s own frame. “Ahead 3” means 3 miles the way the ship faces now. “Starboard 90” means a quarter turn to its right, where it is. An order h, done from the pose g, is the order done at the start and then carried by g. So the new pose is g ∘ h.

g(P) = R_g P + s_g,\ \ h(P) = R_h P + s_h @@ two poses (20·nav.2)
(g \circ h)(P) = R_g(R_h P + s_h) + s_g @@ do h, then g
= R_g R_h\,P + (R_g s_h + s_g) @@ a turn shares out over a sum (20c2)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{turn part } R_g R_h,\ \ \text{place } s_g + R_g s_h} \quad \blacksquare @@ the facing turns the run before it is added

Voyage technique: a voyage is its orders composed, first order on the left; its facing is the product of the turns, whatever the runs, and its place adds each run turned by the facing at that moment. So the heading-sum technique holds with runs in between.

h(P) = R_{(0,-1)}P + (0,\ 1) @@ the order “ahead 1, then starboard 90”, as one move
h \circ h:\ \ R^2 P + (1,\ 1) @@ the second run, (0, 1) turned a quarter, is (1, 0)
h \circ h \circ h:\ \ R^3 P + (1,\ 0) @@ the third, turned a half, is (0, −1)
\htmlClass{new}{h \circ h \circ h \circ h = \text{doing nothing}} @@ the fourth is (−1, 0); and R⁴ does nothing: home, facing north

A sailboat beating up Puget Sound into a north wind sails 045 and 315 in turn, a quarter turn at every tack. Its log is a voyage of this kind.

20nav4Which orders can be swapped

Stands on 20·nav.3, 20c1 (slides turn about) and 19c (socks and shoes).

Two runs on fixed headings, laid on the chart, are slides. Slides turn about (20c1). Run one and then the other, or the other way round, and the ship ends in the same place. A turn and a run are different.

(R \circ S)(P) = R(P + s) = RP + Rs @@ slide by s, then turn about the origin
(S \circ R)(P) = RP + s @@ turn, then slide
R \circ S = S \circ R \iff Rs = s @@ the two differ by the slide Rs − s
Rs = s \Rightarrow ((a - 1)^2 + b^2)\,s = 0 @@ R = R₍ₐ,ᵦ₎: (a − 1)s₁ − bs₂ = 0 and bs₁ + (a − 1)s₂ = 0, combined
(a - 1)^2 + b^2 = 2 - 2a \ne 0 \text{ unless } (a, b) = (1, 0) @@ a² + b² = 1
\htmlClass{new}{\text{a turn and a slide turn about only when one of them does nothing}} \quad \blacksquare @@ so s = 0, or R does nothing

Order technique: runs can be done in any order, but a turn and a run cannot: the heading comes out the same, and the place is off by the run turned less the run. So a tacking boat may take its legs in any order and reach the same mark, as long as each leg keeps its heading. But “ahead 2, starboard 90” ends 2 north, facing east, and “starboard 90, ahead 2” ends 2 east, facing east.

Turn, run, turn back, run back is 19c’s move aba⁻¹b⁻¹. It does not bring the ship home. “Starboard 90, ahead 1, port 90, astern 1” leaves it facing north, 1 east and 1 south: a slide, the small change that the four orders leave.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

A heading is a course, in degrees true when measured from true north. The pose is the ship’s configuration; the moves R(P) + s with R a turn are the proper rigid motions of the plane, a group with the accepted name SE(2), the special Euclidean group. Taking the turn part of a composed move is a homomorphism onto the turns. The point a pose keeps is its centre of rotation. Orders in the ship’s own frame compose on the right, in the body frame; moves read off the chart compose on the left, in the space frame. Turn, run, turn back, run back is a commutator. The word starboard comes from the steering board, an oar once hung on a ship’s right side; port replaced larboard in the 1840s.

Who said it first

Leonhard Euler (1776) showed that every move of a rigid body that keeps one point is a turn about a line. Michel Chasles (1830) showed that every such move of space is a turn about a line together with a slide along it; in the plane, the pose technique is its shadow: a turn about one point, or a slide. In 20·nav.6.5, the frame of the stars against the frame of the ship is the old argument of 20c.4.5, now about motion: Galileo’s ship (1632), whose cabin cannot tell whether it sails or stands, and Newton’s bucket (1687), whose water climbs the wall when it spins. Seymour Papert’s turtle (1967) taught children to draw with “forward” and “right”, composed exactly as this page’s orders.

Wrong moves, worked

\text{ahead } 2,\ \text{starboard } 90,\ \text{ahead } 2 @@ fine
\text{turn the whole voyage a quarter about the start, then slide by } (0, 2) + (0, 2) @@ not allowed: each run is turned by the facing at its moment
\text{so the ship is at } (0, 4) @@ false: it is at (2, 2), facing east

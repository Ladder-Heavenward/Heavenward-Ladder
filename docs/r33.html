Rates in space and time govern heat and waves

Rung 16 tied a total to its rate: the area to a moving end has the height as its slope. Rung 28 gave the slopes of a function of several inputs, and the spread of a flow. This rung puts the two together: rules that tie the rate in time at a point to the rates in space around it. Three such rules carry almost all of it. Heat runs down its slope, so the rate of warming is the curvature of the temperature. A string’s pull bends it back, so its acceleration is its curvature. Water at rest is balanced, with no curvature at all. Rung 32’s turnings solve all three on a rod, and the three answers behave in three different ways. The rung proves how: heat forgets its start and makes no new extremes, waves carry their shape and keep their energy, and a balanced function is the average of its surroundings, fixed by its edge. Then come the first-slope rules that break, the shapes of a tied rule that build every answer, and the drum, whose tones are not a chord. The techniques solve by hand and on a grid, and the annexes take the rules into the ground, the nerve, the sky, a leopard’s skin, a cracked corner, and a question nobody has answered.

Heat smooths and forgets, waves carry and keep, and still water is the average of its surroundings. One kind of rule, three behaviours, each proved from the books of the rule.

What it means to say it

A rule in several inputs ties the slopes of an unknown function u(x, t) to one another: the rate of change in time at a point to the curvature in space at that point, or the two curvatures to each other. To say that such rules govern heat and waves is to say that the temperature in a rod, the height of a string and the level of still water each obey one, and that the rule, with the start and the edge, fixes the answer.

Heat obeys ut = κΔu and forgets its start; a string obeys ytt = c²yxx and keeps its energy; still water obeys Δu = 0 and is the average of its surroundings. The rung proves all three behaviours from the rules, and says which parts are measured laws held in trust.

Where it comes from

A vibrating string was given its rule, and its answer as two running shapes, in the middle of the eighteenth century, and the argument over whether a sum of sines could make every shape ran for fifty years. The still-water rule came from the pull of gravity on the planets, in the same century. A French mathematician wrote the heat rule in 1807 and solved it by the series of rung 32, and was not believed for fifteen years. The circle average is older than its proof; the edge that rules inside was proved in the nineteenth century. The breaking of waves and the speed of the jump came from gas dynamics in the 1860s to 1940s, and the stability rule for grids in 1928. The tones of a drum were found with the drum functions of rung 24. The names belong to the steps, and each step’s basement gives them.

The principles, in plain words

The rung proves seven things, each from the ones before.

First, heat that runs down its slope and is kept obeys the heat rule: the heat-rule technique. On a rod with its ends at 0 its answers are sums of cooling shapes, and the fading series solves it from any start whose coefficients settle.

Second, the heat rule makes no new hottest point, so it fixes one future. On a long bar its answer is the start smoothed by a spreading bell, and on a rod it forgets its start at a rate set by its first shape.

Third, the string’s pull gives the string rule. Every answer is two waves running opposite ways; a tied string sounds at whole multiples of one tone, the string-tones technique; and its energy is kept.

Fourth, the real part of every function with a complex slope is balanced, and each balanced function is its own circle average. So the edge rules inside, there is one still state for each edge, and the disc series builds it from its rim.

Fifth, a first-slope rule carries its start along riding paths. When the speed is the answer the paths cross, at the breaking time, and the jump that forms keeps the books by moving at its jump speed.

Sixth, a tied second-slope rule lets the slopes swap by parts, so its shapes are perpendicular, as rung 31 asks of arrows. The answer to a single kick builds the answer to every load, and it is written in shapes.

Seventh, the round rate takes the rule into a disc, the drum shapes are the drum functions of rung 24, and the drum is unharmonic: its second tone is no whole multiple of its first. A round ball cools by the same steps.

Three of the second kind of care return on the steps of this rung, each now inside a technique, and two more on its annexes. 33a carries the ledger, at every point: the heat rule is a lake’s books kept at every point at once, and a place where the books do not close is a source. 33b carries the middle of a change, at once: the heat rule makes every jump continuous for every t > 0, and pays for it with heat that reaches everywhere at once, a law of the crowd. 33i carries the cloud, on a grid: a grid answer is reported with its width, found by running the grid twice. 33·then carries the cloud, under the ground: the rule is exact and the depth of the cellar is still a width, set by the soil. 33·now carries the cloud, proved: a computer proof keeps every number as a pair of bounds, and so the width is part of the proof.

What follows

With those seven, the techniques compute. 33h solves sealed rods, rods with ends at two temperatures by a shift to the line, and plates with one warm edge. 33i replaces slopes by second quotients, solves a plate by the grid average, and finds the condition on the tick that keeps the steps from blowing up. 33j adds sources: the pull of a ball by shell flux, a tunnel through the earth, and the flow down a pipe. The annexes take these into the ground, the nerve, wells, prices and matter; gravity as a rule on rung 29’s ruler; spots and stripes on a leopard; stress at a corner; and the open question whether flowing water can break.

What you can do because you know it

You can write the rule a process obeys from its books, choose its start and its edge, and solve it on a rod, a plate, a drum or a ball. You can say whether it will forget its start or keep it, where its greatest value is, how fast it fades, when a wave will break and how fast the break will move. You can solve it on a grid, and choose the tick so the answer does not blow up.

From here on, a field that changes in space and time is a rule with a start and an edge, and most questions about it are questions about the rule.

What it costs

Nothing new is laid down. The rung spends rung 16’s area with the height as its slope, rung 22’s endless sums, rung 24’s drum functions, rung 26’s functions with a complex slope, rung 28’s slopes, spread and change of variables, rung 30’s totals and their limits, rung 31’s perpendicular arrows, and rung 32’s turnings and bell. Measured laws are held in trust, as laws: the flow of heat down its slope, the pull of a string, the pull of a mass, and the flow of a sticky fluid. One step is held in trust on 33j: that a total of pulls has the right second slopes. At the end of the rung you will know what it means to say rates in space and time govern heat and waves, why it is true, and what you can do because you know it.

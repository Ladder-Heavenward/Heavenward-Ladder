20·crypt · cryptography

Can a guessed word break the machine?

Can a guessed word break the machine? 19·crypt’s machine has about a hundred and sixty billion billion settings, far too many to try. But messages are not random. A weather station sends a forecast at six every morning, and the forecast says the word WEATHER somewhere near the start. Codebreakers called such a guess a crib. What makes it hard is that a crib gives a few letters, while the key is a setting of wheels and a board of pairs. This page shows, with 19·crypt’s algebra and 7e’s proof by contradiction, that a crib rules out places without trying a setting, that a loop in the crib makes the board cancel, and that every wrong setting then refutes itself.

A crib cannot sit where any of its letters meets itself. Where a crib’s pairs form a loop, the board cancels from the loop’s shuffle, so a setting can be tested with no board at all. Guess where one letter is plugged, follow what it forces, and a wrong setting reaches a clash.

20crypt1Where a crib can sit

Write the crib under the secret text, and slide it along. At each place, the crib claims that the machine sent each crib letter to the secret letter above it. By no-self, the machine never sends a letter to itself.

Crib-place technique: a crib cannot sit at any place where one of its letters lies under the same letter. Each such place is ruled out without trying a single setting.

\begin{smallmatrix} X & W & H & A & E & Q & T \\ W & E & A & T & H & E & R \end{smallmatrix} @@ place 1: no letter meets itself, so it stays
\begin{smallmatrix} W & H & A & E & Q & T & R \\ W & E & A & T & H & E & R \end{smallmatrix} @@ place 2: W over W, A over A, R over R
\htmlClass{new}{\text{place 2 is ruled out}} \quad \blacksquare @@ no-self (19·crypt.3)

20crypt2A loop makes the board cancel

Number the letters of a placed crib 1, 2, 3, …. At letter i the machine’s shuffle is Eᵢ = P Mᵢ P, where P is the board and Mᵢ the wheels with the bounce wheel at that letter. P swaps pairs, so P P = e.

Suppose the crib pairs A with E at letter 1, E with T at letter 2, and T with A at letter 3. Then A goes to E, to T, and back to A: a loop. Loop technique: round a loop in the crib, the board cancels, and the wheels alone keep the letter P(A).

E_3 E_2 E_1\,A = A @@ round the loop
P M_3 P\, P M_2 P\, P M_1 P\,A = A @@ through-and-back, at each letter
P M_3 M_2 M_1 P\,A = A @@ P P = e, twice
\htmlClass{new}{M_3 M_2 M_1\,(P A) = P A} \quad \blacksquare @@ apply P to both sides

The board had 1.5 × 10¹⁴ settings. Round a loop, it has none: only the letter P(A) is unknown, and it has 26 values.

20crypt3Every wrong setting refutes itself

Choose a setting of the wheels, so every Mᵢ is known. Guess P(A) = x. The crib then forces more of the board: at letter 1, P M₁ P A = E gives P(E) = M₁ x. Follow the crib letter by letter, writing down every pair of the board it forces.

Clash technique: if a guess forces one letter to be plugged to two different letters, the guess is refuted; if all 26 guesses are, the setting is.

\text{assume the setting, and } P(A) = x @@ aiming to refute
P(E) = M_1 x,\ \ P(T) = M_2 M_1 x,\ \dots @@ forced by the crib, letter by letter
P(L) = y \text{ and } P(L) = z,\ \ y \ne z @@ the board plugs each letter to one
\bot,\ \ \text{so } P(A) \ne x @@ refute (7e)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{all } 26 \text{ refuted} \Rightarrow \text{the setting is wrong}} \quad \blacksquare @@ refute the setting, the same way

A wrong setting does not need to be tried on the message. It throws itself out. The machines that hunted settings this way ran through every turn of the wheels and stopped only where no clash appeared. A stop was not a proof of the right setting, only a setting that survived; it was then tried by hand.

20crypt4The work, counted

60 \cdot 26^3 = 1\,054\,560 @@ wheel orders and starting turns: the board is gone
1\,054\,560 \cdot 26 \approx 2.7 \times 10^{7} @@ one guess for P(A) at each
\htmlClass{new}{\tfrac{1.6 \times 10^{20}}{2.7 \times 10^{7}} \approx 6 \times 10^{12}} @@ how many times less work the loop leaves

A guessed word does not find the key; it lets every wrong key refute itself, fast. A machine that ran through the 17 576 turns of one wheel order in about twenty minutes covers all 60 orders in twenty hours; a few dozen such machines finish within the hour.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

A guessed word is a crib. The pairs of a placed crib, drawn as letters joined by numbered links, make the menu; its loops are closures. The machines that ran through the settings were the bombes, and a setting that survived was a stop. The clash technique runs on what is now called a contradiction search, and the board cancelling is a conjugate cancelling.

Who said it first

Marian Rejewski’s team built the first bomba in Warsaw (1938). Alan Turing designed the British bombe (1939–40) around cribs, and Gordon Welchman added the diagonal board, which carried each forced plug both ways and made clashes come faster. Weather reports, which repeated the same words daily, were among the best cribs. In 20·crypt.6.5, the British also counted on operators’ habits: lazy settings, repeated greetings, and the rule that no letter was its own secret.

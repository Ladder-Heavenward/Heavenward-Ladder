27b · proof

Dimension

The plane has two directions and space has three. But what is a direction in a space of polynomials, or of solutions to a rate rule? This step counts directions without pictures. Some arrows build every arrow by adding and stretching. Some arrows waste none of their number. A list that does both at once is a set of measuring directions, and every such list in a space has the same length. That length is the dimension.

If some m arrows build every arrow in a space, then no more than m arrows can be free of each other. So every list that builds the space with nothing wasted has the same length, the dimension of the space.

27b1Building and wasting nothing

Stands on 27a1.

A mix of arrows v₁, …, vₖ is any t₁v₁ + ⋯ + tₖvₖ. The arrows build V if every arrow of V is a mix of them. They are free if the only mix equal to 0 is the one with every tᵢ = 0. A list that builds V and is free is a set of measuring directions for V; accepted name, a basis.

Spare-arrow technique: if v₁, …, vₖ are not free, and v₁ ≠ 0, then some vⱼ is a mix of the arrows before it, and dropping vⱼ leaves every mix the list can make.

t_1v_1 + \cdots + t_kv_k = 0,\ \text{not all } t_i = 0 @@ not free
j = \text{the last index with } t_j \neq 0;\ j \ge 2 @@ if j were 1, then t₁v₁ = 0 with t₁ ≠ 0, so v₁ = 0
v_j = -\tfrac{t_1}{t_j}v_1 - \cdots - \tfrac{t_{j-1}}{t_j}v_{j-1} @@ move the rest across and stretch by 1/tⱼ
\htmlClass{new}{\text{any mix using } v_j \text{ is a mix of the others}} \quad \blacksquare @@ put that in for vⱼ, and gather

27b2No more free than builders

Stands on 27b1 and 3 (induction).

Exchange technique: if w₁, …, wₘ build V, and u₁, …, uₖ in V are free, then k ≤ m. Swap the free arrows in, one at a time, and each time a builder goes out.

u_1, w_1, \ldots, w_m \text{ builds } V \text{ and is not free} @@ u₁ is a mix of the w’s; u₁ ≠ 0, since the u’s are free
\text{drop a spare } w;\ \text{still } m \text{ arrows, still builds} @@ the spare-arrow technique (27b1): the spare is not u₁, which is first
u_1, \ldots, u_i \text{ and } m - i \text{ of the } w\text{’s build } V @@ repeat, by induction on i; the spare is never a u, since the u’s are free
i = m + 1 \le k \Rightarrow u_{m+1} \text{ is a mix of } u_1..u_m @@ if k > m, at i = m no w’s are left, and u₁..uₘ build V
\htmlClass{new}{k \le m} \quad \blacksquare @@ the last line breaks the freedom of the u’s

27b3Every basis is the same size

Stands on 27b2.

Same-size technique: if a space has a basis of n arrows, every basis of it has n arrows. That n is the dimension of the space.

v_1..v_n \text{ and } u_1..u_k \text{ are both bases} @@ given
k \le n @@ the exchange technique: the v’s build, the u’s are free
n \le k @@ the same, the other way round
\htmlClass{new}{k = n} \quad \blacksquare @@ the two lines above

Grow-to-basis technique: in a space of dimension n, every free list can be grown into a basis, and every list of n free arrows is already one. Add any arrow the list cannot make; the longer list is still free, since a mix equal to 0 could not use the new arrow. By the exchange technique this stops by n arrows.

Lists of n numbers have dimension n: the lists with a single 1 in one place build them and are free. Polynomials of degree at most n have dimension n + 1: 1, x, …, xⁿ build them, and are free because a polynomial that is 0 everywhere has every coefficient 0 (9g). The solutions of y″ = −y have dimension 2: cos and sin are free, and by 24h every solution is y(0) cos x + y′(0) sin x.

27b4Illegal moves

Stands on 27b1 to 27b3.

\text{three arrows in the plane are free}\,? @@ the plane has dimension 2: at most two free arrows
v_1, v_2 \text{ not parallel in space} \Rightarrow \text{they build space}\,? @@ two arrows build at most a plane through 0; space has dimension 3
\text{each pair free} \Rightarrow \text{the list is free}\,? @@ (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1): every pair is free, the three are not
\text{every space of arrows has a dimension}\,? @@ only those with a finite list that builds them; all polynomials together have none

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

A mix is a linear combination; arrows that build V span it, and the arrows a list can make are its span. Free arrows are linearly independent; arrows that are not free are linearly dependent. Measuring directions are a basis, and the size of a basis is the dimension. The exchange technique is the Steinitz exchange lemma. A space with no finite basis is infinite-dimensional.

Who said it first

Grassmann used the idea of dimension in 1844. Ernst Steinitz proved the exchange lemma in 1913, in a paper on fields.

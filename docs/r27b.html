27b · proof

Dimension

The plane has two directions and space has three. But what is a direction in a space of polynomials, or of solutions to a rate rule? This step counts directions without pictures. Some vectors build every vector by adding and stretching. Some vectors waste none of their number. A list that does both at once is a set of measuring directions, and every such list in a space has the same length. That length is the dimension.

If some m vectors build every vector in a space, then no more than m vectors can be free of each other. So every list that builds the space with nothing wasted has the same length, the dimension of the space.

27b1Building and Wasting Nothing

A mix of vectors v₁, …, vₖ is any t₁v₁ + ⋯ + tₖvₖ. The vectors build V if every vector of V is a mix of them. They are free if the only mix equal to 0 is the one with every tᵢ = 0. A list that builds V and is free is a set of measuring directions for V; accepted name, a basis.

Spare-vector technique: if v₁, …, vₖ are not free, and v₁ ≠ 0, then some vⱼ is a mix of the vectors before it, and dropping vⱼ leaves every mix the list can make.

t_1v_1 + \cdots + t_kv_k = 0,\ \text{not all } t_i = 0 @@ not free
j = \text{the last index with } t_j \neq 0;\ j \ge 2 @@ if j were 1, then t₁v₁ = 0 with t₁ ≠ 0, so v₁ = 0
v_j = -\tfrac{t_1}{t_j}v_1 - \cdots - \tfrac{t_{j-1}}{t_j}v_{j-1} @@ move the rest across and stretch by 1/tⱼ
\htmlClass{new}{\text{any mix using } v_j \text{ is a mix of the others}} \quad \blacksquare @@ put that in for vⱼ, and gather

27b2No More Free Than Builders

Exchange technique: if w₁, …, wₘ build V, and u₁, …, uₖ in V are free, then k ≤ m. Swap the free vectors in, one at a time, and each time a builder goes out.

u_1, w_1, \ldots, w_m \text{ builds } V \text{ and is not free} @@ u₁ is a mix of the w’s; u₁ ≠ 0, since the u’s are free
\text{drop a spare } w;\ \text{still } m \text{ vectors, still builds} @@ the spare-vector technique (27b1): the spare is not u₁, which is first
u_1, \ldots, u_i \text{ and } m - i \text{ of the } w\text{’s build } V @@ repeat, by induction on i; the spare is never a u, since the u’s are free
i = m + 1 \le k \Rightarrow u_{m+1} \text{ is a mix of } u_1..u_m @@ if k > m, at i = m no w’s are left, and u₁..uₘ build V
\htmlClass{new}{k \le m} \quad \blacksquare @@ the last line breaks the freedom of the u’s

27b3Every Basis Is the Same Size

Same-size technique: if a space has a basis of n vectors, every basis of it has n vectors. That n is the dimension of the space.

v_1..v_n \text{ and } u_1..u_k \text{ are both bases} @@ given
k \le n @@ the exchange technique: the v’s build, the u’s are free
n \le k @@ the same, the other way round
\htmlClass{new}{k = n} \quad \blacksquare @@ the two lines above

Grow-to-basis technique: in a space of dimension n, every free list can be grown into a basis, and every list of n free vectors is already one. Add any vector the list cannot make; the longer list is still free, since a mix equal to 0 could not use the new vector. By the exchange technique this stops by n vectors.

Lists of n numbers have dimension n: the lists with a single 1 in one place build them and are free. Polynomials of degree at most n have dimension n + 1: 1, x, …, xⁿ build them, and are free because a polynomial that is 0 everywhere has every coefficient 0 (9g). The solutions of y″ = −y have dimension 2: cos and sin are free, and by 24h every solution is y(0) cos x + y′(0) sin x.

27b4Moves to Watch Out For

\text{three vectors in the plane are free}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-p">outside the plan</span> the plane has dimension 2: at most two free vectors
v_1, v_2 \text{ not parallel in space} \Rightarrow \text{they build space}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-p">outside the plan</span> two vectors build at most a plane through 0; space has dimension 3
\text{each pair free} \Rightarrow \text{the list is free}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-p">outside the plan</span> (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1): every pair is free, the three are not
\text{every space of vectors has a dimension}\,? @@ <span class="vd vd-p">outside the plan</span> only those with a finite list that builds them; all polynomials together have none

The accepted names

A mix is a linear combination; vectors that build V span it, and the vectors a list can make are its span. Free vectors are linearly independent; vectors that are not free are linearly dependent. Measuring directions are a basis, and the size of a basis is the dimension. The exchange technique is the Steinitz exchange lemma. A space with no finite basis is infinite-dimensional.

Who said it first

Grassmann used the idea of dimension in 1844. Ernst Steinitz proved the exchange lemma in 1913, in a paper on fields.

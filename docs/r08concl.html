8·∎ · conclusion

The line has no holes

Wherever a heap of numbers has something above it all, there is a least such thing, sitting exactly at the top of the heap. The fractions had holes; the line has none.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 8f two has a square root on the line is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 8 promised to fill the holes 5f found in the fractions, with one idea and no new axiom. 8a made a real number a cut: a set of fractions shaped like the lower part of a split. Order became being a part of. Each fraction sat on the line as r*, and 5f's hole became a cut that belongs to no fraction. 8b proved that any two reals compare, by rung 7's either-way principle. 8c proved that a heap of cuts with a member and a bound has a least upper bound, its union.

8d gave the line its adding and multiplying and proved the twelve techniques again. 8e proved that counting passes any real, and that a fraction sits between any two. 8f used all of it to build a real whose square is exactly 2. This page states the rung's claim in full and joins those techniques into one proof.

The claim, in full

Every set of reals that has a member and something above it all has a least thing above it all, so the line has no holes.

Let ℝ be the set of cuts, with α ≤ β meaning α ⊆ β (8a).

Each fraction r sits in ℝ as r*, and r ≤ s exactly when r* ≤ s*.

Any two reals compare: α ≤ β or β ≤ α.

Let S be a set of reals with a member and an upper bound. Then S has a least upper bound, and sup S = ⋃S.

With 8d's adding and multiplying, ℝ is a regular number system with no holes.

So 5f's hole is filled: some positive real squares to exactly 2, and it is no fraction's cut.

The proof

The first parts say that the line holds the fractions, with their order, and that its own order leaves no pair of reals uncompared. They are 8a's fraction-cut and fractions-keep-order, and 8b's any-two-compare on ℝ, whose column is here again.

The heart of the claim is 8c's no-holes. The union of the heap is a cut, by union-of-cuts, and it is the least cut above the heap.

The arithmetic is 8d's, cited here rather than run again: each operation gives a cut, the twelve techniques are proved again, the fractions keep their sums and products, and the order read from “positive” is the order ⊆ that no-holes used.

Last, the hole that started the rung. 8f's root-of-two spends no-holes at one line, and everyday-counting-passes (8e) gives steps too small to overshoot.

\htmlClass{new}{\sigma \ne r^{*} \text{ for every fraction } r} \quad \blacksquare @@ hole-is-no-fraction's-cut (8a)

Run the same column over the fractions and every line survives but one: the line that takes the sup. The fractions whose squares are below 2 have no least fractional upper bound (no-least-fraction-bound, 8c). That one line is the whole difference between the fractions and the line.

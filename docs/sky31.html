Is the Gap Between the Harmonic Sum and the Log a Fraction?

Is the gap between the harmonic sum and the log a fraction? Add one, a half, a third and so on up to one over n, and take away the natural log of n. As n grows the gap settles, at about 0.5772, a number that has been computed by hand and by machine to a great many digits. Nobody knows whether it is a fraction. If it is, the digits already known force the bottom of the fraction to be huge. Most numbers that turn up in mathematics are expected not to be fractions, and for very few has it been proved. One of them is the total of one over each whole number cubed, about 1.202. In 1978 a proof that it is no fraction was announced, so strange that much of the audience dismissed it, and within a year a second proof was found that fits on a page: a double integral of two polynomials that is positive, smaller than any power of a fixed small number, and equal to a whole combination of 1 and that total, divided by a cube of a least common multiple. A fraction cannot be that small without being zero. This page fences the gap, shows what the digits rule out, gives that proof in full, and tells what has been proved since about the totals of odd powers, where a method of 2024 reached a number no earlier method could.

γ = lim (Hₙ − ln n) ≈ 0.577 215 66, fenced within 1/(2n(n + 1)) by the half-step fence. If x = p/q, every other fraction r/s is at least 1/(qs) away, so a convergent close to γ forces q past the next convergent’s bottom. A number x with whole Aₙ, Bₙ and 0 < |Aₙ + Bₙx| → 0 is no fraction. For ζ(3): Iₙ = ∫∫ −ln(xy) Pₙ(x)Pₙ(y)/(1 − xy) dx dy = (Aₙ + Bₙζ(3))/dₙ³ with dₙ the least common multiple of 1 to n, and 0 < Iₙ ≤ 2ζ(3)(√2 − 1)⁴ⁿ; since dₙ³ is about e³ⁿ and e³(√2 − 1)⁴ ≈ 0.59 < 1, ζ(3) is no fraction.

sky31.1The Gap, Fenced

22f4 showed that gₙ = Hₙ − ln n falls and stays above 0, so it settles at a number γ. Each fall gₖ − gₖ₊₁ is the area between 1/x and the level 1/(k + 1) over the strip from k to k + 1. Since 1/x bends upward, its chord lies above it and its tangent at the middle below it, and those two pin each strip.

Half-step-fence technique: for every n ≥ 1, 1/(2(n + 1)) < gₙ − γ < 1/(2n).

g_k - g_{k+1} = \int_k^{k+1} \frac{dx}{x} - \frac{1}{k+1} @@ 22f4
\int_k^{k+1} \frac{dx}{x} < \frac12\Big(\frac1k + \frac1{k+1}\Big) \Rightarrow g_k - g_{k+1} < \frac12\Big(\frac1k - \frac1{k+1}\Big) @@ the chord lies above the curve: 1/x bends upward, its second slope 2/x³ > 0
\int_k^{k+1} \frac{dx}{x} > \frac{1}{k + \frac12} \Rightarrow g_k - g_{k+1} > \frac{1}{2(k + \frac12)(k+1)} > \frac12\Big(\frac{1}{k+1} - \frac{1}{k+2}\Big) @@ the tangent at k + ½ lies below; (k + ½)(k + 1) < (k + 1)(k + 2)
g_n - \gamma = \textstyle\sum_{k \ge n} (g_k - g_{k+1}) @@ the falls add up to the whole drop
\htmlClass{new}{\frac{1}{2(n+1)} < g_n - \gamma < \frac{1}{2n}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the sums telescope

At n = 10: H₁₀ = 2.928 968, ln 10 = 2.302 585, g₁₀ = 0.626 383, so 0.576 383 < γ < 0.580 929. At n = 1000 the fence is 0.577 215 58 < γ < 0.577 216 08, half a millionth wide. The true value begins 0.577 215 664 901 532 86. No fence of this kind can say whether γ is a fraction: every fraction close to γ sits inside some early fence too.

sky31.2What a Fraction Would Need

Digits can never prove a number is not a fraction, but they can push any fraction it might be far out. Two different fractions with bottoms q and s differ by at least 1/(qs), since their difference is a whole number other than 0 over qs.

Far-fraction technique: if x = p/q and r/s is a fraction other than x, then |x − r/s| ≥ 1/(qs); so if a convergent pⱼ/qⱼ of x is not x itself, every bottom q with x = p/q is larger than qⱼ₊₁.

\Big|\frac pq - \frac rs\Big| = \frac{|ps - rq|}{qs} \ge \frac{1}{qs} @@ ps − rq is whole and not 0
\Big|x - \frac{p_j}{q_j}\Big| < \frac{1}{q_j\,q_{j+1}} @@ the convergent-bound technique (43k3)
\frac{1}{q\,q_j} < \frac{1}{q_j\,q_{j+1}} @@ the two lines together
\htmlClass{new}{q > q_{j+1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the far-fraction technique

The continued fraction of γ begins [0; 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 4, 3, 13, 5, 1, 1, 8, 1, 2, 4, 1, 1, 40, 1, 11, …], with convergents 1/2, 3/5, 4/7, 11/19, 15/26, 71/123, 228/395, … ; the twentieth is 376 566 901/652 385 103, which misses γ by about 2 · 10⁻¹⁹, and the next bottom is 7 812 920 633. Twenty correct digits already fix these twenty two terms. So if γ is a fraction, its bottom is above seven billion, from twenty digits alone. With D correct digits the convergents can be trusted while their bottoms stay below about 10^(D/2), so a record of D digits pushes the bound to roughly D/2 digits.

sky31.3A Test for No Fraction

23g1 showed e is no fraction by finding whole numbers that are smaller than 1 and above 0. The pattern is a test.

Small-combination technique: if there are whole numbers Aₙ, Bₙ with Aₙ + Bₙx ≠ 0 for every n and Aₙ + Bₙx → 0, then x is not a fraction.

x = p/q,\ q \ge 1 @@ suppose so, toward ⊥
q(A_n + B_n x) = qA_n + pB_n \text{ is whole and not } 0 @@ Aₙ + Bₙx ≠ 0
|A_n + B_n x| \ge 1/q \text{ for every } n @@ a whole number other than 0 has size at least 1
\htmlClass{new}{A_n + B_n x \not\to 0:\ \bot} \quad \blacksquare @@ the small-combination technique

For e, 23g1’s x = q!(e − Σ 1/k!) is such a combination with Bₙ = q!, and it is below 1/q. The target here is ζ(3) = 1 + 1/8 + 1/27 + 1/64 + ⋯ = 1.202 056 903 159 594 …, which settles by 22f’s p-sum technique. The test asks for combinations that shrink to 0 without ever reaching it, and the next two footings build them: 10ζ(3) − 12 = 0.020 569 …, (1168ζ(3) − 1404)/8 = 0.000 307 86 …, and on, each from a double integral.

sky31.4Integrals That Hold ζ(3)

Two pieces. First, double integrals of xʳyˢ against −ln(xy)/(1 − xy) over the unit square give ζ(3) or fractions. Second, polynomials with whole coefficients turn them into whole combinations. Write dₙ for the least common multiple of 1, 2, …, n.

Kernel-integral technique: for whole r, s ≥ 0, the integral of −ln(xy) xʳyˢ/(1 − xy) over the unit square is 2(ζ(3) − 1 − 1/8 − ⋯ − 1/r³) when r = s, and (1/(s + 1)² + ⋯ + 1/r²)/(r − s) when r > s; so for r, s ≤ n it is a whole multiple of ζ(3) plus a fraction with bottom dividing dₙ³.

\iint \frac{x^{r+t}y^{s+t}}{1 - xy} = \sum_{k \ge 0} \iint x^{k+r+t} y^{k+s+t} = \sum_{k \ge 0} \frac{1}{(k + r + 1 + t)(k + s + 1 + t)} @@ 1/(1 − xy) = Σ (xy)ᵏ, summed in either order (30e’s either-order technique)
r > s:\ \ \frac{1}{(k+r+1+t)(k+s+1+t)} = \frac{1}{r-s}\Big(\frac{1}{k+s+1+t} - \frac{1}{k+r+1+t}\Big) \Rightarrow \text{sum} = \frac{1}{r-s}\sum_{j=s+1}^{r} \frac{1}{j+t} @@ the tail cancels: only r − s terms are left
\frac{d}{dt}\,x^{r+t}y^{s+t} = \ln(xy)\,x^{r+t}y^{s+t} @@ so the slope in t at t = 0 brings down ln(xy)
-\iint \frac{\ln(xy)\,x^r y^s}{1-xy} = \frac{1}{r-s}\sum_{j=s+1}^{r} \frac{1}{j^2},\ \ \ \ r = s:\ \ 2\sum_{k > r} \frac{1}{k^3} @@ the slope of each side at t = 0, with signs
\htmlClass{new}{\text{bottoms divide } d_n^3 \text{ when } r, s \le n} \quad \blacksquare @@ r − s ≤ n and j ≤ n both divide dₙ

Now Pₙ(x) = (1/n!) times the n-th slope of xⁿ(1 − x)ⁿ. Expanding, Pₙ(x) = Σₖ (−1)ᵏ C(n, k) C(n + k, n) xᵏ, whole coefficients: P₁ = 1 − 2x, P₂ = 1 − 6x + 6x². They are 31m3’s family for the weight 1 on [0, 1], at right angles under the area product. Multiplying out, Iₙ = ∬ −ln(xy)Pₙ(x)Pₙ(y)/(1 − xy) is (Aₙ + Bₙζ(3))/dₙ³ with Aₙ, Bₙ whole. Checked: I₁ = 10ζ(3) − 12 = 0.020 569 0, I₂ = (1168ζ(3) − 1404)/8 = 0.000 307 86, I₃ = (624 240ζ(3) − 750 372)/216 = 0.000 005 687. Taking the slope under the integral is held in trust from 30d’s fenced-limit technique.

sky31.5Small, but Not Zero

It remains to show that Iₙ is above 0 and shrinks fast enough to beat dₙ³.

Shrinking-integral technique: 0 < Iₙ ≤ 2ζ(3)(√2 − 1)⁴ⁿ for every n ≥ 1.

\frac{-\ln(xy)}{1 - xy} = \int_0^1 \frac{dz}{1 - (1 - xy)z} @@ ∫₀¹ dz/(1 − cz) = −ln(1 − c)/c with c = 1 − xy
I_n = \iiint \frac{(xyz)^n (1 - x)^n P_n(y)}{(1 - (1 - xy)z)^{n+1}} @@ n times by parts in x: xⁿ(1 − x)ⁿ and its first n − 1 slopes are 0 at 0 and 1
w = \frac{1 - z}{1 - (1 - xy)z}:\ \ I_n = \iiint \frac{(1 - x)^n (1 - w)^n P_n(y)}{1 - (1 - xy)w} @@ the change of variable carries [0, 1] to [0, 1]
I_n = \iiint \frac{x^n(1-x)^n\,y^n(1-y)^n\,w^n(1-w)^n}{(1 - (1 - xy)w)^{n+1}}\,dx\,dy\,dw > 0 @@ n times by parts in y, the same way; the inside is positive
\frac{x(1-x)\,y(1-y)\,w(1-w)}{1 - (1 - xy)w} \le (\sqrt2 - 1)^4 \approx 0.029\,44 @@ its largest value, where its three slopes vanish: x = y = √2 − 1, w = 1/√2
\htmlClass{new}{0 < I_n \le (\sqrt2 - 1)^{4n} \iiint \frac{dx\,dy\,dw}{1 - (1 - xy)w} = 2\zeta(3)(\sqrt2 - 1)^{4n}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the last integral is I₀ = 2ζ(3), by the kernel-integral technique at r = s = 0

Not-a-fraction technique for ζ(3): ζ(3) is not a fraction. By 43g4’s prime-count technique, held in trust there, dₙ = the product over primes p ≤ n of the largest power of p not past n, which is at most n^π(n) = e^(π(n) ln n), and π(n) ln n / n settles on 1; so dₙ < 3ⁿ for all large n (ln dₙ / n is 0.94 at n = 100 and 1.00 at n = 1000). Then 0 < |Aₙ + Bₙζ(3)| = dₙ³Iₙ < 2ζ(3)(27(√2 − 1)⁴)ⁿ = 2ζ(3) · 0.795ⁿ → 0, and the small-combination technique finishes. The triple integral was checked against the double one for n = 1 and 2 to nine places.

sky31.6Since 2000

The same plan, combinations of 1 and a target that are small but not zero, has been pushed to the other odd totals ζ(5) = 1 + 1/32 + 1/243 + ⋯, ζ(7) and on. Stated here, not proved: in 2000 it was shown that endlessly many of ζ(3), ζ(5), ζ(7), … are not fractions, without naming any one past ζ(3); in 2001, that at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is not a fraction, without saying which. Each builds its small combinations from many series at once, and a single combination may hold several totals together.

In 2024 a different road reached a new single number. Take L = 1 − 1/2² + 1/4² − 1/5² + 1/7² − 1/8² + ⋯, the squares of the numbers not divisible by 3, with sign + on the hour 1 of the clock of 3 and − on the hour 2. It was shown that L is not a fraction, and more: no whole combination of 1, ζ(2) and L is 0 except the one with all three whole numbers 0. The method bounds a whole family of functions at once, rather than one integral, and its authors say it has other uses. Whether it reaches ζ(5) is open. A preprint of September 2026 by another road claims that ζ(5) is not a fraction, and checks of it in a proof assistant were reported within weeks; this page has not revisited whether the claim is accepted.

Summing up the kinds of answer: e and π are no fractions (23g); ζ(2) = π²/6 is no fraction either, since π² is not (23g3 holds e and π past fractions altogether); ζ(3) is no fraction (this page); γ is unknown, as are e + π and eπ, and ζ(5) is claimed (2026) but not, as far as this page has checked, settled.

Open questions · nobody has the answer yet

Open questions

These are open: the field is still asking them. There is no key; a good attempt is a reason, a guess, or a better question.

∞31.oq.1Is γ a fraction? It is not even known whether it is a root of a polynomial with whole coefficients.
Why it is hard: γ is a limit of differences, Hₙ − ln n, and no one has found small whole combinations of 1 and γ, or any family of integrals that produce them, the way ζ(3) has them.
∞31.oq.2Is ζ(5) a fraction?
Why it is hard: the integrals that work for ζ(3) give, for ζ(5), combinations of 1, ζ(3) and ζ(5) together, and in every version found so far their size does not beat the growth of the bottoms. The best since 2001 says only that one of four totals is not a fraction, apart from the claimed proof of September 2026 told in sky31.6, which this page has not revisited.
∞31.oq.3Is e + π a fraction? Is eπ? At least one of them is not, since if both were, e and π would be the roots of x² − (e + π)x + eπ, a polynomial with fraction coefficients, and 23g3 holds that e is the root of no such polynomial.
Why it is hard: the proofs for e and for π each lean on a series or an integral special to that number, and none is known for the sum.
∞31.oq.4Can the 2024 method, which bounds a whole family of functions at once, reach ζ(5) or γ?
Why it is hard: the method needs a construction that produces the target with whole coefficients small enough for the bound to bite, and none of the right size is known for ζ(5) or γ.
A short history · told from more than one side

How we got here

The gap has been computed again and again, by hand and then by machine, each time to more digits, and its value has never been the question; its nature is. 22f4 already called whether it is a fraction a question open for nearly three hundred years. It has many formulas, as sums and as integrals, and none of them has yet given small whole combinations of 1 and γ.

In June 1978 a proof that ζ(3) is no fraction was presented in a talk, with sums whose origin nobody could see, and many in the audience dismissed it as flawed. Three listeners checked it, and within two months it was given again in full. Within a year a second proof was found by integrals of the polynomials of 31m3, the one on this page. Two lessons are drawn from the episode, and they pull different ways: a proof can be right and badly explained, and a short road can lie unseen for two centuries.

The years from 2000 pushed the method with many integrals at once. In 2000 it was shown that endlessly many of the odd totals are not fractions, and in 2001 that one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is not. The hope of narrowing four to one was not met by these methods. Some read the slow progress as a sign that the integral method has reached its end; others as a sign that the right family of integrals for ζ(5) has not yet been written down.

In August 2024 three mathematicians posted a proof by another road, bounding how large a whole family of functions with whole coefficients can be, and named a new number that is no fraction, L of sky31.6, together with the independence of 1, ζ(2) and L. Its authors say the method has other uses. What is argued now is whether it opens ζ(5) and γ, or whether its target was one the method happened to suit. In September 2026 a preprint claimed ζ(5) itself, with machine checks of it reported soon after; whether it stands is not settled here.

The cellar · there if you want it

The accepted names

γ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, Euler’s constant. The half-step fence is a case of the Euler–Maclaurin formula, γ = Hₙ − ln n − 1/(2n) + 1/(12n²) − ⋯. ζ(3) is Apéry’s constant, and that it is irrational is Apéry’s theorem; the proof on this page is Beukers’ proof, and the Pₙ are the shifted Legendre polynomials. The small-combination technique is the usual irrationality criterion. The 2000 result is Rivoal’s theorem (Ball–Rivoal); the 2001 result is Zudilin’s theorem. L is the Dirichlet L-value L(2, χ₋₃) of the character of conductor 3, and the 2024 method is the arithmetic holonomy bound of Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang, applied to a construction of Zagier.

Who said it first

Leonhard Euler studied γ and computed it; Lorenzo Mascheroni computed it again. Roger Apéry presented the proof for ζ(3) in June 1978 (Astérisque, 1979); Henri Cohen, Hendrik Lenstra and Alfred van der Poorten checked it. Frits Beukers gave the integral proof (Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society, 1979). Tanguy Rivoal proved the 2000 result; Wadim Zudilin the 2001 one. Frank Calegari, Vesselin Dimitrov and Yunqing Tang posted the 2024 proof in August 2024. Aabir Fauzan posted the September 2026 preprint “ζ(5) is irrational”; Lean formalizations of it were reported by Dan Romik (with stated axioms and one step left open) and in the Zeta5 project (reported complete).

A proof that is not allowed

“γ = 0.577 215 664 9 …, and a fraction with a small bottom near it would show in the digits; none does, so γ is not a fraction.” Not allowed: the far-fraction technique shows only that a fraction equal to γ would have a large bottom. Every bound of that kind is finite, and a fraction with a larger bottom is not ruled out.

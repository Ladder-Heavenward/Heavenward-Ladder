45·econ · economics

Why Do Strangers Keep the Chain Honest?

45i assumed most of the work is honest. In an open network nobody is honest by order, so the rules must make honesty pay. This annex reads 45i as a game of rewards.

Pay for blocks that join the chain. Then wasting work on a losing chain costs money, and a cheat needs a large share of the work to profit.

45econ1Paid to Join

A machine that adds a block that stays in the chain is paid, in new coins and in fees. A block on a chain that loses earns nothing. So work on the chain everyone follows pays, and work on a rival chain is thrown away.

45econ2When Cheating Does Not Pay

An attacker with share q < 1/2 of the work catches up from z behind with chance (q/p)ᶻ (45i3). Every try that fails wastes its work. So for a small share the expected gain is small and the cost is sure.

45econ3Holding Blocks Back

A miner may find a block and keep it secret, building on it, to waste the others’ work on a chain about to be overtaken. Analysis has shown that this can earn more than a fair share once the miner holds about a third of the work, and less in some network conditions. So the rules are safe only while no group comes near that share.

The accepted names

Holding blocks back is selfish mining, analysed by Eyal and Sirer (2014). Pay for work is the block reward with transaction fees. Methods with known members punish cheats by taking their deposits, called slashing, in proof of stake.

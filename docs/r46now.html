What Are the Building Blocks of Symmetry?

What are the building blocks of symmetry? 46e proved that every finite group comes apart into simple pieces, and that the pieces are fixed, as the primes of a number are. So the simple groups are to groups what primes are to numbers, and the obvious question is to list them. The clocks of prime size are some. The even shuffles of five or more things are more. Then come families built from grids over the clock arithmetic of rung 43, endless in number and orderly. And then twenty six that fit no family, the largest of which has more members than there are atoms in the earth. The list was announced complete around 1980, a gap was found, and the gap was closed in 2004, in a proof spread over some ten thousand pages of journals by about a hundred authors. A second, shorter proof is still being written. On the way, the largest group turned out to be tied to a function from the arithmetic of the plane by a coincidence of numbers that nobody could explain, until 1992. This page counts some members of the families, checks the coincidence, and says where the trust in a proof that long rests.

Every finite simple group is a clock of prime size, an even shuffle group of five or more, a group of grids over a finite field, or one of twenty six others. The largest of the twenty six has a number of members with fifty four digits; its smallest representation has dimension one less than a coefficient of a function of the plane. The proof of the list was finished in 2004.

46now1The Primes of Groups

By 46e every finite group has a chain of pieces, and the pieces are fixed. So listing the finite simple groups lists every possible piece. What the list does not do is say how pieces are put together: 46e4 found two groups with the same pieces. That second question is wide open for most sizes.

46now2Groups of Grids Over a Clock

Take the 2 × 2 grids with entries in the clock field ℤ/p and determinant 1. They form a group, SL(2, p), under the product (27d, 27e). The two grids I and −I commute with everything; the quotient by them is PSL(2, p), simple for every prime p ≥ 5.

\text{first column: any of the } p^2 - 1 \text{ that are not } 0 @@ an undoable grid has no zero column
\text{second column with } ad - bc = 1:\ p \text{ choices} @@ given the first column (a, c), the condition is one straight equation in (b, d), with p answers
|SL(2, p)| = p(p^2 - 1),\ \ |PSL(2, p)| = \tfrac{p(p^2 - 1)}{2} @@ −I ≠ I for p odd
\htmlClass{new}{p = 5: 60;\ \ p = 7: 168;\ \ p = 11: 660;\ \ p = 13: 1092} \quad \blacksquare @@ the one of 60 matches A₅

The same construction with grids of every size, over every finite field of 44g, and with grids that keep a form of 27h, gives the endless families of Lie type, sixteen kinds in all. The simple groups below 1000 that are not clocks are of sizes 60, 168, 360, 504 and 660.

46now3Twenty Six That Fit No Family

The first five that fit no family were found in 1861 and 1873, as shuffle groups of 11, 12, 22, 23 and 24 things; the smallest has 7920 members. No more were found for almost a century. Then, from 1965 to 1975, twenty one more, the last and largest predicted in 1973 and built in 1982.

\log_{10}|M| \approx 46(0.301) + 20(0.477) + 9(0.699) + \cdots @@ add the logs (17)
\htmlClass{new}{|M| \approx 8.08 \times 10^{53}} \quad \blacksquare @@ a number with 54 digits

The proof that there are no others is what was finished in 2004. Its last piece, on the groups of a kind called quasithin, ran to two volumes of 1221 pages. The whole is put at ten to fifteen thousand pages in about five hundred papers. A group of authors has been writing a second proof, in a series of books, since 1994; ten volumes have appeared, and it is not finished. No proof assistant has checked it, as 38·now asks of shorter proofs.

46now4A Coincidence of Numbers

The largest group’s whole representations (46·cb27) have dimensions 1, 196 883, 21 296 876, and so on. Separately, a function j of the upper half of the plane, from the arithmetic of rung 26’s functions, has a series in q = e^(2πiτ): j = 1/q + 744 + 196 884 q + 21 493 760 q² + ⋯. In 1978 someone noticed the first coincidence.

196\,884 = 1 + 196\,883 @@ two whole dimensions
21\,493\,760 = 1 + 196\,883 + 21\,296\,876 @@ three
\htmlClass{new}{\text{each coefficient of } j \text{ is a sum of the group’s dimensions}} \quad \blacksquare @@ conjectured 1979 for every coefficient, with a representation behind each

It was called moonshine, for how unlikely it seemed. In 1992 it was proved, by building an object that has the group as its symmetries and a structure from physics, the strings of a theory of 26 dimensions, from which j comes out. The proof earned a prize in 1998. Why the largest group and this function are tied at all, beyond the proof, is still argued over, and newer moonshines for other groups were found in 2010 and after.

The cellar · there if you want it

The accepted names

The list is the classification of finite simple groups. The families of grids are the groups of Lie type, PSL(2, p) the projective special linear groups; the 26 are the sporadic groups, the largest the Monster, and the first five the Mathieu groups. The function j is the j-invariant, a modular function; moonshine is monstrous moonshine, and the structure of 1992 is the moonshine module, a vertex operator algebra. The second proof is the Gorenstein–Lyons–Solomon programme.

Who said it first

Émile Mathieu found the first five sporadic groups in 1861 and 1873. Zvonimir Janko found the sixth in 1965. Bernd Fischer and Robert Griess predicted the Monster in 1973, and Griess built it in 1982. Daniel Gorenstein laid out the programme of classification in 1972 and announced its end in 1983; Michael Aschbacher and Stephen D. Smith closed the quasithin gap in 2004. John McKay noticed 196 884 = 1 + 196 883 in 1978; John Conway and Simon Norton stated the conjecture in 1979; Richard Borcherds proved it in 1992 and received a Fields Medal in 1998. The newer moonshine of 2010 is due to Tohru Eguchi, Hirosi Ooguri and Yuji Tachikawa.

A proof that is not allowed

“A₅ and PSL(2, 5) both have 60 members and are simple, so every simple group is fixed by its size.” Not allowed: the two happen to match, but A₈ and PSL(3, 4) both have 20 160 members, are simple, and do not.

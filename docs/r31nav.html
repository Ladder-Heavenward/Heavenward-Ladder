How Do You Blend a Guess With a Measurement?

How do you blend a guess with a measurement? A ship’s dead reckoning says it is here; a satellite fix says a little to the east. Neither is exact, and each knows roughly how wrong it tends to be. Take the better one and the other is wasted; average them and the worse one spoils the better. This page finds the best blend by rung 14’s slope 0 and rung 30’s spreads, writes it as a gain, runs it step after step as the ship moves, and finds the steady gain it settles to. That loop, in many dimensions, guided Apollo to the moon and runs in every phone.

Blend two estimates with weights inversely as their spreads squared; the blend’s spread squared is smaller than either. Written as a gain, the new estimate is the old plus K times the miss, K = P/(P + R). Between fixes, move the estimate by the known motion and add the motion’s spread squared Q to P. Repeat: P and K settle at the solution of a quadratic.

31nav1Two Estimates, One Blend

Claire navigates a boat along a coast. Dead reckoning puts it at a = 100 m along a buoy line, with errors of spread 5 m; a satellite fix reads b = 106 m, with spread 10 m. Their errors are independent. Blend them as wa + (1 − w)b and choose w.

\text{spread}^2 = w^2 \cdot 25 + (1 - w)^2 \cdot 100 @@ spreads squared add for independent errors (30)
\text{slope in } w:\ 50w - 200(1 - w) = 0 @@ the flat-top technique (14a)
w = \dfrac{100}{125} = 0.8 @@ the weight on the better estimate
\htmlClass{new}{\text{blend} = 0.8 \cdot 100 + 0.2 \cdot 106 = 101.2} @@ spread² = 16 + 4 = 20, below 25

Blend technique: weight two independent estimates inversely as their spreads squared; the blend is better than either alone. Even a poor fix improves a good guess.

31nav2The Blend as a Gain

Rearrange the blend around the guess. From here on, on this page, P is the guess’s spread squared, R the measurement’s, and the gain K is the share of the miss the guess moves by.

\text{blend} = a + (1 - w)(b - a) @@ the same blend
K = 1 - w = \dfrac{P}{P + R} = \dfrac{25}{125} = 0.2 @@ the gain
a + K(b - a) = 100 + 0.2 \cdot 6 = 101.2 @@ move a fifth of the way
\htmlClass{new}{P_{\text{new}} = (1 - K)\,P = 20} @@ the update shrinks P

Gain technique: move the guess toward the measurement by the gain P/(P + R) of the miss, and multiply P by 1 − K. This is 17·logi’s nudge, with the share chosen by the spreads instead of by hand.

31nav3Moving Between Fixes

The boat sails on at a known speed for a minute before the next fix. Its estimate moves with it, but the current and the helm add error: say spread 3 m a minute, independent of the rest.

\hat x \to \hat x + v\,\Delta t @@ move by the known motion
P \to P + Q,\ \ Q = 9 @@ spreads squared add
P:\ 20 \to 29 @@ less sure after a minute
\htmlClass{new}{\text{then the next fix: } K = 29/129 \approx 0.22} @@ a less sure guess listens more

Between fixes the estimate moves with the boat and its spread squared grows by the motion’s; at each fix it is blended and shrinks. The two steps alternate for as long as the boat sails.

31nav4The Gain Settles

Run the two steps again and again with Q = 9 and R = 100.

K:\ 0.225,\ 0.239,\ 0.248,\ 0.253,\ 0.255, \ldots @@ the gain climbs, more slowly each time
\text{steady: } M = P + Q,\ \ P = \dfrac{MR}{M + R} @@ a fixed point (22a)
M^2 - QM - QR = 0 @@ clear the fraction (5h)
M = \tfrac12\big(9 + \sqrt{81 + 3600}\big) \approx 34.8 @@ the positive root
\htmlClass{new}{K \approx 34.8/134.8 \approx 0.26,\ \ P \approx 25.8} @@ where the loop settles

Settling technique: with steady Q and R, the gain and spread settle where predicting and blending balance, at the positive root of a quadratic. A steady boat could use the steady gain from the start; a changing one recomputes it every step.

31nav5Many Numbers at Once

A real craft tracks place and speed in three directions, and a fix may measure only some of them. The same two steps run with lists and grids in place of numbers.

\text{predict: } \hat x \to F\hat x,\ \ P \to FPF^{\mathsf T} + Q @@ F moves the state one step
\text{gain: } K = PH^{\mathsf T}(HPH^{\mathsf T} + R)^{-1} @@ H picks out what the fix measures
\text{update: } \hat x \to \hat x + K(z - H\hat x),\ \ P \to (I - KH)P @@ blend, then shrink P
\htmlClass{new}{\text{one number: } F = H = 1 \text{ gives 31nav2 and 31nav3}} @@ the same loop

The blend is best when the errors are independent and bell-shaped and the motion is straight; it stays a good blend when they are nearly so. A fix measuring only speed still sharpens place, through the grid P that links them.

Accepted names

The loop of predicting and blending is the Kalman filter; K is the Kalman gain, P the error covariance, Q the process noise and R the measurement noise. Weighting by 1/σ² is inverse-variance weighting. The steady gain solves a Riccati equation. Versions for bent motion are the extended and unscented Kalman filters.

Who said it first

Rudolf Kálmán published the filter in 1960. Stanley Schmidt at NASA’s Ames Research Center adapted it for the Apollo guidance computer in the early 1960s.

Statistics floor

Rungs 22 to 30. You arrive with calculus and geometry, and the ground begins to widen. Sums that never end, directions that outnumber the room you stand in, and chance, which looks like the end of all guessing and turns out to be where guessing becomes a craft. Some rungs here hold out longer than any before them, and the view from each is wider for it.

The floor · read it through, then climb on

Introduction

This is the longest floor so far. It carries the tools of the last three floors into larger spaces: sums with no last term, numbers that turn, arrows with many directions, and space that curves.

Rungs 22 to 24 take calculus past its first edges. An endless sum can have a total, a smooth function can be rebuilt from what happens at one point, and a rule about rates can fix a whole function. Rungs 25 and 26 build a number system in which multiplying turns and stretches, and find that growth and turning are one function.

Rungs 27 and 28 give arrows their own algebra and carry slope into many directions at once. Rung 29 shows that flat space is one choice among many, and that curvature can be measured from inside. Rung 30 asks how to give a size to a set, and finds that chance is a kind of size.

On this floor, the guesswork you have practised beside every proof gets its full rigour: a stated chance of being wrong.

The geometry floor gave the scientist's guess its fuller name: induction under the case with predictable variation. The statistician's guess, by large case, is the other kind. No rule says what the next one will do, but you can say how they spread, and how likely each outcome is. This floor builds the tools it needs, and its last rung says exactly how it is a kind of induction.

You have read the anexact boxes since the ground floor. Here they come into their own. This floor is wide, and it will take time, and that is fine. Every statistician alive is still learning how far these tools go.

The rungs of this floor

  • 22 · endless sums can have totals
  • 23 · a smooth function is rebuilt from one point
  • 24 · a rule on its rates fixes a function
  • 25 · finding every root
  • 26 · growth and turning are one function
  • 27 · arrows add and stretch, and have dimension
  • 28 · the derivative is a flat map in every direction
  • 29 · flat is one geometry among many
  • 30 · size and chance add over countably many pieces

At the top of the floor is its conclusion, and beside it the floor’s certification: one fresh question a rung, about an hour in all. It covers the floor’s last rung too, so that rung’s last step needs no certification of its own. Passing it gives you the title statistician, and the Mathematics floor opens above. Like every title on the ladder, it is a word for what you have begun, not for what you have finished.

How Does a Machine Choose What to Attend To?

How does a machine choose what to attend to? ∞ח‎ met the attention step as a soft lookup: each token of a passage asks a question, every token offers an answer, and the asker takes a weighted average of the answers, weighted by how well each fits the question. This step is the working part of the machines that write text, and it is three grid products and one soft weighing. Written so, its properties can be proved. It is blind to the order of the tokens, so order has to be put in by hand. Its scores depend on its two score grids only through their product, so its knobs have many settings for one step. Its cost grows as the square of the passage, and in 2023 that was shown to be hard to avoid. And with the soft weighing left out, one attention step can carry out one step of the walk downhill on the examples in its own passage.

Tokens: the rows of X. Scores S = (XA)(XB)ᵀ = X(ABᵀ)Xᵀ. Weights W: each row of S through e^s, divided by the row’s total. Output WXC. Rows of W are positive and add to 1. Att(PX) = P·Att(X) for a shuffle P. ABᵀ has rank at most k, and (AG, B(G⁻¹)ᵀ) give the same step. One step of the walk from 0 on ½Σ(w·xᵢ − yᵢ)² predicts η Σ (xᵢ·x) yᵢ.

inf85.1The Step as Three Grid Products

A passage is T tokens, and each token is a list of d numbers: the rows x₁, …, x_T of a grid X. The step has three grids of knobs: A and B, each d by k, and C, d by d. Row i of XA is the question token i asks, row j of XB is what token j can be found by, and row j of XC is the answer token j offers. The score of j for i is the dot of question i with finder j, so the grid of scores is S = (XA)(XB)ᵀ. Each row of S is weighed softly as in ∞ח‎: wij is esij divided by the total of esil over the row. The output is Y = W·XC, written Att(X).

Row-average technique: every row of W is positive and adds to 1; adding one number to every score of a row leaves that row of W unchanged; and each place of an output row lies between the least and the greatest of the answers in that place.

w_{ij} = e^{s_{ij}} \big/ \textstyle\sum_l e^{s_{il}} > 0,\quad \textstyle\sum_j w_{ij} = 1 @@ shares of a total
s_{ij} \to s_{ij} + c_i:\quad e^{c_i} \text{ cancels, top and bottom} @@ e^(a + b) = e^a·e^b (17)
y_i = \textstyle\sum_j w_{ij}\,v_j,\quad v_j \text{ row } j \text{ of } XC @@ row i of W times the grid of answers
\htmlClass{new}{\min_j v_{jp} \le y_{ip} \le \max_j v_{jp}} \quad \blacksquare @@ an average with positive weights that add to 1

So the step invents nothing: each token leaves with a blend of what the tokens offered, and only differences of scores within a row matter. The work is in the two T by T grids. S takes about T²k multiplications by the school technique and W·XC about T²d, so a passage twice as long costs four times as much. The fast techniques of ∞w do not help at these sizes.

inf85.2Blind to Order

A shuffle grid P has one 1 in each row and each column and 0 elsewhere. PX is X with its rows reordered, and SPᵀ is S with its columns reordered the same way. P keeps every length, so PᵀP = I by 27m.

Order-blind technique: Att(PX) = P·Att(X) for every shuffle grid P: shuffle the tokens and the outputs are the same rows, shuffled the same way.

PXA,\ PXB,\ PXC @@ the questions, finders and answers of PX: the same rows, reordered
(PXA)(PXB)^{\mathsf T} = P\,S\,P^{\mathsf T} @@ (MN)ᵀ = NᵀMᵀ
\text{weights of } P S P^{\mathsf T} = P\,W\,P^{\mathsf T} @@ the weighing works row by row and treats the entries of a row alike
P W P^{\mathsf T} \cdot PXC = P\,W\,XC @@ PᵀP = I (27m)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{Att}(PX) = P\,\text{Att}(X)} \quad \blacksquare @@ shuffle then attend is attend then shuffle

Read it with arrows. The shuffles act on passages and on outputs, and Att is a map that keeps the action: the square closes. So nothing the step computes can depend on where a token stands. “The dog bit the man” and “the man bit the dog” go in as the same tokens, and each token comes out the same from both. Sequence is exactly what this arrow lets hide, and a machine that reads text must put it back by hand: before the step, each token is given a mark of its place, added to its list.

inf85.3Two Grids, One Form

The grids A and B never act alone.

Score-form technique: sij = xᵢᵀMxⱼ with M = ABᵀ, a reading of two tokens that is straight in each slot; M has rank at most k; and for every k by k grid G that can be undone, AG and B(G⁻¹)ᵀ give the same M, so the same step.

S = (XA)(XB)^{\mathsf T} = X\,(AB^{\mathsf T})\,X^{\mathsf T} @@ (MN)ᵀ = NᵀMᵀ, and brackets may move (27d)
s_{ij} = x_i^{\mathsf T} M\,x_j @@ entry (i, j): straight in xᵢ with xⱼ held, and in xⱼ with xᵢ held (27h2)
M = \textstyle\sum_{s=1}^{k} a_s\,b_s^{\mathsf T} @@ a_s and b_s the columns of A and B: k terms, so rank at most k (47h2)
\htmlClass{new}{(AG)\big(B(G^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\big)^{\mathsf T} = A\,G\,G^{-1}B^{\mathsf T} = AB^{\mathsf T}} \quad \blacksquare @@ G undone by G⁻¹

Two things follow. With k smaller than d, a step compares tokens in k directions only: it can ask one narrow kind of question, and a machine runs many such steps side by side, each with its own A, B and C. And the knobs have a whole family of settings for each step, one for every G. This is ∞9’s two learners in a row again, where u and v counted only through uv: the lowest points of the error are sheets, not points, and the walk downhill picks among them.

inf85.4A Step of the Walk Inside the Step

Let a passage hold m worked examples, each a list xᵢ with its answer yᵢ, and then a new list x with no answer. A reader who knew ∞9 would fit a straight function w·x to the examples by the walk downhill on f(w) = ½Σ(w·xᵢ − yᵢ)², and answer w·x.

One-step technique: one step of size η of the walk from w = 0 gives the answer η·Σᵢ (xᵢ·x)·yᵢ: an attention step with scores xᵢ·x and answers yᵢ, summed with no soft weighing.

\nabla f(0) = -\textstyle\sum_i y_i\,x_i @@ the wide-grid technique (∞9): Xᵀ(X·0 − y)
w_1 = 0 - \eta\,\nabla f(0) = \eta \textstyle\sum_i y_i\,x_i @@ one step downhill
\htmlClass{new}{w_1 \cdot x = \eta \textstyle\sum_i (x_i \cdot x)\,y_i} \quad \blacksquare @@ the dot spreads over a sum

Each example is scored by its overlap with the question, the form of the last footing with M = I, and the answers are added up by score. So an attention step, with the scores used as weights directly, can learn from the examples in its own passage, with no knob changed: the knobs were set once, in training, to make the step a learner. In 2023 this setting of the knobs was written down, and small machines made only of attention steps, trained on such passages, were found to end at it or near it. Whether the large text machines learn from the examples in a passage in this way is not known.

inf85.5What Is Known About the Step

  • The step, many of them side by side, and the marks of place were laid out in 2017.
  • The square in the cost is hard to avoid. In 2023 it was proved that if the scores may be large, no method much faster than T² gives the step even nearly, unless a conjecture on the time that logic puzzles need (41) is false; and that if the scores are kept small, a method of nearly T steps exists.
  • In 2023 the setting of the knobs that makes one step of the walk was given, and trained machines were found near it.
  • Also in 2023 it was proved for one attention step without the soft weighing that training by the walk downhill reaches a lowest point of the error, and that the step so trained fits straight functions to the examples in its passage.

The machines in use stack many steps with other layers between, and for those none of these proofs holds. What a trained stack computes is studied by taking it apart, one step at a time, and reading its grids.

Open questions

These are open: the field is still asking them. There is no key; a good attempt is a reason, a guess, or a better question.

∞十.oq.1Is the square in the cost needed? The proof of 2023 rests on a conjecture, and it speaks of the worst passages. Is there a proof without the conjecture, and can the passages that arise in use be attended to faster with nothing lost?

A short history · told from more than one side

How we got here

Soft weighing over the tokens of a passage was first used in 2014, to help a machine that translated. In 2017 a machine was built from the attention step with almost nothing else.

In 2020 the step was read as a memory lookup, as in ∞ח‎.

In 2023 two threads met on this page: proofs that the square in the cost is hard to avoid, and the finding that a step can carry out the walk downhill on the examples it is shown.

The accepted names

The step is self-attention, in full scaled dot-product attention; the questions, finders and answers are queries, keys and values, and A, B, C are the query, key and value matrices. The soft weighing is the softmax, and W the attention matrix. Steps side by side are heads, and the whole is multi-head attention, the core of the transformer. Order-blind is permutation equivariance; a shuffle grid is a permutation matrix; the marks of place are positional encodings. The form xᵀMy is a bilinear form and ABᵀ a low-rank factorization; the freedom G is a gauge, or reparameterization, symmetry. Attention with no soft weighing is linear attention. Learning from the examples in a passage is in-context learning, and a trained machine that runs a learner inside is a mesa-optimizer. The conjecture is the strong exponential time hypothesis. Reading a trained machine’s grids is mechanistic interpretability.

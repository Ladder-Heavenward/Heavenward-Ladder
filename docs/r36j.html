Where it keys in

The rung spent six rungs of mathematics on three numbers. This step says, one footing per rung, what each of 30 to 35 gave and what colour gave back, and ends with the rung’s last technique, the one its name is about.

One footing for each of rungs 30 to 35: what each gave, and what colour gave back. Then the rung’s last technique, the one its name is about.

36j1Rung 30

30 gave chance as a size and guessing as a technique. The three curves of 36b3 were drawn by matching: an observer sets three lamps until a mix looks the same as a test light, many observers, many lights, and the curve is 30r’s most likely parameter with 30q’s width around it. 30s says when two observers’ matches differ for real and when by chance. And 30w gave the rung its account of itself: a fact by cases enters a column with its rate (36g3), which is 30w1’s axiom under every line read for colour. Colour gave back the cleanest matching experiment there is, one where the residual has a known direction: a colour outside the gamut is named by the nearest point of the triangle, and the error always points outward (36f4), which 30r’s residuals do not do in general.

36j2Rung 31

31 gave functions lengths and angles: two functions have a dot product, a total of their product across the interval, and so an angle between them. The whole rung stands on it. The three numbers of a spectrum are its dot products with the three curves (36b3); col keeps adding and stretching because dot products do (36b3); and metamers exist because the space of spectra has endlessly many directions and col reads three of them, so two spectra that differ only in a direction at right angles to all three curves land on one triple (36c1). Colour gave back the plainest example of a projection that throws away almost everything and keeps enough to use.

36j3Rung 32

32 gave the sum of turnings: a periodic function is a sum of sines and cosines, with a coefficient for each, found as 31’s dot product with that turning. A spectrum is such a function on W (36a1), and a coefficient against a hill (36b3) is a coefficient of 32’s kind with a hill for a turning. Colour gave back a second place where 32’s idea lives: hue is an angle (36d2), so a function of hue is a periodic function of θ, and a hue shift (36d3) is 32’s shift of phase. Anything true of periodic functions is true of functions on the colour circle.

36j4Rung 33

33 gave rates in space and time: the heat equation, where a quantity spreads to the average of its neighbours, and the wave equation, where it swings. A dye dropped in still water spreads by the heat equation with each coordinate of colour in place of temperature, and a blur is the same equation on a grid of places (36i3’s seen mix is one step of it). Light itself is 33’s wave (36b1), and a spectrum is what the wave equation gives when its solutions are sorted by wavelength. Colour gave back a visible maximum principle: a blurred picture never gains a wavelength it did not have, and no amount in it passes the greatest of its neighbours’, because the average of neighbours is never past the greatest of them, and the eye can check it.

36j5Rung 34

34 gave the words for shapes without distances: the loop and the circle are closed curves and bend is a bijection between them that keeps nearness (36d1); the cylinder is a product of a disc and an interval (36e2), pinched at its ends into another closed surface (36e4); G and the gamut are bounded, closed, connected and convex, with a boundary (36f1). Colour gave back a region whose boundary is bowed everywhere but once, and the once is the purples (36f3), and a surface, the pinched cylinder, that a student can hold in the hand as a lump of clay.

36j6Rung 35

35 gave infinities their sizes: the counting numbers are the smallest endless set, the line is larger, and no list reaches it. A colour is a class of spectra (36g2), and the class is uncountable: 36c1’s metamer technique gives one spectrum for every real k, and the reals are 35’s larger size. The set of colours is itself uncountable, as triples of reals are. And the three numbers are the blindness measured: endlessly many directions in (36j2), three out, and 35 says the “endlessly many” is the line’s size, not the counting numbers’. Colour gave back a case where a student can see the uncountable collapsed to the finite and know exactly what was lost.

36j7Spectra remain true even when dimension is reduced

The rung’s last technique, and what its name means. col sent a spectrum with endlessly many coefficients to three numbers, and the sending was many-to-one: the eye is blind to every coefficient but three. Nothing about the spectrum changed in the sending.

Reduction technique: every statement about a spectrum that was true before col was applied is true after. Proof: col is a function from spectra to triples (9a: a function is its pairs), and a function does not alter its inputs; the spectrum S is the same object whether or not col(S) has been written down. So two lights that land on one triple are still two functions (36c1), a prism still spreads them differently (36·phys), a filter still scales them differently, and their energies still differ (36b4). What the reduction loses is the eye’s ability to tell them apart, not the facts that tell them apart; and the same holds one level down, where the mix forgets the pair and the picture remains (36i2).

The rung’s name is this technique said as a warning. Colouring the blindness in does not cure it. A student who works in the three numbers, the circle or the cylinder must remember that every point stands for a crowd of spectra, and every mix for a crowd of pairs, and that the crowd is still there, exactly as it was, behind the number that replaced it.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

The three curves were found as a maximum-likelihood fit to colour-matching data, with confidence bands for their widths. The dot product of 31 is the inner product, and col is a projection, or a linear map of rank three, from an infinite-dimensional space; metamers differ by a vector in its kernel, the null space. The heat equation applied to a picture is Gaussian blur. The reduction technique is the plain fact that applying a function leaves its argument unchanged; reducing a dataset to fewer coordinates is called dimensionality reduction.

A proof that is not allowed

“Two lights land on one triple. A colour is its triple. So the two lights are the same.” Not allowed: col is many-to-one, so one triple does not fix one spectrum (36c1), and the reduction technique keeps the two spectra apart however they look.

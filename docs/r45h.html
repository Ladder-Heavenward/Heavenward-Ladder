45h · proof

Tensor Products

Rung twenty seven read two arrows at once with a grid of numbers: a reading straight in each slot, when the other slot is held still. It is not straight in the pair, since doubling both arrows multiplies it by four. So readings of two arrows are not the linear maps out of the product of the two spaces. This step builds a new space out of which they are exactly the linear maps. Its dimension is the product of the two dimensions, not their sum, and that is the first surprise. The second is that most of its members are not made of one arrow from each space: they are sums of such, and the fewest terms a member needs is the rank of a matrix. The third comes when the numbers are replaced by the members of a ring: the same build, done with the clock of two and the clock of three, gives nothing at all. And to say exactly what goes missing there, the step names a row of spaces and maps in which each map reaches just what the next one loses.

The tensor product of two spaces of arrows is the space through which every reading straight in each slot runs as exactly one linear map. Its dimension is the product of the dimensions; its members are sums of products of one arrow from each space, and one member needs as many terms as the rank of its grid. Over the whole numbers, the clock of two against the clock of three gives nothing. A row of maps is exact when each one reaches just what the next one loses.

45h1Straight in Each Slot

Let V and W be spaces of arrows over a field k, of dimensions m and n, with bases e₁, …, e_m and f₁, …, f_n. A map β from V × W to a space U is straight in each slot when it is straight in v with w held, and in w with v held, as 27h2’s readings were. A tensor product of V and W is a space T with a map ⊗ : V × W → T, straight in each slot, such that every β straight in each slot runs through it as exactly one linear map β̄ : T → U with β̄(v ⊗ w) = β(v, w). By 45d2’s proof, any two are matched in exactly one way that agrees with ⊗; write V ⊗ W.

Grid-space technique: the space with basis the mn symbols eᵢ ⊗ f_j, and v ⊗ w = Σ vᵢ w_j eᵢ ⊗ f_j, is a tensor product of V and W, so V ⊗ W has dimension mn.

T = \text{lists of } mn \text{ numbers, entry } (i, j) \text{ the count of } e_i \otimes f_j @@ a space of dimension mn, the grid of 27d read as one list
v \otimes w = \textstyle\sum_{i,j} v_i\, w_j\; e_i \otimes f_j @@ the grid v wᵀ: straight in v with w held, and in w with v held
\bar\beta(e_i \otimes f_j) = \beta(e_i, f_j) @@ a linear map is fixed by where a basis goes, and it may go anywhere (27d1)
\bar\beta(v \otimes w) = \textstyle\sum v_i w_j\, \beta(e_i, f_j) = \beta(v, w) @@ the two-slot technique’s spreading, one slot at a time (27h2)
\bar\beta' \text{ with } \bar\beta'(v \otimes w) = \beta(v, w) \Rightarrow \bar\beta' = \bar\beta \text{ on each } e_i \otimes f_j @@ take v = eᵢ, w = f_j
\htmlClass{new}{V \otimes W \text{ exists, with } \dim = mn} \quad \blacksquare @@ the basis is mn symbols

ℝ² × ℝ³ has dimension 5, as a product in Vect (45d1, the pairs added entry by entry). ℝ² ⊗ ℝ³ has dimension 6. With U = k, the tensor product says: readings straight in each slot are the readings of V ⊗ W. That is 27h2’s grid G, now one reading of a space of dimension mn.

45h2Not Every Member Is One Product

Read a member t = Σ tᵢⱼ eᵢ ⊗ f_j as the m by n grid of its counts. Then v ⊗ w is the grid v wᵀ, every row a multiple of wᵀ. Its rank, the dimension of what the grid reaches (27c), is at most 1.

Fewest-terms technique: a member of V ⊗ W is a sum of r terms v ⊗ w, and of no fewer, exactly when its grid has rank r.

t = \textstyle\sum_{s=1}^{r} v_s \otimes w_s \Rightarrow \text{grid } \textstyle\sum v_s w_s^{\mathsf T} @@ the grid of a sum is the sum of the grids
\text{what it reaches lies in the span of } v_1, \ldots, v_r,\ \text{so rank} \le r @@ each v_s w_sᵀ x is a multiple of v_s
\text{rank } r:\ \text{columns } c_1, \ldots, c_n \text{ lie in a span of } r \text{ arrows } v_1, \ldots, v_r @@ a basis of what the grid reaches (27b)
c_j = \textstyle\sum_s b_{sj} v_s \Rightarrow \text{grid} = \textstyle\sum_s v_s\, b_s^{\mathsf T} @@ b_s the row of the counts bₛⱼ
\htmlClass{new}{t = \textstyle\sum_{s=1}^{r} v_s \otimes w_s \text{ with } r = \text{rank, and no fewer}} \quad \blacksquare @@ w_s the arrow with entries bₛⱼ; the line before gives no fewer

e₁ ⊗ f₁ + e₂ ⊗ f₂ in ℝ² ⊗ ℝ² has the grid with rows (1, 0) and (0, 1), of rank 2, so it is no v ⊗ w. The same sum, of rank 2, is the joint state of two particles that 40·phys could not split into one state for each: how two systems combine in physics is this tensor product, and the members of rank above 1 are the ones the field calls entangled.

45h3Over a Ring, Things Go Missing

43i’s modules over a ring R add and are stretched by members of R as spaces of arrows are by a field. Straight in each slot, and the tensor product, mean what they meant in 45h1, with R in place of k. A tensor product over R exists for any two modules: take a sum of copies of R, one for each pair (v, w), and force the rules straight in each slot by 45e2’s classes. But there may be no basis to count with, and things go missing.

Coprime-clocks technique: over the whole numbers, the clock of two tensored with the clock of three is nothing.

g \otimes h \in \mathbb Z/2 \otimes \mathbb Z/3 @@ g from the clock of 2, h from the clock of 3 (42)
g \otimes h = 3(g \otimes h) - 2(g \otimes h) @@ 3 − 2 = 1
3(g \otimes h) = g \otimes (3h) = g \otimes 0 = 0 @@ straight in each slot: stretch the second side; 3h = 0 on the clock of 3
2(g \otimes h) = (2g) \otimes h = 0 \otimes h = 0 @@ 2g = 0 on the clock of 2
\htmlClass{new}{g \otimes h = 0 - 0 = 0,\ \text{so } \mathbb Z/2 \otimes \mathbb Z/3 = 0} \quad \blacksquare @@ every member is a sum of such g ⊗ h

A row of maps A → B → C is exact at B when what the first reaches is exactly what the second loses. 0 → V → W → W/V → 0, a part V of W and its classes (45e2), is exact at every place. By 27c2’s lost plus reached, in such a row of spaces of finite dimension dim W = dim V + dim W/V. Now take 0 → ℤ → ℤ → ℤ/2 → 0, the first map doubling: it is exact. Tensor each place with ℤ/2. Doubling becomes 0 on the clock of 2, and the first map, once one-to-one, now loses everything. Tensoring keeps a row exact at its end and can break it at its start; how much breaks is measured much higher up the ladder.

The accepted names

A map straight in each slot is bilinear, and the tensor product V ⊗ W represents bilinear maps; the grid-space technique gives the basis eᵢ ⊗ f_j. A member v ⊗ w is a pure, simple or decomposable tensor, and the fewest-terms number is the tensor rank, which for two factors is matrix rank. The matching of 45h.5.1 is the tensor–hom adjunction. ℤ/m ⊗ ℤ/n = ℤ/gcd(m, n). An exact sequence 0 → A → B → C → 0 is short exact; tensoring is right exact and not left exact, and what it fails to keep is measured by Tor, the start of homological algebra. In quantum mechanics the state space of a joint system is the tensor product of the two, and states of rank above 1 are entangled.

Who said it first

Tensors in coordinates came from Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and Tullio Levi-Civita (1900). Hassler Whitney defined the tensor product of abelian groups by its property in 1938. Henri Cartan and Samuel Eilenberg’s Homological Algebra (1956) made exact sequences and Tor standard. William Lawvere proposed in 1964 to found mathematics on the category of sets described by arrows alone (the elementary theory of the category of sets), and in 1966 on the category of categories; Ernst Zermelo’s axioms of 1908 are the ladder’s rung 2. Norman Steenrod is said to have coined “general abstract nonsense”, said fondly.

A proof that is not allowed

“ℤ/2 ⊗ ℤ/3 is a product of two non-empty sets, so it has six members.” Not allowed: the tensor product is not the product. Its members are sums of g ⊗ h, the rules straight in each slot are forced on them, and on these two clocks the rules force every g ⊗ h to be 0.

16j · technique

How Close Is a Strip Sum?

Most areas cannot be found by running the table backwards: e^(−x²) has no antiderivative among the functions of this ladder. A machine adds strips instead. Topping each strip with a slanted line, not a flat one, makes the sum far better, and how much better can be proved: the error is fenced by the bend of the curve and the square of the strip width. This step proves the fence for one strip by 16e’s parts, adds it over all strips, and checks it on x², where the fence is met exactly.

On one strip of width h, the slanted top misses the area by exactly the integral of t(h − t)/2 times f″, so by at most M h³/12, where |f″| ≤ M. Over n strips across [a, b], at most (b − a) M h²/12: halve the width and the error falls to a quarter.

16j1Slanted Tops

Split [a, b] into n strips of width h = (b − a)/n. Top each strip with the straight line joining the curve at its two ends: each strip is then a trapezium.

\text{one strip: } \tfrac{h}{2}\big(f(x_{i}) + f(x_{i+1})\big) @@ average height times width
T_n = h\Big(\tfrac12 f(a) + f(x_1) + \cdots + f(x_{n-1}) + \tfrac12 f(b)\Big) @@ inside ends counted twice, halved
\int_0^1 x^2\,dx,\ n = 4:\ T_4 = \tfrac14\big(\tfrac12 \cdot 0 + \tfrac1{16} + \tfrac4{16} + \tfrac9{16} + \tfrac12 \cdot 1\big) = 0.34375 @@ against 1/3
\htmlClass{new}{\text{error} = 0.34375 - 0.33333 = \tfrac{1}{96}} @@ the curve bends up, so the tops sit above

From here on, Tn is the slanted-top sum with n strips.

16j2One Strip, Exactly

One-strip technique: if f″ is continuous on [0, h], then ∫₀ʰ f − (h/2)(f(0) + f(h)) = −∫₀ʰ w f″, where w(t) = t(h − t)/2.

w(0) = w(h) = 0,\ \ w^{\prime} = \tfrac h2 - t,\ \ w^{\prime\prime} = -1 @@ a bump on the strip
\int_0^h w f^{\prime\prime} = \big[w f^{\prime}\big]_0^h - \int_0^h w^{\prime} f^{\prime} = -\int_0^h (\tfrac h2 - t) f^{\prime} @@ parts (16e); the bracket is 0
= -\big[(\tfrac h2 - t) f\big]_0^h - \int_0^h f = \tfrac h2\big(f(0) + f(h)\big) - \int_0^h f @@ parts again
\htmlClass{new}{\int_0^h f - \tfrac h2\big(f(0)+f(h)\big) = -\int_0^h w f^{\prime\prime}} \quad \blacksquare @@ turn the line round

16j3The Fence

|f^{\prime\prime}| \le M \text{ on } [a, b] @@ a bound on the bend
\Big|\int_0^h w f^{\prime\prime}\Big| \le M \int_0^h \tfrac{t(h-t)}{2}\,dt = \dfrac{M h^3}{12} @@ w ≥ 0; ∫ t(h − t) = h³/6
n \text{ strips: } |T_n - \textstyle\int_a^b f| \le n \cdot \dfrac{M h^3}{12} = \dfrac{(b-a) M h^2}{12} @@ nh = b − a
\htmlClass{new}{x^2 \text{ on } [0,1],\ n = 4:\ \tfrac{1 \cdot 2 \cdot (1/4)^2}{12} = \tfrac{1}{96}} \quad \blacksquare @@ met exactly: f″ is the constant 2

Slanted-top technique: with |f″| ≤ M on [a, b], the slanted-top sum with strips of width h is within (b − a)M h²/12 of the area. The error goes as h²: ten times the strips, a hundredth of the error. Flat tops do only as well as h.

16j4A Curve With No Antiderivative

f^{\prime\prime} = (4x^2 - 2)e^{-x^2},\ \ |f^{\prime\prime}| \le 2 \text{ on } [0,1] @@ largest at x = 0
\dfrac{1 \cdot 2 \cdot h^2}{12} \le 5 \times 10^{-6} \iff h \le 0.0055 @@ solve the fence
n = 183 \text{ strips suffice} @@ h = 1/183
\htmlClass{new}{T_{183} \approx 0.74682} @@ good to the fifth place, guaranteed

The fence says in advance how many strips buy a given accuracy, which is what a machine needs: it cannot look at the answer to see whether it is good enough. Bending the tops into pieces of parabola, three heights per pair of strips, raises the power from h² to h⁴.

Accepted names

The slanted-top sum is the trapezoidal rule; its fence is the error bound (b − a)h²max|f″|/12. Parabola pieces give Simpson’s rule, with error (b − a)h⁴max|f⁗|/180. Computing integrals by sums is numerical quadrature.

Who said it first

Thomas Simpson published his rule in 1743, though Cavalieri and Kepler used it earlier; error fences of this kind were worked out in the nineteenth century, by Peano among others.

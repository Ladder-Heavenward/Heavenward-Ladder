36g · proof

A Colour Can Be a Name

Serena is s, and s is a name. Anger is red, and so the patch 🟥 is a name. This step says what that means with 0a’s and 0b’s care, and finds that a colour is three things at once, each resting on the one before: a name, in both of 0a’s meanings, the colour itself and not only its word; a set, the class of every spectrum that lands on one triple, with the triple as the class’s name; and an inductive object, as the number 7 is one and as a pigment in a jar is one, subject to induction over the arrangement and to induction over cases alike. The chemistry of the pigment is the mathematics that makes the cases, and the rung says so.

The colour itself is a name, as a letter is. A colour is a set of spectra, and its triple names the set. A colour is an object of both inductions: statements about triples are columns, and statements about pigments are cases with a rate, and a case enters a column as a line with its rate attached.

36g1The Colour as a Name

0a2 gave a letter for a thing: s for Serena. A colour is a name of the same kind, written in light instead of ink. The patch 🟥 stands for something, the way that letter does; the word “red” stands for the same something in letters; and the triple (l, m, s) stands for it in marks. None of the three is the thing. What they name is what the matter at hand says (0b): the light that made the patch, or the feeling the word is used for, or whatever a statement puts in the gap.

From here on, a colour name is any of the three, patch, word or triple, and the sameness between two colour names is 0b’s: they name one thing exactly when the matter at hand makes them. When the matter at hand is light, two patches are the same name exactly when their triples are (36c1). When it is everyday speech, “red” names many triples and the sameness is loose, as “the cat” is.

Use against mention (0a9). “The sky is 🟦” uses the patch as a name; “the patch on the wall is 🟦” mentions it, a statement about the name itself. 0b’s distinction holds for colours as for letters, and the moves 0b withheld (swapping inside a mention) are withheld here.

Name technique: on the matter of light, colour names are the same exactly when their triples are. Proof: 36c1’s sameness relation, and 0b’s sameness on triples by components (2h5).

36g2The Colour as a Set

36c1 said what a colour is when the matter at hand is light: the class of every spectrum with one triple. So a colour is a set, 2’s kind, and its members are spectra. The class is uncountable (36j6), and 2h15 names a class by a representative; here the representative is not a member but the triple every member shares, and the triple is the set’s name as s is Serena’s. The patch 🟥, the word, the triple (l, m, s) and the cylinder point (r, θ, z) are four names for one set (36e5: two charts, one colour).

From here on, S belongs to c, written S ∈ c, exactly when col(S) is c’s triple. 2d12 holds: a spectrum is in the colour, and is not part of it, and the colour is no spectrum. A colour mentioned is a set mentioned; a colour used names its set.

Set technique: for colours c and d, c = d exactly when their triples are equal, and the mix c + d is not the union c ∪ d.

36g3The Colour as an Inductive Object

The number 7 is an inductive object: 4b built it as the next one after 6, 4c made every claim about it a base and a step, and 4i wrote it down, and it was tested by cases, seven stones, seven days, long before any of that. A pigment in a jar is an inductive object in exactly that sense, and its colour is the mathematics of physical chemistry read off through 36b3. Here is the chain. A pigment’s molecules absorb at the wavelengths that match the energy gaps of their bonds (17·phys’s dots, with 36·phys saying why a gap picks a wavelength); light through the pigment is multiplied, wavelength by wavelength, by what is not absorbed (18·chem, how much colour is in the glass); the solvent shifts the gaps a little, by its own bonds to the molecule; and col of the result is the colour in the jar. Every link is a column, and each column ends in a line with a rate, since the gaps are measured to a precision and the solvent’s shift is known to a spread. That is 30w’s induction, recurrence as evidence (30w4): strong evidence with its rate, and the rate is the whole difference from a line without one.

From here on, a colour fact by cases is a statement about which spectrum, and so which colour, a material produces, and it carries its rate: the spread of the cases it was drawn from.

Cases technique: a colour fact by cases enters a column as a line, with its rate attached, and the conclusion of the column carries the rate forward. Proof: 30w1, the axiom under every line, and 30logi5, a promise with a known exception rate, applied line by line; 28p2, shares add, says a column whose every line is exact except one is exact to that one’s rate.

So the rung’s three laid-down lines, the three curves (36b3), the bow of the spike curve (36c3) and the pinch at white (36e4), are not unproved. Each is a column of physical chemistry and measurement that ends in a line with a rate, and the rung cites the line rather than reprinting the column, as 18 cites 8’s arithmetic and 30·then cites the pea counts. “Laid down” on this rung means laid down with its rate, and a student who wants the column can find it in 36·phys.

A table of pigments in one solvent is the small case and the large case together: each jar is one case, the solvent is the step applied to every case, and the rule “this solvent darkens this family” is held at the rate the rows give. Change the solvent and the rate changes; a new pigment is a new case, and whether it fits the rule is a calculation from its bonds first and a look second. Induction by colour is induction, as induction by number is.

Anexact, yet rigorous · two sizes of truth

A fact by cases is true at the size of a crowd: the jars tried, with a spread. A technique is true at the size of one: every triple. Both are truths; the rung marks which size each line lives at, and a student who reads a rate as a doubt, or drops a rate to make a line look exact, has crossed sizes.

36g4Colours Under the Arrangement

A statement about colours is a statement about triples, and a proof about triples is 4a12’s column: each line let in by its reason, and the reasons are 5d’s twelve techniques and 8d’s for the line, applied in each coordinate, then 2b5’s speaking of everything by speaking of anything to cover all three at once. Every technique on this rung was proved that way, and the proof is anexact, since it needs no colour to exist, and rigorous, since the same column carries the real case.

Component technique: a statement of the form “for all colours c, P(c)” where P is built from the coordinates by 5’s arithmetic is proved by proving it for each coordinate and joining with and. Proof: 2h5’s equal pairs, twice, and 1c2’s join.

So colours are subject to deduction exactly as numbers are, and nothing about their being seen enters a column except as a line with a rate (36g3). Above those lines everything is arrangement.

The accepted names

Use against mention keeps its accepted name. The triple as the name of a class is a representative, or here a label, of an equivalence class. A colour fact by cases with its rate is an empirical law with its uncertainty; a table of pigments in solvents shows solvatochromism. Proving a statement about triples coordinate by coordinate is a componentwise proof.

Who said it first

The difference between using a name and mentioning it was set out with care by logicians of the twentieth century, and the quotation marks that keep it are a convention of their writing. That a dye’s colour moves with its solvent was studied in the early twentieth century, and the word solvatochromism came later.

A proof that is not allowed

“Cobalt blue in oil darkened in every jar we tried. So every blue pigment darkens in oil, and that line is as sure as 2 + 2 = 4.” Not allowed: the first line is a fact by cases with a rate drawn from the jars tried, and the cases technique carries the rate forward. Dropping it crosses sizes.

Thinking Logically

The Ladder is not alien to you. It is what makes your language work. The only thing you need to climb it is the promise that everything you can say or sense has a home on the ladder. So the only real hurdle is finding out how the writing system turns everyday speech and observation into its funny little symbols. One foot in front of the other, one step at a time.
This is the floor the whole ladder stands on. There is nothing beneath it: no earlier rung, no school, no book you are expected to have read. What is here is the English language, the letters of the alphabet, and a very small claim that nobody can seriously deny: a thing is the same as itself. Add the plain fact that what is true of a thing is true of what is the same as it. From those two, every rung above this one will be built, with nothing smuggled in. This page says in words what the claim means and what it lets you do. The steps do the work by hand.

A thing is the same as itself. Whatever may be said of a thing may be said of anything that is the same as it. So a fact may be carried from a name to another name of the same thing, and from nothing else.

What this ladder is

The raw material

If nothing may appear before it is built, what is there to build with on the ground floor? The English language, and the letters of the alphabet. The ladder trusts your English completely.

Stare too long at any part of the language and it starts to melt. For that reason the ladder says “so” a lot. It is a gesture: a vague waggling of the hands toward something that exists. “Not so” is a stiff shrug in the face of something that isn't seen to exist.

Rung 0 shows how words and statements turn into logical forms, and gives a few techniques for manipulating the logic.

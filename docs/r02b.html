2b · proof

Why Gather?

Before the rung builds a single mark for collections, it asks why anyone would want one. Things come in groups, and groups sit inside groups. People do their work in groups: a team, a crew, a working group. Most of what is said at work is said about a group, not about one person. This step looks at a cup of water and at a team on a project, and finds in each the questions the rest of the rung gives tools for.

A group is one thing with members, and a claim can be about the group or about each member. Each thing that happens in a group needs a mark to bring it into the language, and the rung builds them.

2b1Inside a Cup of Water

A cup of water contains molecules of water, but even the molecules of water are a set containing (oxygen, hydrogens). And the hydrogens are a group of their own: there are two of them. So the cup holds molecules, a molecule holds an oxygen and its hydrogens, and the hydrogens hold one hydrogen and another.

Each group is one thing. You can point at the cup, at one molecule, at the pair of hydrogens. Each is also a gathering of the things in it. A group can be a member of a bigger group, and that is what lets groups sit inside groups.

What is true of the cup need not be true of a molecule in it. The water in the cup is cold, and it pours. A molecule is not cold, and it does not pour. Keeping a claim about the group apart from a claim about each member is half of what this rung is for.

2b2A Team for a Project

Joy leads a project at work: a new footbridge over a creek. Her team is Evita, Sarah, Claire, Randy, Jean, Terry and Mike. The team is one thing. It has a name on the plan, it meets on Mondays, and it can be thanked as a whole. It is also nothing but its members. Swap Mike for someone else and it is a different team, even if it keeps its name.

Now listen to what gets said at the Monday meeting.

  • “Everyone on the team has done the safety course.”
  • “Someone on the team can drive the truck.”
  • “The Tuesday crew is Terry, Jean and Evita.”
  • “We need the people on the team who can weld.”

The first is a claim about every member. The second asks for one example. The third picks a smaller group out of the team, and every member of it is on the team. The fourth sorts the team by a condition and keeps the ones who pass.

2b3Each Case Needs a Mark

Go back through the cup and the team, one case at a time. Each case is something that happens. So far the language of the ladder has no way to write it. Each one needs a mark to bring it into the language.

A thing is in a group. The molecule is in the cup. The oxygen is in the molecule. Mike is on the team. This case exists, so the language needs a mark for “is in”:

A group is told by what is in it. The molecule is the oxygen and the hydrogens. The team is its eight people. This case exists, so the language needs a mark that gathers names into one thing. The mark is a pair of braces:

A group is in a group. The hydrogens are a group, and that group is in the molecule. Braces go inside braces. One hydrogen is in the hydrogens, and it is not one of the two things the molecule lists. This case exists, so the language needs a mark for “is not in”:

Two groups are the same group, or not. Take Mike off the list and it is a different team. 0b gave = to two names for one thing. A team is a thing, so = already applies to it. What this case needs is a rule that says when = holds between groups: when they have the same members.

A group is part of a group. The Tuesday crew is Terry, Jean and Evita. Call it u. The crew is not a member of the team: the team’s members are people. Everyone on the crew is on the team. This case exists, and ∈ cannot write it, so the language needs a second mark, for “is part of”:

A claim is made of every member. “Everyone on the team has done the safety course.” Let S be 0a’s statement with a gap, “() has done the safety course”. S(Mike) says it of Mike. Nothing yet says it of everyone at once. This case exists, so the language needs a mark for “every”:

A claim is made of some member. “Someone on the team can drive the truck.” Let D be “() can drive the truck”. The line does not say who. This case exists, so the language needs a mark for “some”:

A group is made by a condition. “The people on the team who can weld.” Let W be “() can weld”. Nobody has listed the welders. The condition sifts them out of the team: it lets through the ones who pass and holds back the rest. This case exists, so the language needs a way to write a group by its condition, called a sift. Eight cases, and each brought a mark with it: ∈, the braces, ∉, a rule for =, ⊆, ∀, ∃ and the colon of the sift. You do not have to write them yet. Every mark here is met again in the steps ahead, with the techs that say what may be done with it.

2b4Moves to Watch Out For

Each line below is a move that looks right and is not. The tag says what kind of mistake it is.

  • “The hydrogens are in the molecule, and the molecule is in the cup, so the hydrogens are among the things in the cup.” outside the plan the cup’s members are molecules. A member of a member is not, in general, a member.
  • “The team is pleased with the bridge, so Mike is pleased.” outside the collect a claim about the group, not about each member.
  • “Someone on the team can weld, so not everyone can.” outside the plan some asks for one example and never hints “but not all”.
  • “The Tuesday crew is a team apart from the team.” outside the plan every member of the crew is on the team. The crew is part of the team.

9h · technique

Steps that add, steps that multiply

A function on the counting numbers can be built step by step, as 4c’s definitions by repeating built them: a start, and a step. Two kinds of step are simpler than all the others. In one, each step adds the same amount: a salary that rises by a fixed sum each year, a stack of bricks laid one course at a time. In the other, each step multiplies by the same factor: money at interest, a culture of cells dividing, the energy passed from one feeding level of a food web to the next. This step gives each kind its formula and tells them apart from a table. It builds either one from two readings, and adds up a run of steps that multiply.

Steps that add: each term is the start plus the step added that many times. Steps that multiply: each term is the start times the factor that many times over. Tell them apart by differences and by ratios. A percent change per step is a factor of one plus the percent. And a run of steps that multiply adds up by one short formula, whenever the factor is not 1.

9h1Two kinds of step

Stands on 4b (induction), 4c (recursion) and 8i (powers).

Steps-that-add technique: if f : ℕ → ℝ has f(n + 1) = f(n) + d for every n, then f(n) = f(0) + nd for every n.

Steps-that-multiply technique: if f : ℕ → ℝ has f(n + 1) = b · f(n) for every n, then f(n) = f(0)bⁿ for every n.

f(0) = f(0) + 0 \cdot d,\ \ f(0) = f(0)\,b^0 @@ the base case; b⁰ = 1 (8i)
f(n) = f(0) + nd \Rightarrow f(n + 1) = f(0) + nd + d = f(0) + (n + 1)d @@ the step, for steps-that-add
f(n) = f(0)b^n \Rightarrow f(n + 1) = b\,f(0)b^n = f(0)b^{n+1} @@ the step, for steps-that-multiply; whole-exponent techniques (8i)
\htmlClass{new}{f(n) = f(0) + nd,\ \ f(n) = f(0)\,b^n} \quad \blacksquare @@ induction (4b)

A run with steps that add has the accepted name arithmetic sequence, and d is its common difference. A run with steps that multiply is a geometric sequence, and b is its common ratio. In a model, f(0) is the starting amount; d is the change per step; b is the factor per step.

n:\ 0, 1, 2, 3 \quad f:\ 40, 52, 64, 76 @@ differences 12, 12, 12: steps that add, d = 12
n:\ 0, 1, 2, 3 \quad g:\ 40, 60, 90, 135 @@ ratios 1.5, 1.5, 1.5: steps that multiply, b = 1.5
n:\ 0, 1, 2, 3 \quad h:\ 40, 50, 70, 100 @@ neither: differences 10, 20, 30, ratios 1.25, 1.4, 1.43

9h2Percent per step

Stands on 9h1 and 5g (percent).

A change of r per step, written as a percent, multiplies by 1 + r: a 7% rise is a factor 1.07, a 20% fall is a factor 0.8. Interest of r a year can be added m times a year, each time on the amount so far. That has the accepted name compound interest: after t years, P(1 + r/m)mt.

\$1000 \text{ at } 5\% \text{ a year, } 10 \text{ years}:\ \ 1000 \cdot 1.05^{10} \approx \$1628.89 @@ steps-that-multiply with b = 1.05
\text{added monthly}:\ \ 1000\,(1 + \tfrac{0.05}{12})^{120} \approx \$1647.01 @@ 120 steps, each a factor 1 + 0.05/12
100 \cdot 1.07^{n} \ge 200:\ \ n = 11 @@ try n: 1.07¹⁰ ≈ 1.967, 1.07¹¹ ≈ 2.105
-20\%\text{ then }+20\% \Rightarrow 0.8 \cdot 1.2 = 0.96 @@ factors multiply; a 4% loss overall

9h4Adding up steps that multiply

Stands on 9h1 and 4j (Σ).

Ratio-sum technique: for b ≠ 1 and whole n ≥ 1, Σ_{i<n} bⁱ = 1 + b + ⋯ + bⁿ⁻¹ = (bⁿ − 1)/(b − 1).

S = 1 + b + \cdots + b^{n-1} @@ 4j's Σ
bS = b + b^2 + \cdots + b^n @@ each term times b
bS - S = b^n - 1 @@ every term but bⁿ and 1 appears in both and cancels
\htmlClass{new}{S = \tfrac{b^n - 1}{b - 1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ b ≠ 1, divide (5g)
  
1 + 2 + 4 + \cdots + 2^{63} = 2^{64} - 1 \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{19} @@ grains on a chessboard, one then doubling on each square
1 + \tfrac12 + \cdots + \tfrac{1}{2^9} = \tfrac{1 - 2^{-10}}{1 - \tfrac12} \approx 1.998 @@ ratio-sum with b = ½

9h5Energy up a food web

Stands on 9h1 and 9h2.

Plants fix energy from sunlight; the total is gross primary production, GPP. They spend some of it living, R. What is left, NPP = GPP − R, is net primary production, the energy the next feeding level can eat. These amounts are counted in kilojoules per square metre a year, kJ/m²: a kilojoule is a fixed amount of energy, and this step only compares such amounts, so their ratios are all it uses. Each level up a food chain keeps only a fraction of the energy of the level below, often about a tenth. That fraction has the accepted name the 10% rule. A poison that is stored and not shed does the opposite: each level up concentrates it by a factor. Its accepted name is biomagnification. Its amount is counted in parts per million, ppm: how many parts of the poison in a million parts of the living tissue, by weight.

\text{GPP} = 20\,000,\ R = 12\,000 \Rightarrow \text{NPP} = 8000 \text{ kJ/m}^2 \text{ a year} @@ a salt marsh near Tomales Bay, a model
E(k) = 8000 \cdot 0.1^k @@ steps-that-multiply with b = 0.1; level 0 the plants
E(3) = 8 \text{ kJ/m}^2 @@ a fourth level gets a thousandth
c(k) = 0.002 \cdot 10^k \text{ ppm} @@ a stored poison, a factor 10 per level; c(4) = 20 ppm

An early ecologist described a lake as a chain of feeding levels, each taking its energy from the one below. He found that each level used only a small part of what the level below it had gathered, with most lost at every step.

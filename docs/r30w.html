Inductive Axioms Revisited

Thirty rungs ago the ladder laid down a handful of axioms about sameness, and proved its first techniques from them one line at a time. The lines kept the reason for every move written beside it. By rung fourteen the swap axiom had stopped being named, because it was under every line. It had not gone away. It had become the ground. This step looks back down the climb and counts what kept coming back: the same few axioms and moves, run again and again, each time on new things. It asks what that recurrence is evidence of. The answer is the last kind of guessing on the floor. A move that has held in every case across thirty rungs, in fields as far apart as tides and family names, is a claim about a crowd of cases that no one can predict one at a time. That is induction under the large case, and it is what supports the eight principles the green boxes have carried since rung zero.

The swap axiom and self-sameness stand under every rung, and the same few moves recur as iterations of themselves. That recurrence, counted across the climb, is evidence of the kind statistics weighs, and it is what supports the eight anexact, yet rigorous principles.

30w1The Axiom Under Every Line

0b laid down the swap axiom: a statement about x holds just as well with b’s name where x’s stood, once x = b. Rungs 0 to 13 cite it by name more than three hundred times. From rung 14 on, it is not named at all.

It is still there. Every line whose reason is “the line before”, “put in”, or “by 15c” writes one name where an equal one stood. The technique cited stands on the swap axiom. Here is a line of 30j, with the hidden moves written out.

I^2 = \textstyle\int\!\!\int e^{-x^2 - y^2}\,dx\,dy @@ the line as 30j1 wrote it, citing either-order (30e3)
I = \textstyle\int e^{-x^2}\,dx,\ \text{so } I \cdot I = \big(\int e^{-x^2}dx\big)\big(\int e^{-y^2}dy\big) @@ the name I swapped for what it names, twice; the letter of a total is a placeholder
\big(\textstyle\int e^{-x^2}dx\big)\big(\int e^{-y^2}dy\big) = \int\!\!\int e^{-x^2}e^{-y^2}\,dx\,dy @@ either-order, on f(x, y) = e^(−x²) e^(−y²)
e^{-x^2}e^{-y^2} = e^{-x^2 - y^2} @@ powers add (4g), swapped in under the total
\htmlClass{new}{I^2 = \textstyle\int\!\!\int e^{-x^2 - y^2}\,dx\,dy} @@ the chain technique joins the three; each join is a swap

One line, four swaps. The axiom licenses all the techniques above it, and no single one of them in particular. That is why the ladder stopped naming it: an axiom that stands under everything tells you nothing about which line you are on.

30w2A = a, the Measure of Success

0b also laid down self-sameness: every thing is itself, a = a. Lines of the form x = x appear on 27 of the 31 rungs from 0 to 30. They are not idle. In the series, a = a is the measure of success and failure.

An equation is a question asked within the series: equal or not? Every check ends one of two ways. Put the answer back, work both sides, and arrive at a = a: success. Or arrive at a = b for two numbers that differ, which self-sameness and the order of the numbers refuse: failure.

P(+) = 0.99 \cdot 0.001 + 0.02 \cdot 0.999 = 0.02097 @@ 30f3, worked forward
0.99 \cdot 0.001 + 0.02 \cdot 0.999 = 0.00099 + 0.01998 = 0.02097 @@ the check, worked again from the other side
0.02097 = 0.02097 @@ success: self-sameness
1 \le \textstyle\sum m(V) = 0 @@ 30a4, in the case m(V) = 0
1 \le 0 @@ failure: the case is refused
\htmlClass{new}{\text{every check ends at } a = a \text{ or at its refusal}} @@ the yardstick of the series

So self-sameness is not a fact anyone checks. It is the ruler every check is laid against, and every proof by refusal ends at a line it will not allow.

30w3Moves That Iterate Themselves

Some techniques recur on rung after rung as the same move run again on its own output. 9·cb4 called this iteration: g, then g of that, then g of that.

  • The chain. a = b and b = c give a = c. Every multi-line proof on the ladder is the chain run once per line.
  • The next case. 4c’s induction over the series proves a claim for n + 1 from n. It returns on 22a, 23j, 24b and 30i4, where qₙ = G(qₙ₋₁) is the next case of a list built by its own technique.
  • Halving and the least upper bound. 8c fenced a number between two lists that close in. The same move returns in 12’s in-between values, 15’s lower and upper sums, 22a’s climb under a ceiling, and 30a’s outer size.
  • Slack shared out. ε split as ε/2 + ε/2, or ε/2ⁿ over endless pieces, from 10c to 30a2’s covers.

Each recurrence is the same move, unchanged, applied to new things. The move does not grow. What grows is what it is applied to.

30w4Recurrence as Evidence

The eight anexact, yet rigorous principles were never proved. They are guesses from things, offered beside the proofs: the ledger, two sizes of truth, loose then locked, counting and the triangle, laws with a known exception rate, connections not families, the middle of a change, and the cloud. What supports them?

Their recurrence does. Each principle rides on a move the climb keeps making, and each turned up again, unchanged, in field after field the annexes visited. On rung 30 each became part of a technique.

  • The ledger (1a) rides on the swap: nothing changes when equals are swapped, so the books balance. On 30b it is countable adding.
  • Two sizes of truth (2b) rides on every and some. On 30c it is a claim true except on a set of size 0.
  • Loose, Then Locked (2f) rides on the same-members axiom. On 30d it is the fence that locks a list of totals.
  • Counting, and the triangle (4j, 21h) rides on the next case and on perpendicular parts. On 30g it is spreads that add.
  • Laws with a known exception rate (5e) rides on counting the exceptions. On 30f it is chance itself.
  • Connections, not families (9a) rides on functions as connections. On 30i it is a law known by its chance function.
  • The middle of a change (13a) rides on in-between values. On 30j it is the bell.
  • The cloud (21a) rides on fences that close in. On 30h it is an average with its width.

Now read that list the way 30h reads tries. Each principle has been met dozens of times, in tides, orcas, misprints, orbits and markets, and no single meeting could be predicted from the last. Not one has failed so far. That is evidence about a crowd of cases, with its count and its exception rate, and it can be reported like any other: so many meetings, no exceptions yet, and the claim kept open.

That is induction under the case with unpredictable variation, in full: a guess about the crowd of cases, made where no single case can be foreseen, stated with its count and its exception rate. Its short name is induction under the large case, and its accepted name is statistics.

30w5Three Prepositions

The Logic floor named the kinds of guessing. With the last of them now a technique, here they are together. To induce takes three prepositions. Under names what goes in. Over names the method. Into names what comes out.

  • Under the small case: induction under the case with predictable variation, where each case behaves like the next. Its accepted name is science, and its product is a law.
  • Under the large case: induction under the case with unpredictable variation, where only the crowd can be measured. Its accepted name is statistics, and rung 30 made it a technique.
  • Over the arrangement: from given lines to a foot that cannot fail if they hold. Its accepted name is deduction.
  • Over the series: from a case to the next, for every case at once. Its accepted name is mathematics, or mathematical induction where it runs on whole numbers.
  • Into the plan: how a design or a procedure might change the steps that follow. Its accepted name is abduction.
  • Into action: what to do next, which meal to order, what speed to drive. Its accepted name is transduction.

The ladder’s lessons argued only over the arrangement and over the series. From rung 31 on, the lessons also argue under the large case, with the techniques of this rung, and every such argument carries its width and its exception rate.

The accepted names

The swap axiom is the substitution property of equality, or Leibniz’s law in one direction; self-sameness is the reflexive law, a = a. The chain is transitivity. Induction over the series is mathematical induction. Induction under the large case is statistical inference, and treating a principle’s run of successes as evidence with an exception rate is the problem of induction, in the form statistics gives it.

Who said it first

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz stated the identity of indiscernibles and its converse around 1686. David Hume asked in 1739 and 1748 what justifies any guess from past cases to future ones, and found no argument that was not circular. Pierre-Simon Laplace answered with a rule of succession in 1774: after n successes and no failures, the chance of one more is (n + 1)/(n + 2). Charles Sanders Peirce named abduction in the 1870s and 1900s, beside deduction and induction. Karl Popper argued in 1934 that science does not confirm by recurrence but survives refutation.

A proof that is not allowed

“The ledger has held on every rung so far, so it is proved.” Not allowed: recurrence is evidence, with a count and an exception rate. A proof would be over the arrangement, and the principles are guesses from things. They stay open.

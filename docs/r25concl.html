25·∎ · conclusion

Every polynomial splits in ℂ

ℂ is a field with no order. Its size multiplies and obeys the triangle. Every polynomial that is not constant has a root there, and splits into one line factor for each power.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 25d every polynomial has a root is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 25 asked whether the new numbers find every root. Every polynomial that is not constant, with complex numbers in front, has a root in ℂ and splits there into line factors. The price is order: ℂ has none.

25a joined the three pictures of ℂ and proved the flip keeps arithmetic. 25b showed no order fits. 25c proved sizes multiply and obey the triangle. 25d found a root of every polynomial. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

ℂ is a field with no order, with a size that multiplies and obeys the triangle, in which every polynomial of degree n ≥ 1 splits into n line factors.

ℝ sits inside ℂ as the floor, and the flip z ↦ z̄ keeps sums and products, with z z̄ = |z|².

No choice of positives in ℂ obeys the three-way, positive-sum and positive-product techniques.

|zw| = |z||w| and |z + w| ≤ |z| + |w| for all z, w in ℂ.

If p(z) = u₀ + ⋯ + uₙzⁿ with n ≥ 1 and uₙ ≠ 0, then p(z) = uₙ(z − r₁)⋯(z − rₙ) for some r₁, …, rₙ in ℂ, and p has no other roots.

So the survey’s title holds for every polynomial with complex numbers in front. The search for roots, begun with the quadratic formula, needs no new numbers past ℂ.

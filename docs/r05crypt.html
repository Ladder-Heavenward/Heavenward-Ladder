5·crypt · cryptography

Is There a Recipe for Working Out Secrets?

A secret that nobody can work out has not been sent. Two friends hide a number by doing things to it, and the one who receives it undoes them, last thing first. An algorithm can do that much. What it hands back may still be a recipe for biscuits and gravy.

5crypt1A Secret Nobody Can Read

Is there a recipe for working out secrets?

Evita wants to tell Randy which bus to take, and Mike is listening. So she hides the number as well as she can. She adds to it, she multiplies it, she squares it, and she tells nobody what she did. Then she says the result out loud: “1,370.”

Mike cannot get the bus number out of that. Neither can Randy.

Evita hid the number to the best standard on every count, and the hiding stopped caring who was at the other end. Mike and Randy stand in the same place. Each of them holds 1,370 and nothing else.

A secret that nobody can work out has not been sent.

So hiding is half of the work. The other half is the way back, and it has to be made at the same time as the hiding. The way back is this step's subject. Rung 5 is the floor for it: the rung made room to undo.

From here on, in this step, the message is the number Evita means, the sent number is the number she says out loud, and working it out is getting the message from the sent number.

5crypt2Hiding Is a Story of What Was Done

What does Randy have to hold, to get the message back?

Start again, and this time Evita and Randy settle the hiding beforehand, away from Mike. “Multiply the bus number by 3, then add 5.”

Call the message m and the sent number s. 5g1 gives a letter this job: it stands for a number not yet known.

The bus is number 9. Evita works out 3 · 9 + 5 and says, “32.”

5g5 read a line like this as a story of what was done to the letter: m was multiplied by 3, and then 5 was added. The story has steps, the steps have an order, and each step has a number of its own.

The sent number can be heard by anyone. What is kept is the story: the steps, their order, and the number in each step.

Mike holds 32. Randy holds 32 and the story.

5crypt3Undo From the End

Randy holds 32 and the story. How does he get to 9?

5g5 gave the order: undo the story from its end. The last thing done is the first undone. It is the order of shoes and socks. On go the socks and then the shoes. Off come the shoes and then the socks.

The last thing Evita did was add 5. Adding −5 undoes that. Before it she multiplied by 3. Multiplying by 1/3 undoes that.

3 \cdot  m + 5 = 32 @@ the story, and the sent number
3 \cdot  m = 27 @@ add −5 to both sides, undoing the last thing done (5g5)
m = 9 @@ multiply both sides by 1/3, which is not 0 (5g5)
3 \cdot  9 + 5 = 32 @@ the check: run the story forwards on the answer

Each line is licensed by 5g: the same change to both sides, and the answers are kept.

The last line is not decoration. Randy runs Evita's story forwards on his answer and gets the number she said. If he had not, he would know that something went wrong before he got on a bus.

Working it out: undo the last step first, then the one before, to the start of the story. Then run the story forwards as a check.

See what the wrong order gives. Multiply 32 by 1/3 first and then add −5, and the answer is 17/3. No bus has that number.

5crypt4Every Step Needs Its Undo

Can every step be undone?

Rung 5 was built to answer that. Among the counting numbers, adding and multiplying run forwards and get stuck running backwards (5a10). The integers and the fractions made room.

Adding a number c is undone by adding −c, and every number has its negative (5b13). So an adding step can always be undone.

Multiplying by a number p is undone by multiplying by its reciprocal, and p has a reciprocal exactly when p is not 0 (5c10, 5e4).

Now let Evita use a step with no undo. “Multiply by 0, then add 5.” Anything times 0 is 0 (5e2). Bus 9 gives 5. Bus 14 gives 5. Every bus gives 5. No number times 0 gets the message back (5e5). Mike learns nothing from the 5, and Randy learns nothing either.

There is a quieter case. “Square it, then add 1.” The message 9 gives 82. So does −9, since (−p)² = p² (5g6). The squaring can be walked back, and the way back forks.

StepIts undoWhen the undo is there
add cadd −calways (5b13)

squarenone that gives one answertwo messages share each sent number (5g6)

A step with no undo hides the message from everybody, the friend as well. A step whose way back forks leaves the friend with cases.

A fork can be closed, and rung 2 has the tool. If both friends know beforehand that the message is a bus number, the domain of m is the positive numbers (2c2), and of the two ways back from 82 only 9 is in it.

5crypt5What the Receiver Holds

What is the whole of what Randy needs?

Put the last three footings in one list. This is the method for working it out.

What is heldWhere it comes from
1the sent numbersaid out loud; anyone has it
2the steps of the story, in ordersettled beforehand, and kept
3the number in each stepsettled beforehand, and kept
4an undo for each steprung 5: a negative, or a reciprocal
5the domain of the messagesettled beforehand; it closes any fork
6the checkthe story, run forwards on the answer

Only the first row travels. Rows 2, 3 and 5 are the secret, and they were held before anything was said. Rows 4 and 6 are not secret at all. They are rung 5's techniques, and Mike has them too.

The one who receives needs the story. The undoing itself is open to anyone who has the story.

That is why the story has to be kept. Nothing in the undoing is hard. If Mike gets rows 2 and 3, he does what Randy does, in the same four lines.

5crypt6The Undoing Is an Algorithm

Does Randy have to think it through afresh each time?

He does not. Look at what he did in 5crypt3. He held a number, and a list of steps not yet undone. He undid the last step on the list and struck it off. Then he looked whether any step was left.

4l1 has a name for work of that shape. It is an algorithm, and it has five parts.

Part (4l1)In working it out
statethe number in hand, and the steps not yet undone
startthe sent number, and the whole story
stop testno step is left
moveundo the last step left, and strike it off
outputthe number in hand

It stops, by 4l2. Each move leaves one step fewer, and a count of steps cannot get smaller for ever.

Its answer is right, by 4l3. One statement is true at the start and is kept by every move: the steps not yet undone, run forwards on the message, give the number in hand. At the stop no step is left, so the number in hand is the message.

Evita's hiding is a function (4d2): each message is paired with one sent number. The undoing is an algorithm that walks the same pairs the other way.

An algorithm can match a function. Given the story, it gets the message back every time, and nobody has to think.

5crypt7Working It Out Without the Story

Mike has no story. Is there anything for him to work with?

There is, and it is the same method. 5g1 said what to do with a number you do not know: give it a letter. Mike does not know the number multiplied by, so he calls it p. He does not know the number added, so he calls it b. He guesses the shape of the story, one multiplying and one adding, and writes it down.

Now he needs facts. On Monday he heard 32, and later he saw the friends board bus 9. On Tuesday he heard 20 and saw them board bus 5. Each day is one condition on his two letters.

p \cdot  9 + b = 32 @@ Monday
p \cdot  5 + b = 20 @@ Tuesday
p \cdot  4 = 12 @@ take the second line from the first (5g8)
p = 3 @@ multiply both sides by 1/4 (5g5)
3 \cdot  5 + b = 20,\ \text{so}\ b = 5 @@ put p into Tuesday's line and add −15
3 \cdot  9 + 5 = 32 @@ the check, on Monday

Two unknowns, two conditions (5g8). Mike holds the story now, and on Wednesday he will work the message out as fast as Randy does.

So a hiding made only of steps lasts until a listener has seen enough. If Evita and Randy want more than that, they need a hiding that is not made of steps.

Working it out without the story is the same work, one level up: a letter for each thing not known, a condition for each thing seen, the same change to both sides, and a check.

Some things come out with no bus seen at all. 5k2 showed that a process can look closed and still give up facts. Suppose the story is “double it, then add 1” and the messages are counting numbers. Then every sent number is odd, whatever the message is. A listener who hears only odd numbers, day after day, has learned something about the story, and no letter was filled in.

5crypt8More of the Same Is Not Deeper

Evita hears what Mike did. Can she make the story safe by making it longer?

She tries four steps. “Multiply by 3, add 5, multiply by 2, add 4.”

Randy now has four undos to do where he had two. Look at what Mike has. Spreading (5d5) opens the bracket.

The four steps are one multiplying and one adding. Mike's guess at the shape was right before and it is right again. Two days of watching still give him p = 6 and b = 14.

A longer story of the same kind of step costs the friend more work, and costs the listener nothing more.

So there are two costs to keep apart. One is what the friend pays to work the message out with the story. The other is what a listener pays to work it out without. Hiding is worth doing when the second is far above the first.

Evita's first try, in 5crypt1, sent both costs out of reach together. Her long story raised only the friend's. What she wants is a step that is quick to do, quick to undo with the story, and slow to work out without it. Adding and multiplying on the fractions are not such steps. Rung 5 made them easy to undo for everybody, and that was the whole point of the rung.

Each weakness found is a reason to go deeper and look for another kind of step. More of the same kind does not go deeper.

What Evita and Randy need is a kind of hiding that no algorithm walks back. They have had one since rung 0, and 5crypt9 comes to it.

5crypt9What the Algorithm Hands Back

The algorithm has stopped, and its output is right. Has Randy worked the secret out? What if the code isn't simple, but hides more code?

This time Evita sends a long message, one number for each letter, as the two of them settled. Randy's algorithm turns the numbers back into letters. He reads what came out.

“Biscuits and gravy. Six cups of flour. Two cups of butter. Keep the butter cold. Rest seven minutes.” It goes on like that for more than twenty lines.

Nothing went wrong. The check passes on every number. And a recipe for biscuits and gravy is not what Evita meant to tell him.

The message was hidden twice. The second hiding was done with numbers, and an algorithm has undone it. The first hiding was done before any number was written, with the tools of rungs 0 to 3, and it is still on.

RungWhat it lets the two of them doInside the recipe
0give a thing any name they choose (0a2), and let a whole sentence be the name of a statement (0a9)“biscuits” names the bus stop by the mill
1say a line that is true and beside the pointthe butter should be kept cold, and that line carries nothing; so do twenty others
2settle the domain beforehand (2c2), so that a number picks from what they chosethe “two” of the butter is taken from the buses, not from the cups; the “seven” of the rest is taken from the hours, not from the minutes
3let what a word names move on when the week does (3c1)next week the dish is cobbler, and it names another stop

Call that first hiding style and guile. Style is how a thing is said. Guile is saying one thing to mean another.

The algorithm gives back what was sent. It does not give back what was meant.

Randy can read the recipe, because he was there when the names were chosen. So the list in 5crypt5 was one row short.

5crypt10No Algorithm for Style and Guile

Mike has the story by now (5crypt7). Can he build an algorithm for the rest?

He tries. He watches for a month and works out the agreement: in a recipe for biscuits and gravy, the cups of butter are the bus, and the first word is the place. He writes that down as an algorithm. Find the line with the butter. Read off its number.

The next week the message comes out as a recipe for berry cobbler. The bus is in the berries now. His algorithm finds a butter line, reads its number, and sends him to the wrong stop.

The change cost Evita and Randy one new agreement, made in a minute. A name is chosen, not found (0a2), so it can be chosen again. It cost Mike his whole algorithm. 4l1 said that an algorithm is its five parts, and that nothing else may happen between moves. Noticing that the subject has changed is not one of the parts.

Suppose that by some miracle Mike builds an algorithm that reads every kind of recipe. The next message comes out as a schedule for the maintenance of a motor pool: forklifts and telehandlers, oil changes and tyre checks, for a fleet that does not exist anywhere.

An algorithm can match an algorithm or a function. It cannot be tuned to a choice that is made again as soon as it is caught.

So working it out has two parts, and they are not the same kind of work. Undoing what was done is an algorithm, and anybody with the story can run it. Reading what was meant is not an algorithm. It takes somebody who was party to the choosing, or somebody with guile enough to guess it.

That is also the answer to 5crypt8. The other kind of hiding Evita was looking for is cheap for the friend, who holds the agreement, and it is out of reach of a listener's algorithm. It is not out of reach of a listener who thinks.

The cellar · there if you want it

Accepted names

Hiding a message by steps is encryption, and working it out with the story is decryption. The message is the plaintext and the sent number is the ciphertext. The kept numbers of the story are the key. A step that can be undone is invertible, and its undo is its inverse. A story of one multiplying and one adding is an affine cipher. Working a story out from messages that were seen together with their sent numbers is a known-plaintext attack. A step that is quick to do and slow to undo without something kept is a trapdoor function.

An agreement that some words stand for other things is a code, as against a cipher, which works on the letters or numbers whatever they say. Hiding a message inside another that looks ordinary is steganography. Working a hidden message out without the story is cryptanalysis.

A bus schedule in a recipe

Here is the whole thing done once, both hidings, on a real message. Evita wants to tell Randy: take bus 2, at 7 o'clock, from the stop by the mill.

The first hiding: style and guile. On Sunday the two of them agree what the parts of a recipe stand for. Nothing is written down.

Those twenty-six lines are what Evita sends, where Mike can read them.

Working it out, with the story. Randy holds the story, “add 3”. Its undo is “add −3”, and the algorithm of 5crypt6 runs it on every letter: E back to b, L back to i, V back to s. Out comes the recipe, all twenty-six lines. Then he reads it by the agreement, and he reads only three lines of it. Line 1 names the dish, biscuits, so the stop is the mill. Line 7 has the cups of butter, two, so bus 2. Line 21 has the minutes of rest, seven, so 7 o'clock. He passes over the rest.

Working it out, without the story. Mike has only the capitals. He does what 5crypt7 did: he gives the unknown shift a letter, k, and looks for a condition on it. W comes 58 times, more than any other letter, and the word WKH comes 11 times. In English t is among the commonest letters and “the” is the commonest word. If W is t, then 19 + k = 22, and k = 3. He tries it and the lines turn into words, which is his check. A shift has only 25 stories to try in any case.

So Mike's algorithm stops, and its output is right: a recipe for biscuits, twenty-six lines of it. Now he holds eleven numbers: twenty four, six, two, one, one, two, two, four, one, seven, fifteen. Nothing in his output says that any of them is a bus, or which. He does not hold the bus, the hour or the stop, and no further step of his algorithm will make them. The longer and better the recipe, the more places there are for three lines to sit. Next week the dish is cobbler.

A scrambled alphabet in place of the shift would give Mike more counting to do and Randy a longer table to hold. It would not change what comes out at the end. The recipe is where the secret is.

31·∎ · conclusion

One product gives functions lengths, angles and closest points

A product gives sizes, angles and right angles. Closest points are shadows with misses at right angles, symmetric maps have right-angled axes, and every grid is a turn, a stretch and a turn. Functions sized by totals, with size-zero differences ignored, make spaces with no holes, where readings keep energy and polynomials are near every continuous function.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 31j polynomials are everywhere near is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 31 asked what it means for a function to be an arrow with a length and an angle. One product, straight, indifferent to order and positive, gives every arrow a size and every two arrows an angle, and in a space with no holes every closed flat piece has a closest point, the shadow, whose miss stands at right angles. Functions are such arrows, in a space with endlessly many directions.

31a laid down the product and proved the bound and the triangle. 31b found closest points as shadows, and the best line among them. 31c straightened any basis, and 31d gave symmetric maps right-angled axes. 31e read quadratic forms along those axes, and 31f gave every grid two sets of axes. 31g sized functions and bent the same-members axiom, 31h built the plane with endlessly many directions, 31i looked at complete spaces without a product, and 31j showed polynomials are near every continuous function. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

A product on a space of arrows gives a size with |⟨f, g⟩| ≤ ‖f‖ ‖g‖ and the triangle. The closest point of a flat piece built by finitely many arrows is the one whose miss is at right angles to the piece, and least squares is that shadow. Every free list can be straightened to a right-angled one.

A symmetric map has real stretches and a right-angled basis of axes, so a quadratic form is a sum of squares whose signs no change of directions can change; and every grid is a turn, a stretch and a turn, with the largest pieces giving the closest simpler grid.

Taking functions that agree but for size zero as the same, the total and energy sizes are true sizes, and L¹ and L² have no holes. In a complete product space the readings along a right-angled list obey the energy bound, rebuild every arrow and keep its energy exactly when the list leaves no direction out, and every closed flat piece has one closest point. A straight map is continuous exactly when it is bounded, and every bounded reading of a complete product space is a product. Every continuous function on a closed interval has polynomials within any ε at every input at once.

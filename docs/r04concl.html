4·∎ · conclusion

Counting Is an Object, so Induction Works

Throw away everything an inductive set holds that is not on the run from 0, and what is left is the set of counting numbers. Because nothing spare is in it, whatever is true at 0 and passes from each number to the next is true for all of them.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 4g multiplying and powers is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 4 promised that the counting numbers are an object, and that because they are, induction is a technique rather than an article of faith. 4b sifted ℕ from an inductive set and proved it is inductive, lies below every inductive set, throws out strays, and has different next ones for different sets. 4c proved the induction technique, in a set form and a condition form, and put it to work.

4d proved that a start and a step fix exactly one function on the numbers. 4f and 4g used it to define adding, multiplying and powers, proved that one and three make four, and proved the techniques of arithmetic by induction. This page gathers the claim: ℕ is one set, it is the smallest inductive set, and so induction works.

The claim, in full

The counting numbers are one set, the smallest inductive set, and because nothing else is in it, whatever is so of 0 and passes from each number to the next is so of every number.

Let φ(x) be the condition: x is in every inductive set.

Sifting any inductive set by φ gives the same set. That set is ℕ.

ℕ is inductive, and ℕ is a part of every inductive set.

0 is nobody's next, and different sets have different next ones.

Induction: from A ⊆ ℕ, 0 ∈ A, and S(n) ∈ A for every n ∈ A, you may write A = ℕ.

So a fact about every number needs only a base and a step. For instance 0 + p = p for every number p, though adding was defined only by p + 0 = p and p + S(b) = S(p + b).

The Proof

The first part is why ℕ may be named at all. Two people, each with an inductive set, sift it by φ and get the same set.

I \text{ is inductive},\quad I' \text{ is inductive} @@ given: each from the infinity axiom, by use a some
\text{let } y \text{ be anything} @@ aiming at same-members
y \in \{x \in I : \varphi(x)\} \Leftrightarrow \varphi(y) @@ the whichever-inductive-set technique (4b), for I
y \in \{x \in I' : \varphi(x)\} \Leftrightarrow \varphi(y) @@ the whichever-inductive-set technique, for I′
\htmlClass{new}{y \in \{x \in I : \varphi(x)\} \Leftrightarrow y \in \{x \in I' : \varphi(x)\}} @@ an exactly-when turns about and chains (1d)
\htmlClass{new}{\{x \in I : \varphi(x)\} = \{x \in I' : \varphi(x)\}} \quad \blacksquare @@ speak of everything; the same-members axiom

It does not matter which inductive set you sift, and the infinity axiom says there is one to sift. That one set is ℕ, and its membership line is the membership-in-ℕ technique.

0 \in \mathbb N @@ the zero-is-a-number technique (4b)
\forall n\,(n \in \mathbb N \Rightarrow S(n) \in \mathbb N) @@ the next-of-a-number technique (4b); speak of everything
\htmlClass{new}{\mathbb N \text{ is inductive}} @@ join; ︿ “inductive” (3d)
\htmlClass{new}{\forall J\,(J \text{ is inductive} \Rightarrow \mathbb N \subseteq J)} @@ the lies-below technique (4b); prove an if; speak of everything
\forall x\;S(x) \ne 0 @@ the next-never-empty technique (3c): 0 names ∅
\forall x\,\forall y\,\big(S(x) = S(y) \Rightarrow x = y\big) \quad \blacksquare @@ the different-nexts technique (4b)

The third line and the fourth together are the smallest-inductive-set technique. The strays technique of 4b and the back-from-a-next technique of 4c then throw every link of every cargo chain out of ℕ.

The third part is the rung's title. A part of ℕ that holds 0 and never runs out is inductive, so ℕ lies inside it, so it is ℕ.

A \subseteq \mathbb N @@ given
0 \in A \;\land\; \forall n\,(n \in A \Rightarrow S(n) \in A) @@ given: the base part and the step part, joined
\htmlClass{new}{A \text{ is inductive}} @@ ︿ “inductive” (3d)
\mathbb N \subseteq A @@ the second part's fourth line: use the every, with A; carry an if through
\htmlClass{new}{A = \mathbb N} \quad \blacksquare @@ the double-inclusion technique (2c), with the given A ⊆ ℕ

Sift ℕ by a condition φ and the same column gives the condition form of 4c: from φ(0), and φ(n) ⇒ φ(S(n)) for every number n, φ(n) for every number n. That is the induction technique.

The last part shows induction doing what nothing else can. Adding was defined by a start and a step: plus-zero, p + 0 = p, and plus-a-next, p + S(b) = S(p + b). Neither says 0 + p = p. Induction does.

\varphi(a):\ \ 0 + a = a @@ the condition, with its gap at a
0 + 0 = 0 @@ the plus-zero technique, with a = 0: the base
\text{let } a \in \mathbb N;\ \ \text{assume } 0 + a = a @@ the step opens: the induction hypothesis
\qquad 0 + S(a) = S(0 + a) @@ the plus-a-next technique
\qquad \phantom{0 + S(a)} = S(\htmlClass{keep}{a}) @@ the hypothesis, inside (the swap axiom)
\htmlClass{new}{\forall a \in \mathbb N\;\; 0 + a = a} \quad \blacksquare @@ prove an if; speak of everything; the induction technique

The same shape, base and step, proves on 4f that a sum is the same in either order and in any grouping, and that a number added to both sides can be cancelled. 1 + 3 = 4 needs no induction at all: plus-a-next three times, plus-zero once, and the names of 3c.

What the rest of the rung adds

Each step and annex after the proof steps adds a line to the claim. A line appears here once you have finished its page.

  • 4h order: Reads order off membership: p < b when p ∈ b, which agrees with reaching b from p by adding something other than 0. Any two numbers compare, a settled set of numbers with a member has a least one, and induction gets a strong form that leans on every earlier case at once.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4i writing numbers: Names the numbers past four and proves that splitting by ten, again and again, gives each number one row of digits and no other. Column adding and multiplying are 4f's and 4g's techniques used in a fixed order.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4j divides, remainders and primes: Divides with remainders, finds the primes, and proves that every number other than 0 is a product of primes in exactly one way. It also proves that the primes never run out.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4k counting choices: Says what it means for a set to have n members, by matching it with n. Then it counts: separate piles add, choices made in stages multiply, and the table of choices gives the numbers that appear when (p + b)ⁿ is multiplied out.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4l what a recipe for answers is made of: Takes an algorithm apart into a state, a start, a stop test, a move and an output. A count that falls on every move proves every run stops. A statement every move keeps proves the answer at the stop is right. And its anexact, yet rigorous box: loose, then locked, a recipe gathered by habit and locked by proof.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4·comp can a row of switches add?: Shows that the marks 0 and 1 of rung 1's tables are the sets named 0 and 1, then builds gates from switches and chains them into an adder for numbers written in twos.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4·stat what can counting alone say about a list?: Counts data: the tally of each value, a mode, the range. Chance starts as counting too: of the 2ⁿ ways n coins can fall, exactly n choose k show k heads.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 4·crypt can a shifted alphabet keep a secret?: Counting with remainders makes a shifted alphabet a matching of the letters that one more shift undoes, and counting its 25 keys shows that a lock is only as strong as the number of keys an enemy must try.its line appears when you finish it.
Anexact, yet rigorous · counting

4k set beside the proofs a second kind of care: when coins or leaves fall at random, you cannot say what one will do, but you can say the shape of the pile. Of the 16 ways 4 coins can fall, 6 give two heads and 14 give one, two or three.

Both kinds of care stand on the set this claim builds. Induction proves what is true of every number; counting says how the many cases pile up, and the spread is part of the answer.

Counting has become an object, and you can prove a thing of every number with two short pieces of work. That is a first handful; take it at your own pace.

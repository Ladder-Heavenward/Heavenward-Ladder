20c · proof

Slide, flip, turn

Three kinds of move do all the work of this rung. Slide everything the same way. Turn everything about the origin. Turn the plane over, so that a line stays where it is and everything else swaps sides. Each is written as a formula on pairs, and each is fixed by very little: a slide by where one point goes, a turn by where (1, 0) goes, a flip by its line.

A slide adds the same amount across and the same amount up to every point. A turn about the origin is fixed by where it sends the point one step to the right, a point on the circle of radius one, and every other point follows by the same mixing of across and up. A flip across a line through the origin keeps the part of each point along the line and turns over the part across it. Across the floor, it turns over every height.

20c1Slides

From here on, the slide by (h, k) is the move Sh, k(x, y) = (x + h, y + k). Its accepted name is a translation.

A slide adds the same displacement to every point. Sh, k followed by Sh′, k′ is the slide by (h + h′, k + k′), so the slides are a part-group, and they turn about. A slide is fixed by where it sends any one point: if it sends P to Q, it is the slide by Q − P.

20c2Turns about the origin

From here on, for a pair (a, b) with a² + b² = 1, the turn by (a, b) is the move R(a, b)(x, y) = (ax − by, bx + ay). It sends (1, 0) to (a, b) and (0, 1) to (−b, a), the point a quarter turn on from (a, b). Its accepted name is a rotation about the origin.

A turn about the origin is fixed by where it sends (1, 0). The rest of the plane follows: x copies of (a, b) and y copies of (−b, a). The quarter turn is R(0, 1)(x, y) = (−y, x), and the half turn R(−1, 0)(x, y) = (−x, −y). Rung 21 gives a and b their names.

R_{(0.6,\,0.8)}(5, 0) = (3, 4) @@ 5 copies of (0.6, 0.8)
R_{(0.6,\,0.8)}(0, 5) = (-4, 3) @@ 5 copies of (−0.8, 0.6)
R_{(0,1)}(R_{(0,1)}(x, y)) = R_{(0,1)}(-y, x) = (-x, -y) @@ two quarter turns are a half turn

A turn is a move: R(a, −b) undoes R(a, b), as 20j checks.

20c3Flips

From here on, the flip across a line ℓ is the move Fℓ that leaves each point of ℓ where it is and sends each other point P to the point P′ for which ℓ is the line of points equally far from P and P′ (18d2). The flip across the floor y = 0 is F₀(x, y) = (x, −y). Its accepted name is a reflection.

Flip-formula technique: for the line through the origin along a unit displacement u, the flip is F(P) = 2(P · u)u − P.

20c4Turns and flips about other points

To turn about a point C, slide C to the origin, turn, and slide back. To flip across a line through a point Q, slide Q to the origin, flip across the parallel line through the origin, and slide back. Every turn and every flip is a slide, a move about the origin, and the slide undone.

\text{turn about } C \text{ by } (a, b):\ \ P \mapsto C + R_{(a,b)}(P - C) @@ slide by −C, turn, slide by C
C = (1, 1),\ (a, b) = (0, 1):\ \ (3, 1) \mapsto (1, 1) + R_{(0,1)}(2, 0) = (1, 3) @@ a quarter turn about (1, 1)
\text{flip across } y = 2:\ \ (x, y) \mapsto (x, 2) + F_0(x, y - 2) = (x, 4 - y) @@ slide down 2, flip over the floor, slide up 2

The accepted names

Slides are translations, turns are rotations, flips are reflections, and a flip followed by a slide along its line is a glide reflection (20f meets it). The turn R(a, b) is usually written with an angle, which rung 21 builds.

Who said it first

In 20c.4.5, the philosopher is Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz and the follower of Isaac Newton is Samuel Clarke: the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence (1715–16, printed 1717).

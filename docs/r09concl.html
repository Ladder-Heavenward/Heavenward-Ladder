9·∎ · conclusion

Each input has exactly one output

A function is a set of pairs in which every input appears exactly once. Nothing else is promised, and everything else about functions follows from it.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 9d running backwards is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 9 promised that 4c's one sentence, each input has exactly one output, is the whole truth about what a function is. 9a made the sentence formal with ∃! and named domain, codomain and range. It showed by the diagonal that most functions have no formula, and proved that two functions are the same exactly when they have the same inputs and values. 9b composed functions, showed that the order matters, and proved that the brackets in a chain of three do not.

9c asked the two promises of the outputs: one-to-one, onto, and both at once, a bijection. It showed what survives a chain. 9d swapped the pairs and proved that a function can be run backwards exactly when it is a bijection, with f⁻¹ its only inverse. This page states the claim in full and puts those proofs together.

The claim, in full

A function is nothing but its pairs, one pair for each input, and composing, comparing and running backwards all follow from that.

Let A and B be sets, and f ⊆ A × B.

f : A → B means that each member of A is the left entry of exactly one member of f.

Two functions are the same exactly when they have the same inputs and the same value at each.

If f : A → B and g : B → C, then g ∘ f : A → C, and in a chain of three the brackets do not matter.

f can be run backwards exactly when it is one-to-one and onto B. Then its only inverse is f⁻¹, its pairs swapped.

The proof

The definition is 9a's one-output technique, in its form (iii). The first headline technique says that nothing but the pairs matters. Here is 9a's equal-functions column, for f : A → B and g : C → D.

f = g \Rightarrow A = \operatorname{dom} f = \operatorname{dom} g = C @@ rightward: pairs-fix-the-inputs (9a) twice; the swap axiom
f = g \Rightarrow f(a) = g(a) @@ the swap axiom on the name f(a); a was anything
A = C,\ \forall a \in A\ f(a) = g(a);\ \ p \in f @@ leftward: assume; chase a member of f
p = (a, b),\ \ a \in A,\ \ b = f(a) @@ f ⊆ A × B; 4c's line for f(a)
b = g(a),\ \text{so } p = (a, g(a)) \in g @@ the hypothesis; a ∈ C; 4c's line for g
\htmlClass{new}{f = g} \quad \blacksquare @@ the same chase from g to f; then same-members (2a)

So a function is fixed by its inputs and its values. A formula is one description of the pairs. Two formulas that agree at every input describe one function, and one formula on two sets of inputs describes two.

g \circ f : A \to C,\ \ (g \circ f)(a) = g(f(a)) @@ composite-is-a-function (9b): f(a) ∈ B, so g(f(a)) ∈ C, and rule-makes-a-function (4c)
\text{let } a \in A @@ speak of every input by speaking of any
\big(h \circ (g \circ f)\big)(a) = h\big(g(f(a))\big) @@ composite-is-a-function, twice
\phantom{\big(h \circ (g \circ f)\big)(a)} = \big((h \circ g) \circ f\big)(a) @@ composite-is-a-function read right to left, twice
\htmlClass{new}{h \circ (g \circ f) = (h \circ g) \circ f} \quad \blacksquare @@ same domain A, same value at every a: the first part

The last part joins 9c and 9d. One way, an inverse forces both promises about outputs. The other way, the swapped pairs form a function that undoes f. And any inverse is the swapped pairs.

\text{assume } g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A,\ \ f \circ g = \mathrm{id}_B @@ g undoes f on both sides (9d's line)
\mathrm{id}_A \text{ one-to-one},\ \ \mathrm{id}_B \text{ onto } B @@ the identity's value: id(a₁) = id(a₂) is a₁ = a₂; b = id_B(b)
\htmlClass{new}{f \text{ one-to-one and onto } B} @@ one-to-one-inside and onto-outside (9c)
\text{let } f \text{ be a bijection}:\ \ f^{-1} : B \to A @@ the other way: swapped-pairs-function (9d)
f^{-1} \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A,\ \ f \circ f^{-1} = \mathrm{id}_B @@ there-and-back (9d): f⁻¹ undoes f, so f runs backwards
g = g \circ \mathrm{id}_B = g \circ (f \circ f^{-1}) @@ any g that undoes f: identity (9b); there-and-back, read right to left
\phantom{g} = (g \circ f) \circ f^{-1} = \mathrm{id}_A \circ f^{-1} @@ the second part: brackets don't matter; g ∘ f = id_A
\phantom{g} = \htmlClass{new}{f^{-1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ identity (9b), on the left: the only inverse

That is 9d's running-backwards technique. When a function fails it, keeping part of its inputs can repair it. Squaring, kept to the reals that are not negative, is a bijection onto them, and its inverse is 8f's square root (root-undoes-square, 9d).

  • 9·bio is a cow just a big mouse?: Power laws u · M^b: multiply the mass by k and the output is multiplied by k^b. The three-quarters law of resting energy use, worked with ratios of powers.its line appears when you finish it.

  • 9·crypt can a code be truly unbreakable?: The pad is a function that undoes itself, and for a fixed cipher text the keys are matched one to one with the messages, so the cipher text alone rules out no message of its length.its line appears when you finish it.

Anexact, yet rigorous · connections, not families

9a set beside its proofs a second kind of care. A family name, like “oak”, is an average, and no single oak is the average oak. What a thing does to whatever passes through it is another way to define it, and a claim about cases you have not seen needs both.

This rung's claim is the second way, made exact. A function is defined by what it does to each input, its pairs, and not by its family of formula or name. “Squaring” on ℕ and on ℤ share a family and a formula, and they are two functions, because their pairs differ.

Each input, one output: that is all a function promises, and you now have the whole of it. What people have built on that one promise fills libraries, and you are standing at the first shelf.

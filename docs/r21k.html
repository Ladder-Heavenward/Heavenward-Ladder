21k · technique

Identities

A school course in trigonometry hands out a long list of formulas to remember. Every one of them follows from two facts already proved: a point on the circle is one unit from the centre, and turning by two arcs is turning by their sum. This step shows how to grow the list from those two, how to check a formula without fooling yourself, and how to find every angle that answers a question, not just the first.

Every formula in the list comes from the circle and the sum of turns, by algebra alone. A formula is proved by changing one side until it becomes the other. An equation about an angle usually has endlessly many answers, one family for each turn.

21k1One Side Into the Other

From here on, an identity is an equation true for every θ where both sides are defined. One-side technique: prove an identity by a chain of equals from one side to the other, never by doing the same thing to both sides of what is still unproved. Doing the same to both sides runs the proof backwards: it shows what follows from the claim, not that the claim holds.

\frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{\sin 2\theta} = \frac{1 - (1 - 2\sin^2\theta)}{2\sin\theta\cos\theta} @@ doubles (21d4), with cos² = 1 − sin² (21c)
= \frac{2\sin^2\theta}{2\sin\theta\cos\theta} @@ 1 − 1 = 0
\htmlClass{new}{= \tan\theta} \quad \blacksquare @@ cancel 2 sin θ, allowed where sin 2θ ≠ 0

The chain technique of 0b joins the links: each line equals the next, so the first equals the last.

21k2Halves

Half-arc technique: cos²(θ/2) = (1 + cos θ)/2 and sin²(θ/2) = (1 − cos θ)/2. The sign of cos(θ/2) or sin(θ/2) is then fixed by the quarter that θ/2 ends in.

\cos 2A = 2\cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 A @@ doubles, with sin² = 1 − cos² or cos² = 1 − sin²
\cos^2 A = \tfrac{1 + \cos 2A}{2},\ \ \sin^2 A = \tfrac{1 - \cos 2A}{2} @@ solve each for the square
\htmlClass{new}{\cos^2\tfrac{\theta}{2} = \tfrac{1 + \cos\theta}{2},\ \ \sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2} = \tfrac{1 - \cos\theta}{2}} \quad \blacksquare @@ A = θ/2

21k3Products Into Sums, and Back

Product-to-sum technique: a product of a sine and a cosine is half the sum of two sines; a product of two cosines or two sines is half a sum or difference of cosines. Add and subtract the sum and difference formulas.

\sin(A + B) + \sin(A - B) = 2\sin A\cos B @@ sum + difference: the cos A sin B terms cancel
\cos(A - B) + \cos(A + B) = 2\cos A\cos B @@ the sin A sin B terms cancel
\cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B) = 2\sin A\sin B @@ the cos A cos B terms cancel
\htmlClass{new}{\sin A\cos B = \tfrac12[\sin(A + B) + \sin(A - B)]} \quad \blacksquare @@ and the other two, halved
x = A + B,\ y = A - B:\ \ \sin x + \sin y = 2\sin\tfrac{x + y}{2}\cos\tfrac{x - y}{2} @@ read backwards: a sum into a product

21k4Every Answer, Not the First One

Every-answer technique: if α is one arc with sin α = s, every θ with sin θ = s is α + 2πk or π − α + 2πk; if cos α = c, every θ with cos θ = c is ±α + 2πk. Here k is any whole number.

-1 \le s \le 1:\ \text{some } \alpha \text{ with } \sin\alpha = s @@ arc-picks-a-point (21a3), on the half circle and its flips
\sin(\pi - \alpha) = \sin\alpha @@ half turns (21e4)
\text{in one turn, at most two points have height } s @@ they are (±√(1 − s²), s), by the circle technique (21c)
\htmlClass{new}{\theta = \alpha + 2\pi k \ \text{or}\ \pi - \alpha + 2\pi k} \quad \blacksquare @@ then whole turns (21b)

To solve an equation in sin θ, first solve for sin θ as a number, then use every-answer on each value.

The accepted names

The half-arc formulas are the half-angle formulas; product-to-sum and sum-to-product formulas keep their names. The values α + 2πk and π − α + 2πk are the general solution. Writing sin θ and cos θ in t = tan(θ/2) is the Weierstrass substitution.

Who said it first

In 21k.4.5, the formula is Cardano’s, Ars Magna (1545), and the trouble is the case later called irreducible. François Viète solved it with the triple-angle identity in about 1591; Rafael Bombelli had already, in 1572, calculated boldly with the square roots of negatives and got the right answers.

The Two Triangles

A triangle is decided by a few of its parts, and the circle’s coordinates now let you work out the rest. Two sides and the angle between them give the area and the third side. The sides stand in the same proportion as the sines of the angles across from them. And two sides with an angle that is not between them can leave you with two different triangles, or one, or none: a trap that has caught surveyors and test-takers for centuries.

Two sides and the angle between them fix a triangle’s area and its third side. The sides are in the same ratios as the sines of the angles they face. Two sides and an angle not between them may make two triangles.

21h1Area From Two Sides and the Angle

Write a, b, c for the sides facing the corners A, B, C, and A, B, C also for the sizes of the angles there. Sine-area technique: a triangle’s area is ½ bc sin A. Put A at the origin and the side c along the floor. The corner C is b times the point at arc A, so its height above the floor is b sin A.

A = (0, 0),\ B = (c, 0),\ C = b(\cos A, \sin A) @@ slide and turn: moves keep sides, angles and area
\text{area} = \tfrac12 |c \cdot b\sin A - 0 \cdot b\cos A| @@ triangle-area (18g)
\htmlClass{new}{\text{area} = \tfrac12\,bc\sin A} \quad \blacksquare @@ sin A > 0 for 0 < A < π
Anexact, yet rigorous · the triangle: a motion split into parts

A slanted push of size b, at angle A to the floor, does two things at once: it pushes along the floor by b cos A and up by b sin A. The triangle is how much of each. Nothing in the push is split; the split is in the describing, along two directions you choose.

Split a slanted change into parts along directions you choose, and say which directions you chose.

Choose other directions and the parts change, while the push stays the same. Rung 21’s physics annex adds pushes this way, part by part.

A boat points straight across a river at 3 m/s, and the river carries it downstream at 4 m/s. Which way does it actually go, and how fast? Would a fisherman on the bank split the motion the same way?

21h2The Third Side

Cosine-side technique: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A. Its accepted name is the law of cosines. This is 18f’s two-sides-and-the-angle, with its opening now a cosine. When A is a right angle, cos A = 0, and it is 18a’s right-triangle technique.

a^2 = |C - B|^2 = (b\cos A - c)^2 + (b\sin A)^2 @@ 18a, with the corners of 21h1
= b^2(\cos^2 A + \sin^2 A) - 2bc\cos A + c^2 @@ share out
\htmlClass{new}{a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A} \quad \blacksquare @@ the circle technique

21h3Sides and the Sines They Face

Sine-ratio technique: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C. Its accepted name is the law of sines. Find the area three ways, once from each corner, and divide by ½ abc.

\tfrac12\,bc\sin A = \tfrac12\,ca\sin B = \tfrac12\,ab\sin C @@ the sine-area technique, at each corner
\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c} @@ divide by ½ abc
\htmlClass{new}{\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}} \quad \blacksquare @@ turn each over

21h4Two Sides and an Angle Not Between Them

Two-triangles technique: given A, b and a, with A between 0 and π/2, there are no triangles if a < b sin A, one if a = b sin A or a ≥ b, and two if b sin A < a < b. Put A at the origin and C at b(cos A, sin A). B must be on the floor’s ray, at distance a from C. The circle of radius a about C meets that ray where the distance a reaches down past the height b sin A.

B = (x, 0),\ x > 0,\ (x - b\cos A)^2 + b^2\sin^2 A = a^2 @@ B on the ray, a from C
x = b\cos A \pm \sqrt{a^2 - b^2\sin^2 A} @@ solve the quadratic (8i)
a < b\sin A:\ \text{none};\ \ a = b\sin A:\ \text{one, right-angled at } B @@ the root is not real, or is 0
\htmlClass{new}{b\sin A < a < b:\ \text{both } x > 0,\ \text{two triangles}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the smaller x is positive exactly when a < b

This is why 20f warned that side-side-angle does not decide a triangle. Now it says how many.

The accepted names

The sine-area technique is the area formula ½ab sin C; the cosine-side technique is the law of cosines; the sine-ratio technique is the law of sines; the two-triangles technique is the ambiguous case (SSA). The area from three sides is Heron's formula.

Who said it first

The law of sines for plane triangles is in the work of Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī (1260s); the law of cosines in this form in François Viète (1579). In 21h.4.5, the egg was the Cassinis' view and the orange Newton's (Principia, 1687); the expeditions were Maupertuis's to Lapland (1736–37) and La Condamine and Bouguer's to Peru (1735–44). The orange won.

36·phys · physics

Light as energy, colour as perception

Light as energy, colour as perception: how can one light be both? A lamp in a kitchen in Missoula pours out energy that warms the counter, and the same lamp makes a colour that a cook calls warm white. The rung kept the two apart on purpose. It said that lights shone together add, that light through a filter is multiplied, that a molecule’s bonds pick the wavelengths it takes away, and that some light carries energy and no colour at all. Each of those was laid down, or held for this page. What makes the question hard is that every one of them is a guess from things, made by people who could not see a photon, and checked against meters and eyes for a long time. This page shows the guesses at work, with the rung’s own tools: sums of spectra, the three numbers, and a photon’s energy run backwards.

Two lamps whose light arrives independently add, wavelength by wavelength. Light through a filter keeps a share at each wavelength, and two filters keep the product of their shares. A molecule takes away the wavelength whose photon fits one of its gaps, so the gap fixes the colour, and the gap’s doubt passes to the wavelength unchanged. Light past the window adds energy and no colour, and a prism sorts out what the eye cannot.

36phys1Why lights add

Light arrives in photons (17·phys). Shine two lamps on one wall. In any second, the photons near a wavelength λ that reach a patch of the wall are those from the first lamp and those from the second, and no photon is from both. So the count is a count of two separate sets, and counts of separate sets add.

Counts-add technique: for two lamps whose light arrives independently, the amount at each wavelength of the two together is the sum of their amounts. Each photon at λ carries E(λ) (36b2), so the energies add too. This is the “together” that 36a3 laid down, and the reason it can be laid down.

N_1(\lambda),\ \allowbreak N_2(\lambda) \text{ the }\allowbreak \text{photons }\allowbreak \text{per }\allowbreak \text{second }\allowbreak \text{near } \lambda \text{ from }\allowbreak \text{each }\allowbreak \text{lamp} @@ the two sets share no photon
N(\lambda) = N_1(\lambda) + N_2(\lambda) @@ counts of separate sets add (2, 4)
\text{amount }\allowbreak \text{at } \lambda = N(\lambda)\,E(\lambda) = N_1(\lambda)E(\lambda) + N_2(\lambda)E(\lambda) @@ 36b2: each photon carries E(λ); 5d spreading
\htmlClass{new}{S = S_1 + S_2} \quad \blacksquare @@ the definition of the sum of spectra (36a3)

The guess has an edge. Split one laser beam in two and bring the halves back together, and where the waves meet half a turn apart they cancel, as 32·eng’s headphones cancel noise. Then the amounts do not add; the waves do. Two ordinary lamps never keep time with each other for long enough to show it, and that is a fact by cases with a rate.

36phys2One through the other

A filter keeps a share t(λ) of the light at each wavelength, a number from 0 to 1, and stops the rest. 18·chem found the share for a path of length ℓ through a solution: t = 10^(−εℓc), with ε fixed by the molecule at that wavelength. So light S through the filter comes out as the spectrum t·S, wavelength by wavelength. That is multiplying, not adding: it is the pigment mix of 36h1 seen in the light itself.

Through-filters technique: light S through two filters with shares t₁ and t₂ comes out as t₁t₂S, wavelength by wavelength, in either order.

\text{after }\allowbreak \text{the }\allowbreak \text{first }\allowbreak \text{filter: } t_1(\lambda)\,S(\lambda) @@ the first keeps its share
\text{after }\allowbreak \text{the }\allowbreak \text{second: } t_2(\lambda)\,\big(t_1(\lambda)\,S(\lambda)\big) @@ the second keeps its share of what reached it
= t_1(\lambda)\,t_2(\lambda)\,S(\lambda) @@ 5d: grouping and turn-about for times
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{same }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{in }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{either }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{order}} \quad \blacksquare @@ 5d: turn-about for times

For two layers of one dye, t₁t₂ = 10^(−ε(ℓ₁ + ℓ₂)c): the paths add, as 18·chem’s absorbance technique says. A pot of mixed paint is not two filters in a row. Some light bounces back off grains near the top and never passes through anything, so the colour of a pot is not the product, and 36h6 says so.

36phys3A gap picks a wavelength

A molecule holds its energy in steps. When a photon’s energy matches the gap ΔE between two steps, the molecule can take the photon and climb; a photon that fits no gap passes by. 36b2 gave the energy of a photon at λ as E(λ) = hc/λ, with hc about 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ joule metres. Run that backwards (9d), and the gap picks its wavelength.

Gap-to-wavelength technique: a molecule with an energy gap ΔE takes away light at λ = hc/ΔE, and a gap known to a share s gives the wavelength to the same share s.

E(\lambda) = hc/\lambda = \Delta E @@ the photon fits the gap
\lambda = hc/\Delta E = hc\,(\Delta E)^{-1} @@ 5b: divide both sides
\text{a share } s \text{ in } \Delta E \text{ gives }\allowbreak \text{a }\allowbreak \text{share } |{-1}|\,s = s \text{ in } \lambda @@ 13m: through a power k the share is multiplied by |k|
\htmlClass{new}{\lambda = hc/\Delta E,\ }\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{to }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{the }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{share }}\allowbreak \htmlClass{new}{ \text{of } \Delta E} \quad \blacksquare @@ the two lines together

One example first. A dye whose gap is 3.40 × 10⁻¹⁹ J takes away light at 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 3.40 × 10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 5.84 × 10⁻⁷ m, that is 584 nm, in the yellow. What is left is the short and long ends of W, and the dye looks blue to violet. If the gap is known to 1%, the wavelength is known to 1%, about 6 nm either way. A solvent that pulls on the molecule moves the gap a little, and the colour with it: the chain of 36g3, with its rate at every link.

36phys4Light the eye does not see, and a prism that sees it

The warmth from a stove is light past 700 nm. The three curves are 0 there, so its three numbers are (0, 0, 0), and its energy is not 0: it warms the hand. Add it to any light and the colour stays and the energy grows. That is the spectrum D of 36c1, a light the eye does not see, and it is the two weightings of 36b4 pulled as far apart as they go.

Unseen-light technique: adding light from outside the window to any light keeps its colour and adds to its energy. Proof: col(S + D) = col(S) + col(D) = col(S) by three-numbers, since col(D) = (0, 0, 0); the energy of S + D is the energy of S plus that of D (36b2), and the energy of D is more than 0.

A prism sees what the eye drops. Glass slows short waves a little more than long ones, so a prism bends violet further than red, and each wavelength lands at its own place on a wall. Two metamers that look the same to the eye spread into two different patterns on the wall: a spike at 580 nm makes one line, and a red and a green light make two. The reduction technique of 36j7, seen with a piece of glass.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

Light that adds by counts is incoherent light; light whose waves add and cancel is coherent, and the cancelling is interference. The share a filter keeps is its transmittance, and t = 10^(−εℓc) is the Beer–Lambert law. The gap a photon fits is an electronic transition, and the gap-to-wavelength technique is the Planck–Einstein relation run backwards. The warmth past 700 nm is infrared light. The prism’s sorting is dispersion, set by the glass’s refractive index changing with wavelength. A solvent that moves a dye’s colour shows solvatochromism.

Who said it first

Isaac Newton held that white light is a sum of coloured lights, and showed in the 1660s that a second prism does not change a colour the first has picked out. Johann Wolfgang von Goethe argued in his Theory of Colours of 1810 that colour is made at the edges of light and dark; that argument is the closer of this page. William Herschel found the warmth past the red end in 1800 with a thermometer in a prism’s band. Thomas Young showed in the years from 1801 to 1803 that two parts of one light can cancel. August Beer gave the law of the share kept in 1852.

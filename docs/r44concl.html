44·∎ · conclusion

Past degree four, no formula

The roots of an equation make a field, and its symmetries a group as large as the field’s degree. Middle fields match part-groups. A formula by roots exists exactly when the group comes apart into prime clocks, and the shuffles of five do not. Towers of square roots are what compass and straightedge reach.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 44i what compass and straightedge can build is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 44 asked why the formulas stop at degree four. An equation has a formula by roots exactly when the group of symmetries of its roots comes apart, step by step, into clocks of prime size; the shuffles of five or more roots do not, so past degree four there is no formula. On the way, the same tools settle what compass and straightedge can build.

44a solved the cubic and quartic. 44b showed symmetric quantities are read off the coefficients. 44c built the group of an equation and counted it; 44d matched its part-groups with middle fields. 44e took groups apart into fixed pieces, and 44f found a piece that is not a clock. 44g joined fields and groups into the test for a formula, and found a quintic with none. 44h found part-groups of every prime-power size, and 44i used them on the old constructions. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

Let F be a field inside ℂ, f a polynomial over F, K its splitting field and G = Aut(K/F). Then |G| = [K : F]; H ↦ K^H is a matching of the part-groups of G with the fields between F and K, reversing containment, with [K : K^H] = |H|, and K^H is a splitting field over F exactly when H is normal, with group G/H. Every finite group has a chain of simple pieces, the same for every chain; f has a formula by roots exactly when every piece of G is a clock of prime size.

A₅ is simple and not turn-about, so Sₙ is not solvable for n ≥ 5, and x⁵ − 4x + 2 has group S₅ and no formula by roots, while every equation of degree at most four has one. An irreducible cubic over ℚ with three real roots has no formula by real roots. A real number is constructible exactly when it lies in a tower of square roots over ℚ, so its degree is a power of 2: ∛2, cos 20° and cos(2π/7) are not constructible, and cos(2π/17) is.

So the rung’s claim holds: the group of an equation decides whether it has a formula by roots, and five roots are where the groups stop coming apart. Nothing in the proof is held in trust; the steps after it hold two facts, on 44l and 44·cb27.

The Techniques of Plus and Times Stand on Their Own

Rings and fields are collections where plus and times keep their techniques. Quotients by ideals are rings, and fields exactly when the ideal is largest. Division with remainder gives unique factoring, and ideals repair it where it fails. Degrees multiply up towers of fields, there is one finite field of each prime-power size, the line is the only complete ordered field, and modules over a principal ring split into clocks.

The claim, in full

Let R be a ring. If 1 = 0 then R has one member, and a finite cancelling ring is a field. For an ideal I, R/I is a ring; what a ring map loses is an ideal, and its quotient matches what the map reaches. If R turns about, R/P is cancelling exactly when P is prime, and R/M is a field exactly when M is largest; so ℤ/p is a field for every prime p.

Over a field F, F[x] has division with remainder, and every ideal of F[x] is (d) for one d. In a principal ring every member other than 0 that is not undoable factors into unsplittables in one way, up to order and partners. In ℤ[√−5] it does not, and there its ideals factor uniquely into prime ideals.

If K ⊆ L ⊆ M are fields, [M : K] = [M : L][L : K]; if α has an unsplittable least polynomial m of degree d over K, then K(α) is a copy of K[x]/(m) and [K(α) : K] = d. Every finite field has pⁿ members for a prime p; for each pⁿ there is one; and its members other than 0 are the powers of one member. Any two complete ordered fields are matched by exactly one map keeping sums, products and order. Every finitely made module over ℤ or F[x] is a copy of Rʳ × R/(d₁) × ⋯ × R/(dₖ) with d₁ ∣ ⋯ ∣ dₖ, and every finite group that turns about is a copy of ℤ/d₁ × ⋯ × ℤ/dₖ for one such list.

Held in trust: that the ideals of ℤ[√−5] factor uniquely, that a field of each prime-power size is the only one, and that a module’s list of clocks is the only one.

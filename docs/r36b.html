36b · proof

The band, and the window in it

The wavelength interval of 36a is one small window in a band that runs from radio waves longer than a house to rays shorter than an atom. This step places the window, gives each wavelength its photon energy, and lays down the one thing on this rung that is measured rather than derived: the three curves by which an eye reads a spectrum. From them, a colour is three numbers, 32’s coefficients, and the rung’s blindness is stated exactly.

The visible window is a short closed interval of the band. A photon at wavelength λ carries energy proportional to 1/λ. Three fixed curves on the window, laid down as the eye’s, turn a spectrum into three coefficients, and a colour is those three. Energy and brightness are two different weightings of one spectrum.

36b1The band

Light is a wave as well as a flow of dots (17·phys), and a wave has a wavelength. The whole family runs along one line of 8, from wavelengths of kilometres (radio) through metres and millimetres (the oven’s microwaves), through the thousandths of a millimetre the skin feels as warmth, into the window an eye can use, past it into the shorter waves that burn skin, and on down to the waves an atom’s nucleus gives off. The accepted name is the electromagnetic spectrum; the ladder calls it the band, to keep “spectrum” for 36a’s function.

From here on, the window W of 36a1 is the closed interval of wavelengths from 380 to 700 nanometres, a nanometre being a thousand-millionth of a metre (8·phys’s ladder of sizes). Outside W a spectrum may have any amount, and an eye reports none of it. From here on a spectrum is taken continuous on W, which lets 36a2’s peak exist and which 36·phys says is so of every real light.

36b2A photon’s energy

Light arrives in dots, 17·phys’s photons, and each packet’s energy is set by its wavelength and nothing else. From here on, the energy of a photon at wavelength λ is E(λ) = h·c/λ, where h·c is one fixed number for all light. E is an operator: carried out on a wavelength, it produces an energy, in 14·phys’s joules.

Shorter wavelength, more energy, by 5j3’s inverse proportion: a photon at 400 nm carries 700/400 = 1.75 times the energy of one at 700 nm. The total energy a spectrum carries is its amounts weighted by E and added across W, which is 15’s area under the curve S(λ)·E(λ). That number heats what the light lands on, and it is the first of the rung’s two weightings of a spectrum.

Energy-weighting technique: for spectra S and T, the energy of S + T is the energy of S plus the energy of T. Proof: 15e1, areas add, and 15e2, areas scale, with E(λ) a fixed multiplier at each λ.

36b3The three windows

Here is the rung’s one laid-down fact. From here on, the three curves are three fixed functions L, M and S on W, each non-negative, each a single hill, with peaks near 565, 540 and 440 nanometres; they are laid down as measured, the eye’s, and the ladder does not derive them. Their accepted names are the long, medium and short cone sensitivities.

From here on, the three numbers of a spectrum S are its coefficients against the three curves, each 15’s area under the product: l = area of S·L over W, m = area of S·M, s = area of S·S̃, where S̃ is the short curve (the letter clash is noted and the curve is written S̃ on this rung). This is 32’s coefficient exactly, with a hill in place of a turning, and 31’s angle between two functions read as a number. From here on, a colour is a triple (l, m, s) of non-negative reals, and the colour of a spectrum is its three numbers. The operator that takes a spectrum to its colour is written col; col is a function from spectra to triples.

Three-numbers technique: col(S + T) = col(S) + col(T), and col(kS) = k·col(S).

36b4Energy is not brightness

The energy of a spectrum weights it by E(λ), which rises toward the short end. The eye weights it by L + M, which is a hill in the middle of W and near 0 at both ends. So a spectrum can carry much energy and feed the three curves little, or the reverse. From here on, the brightness of a spectrum is its coefficient against the curve L + M; its accepted name is luminance, and the weighting curve is the luminosity function.

Two-weightings technique: there are spectra S and T with energy(S) > energy(T) and brightness(S) < brightness(T). Proof by example: S a spike at 400 nm of height 1, T a spike at 555 nm of height 1; E(400) > E(555) by 36b2, and (L + M)(400) is near 0 while (L + M)(555) is near the peak, so the areas go the other way.

This is the first place the rung says plainly what its name means: the eye’s numbers are a weighting the eye supplies, and the physics does not supply it.

The accepted names

The band is the electromagnetic spectrum, and the window is the visible spectrum. E(λ) = hc/λ is Planck’s relation, with h Planck’s constant and c the speed of light. The three curves are the cone fundamentals, or the long, medium and short cone sensitivities; the three numbers are the LMS cone responses, or tristimulus values when another set of curves is used. Brightness is luminance, and L + M stands in for the luminosity function V(λ).

Who said it first

Max Planck wrote energy in lumps of h times the frequency in 1900, and Albert Einstein gave light itself those lumps in 1905. William Herschel found the warmth past the red end of the window in 1800, and Johann Ritter the burning rays past the violet end in 1801. The three curves were measured by colour matching and from the eyes of people who lack one kind of cone; the curves used today date from the 1990s and 2000s.

A proof that is not allowed

“A spike at 400 nm carries 1.75 times the energy of a spike at 700 nm of the same height. So it looks 1.75 times as bright.” Not allowed: brightness is the coefficient against L + M, not against E, and the two-weightings technique shows they can go opposite ways.

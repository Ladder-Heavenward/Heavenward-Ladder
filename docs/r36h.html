36h · proof

Functions of Colour’s Own

Colours make functions that nothing earlier on the ladder made for them: two ways of mixing that disagree, a dodge and a burn that hit the walls, a screen that is multiply turned inside out, and a darken and lighten that are 1e’s tables at the corners. Each is a function in 9a’s sense, from pairs of colours to colours, and each is one line of arithmetic in the coordinates of 36b3 scaled into [0, 1]. This step defines them and proves what they do; it does not say where programs put them, which is 36·comp.

Mixing light adds; mixing pigment multiplies. Dodge adds and clamps; burn subtracts and clamps. Screen is multiply inside invert. Darken is the least in each place, lighten the greatest, difference the gap. At the corners 0 and 1 the functions are 1e’s tables.

36h1Two Mixings

From here on, colours on this step are triples with each coordinate scaled into [0, 1], and every function is applied in each coordinate. The light mix of c and d is c + d, clamped at 1 (36c2 with a wall). The pigment mix of c and d is c·d, each coordinate the product: 18·chem’s transmission, one material through the other. Light mix is never darker than either input; pigment mix is never lighter. Light mix has (0, 0, 0) as its do-nothing and pigment mix has (1, 1, 1): 6a4’s zero and one, for free, in colour.

Two-mixings technique: for all c, d in [0, 1]³, c·d ≤ min(c, d) ≤ max(c, d) ≤ c + d clamped, coordinate by coordinate. Proof: for x, y in [0, 1], xy ≤ x since y ≤ 1 (4h10 and 5d8, multiplying keeps order), and x ≤ x + y since y ≥ 0; the rest is the definition of min and max.

So the same two colours have two mixes, and which one the world uses is a fact by cases with its rate (36g3): lights add, pigments multiply, and a student who adds pigments has used the wrong function.

36h2Dodge and Burn

From here on, dodge(c, d) = clamp(c + d), each coordinate; and burn(c, d) = clamp(c + d − 1). Dodge is the light mix with its wall made explicit; burn is the same addition shifted down by one, so that it only leaves 0 when the two together pass 1. Their accepted names are linear dodge and linear burn.

Dodge–burn technique: burn(c, d) = inv(dodge(inv c, inv d)), where inv(x) = 1 − x in each coordinate.

\mathrm{inv}(\mathrm{dodge}(\mathrm{inv}\,c, \mathrm{inv}\,d)) = 1 - \mathrm{clamp}((1-c) + (1-d)) @@ the definitions
= 1 - \mathrm{clamp}(2 - c - d) @@ 5d3, the adding rules
= \mathrm{clamp}(c + d - 1) @@ 1 − clamp(2 − t) = clamp(t − 1) for every real t: check t < 1, 1 ≤ t ≤ 2, t > 2
\htmlClass{new}{= \mathrm{burn}(c, d)} \quad \blacksquare @@ the definition

Both are associative on all of [0, 1]: dodge(dodge(c, d), e) = clamp(c + d + e) = dodge(c, dodge(d, e)), because no coordinate is negative, so a wall once hit stays hit. Burn is the same seen through inv. The walls still matter for other functions: difference is not associative (36h6). Here e means a third colour, beside c and d, not 17e's number e.

36h3Screen

From here on, screen(c, d) = 1 − (1 − c)(1 − d), each coordinate.

Screen technique: screen(c, d) = inv(c·d conjugated), that is, screen is the pigment mix done on the inverts and inverted back: screen(c, d) = inv(inv(c)·inv(d)). Proof: the definition, read as 1 − (inv c)(inv d).

So screen is to pigment mix as burn is to dodge: the same function seen through inv. Screen is never darker than either input (since (1 − c)(1 − d) ≤ 1 − c), and (0, 0, 0) is its do-nothing. It is the mix of two lights where each light’s amount is read as a fraction of what is left, rather than added outright, which is why it never hits a wall.

Inv is 1b’s not at the level of coordinates, and “screen is pigment-mix through inv” is 1f7’s pushing a not inward: the not of an and is the or of the nots. 36h5 makes that exact.

36h4Darken, Lighten, Difference

From here on, darken(c, d) = min(c, d), lighten(c, d) = max(c, d), and difference(c, d) = |c − d|, each coordinate (8h’s absolute value). Darken and lighten keep whichever input is less or more in each place; difference is 0 exactly where the two agree, which is why it finds what changed between two pictures.

Lattice technique: darken and lighten are each commutative, associative and idempotent, and each distributes over the other. Proof: these are the laws of least and greatest on a line where any two compare (8b2), coordinate by coordinate.

Colour-difference technique: difference(c, d) = (0, 0, 0) exactly when c = d; difference(c, d) = difference(d, c). Proof: 8h’s |x| is 0 only at 0, and |x − y| = |y − x|.

36h5The Corners

Take the corners only: colours whose coordinates are each 0 or 1. On the corners, 0 is F and 1 is T, 1e1’s marks for the ways a statement can come out and 4·comp’s switches, and each function of this step is one of 1e8’s tables, coordinate by coordinate. Pigment mix is and (1 · 1 = 1, anything · 0 = 0). Screen is or. Darken is and and lighten is or again. Difference is or-but-not-both (1c11). Inv is not. Dodge is or with the wall (1 + 1 clamped is 1), and burn is and with the floor.

Corners technique: on the corners, pigment mix = darken = and, screen = lighten = or, difference = xor, inv = not. Proof: 1e8’s tables, checked at the four pairs (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1) in each coordinate.

Between the corners the functions differ: pigment mix and darken agree at the corners and not at (½, ½), where one is ¼ and the other ½. So the corners are a world in which every entry of 1f’s rulebook holds, and the whole interval is a world of 7b6’s kind, where some hold and some fail. Pushing a not inward (1f7) holds for pigment mix and screen everywhere (36h3: screen is inv around multiply). The either-way principle fails between the corners for lighten: lighten(x, inv x) = max(x, 1 − x) is 1 only at the corners and ½ at ½. That is 7b1’s principle, true in the two-marks world and false in the one on [0, 1], by calculation; 7b7 built a two-stage world to show the same, and the strip of greys is such a world made of paint.

The accepted names

Light mix is additive mixing, and in picture programs the add or linear dodge blend; pigment mix is multiply, and its accepted name in colour science is subtractive mixing. Screen, darken, lighten and difference keep their names as blend modes. inv is the invert, or the complement. The corners are the Boolean values, and the corners technique says these blends extend Boolean algebra to [0, 1]; min and max with 1 − x are the operations of fuzzy logic. The lattice technique says min and max make a distributive lattice.

Who said it first

George Boole wrote the algebra of the two values 0 and 1 in 1847 and 1854, with multiplication for and; the corners of this step are his tables. Lotfi Zadeh took min, max and 1 − x onto all of [0, 1] in 1965, the world of 7b6’s kind this step finds in the strip of greys. Thomas Porter and Tom Duff set out the arithmetic of combining pictures in 1984.

A proof that is not allowed

“Pigment mix and darken agree at all four corner pairs. A function on [0, 1] is fixed by its values at 0 and 1. So they agree everywhere.” Not allowed: the second line is false; a function is its pairs (9a), all of them. At (½, ½) one gives ¼ and the other ½.

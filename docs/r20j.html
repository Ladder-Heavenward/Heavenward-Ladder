20j · technique

Turns in coordinates

A turn about the origin is decided by one point: where it sends (1, 0). Record that point, and the turn is a pair of numbers. This step works with turns as pairs. Two turns make a third by a rule for pairs, the rule works in either order, and undoing changes one sign. A pair whose squares do not add to 1 is still a move: a turn and a stretch.

Every other point follows by the same mixing of across and up. One turn then another is a turn, and its pair comes from the two pairs by a fixed rule of multiplying. Turning the second number’s sign gives the turn that undoes it.

20j1A turn is a pair

Turn-is-a-pair technique: a turn about the origin is fixed by where it sends (1, 0). If it sends (1, 0) to (a, b), it sends (0, 1) to (−b, a), and every (x, y) to (ax − by, bx + ay).

R(0, 0) = (0, 0),\ \ R(1, 0) = (a, b) @@ a turn about O; a² + b² = 1, since R keeps the length 1
R(0, 1) \text{ is 1 from } O \text{ and } \sqrt{2} \text{ from } (a, b) @@ R keeps distances (20d)
(-b, a) \text{ and } (b, -a) \text{ fit; only } (-b, a) \text{ keeps } D @@ D(O, (a, b), (−b, a)) = a² + b² = 1, as for (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1)
\htmlClass{new}{R(x, y) = (ax - by,\ bx + ay)} \quad \blacksquare @@ three points decide (20f2)

From here on, R(a, b) is the turn by (a, b), and the pair is called the turn. These are 18f2’s turns. 18f2 turned points by pairs; this footing shows that every move that turns the plane about the origin is one of them.

20j2Turns combine by a rule for pairs

Pair-product technique: the turn by (a, b) after the turn by (c, d) is the turn by (ac − bd, ad + bc). Follow (1, 0): the first turn sends it to (c, d), and the second sends (c, d) to (ac − bd, bc + ad).

R_{(a,b)}(R_{(c,d)}(1, 0)) = R_{(a,b)}(c, d) @@ 20j1
= (ac - bd,\ bc + ad) @@ the formula, at (c, d)
\htmlClass{new}{R_{(a,b)} \circ R_{(c,d)} = R_{(ac - bd,\ ad + bc)}} \quad \blacksquare @@ a turn after a turn is a turn (20e); 20j1 fixes it

This is 18f2’s product of turns, now for every turn of 20c. The rule does not change when (a, b) and (c, d) swap places. So turns about the same point give the same turn in either order.

20j3Undoing a turn

Pair-undo technique: the turn by (a, −b) undoes the turn by (a, b).

(a, b) \cdot (a, -b) = (a^2 + b^2,\ -ab + ab) @@ the pair product
= (1, 0) @@ a² + b² = 1: the turn that does nothing
\htmlClass{new}{R_{(a,b)}^{-1} = R_{(a,-b)}} \quad \blacksquare @@ either order, by 20j2

So the undo of a turn is its flip over the floor: (a, b) and (a, −b) are mirror points.

20j4Turn and stretch

Turn-and-stretch technique: for any pair (a, b) other than (0, 0), (x, y) ↦ (ax − by, bx + ay) is a turn followed by a stretch by r = √(a² + b²). And such pairs combine by the same rule, with the stretches multiplying.

(a, b) = r\,(a/r,\ b/r),\ \ (a/r)^2 + (b/r)^2 = 1 @@ r = √(a² + b²) > 0
(ax - by,\ bx + ay) = r \cdot R_{(a/r,\ b/r)}(x, y) @@ take r out of each coordinate
(ac - bd)^2 + (ad + bc)^2 = (a^2 + b^2)(c^2 + d^2) @@ share out; the 2abcd terms cancel
\htmlClass{new}{\text{lengths multiply, turns combine}} \quad \blacksquare

20j.4.5seamPairs of numbers turn the plane, and multiply like the turns they are. A mathematician spent fifteen years looking for triples of numbers that would turn space the same way. The accepted names

A turn by (a, b) is a rotation, usually written with the cosine and sine of its angle, which arrive on rung 21. The pair product is complex multiplication, and the pairs with squares adding to 1 form the circle group. The fraction pairs of 20j.4.2 are rational points on the circle, made from Pythagorean triples.

Who said it first

In 20j.4.5, the mathematician is William Rowan Hamilton, who carved i² = j² = k² = ijk = −1 into Broom Bridge, Dublin, on 16 October 1843: the quaternions. In 1877 Frobenius proved that no number system with three parts could divide the way pairs do.

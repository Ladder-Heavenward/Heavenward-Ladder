19·∎ · conclusion

Moves combine, undo, and include doing nothing

Ask only that moves combine, undo, and include doing nothing. Then doing nothing and undoing are unique, undoing runs backwards, parts divide the whole, and shuffles keep their parity.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 19e parity of shuffles is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 19 asked what is left of a family of moves when you forget what is moved and keep only how the moves combine. Three demands were left, with brackets that do not matter, and the survey promised that they force a great deal. Everything this rung proves about groups follows from how their members combine, and from nothing else.

19a laid down the demands and showed the shuffles of any set meet them. 19b proved there is one way to do nothing and one way to undo, and from it cancelling. 19c undid two moves, the last first. 19d stacked a group with equal copies of any group inside it. 19e proved every shuffle even or odd. This page puts the claim together.

The claim, in full

In any group, doing nothing and undoing are unique, undoing runs backwards, parts divide the whole, and shuffles keep their parity.

Let G be a set with an operation ab.

Suppose (ab)c = a(bc) for all a, b, c.

Suppose some e has ea = ae = a for all a.

Suppose each a has some a⁻¹ with aa⁻¹ = a⁻¹a = e.

Then e is the only member that does nothing, and each a has only one inverse.

ab = ac gives b = c, and ax = b has exactly one answer.

(ab)⁻¹ = b⁻¹a⁻¹.

If G is finite and H is a part-group, |H| divides |G|. The order of each member divides |G|.

Every shuffle of {1, …, n} is a string of swaps, and all its strings are even, or all are odd.

The proof

Each part below is a technique already proved on a step of this rung. The proof cites them by name and joins them.

e = ee' = e' @@ the one-identity technique
b = b(ac) = (ba)c = c @@ the one-inverse technique
ab = ac \Rightarrow b = c,\ \ ax = b \iff x = a^{-1}b @@ the cancel and one-answer techniques
\htmlClass{new}{a^m a^n = a^{m+n}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the powers technique
  
(ab)(b^{-1}a^{-1}) = a(bb^{-1})a^{-1} = e @@ regroup, twice
\htmlClass{new}{(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the socks-and-shoes technique
  
|aH| = |H| @@ the copies-same-size technique: h ↦ ah, one-to-one by cancelling
aH \cap bH \ne \varnothing \Rightarrow aH = bH @@ the copies-never-overlap technique
|G| = k\,|H| @@ the part-divides-whole technique
\htmlClass{new}{n \mid |G|,\ \ a^{|G|} = e} \quad \blacksquare @@ the order-divides technique
  
\sigma = t_1 \cdots t_m @@ the swaps-build-every-shuffle technique
N \text{ changes by an odd number at each swap} @@ the swap-is-odd technique
\htmlClass{new}{m \text{ and } N(\sigma) \text{ are both even or both odd}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the parity technique

No line above asks what the moves are. Cards, wheels, numbers under adding, the corners of a square: each line holds for all of them at once. That is what it means to say that moves combine, undo, and include doing nothing.

What the rest of the rung adds

Each step and annex after the proof steps adds a line to the claim. A line appears here once you have finished its page.

  • 19f symmetries by hand: the moves that leave a polygon looking the same are a group of 2n for a regular n-gon, counted by sending one corner somewhere and its neighbour beside it. A square card has eight: four turns on the table and four half turns in space, and every technique of the rung can be watched on them.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 19·chem which moves leave a molecule unchanged?: A molecule’s moves sort the ways of swapping in new atoms into classes, one class to a compound; class times keepers is the number of moves, and the count of compounds tells a square methane from a tetrahedron.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 19·bio why not just burn the sugar?: Electrons counted like a ledger, an energy drop that depends only on the ends, coins of a fixed size that a fire cannot bank, and a membrane whose proton coin is worth RT ln(ratio) plus a charge times a voltage.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 19·eng what limits an engine’s work per turn?: Work is an area under pressure against volume; air squeezed fast heats as r^0.4; a loop’s area is the work per turn, and the four strokes turn at most 1 − r^(−0.4) of the heat into work.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 19·crypt can a letter ever stand for itself?: A rotor machine’s shuffle is S⁻¹RS by socks and shoes, so it never sends a letter to itself and undoes itself; its settings number about 1.6 × 10²⁰.its line appears when you finish it.
  • 19·comp how many ways can a puzzle cube be scrambled?: It shows group facts alone bounding what can be reached: parity, copies of a part-group and socks-and-shoes show twisting reaches at most one way in twelve of putting the cube back.its line appears when you finish it.

That is the first algebra of moves. Every line in it holds for every group there is. You have only just begun to move things.

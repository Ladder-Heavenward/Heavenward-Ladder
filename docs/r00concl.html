Things have ways to be the same

Sameness behaves the way “the same” ought to. Each thing is the same as itself, sameness turns about and chains, and two things the same as one thing are the same as each other.

The conclusion · the rung's claim, in full, and its proof

This page opens once every question on 0c make your case is ticked. The claim below is proved from the steps of this rung, so it waits for them.

What the rung set out to show

The survey of rung 0 promised a floor with nothing beneath it: a thing is the same as itself, and a fact may be carried across a sameness. 0a gave the names and the statements. 0b gave sameness its mark and its two axioms. 0c gave an argument its form and argued the first two techniques, one move to a sentence.

This page puts the rung's claim together in one place. The title says “things have ways to be the same”. The rung laid down three axioms, and each is a way. One claim can take several shapes and stay one claim. A thing is the same as itself. A thing under two names is one thing. The ways sameness behaves follow, and each is proved here from those axioms alone.

The claim, in full

Sameness is a sameness: it is true of each thing with itself, it turns about, it chains, and two things the same as a third are the same as each other.

One claim, said in words or written in marks, is one claim in two shapes.

Let x, b and c be names.

Then x = x.

From x = b, you may write b = x.

From x = b and b = c, you may write x = c.

From x = c and b = c, you may write x = b.

The proof

The part about shapes is not proved. It is 0a's possibility axiom: whatever can be said can be written down, and the writing is a way of writing that one claim.

The first part is not proved. It is the self-sameness axiom, filled with a name, and there is nothing beneath it.

By the self-sameness axiom, a = a. ∎

The second and third parts are the turn-about technique and the chain technique, argued on 0c. Here they are again, so the whole claim stands on one page.

The second part. a = b is given. By the self-sameness axiom, a = a. Read a = a as a statement about a, with its gap at the left place only. By the swap axiom, on a = b, swap b in there: b = a. ∎

The third part. a = b is given, and b = c is given. Read a = b as a statement about b. By the swap axiom, on b = c, say c where b stood: a = c. ∎

The fourth part is new on this page. Two things that are each the same as one thing are the same as each other. It needs no new axiom. Turn one sameness about, and the chain does the rest.

a = c is given, and b = c is given. By the turn-about technique, on b = c, c = b. By the chain technique, on a = c and c = b, a = b. ∎

The fourth part is the oldest of the four in writing. It stands first in the short list of things the oldest whole book of geometry asks you to grant. There it is granted. Here it is proved, from a smaller grant.

Anexact, yet rigorous · the ledger

0c set beside the argument a second kind of care: the ledger, which measures what it cannot prove, and says in numbers how wrong it might be.

Each argument on this page is a ledger of its own. Every sentence is paid for by its reason, and nothing is claimed that was not paid for. Nature is checked in the same way, one entry at a time.

That is the ground floor, proved. Everything above it is still ahead of you, and the ladder has no top.

Why Is Stress a Tensor?

Why is stress a tensor? 33·eng wrote the push on a cut through a sheet as a grid of numbers, and 27·phys wrote the spin of a turning body the same way. Both grids were written on axes someone chose. Turn the axes and every number in the grid changes, yet the sheet and the body have not moved. This page finds what the numbers must do when the axes turn, and why they cannot do anything else: the technique is 47h’s tensor product carrying maps along. A quantity that obeys it belongs to the thing measured. A quantity that breaks it belongs to whoever drew the axes.

Stress and inertia are readings of two vectors, so each is one reading of the tensor product of space with itself. When the axes turn to the columns of a length-keeper Q, a vector’s list becomes Qᵀx and the grid of such a reading becomes QᵀGQ. That technique is forced: sending each space V to V ⊗ V and each map f to f ⊗ f is a map of categories. Because of it, the stretches of the grid and its rank are the same on every axes: the principal stresses and the principal inertias belong to the sheet and the body.

47·phys.1Two Grids, Read Two Ways

33·eng6 wrote the push per area on a cut whose vector out of the cut is n, of length 1, as σn, and found σ symmetric. 27·phys4 wrote the spin of a body turning at ω as Θω, with Θ = Σ mᵢ(|rᵢ|²I − rᵢrᵢᵀ), also symmetric. A grid that turns a vector into a vector can also be read on two vectors: read the vector it makes along a second vector.

Face-and-axis technique: the stress grid read on two vectors of length 1, mᵀσn, is the push along m on the cut facing n; the inertia grid read twice on one vector u of length 1, uᵀΘu, is the rotational inertia about the axis along u. So each grid is a reading straight in each of two slots, and one reading of ℝ³ ⊗ ℝ³. Proof: mᵀσn = m · (σn) is the part of the push σn along m (27i). For Θ, uᵀΘu = Σ mᵢ(|rᵢ|² − (rᵢ · u)²), and by the right-triangle law (18a) |rᵢ|² − (rᵢ · u)² is ρᵢ², the square of the distance of the piece from the axis, so the sum is 27·phys3’s I = Σ mρ². Each reading is straight in each slot, so by the two-slot technique (27h2) it is xᵀGy, and by 47h1 with U = k it runs through ℝ³ ⊗ ℝ³ as one reading.

47·phys.2Turning the Axes

Choose new axes: a right-angled basis of vectors of length 1, written as the columns of a grid Q. By the length-keeper technique (27m1) QᵀQ = I, so Q is undone by Qᵀ. A vector whose list on the old axes is x has the list x′ on the new axes with x = Qx′, so x′ = Qᵀx.

New-axes technique: when the axes turn to the columns of a length-keeper Q, a vector’s list becomes Qᵀx, and the grid of a reading of two vectors becomes QᵀGQ; the number the reading gives does not change. Proof: B(x, y) = xᵀGy = (Qx′)ᵀG(Qy′) = x′ᵀ(QᵀGQ)y′, since the turn of a row of two grids is the row of their turns the other way round.

\sigma = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \tau\\ \tau & 0\end{pmatrix},\ \ Q = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & -1\\ 1 & 1\end{pmatrix} @@ 33·eng.4’s sliding sheet; Q turns the axes by 45°
\sigma Q = \tfrac{\tau}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix} @@ row by column
Q^{\mathsf T}\sigma Q = \tfrac{\tau}{2}\begin{pmatrix}2 & 0\\ 0 & -2\end{pmatrix} @@ the new-axes technique
\htmlClass{new}{Q^{\mathsf T}\sigma Q = \begin{pmatrix}\tau & 0\\ 0 & -\tau\end{pmatrix}} \quad \blacksquare @@ a pull τ and a push τ, no shear: 33·eng6’s stress-axes technique, read as a turning of the axes

Nothing in the sheet changed. On the first axes the push on every cut slides along the cut; on the turned axes it pulls straight across one cut and pushes straight across the other. One stress, two lists of four numbers.

47·phys.3The Technique Is a Map of Categories

Why must a stress obey the new-axes technique? Because the technique is not about grids at all. Let f : V → V′ be a straight map. The map (v, w) ↦ f(v) ⊗ f(w) from V × V to V′ ⊗ V′ is straight in each slot, so by 47h1 it runs through V ⊗ V as exactly one straight map, written f ⊗ f, with (f ⊗ f)(v ⊗ w) = f(v) ⊗ f(w).

Square-the-space technique: sending each space of vectors V to V ⊗ V, and each straight map f to f ⊗ f, is a map of categories (47b): (g ∘ f) ⊗ (g ∘ f) = (g ⊗ g) ∘ (f ⊗ f), and the do-nothing map goes to the do-nothing map. A reading B of V ⊗ V is carried to the new axes as B ∘ (Q ⊗ Q), and in grids that is QᵀGQ. Proof: both sides of the first line send v ⊗ w to g(f(v)) ⊗ g(f(w)); the v ⊗ w reach every member of V ⊗ V as sums (the grid-space technique, 47h1), and two straight maps that agree on the eᵢ ⊗ eⱼ agree everywhere. For the last line, B ∘ (Q ⊗ Q) sends x′ ⊗ y′ to B(Qx′ ⊗ Qy′) = (Qx′)ᵀG(Qy′), which is the new-axes technique; readings run the map first, turned round, as 47b3’s turned-round technique says.

\text{turn by } Q_1 \text{, then by } Q_2:\ \ x'' = Q_2^{\mathsf T}Q_1^{\mathsf T}x @@ the new-axes technique twice
G'' = Q_2^{\mathsf T}(Q_1^{\mathsf T}GQ_1)Q_2 = (Q_1Q_2)^{\mathsf T}G\,(Q_1Q_2) @@ the turn of a row, the other way round
\htmlClass{new}{\text{one turn by } Q_1Q_2 \text{ gives the same grid}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the square-the-space technique: the technique respects running in a row

So a grid that claims to describe the sheet cannot pick its own technique for new axes. Suppose a technique said “on any axes, the stress has 1 in the top left corner and 0 elsewhere.” Turn the axes a quarter turn: the new-axes technique sends that grid to the one with 1 in the bottom right. The two claims disagree, so the technique describes no sheet. Physics calls a grid of measurements a tensor exactly when its numbers change by the new-axes technique, and the square-the-space technique says that is the same as being one member, or one reading, of a tensor product.

47·phys.4What Every Axes Agree On

Some things about a grid survive every turning of the axes. Those belong to the sheet or the body itself.

Same-on-every-axes technique: QᵀGQ has the same stretches as G and the same rank. So the principal stresses of a sheet, the principal inertias of a body, and the rank of any reading of two vectors are the same on every axes. Proof: QᵀGQ v′ = λv′ exactly when G(Qv′) = λ(Qv′), multiplying by Q or by Qᵀ, since each undoes the other; so the stretches agree, and the stretched vectors are the same vectors written on the new axes. Q and Qᵀ can be undone, so QᵀGQ reaches the vectors Qᵀ sends from what G reaches, a space of the same dimension (27c).

By the spectral technique (31d3) a symmetric σ is λ₁ u₁ ⊗ u₁ + λ₂ u₂ ⊗ u₂ along its square axes, writing u ⊗ u for the grid u uᵀ. A rope in tension carries a stress of rank 1: one pull along one line. The fewest-terms technique (47h2) then says how many single pulls a stress needs.

\sigma = \tau\,u_1 \otimes u_1 - \tau\,u_2 \otimes u_2,\ \ u_{1,2} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, \pm 1) @@ the stretches ±τ of 47·phys.2
\text{rank } \sigma = 2 @@ two stretches that are not 0
\htmlClass{new}{\text{no } \lambda\, n \otimes n \text{ is a pure shear, on any axes}} \quad \blacksquare @@ the fewest-terms technique and the same-on-every-axes technique

It is the arithmetic of 47h2’s pair of particles. A pure shear needs a pull and a push, as that joint state needs two terms; neither can be written as one product, and turning the axes, or the lab, does not change that.

The accepted names

σ is the Cauchy stress tensor and Θ the inertia tensor; both are tensors of order two, also called second-rank tensors, a use of the word rank different from the rank of a grid on this page. The new-axes technique is the tensor transformation law. A list that changes by Qᵀ is a vector; readings change the other way, and the two kinds are called contravariant and covariant, a difference that vanishes for length-keepers because Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ. The square-the-space technique says the tensor square is a functor. Quantities kept by every turning are invariants; the principal stresses are its eigenvalues. A stress p I is hydrostatic, and σ minus its hydrostatic part is the deviatoric stress. The rank of a joint state is its Schmidt rank.

Who said it first

Augustin-Louis Cauchy described the push on every face of a small tetrahedron by a symmetric grid in 1822, published in 1823 and 1827. The word tensor, used earlier by William Rowan Hamilton for a length, took its present sense in Woldemar Voigt’s crystal physics of 1898. Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and Tullio Levi-Civita set out the calculus of quantities that change by the rule in 1900, and Albert Einstein wrote gravity in it in 1915. Hassler Whitney defined the tensor product of groups by its readings in 1938, and Nicolas Bourbaki made the universal property the standard definition in 1948.

A proof that is not allowed

“The principal stresses are the largest and smallest pushes, and turning the axes does not change the sheet, so they cannot change.” Not allowed as a proof: the conclusion is right, but the line uses what it should show. That the grid on new axes describes the same sheet is exactly the new-axes technique, and that its stretches are the extreme pushes is 33·eng6; the same-on-every-axes technique is what ties the two.

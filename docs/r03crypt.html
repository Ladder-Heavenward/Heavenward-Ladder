Can a Secret Be Figured Before It Goes Stale?

Two friends talk about the game where anyone can hear. A listener tries to work out what they mean. He has until the week turns.

3crypt1A Name That Does Not Stay Put

Can a secret be figured before it goes stale?

On Monday Claire says to Jean, “Did you see the game?” She does not say which game. She does not need to. Their school's team played on Saturday, and on the Monday after, “the game” names the game just played.

A week later she asks the same thing in the same words. Now “the game” names another game.

Mike sits behind them and hears every word. He wants to know what they mean. This step is about how much he can work out, and how long it is good for.

Start with the name. 0a2 gave a letter to a thing and left it there. “The game” does not stay put. It is handed along a run of things, one at a time.

The school's team plays five games this season. Take them in the order they are played, and name them with 2e15's low marks. The other sides are told apart here by the colour of their shirts:

NameGame
g₀against the reds
g₁against the greens, at home
g₂against the blues
g₃against the yellows
g₄against the greens, away

From here on, in this step, the place of a thing in a run is its low mark.

The places here are 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4, the names 3c5 laid down.

From here on, in this step, a moving name is a name that goes with a run and a place in it.

At place n, the moving name names the thing at place n, and no other.

So at place 1, “the game” = g₁. That sameness is good at place 1. It is not laid down for any other place.

Look at g₁ and g₄. Both are against the greens. They are two games all the same: two days, two scores. What tells them apart is the place. The name of a game is not who played in it.

3crypt2The Place Takes the Next One

How does the name get from one game to the next?

The place takes the next one. That is 3c1's move: from n to S(n). At place 0 the name names g₀. When the week turns, the place is S(0), which is 1, and the name names g₁.

Nobody says, “Let us move to the next place.” The week does it. Claire and Jean each take the step alone, on the same day, without a word passing between them.

The place moves by the next one, and both sides take the step without sending anything.

Between one step and the next, the place stands still. “The game” names one game all week. That week is all the time Mike has.

3crypt3Words Made on the Spot

Claire and Jean do not only say “the game”. They say “the save”, “the whistle”, “that last kick”. Where do those names come from?

From the game. Each one is made on the spot, out of something both friends saw. Nobody laid them down beforehand. Two people who watched g₂ and talk about “the save” both mean the same save, because one save in g₂ stood out.

2c2 said that a gap takes only the things in its domain, and that the domain is said first. Here the domain is not said at all. It is the current game.

The things in the current game are the domain, and the place settles which game that is.

Here is the talk put to use. Claire wants Jean to bring some folding chairs on Saturday, and she does not want Mike to know how many. She says:

“Bring as many as there were goals.”

Jean watched. There were three. He brings three chairs.

So a message in this talk has two parts. One is the sentence, said out loud. The other is never said.

From here on, in this step, the key is what the two friends hold and never send.

Here the key is the place together with what happened there. The secret is what the sentence and the key give together: three chairs.

The sentence can be heard by anyone. The key is never sent.

3crypt4What Mike Can Work Out, and What He Cannot

Mike has the sentence. He needs the key. Can he work it out?

The key has two parts, so ask of each.

The place first. Mike can read a calendar. The place moves by a technique anyone can run: from n, write S(n). If he ever knew the place, he can keep up with it for good. So he can work out the place.

Now what happened there. Nothing in g₂ lets you write down the goals of g₃. No technique goes from one game to the next. So there is nothing for Mike to work from.

The place is reached by a technique. What is at the place is not reached from what was at the place before.

Then how could Mike ever get the secret? Not by working it out. He would have to watch the game himself, or be told what happened. Those are ways of finding out. They are not ways of working out.

3crypt5Stale

Suppose Mike does find out. He asks around, and by Thursday he knows there were three goals. How long is that good for?

Until the place takes its next one. After that, “as many as there were goals” is a sentence about another game.

From here on, in this step, a secret is stale once the place it was made at is no longer the current place.

Is it sure that the old place is no longer current? One step first.

Moved-on observation: from “the place took the next one after n”, you may write “the current place is not n”.

That settles one step. After many steps, can the place come round again?

No-way-back observation: from “m is a member of the current place”, you may write “m is not the current place”.

Each named place is a member of each later one (3c10). So among the named places, a place once left is never the current place again. A stale secret stays stale.

Now the question of the step can be answered.

A listener can work out the place. He cannot work out what is at the place; he has to watch it or be told. And whatever he finds out is good until the week turns, and no longer.

So Mike is in a race he mostly loses. Each week he starts with nothing but the place. If he finds out what happened, he has the rest of that week. On Monday he starts again.

3crypt6Out of Step, and Back In

A secret goes stale for a listener. Can it go stale for a friend?

Jean is away for a week and sees nothing. He comes home. Claire is at place 3. Jean is at place 2. He did not take the step, because for him nothing turned the week.

From here on, in this step, two sides are out of step when they hold different places.

How do they find out? Not by looking at their words. Claire says “the save”. Jean thinks of a save in g₂. Both lines read well. They are about two games.

They find out when a line fails. Claire says, “As many as there were goals,” and Jean brings two chairs where three were wanted. Or sooner, if Jean says, “What save?”

Now the repair. Which of them moves?

Jean does. 2 ∈ 3, by 3c7: each named set is the set of those before it. So Jean's place is a member of Claire's. His place is behind, and he goes forward to hers.

The catch-up practice: the side whose place is a member of the other's moves forward to the other's place. Nobody moves back.

Why never back? If Claire went back to 2 to meet Jean, she would make an old place current. Every secret that went stale when the week turned would be fresh again, and Mike has had a week longer to find it out.

Jean cannot catch up by being told the place. He has to hold what is there. So he watches the game he missed, or Claire tells him what happened. If she tells him where Mike can hear, she has sent the key.

3crypt7A Run With a Last Place

Every week the secret goes stale and a new one takes its place. Does that go on for good?

Not over a season. Take the five games again. Their places are 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4. Gather them: J = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}. This is the set of 3d1, and 3d1 proved the fact needed here: S(4) is not a member of J.

So when the week turns after g₄, the place is S(4), and the list has no game there.

A moving name over a run with a last place names nothing once the place has passed it.

A name that names nothing cannot fill a gap. “Did you see the game?”, asked in the spring, is not a wrong question. It is a question that cannot be read. The last secret has gone stale and no fresh one comes.

The friends have two ways on. They can wait for a new list. Or they can pick a run that has no last place.

3crypt8A Run With No Last Place

“Last Tuesday” is a moving name too. Over what run?

Over the Tuesdays. From Wednesday through the Tuesday after, “last Tuesday” names one day. Then the place takes the next one, and it names the day a week on. It holds still for a week and then it shifts.

Is there a last Tuesday of all? Nothing the friends hold says so. After any Tuesday comes a next one, and nothing stops the run. That is the process of 3b5: a next one after each, and no clash in supposing it never runs out.

A moving name over a run with no last place always names something.

So there is always a fresh secret to be had. The moved-on and no-way-back observations hold as before: each step takes the place somewhere it has not been.

Accepted names

A word whose thing depends on when, where and by whom it is said is an indexical, and the habit of speech is deixis: “now”, “here”, “last Tuesday”, “the game”. What two speakers both hold and can lean on without saying is their common ground. Playing back something overheard, in the hope that it still works, is a replay. A key that changes with each use is a rolling key. Two sides holding the same place are in step, or synchronised; getting back in step is resynchronising. A list of keys in which each is used once and then thrown away is a one-time pad. When learning one key gives a listener none of the keys after it, the talk has forward secrecy.

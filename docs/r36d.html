36d · proof

The Circle of Hue

36c ended with a closed loop: the open curve of the spectrum’s spikes joined to the straight segment of the purples. This step bends that loop onto 21a’s unit circle, with purple at angle 0, and finds that at full saturation a colour is an angle and nothing else. Then the functions that live on a circle, 20’s turns and reflections, become functions on colour, and mixing two hues is a point on a chord.

Hue is an angle on the unit circle, purple at 0°. Shifting every hue by the same amount is a turn. The hue opposite a hue is a half turn away. Mixing two hues at equal strength is the midpoint of their chord, which is inside the circle unless the hues are the same.

36d1Bending the Line

The loop of 36c3 is a closed curve: 34’s word for a path that comes back to its start without crossing itself. The unit circle is another. From here on, bend is a function from the loop to the unit circle that is one-to-one and onto, sends the midpoint of the purple segment to the point at angle 0, and runs the spikes from 380 nm at a small positive angle round through the blues, greens and yellows to 700 nm just short of a full turn, with the purples filling the small arc that remains at 0. The ladder lays bend down as a choice: any one-to-one onto function from one closed curve to another would do (34 says they are the same kind of thing: nearness survives the bending), and this one is chosen so that the colour the band lacks sits at the circle’s 0.

From here on, the hue of a colour on the loop is the angle of bend(colour) on the unit circle, in 21j’s radians or in degrees, which are 18f2’s quarter turns counted and made fine. Purple is hue 0; by the choice of bend, blue sits near 90°, green near 180°, yellow near 240°, red near 330°; the numbers are the choice’s, and another bend would give others. Past the ends, ultraviolet and infrared would run on round the circle into the purples’ arc, but the eye gives them three zeros, so as colour they fall to the centre.

Hue-bend technique: bend is a bijection (9d), so every point of the circle is one hue and every hue is one point.

36d2Hue as an Angle

Once hue is an angle, 21a’s facts about angles are facts about hue. Angles wrap: hue 370° is hue 10°, by 21b3’s every real θ and 20nav1’s turns that start again past 360°. Angles have a between: the hues from 180° to 240° are the arc from green to yellow. And an angle is a point on the circle, so a hue is a pair (cos θ, sin θ) of 21b’s coordinates, and two hues are the same exactly when their pairs are.

From here on, for a hue θ, h(θ) is the point (cos θ, sin θ) of the unit circle. h is a function from angles to points, repeating after a full turn (21i2).

Hue-wraps technique: h(θ + 2π) = h(θ). Proof: 21i2, how long until it repeats.

36d3Turning and Reflecting

A function from the circle to itself that keeps distances is one of 20c’s moves: a turn, or a flip across a line through the centre. On hues these are the functions colour has that nothing else has.

From here on, the hue shift by α is turn_α(θ) = θ + α: every hue moved round by the same angle.

Hue-shift technique: turn_α after turn_β is turn_{α+β}; turn_0 is the identity; turn_{−α} undoes turn_α. Proof: 21d2, turning adds arcs, or 20j2, turns combine by a rule for pairs; the hue shifts are 20’s turns of the plane restricted to the circle.

From here on, the opposite hue of θ is opp(θ) = θ + π, a half turn.

Opposite technique: opp(opp(θ)) = θ, and h(opp(θ)) = −h(θ). Proof: two half turns are a full turn (hue-wraps); cos(θ + π) = −cos θ and the same for sin (21e4). The accepted name is the complementary hue.

A flip across the line through 0 and π sends θ to −θ: purple and the hue opposite purple stay, and every other hue is sent to its mirror across the purple–green line. From here on, flip(θ) = −θ. It is 20c3’s flip, 21e1’s reversed angle, and the only one that keeps purple at 0.

36d4Mixing on the Circle

Two hues mixed at equal strength are, by 36c2, the sum of their triples, and halved to keep the strength, the average: in the circle’s coordinates, the midpoint of the chord from h(θ) to h(φ) (18g3). That midpoint lies inside the unit circle unless θ = φ, and on the centre when φ = opp(θ). Here φ (little phi) means a second hue angle, after θ, not 2e's condition.

Hue-chord technique: for hues θ ≠ φ, the mix at equal strength has distance from the centre |cos((θ − φ)/2)|, which is less than 1; it is 0 exactly when φ = opp(θ).

So a mix of two hues is not a hue: it has left the circle. Its direction from the centre is the hue (θ + φ)/2, halfway round the short way, and its distance from the centre is less than 1. That distance is what 36e names saturation, and the hue-chord technique is the reason saturation is needed at all: the circle is not closed under mixing.

The accepted names

The circle of hue is a colour wheel, or hue circle; hue is the hue angle. The opposite hue is the complementary hue, and a mix of a hue with its opposite that lands on the centre makes the two a complementary pair. A hue shift is a hue rotation.

Who said it first

Isaac Newton drew the first colour circle in the Opticks of 1704, bending the prism’s band round and joining its ends, and put the mix of colours at their centre of weight, an early form of the hue-chord technique. Johann Wolfgang von Goethe drew his own circle in 1810 and argued against Newton’s account of colour for the rest of his life.

A proof that is not allowed

“Hue 350° and hue 10° average to (350 + 10)/2 = 180°. So their mix has hue 180°.” Not allowed: the hue-chord technique’s direction is the hue halfway round the short way, and the short way from 350° to 10° passes 0°. The average of the numbers is the long way round.

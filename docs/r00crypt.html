How Can a Word Keep a Secret?

Rung 0 gave names to things, and it gave sameness two axioms and two techniques. Three abilities came with that, and the rung used them without stopping to look at them. A name that is the same as another passes wherever the other stood, and nobody asks a second question. A thing can be given any name you like, and as many as you like. And a whole statement can be given a name too, and the name can even be another sentence. Keeping a secret with words uses the same three abilities and nothing more: a password is the first, a codename is the second, and a warning said in cover words is the third.

0crypt1A Password: One Question, and No Others

A guard stands at a gate and holds a card. One word is written on the card. A visitor walks up and says a word. If the word the visitor says is the word on the card, the gate opens.

Give the two words letters, the way 0a2 gave the cat one. Let v be the word the visitor says. Let g be the word on the guard's card. The guard's whole check is one sameness (0b2):

Look at what the guard does not ask. Not who the visitor is, not where they came from, not why they want in. Sameness is the only question. 0c6 said it already: carrying a fact is licensed by one thing only, that the things are the same thing.

Look also at what v and g name. They name words. If the password is “willow”, nobody at the gate is talking about a tree. The word is mentioned, not used (0a9). A password is a word that does its work as a word.

Now a fact gets carried. The word on the card opens the gate. Call “… opens the gate” O, so that is O(g).

v = g @@ given: the guard heard the visitor
O(g) @@ given: the card's word opens the gate
O(v) @@ swap axiom (0c1): v written where g stood

The visitor's word opens the gate. No one argued for it. The sameness was enough, and that is what “passes without question” means.

Passing down a row. The guard's card was copied from a book that stays locked in the office. Let k be the word in the book. The card was copied correctly, so g = k.

v = g @@ given
g = k @@ given: the card was copied from the book
v = k @@ chain technique (0c3)

The visitor said the book's word, and the book never left the office. A second gate has its own card, with the word h on it, copied from the same book: h = k.

v = k @@ from the column above
h = k @@ given: the second card was copied from the book
k = h @@ turn-about technique (0c2)
v = h @@ chain technique (0c3)

The word passes the second gate too. Each guard knows one sameness, their own. The samenesses run in a row, and the word goes down the row. However long the row is, no new technique is needed: chain, and chain again.

What the check earns. It earns v = g, and it earns nothing else. It does not earn “the visitor is a friend”. Anyone who says the word passes, friend or not.

0crypt2A Codename: One Thing, as Many Names as You Like

0a2 had a cat asleep on a bed. She was called cat, she was called Serena, and in logic she was c or s. Nothing made the list stop there. She can be “kitty”. She can be “sleepyhead”. A name is chosen, not found, and choosing a new one changes nothing about the cat.

Each new name comes in with one sameness. Let k be “kitty” and h be “sleepyhead”: k = s and h = s. The new names agree with each other, and rung 0 can show it.

k = s @@ given: “kitty” names Serena
h = s @@ given: “sleepyhead” names Serena
s = h @@ turn-about technique (0c2)
k = h @@ chain technique (0c3)

A codename is a name whose sameness is told to some people and not to others. Evita and Randy are planning a surprise party for Mike. They agree to call the party “Bluebird”. Let b be Bluebird and p be the surprise party for Mike. Their agreement is one sameness, b = p, and only the two of them have it.

At lunch, with Mike sitting right there, Evita says “Bluebird is on Friday.” Call “… is on Friday” F, so she said F(b). Randy works it out:

F(b) @@ given: said out loud at lunch
b = p @@ given: the agreement, held by Evita and Randy
F(p) @@ swap axiom (0c1)

The party is on Friday. Mike heard the same sentence. He has F(b), and he has no sameness with b in it, so he has nothing to swap. To him “Bluebird” does the job of 0a8's “something”: there is a slot, and what fills it is still to be worked out.

So the sentence was said in the open, and the secret was kept. The secret was never in the sentence. It was in the sameness, b = p. A sameness kept back like this is called a key.

One thing, many names: fine. One name, two things: not fine. Serena may have ten names, and the column above shows that they all agree. Now suppose “kitty” is also used for Murphy, her look-alike from 0c6. Call Murphy u.

k = s @@ given: “kitty” names Serena
k = u @@ given: “kitty” names Murphy
s = k @@ turn-about technique (0c2)
s = u @@ chain technique (0c3)

The column says Serena is Murphy, and she is not. Every move was licensed, so the fault is in the givens: one name was handed to two things. Anyone who trusts both givens will carry Serena's facts over to Murphy.

A name that tells. 0a9 showed that some names carry a description inside them. “Sleepyhead” does: it tells a listener that the thing sleeps a lot. “Bluebird” tells nothing about a party. A good nickname tells you everything inside it, a good codename carries nothing, so the only way from the name to the thing is the key.

0crypt3A Warning: A Sentence as the Name of a Statement

A name does not have to be short, and the thing named does not have to be a cat. The possibility axiom (0a1) says any statement can be represented. So a whole statement can be the thing, and it can be given a letter.

Take the statement “We need to leave, because it isn't safe.” Call the whole statement S. Now S is a name, and the statement is the thing it names, just as s is a name and the cat is the thing.

By 0crypt2, a thing can have any name you like. So this statement can have another name, and the other name can be a sentence. 0a9 said that a sentence between quote marks is itself a name. A family agrees ahead of time: among us, “We should start carpooling” names the statement that we need to leave because it isn't safe. Let w be that sentence, taken as a name. The agreement is one sameness, w = S: one statement under two names. It is the family's key.

At a crowded gathering, Dad says “We should start carpooling.” Two kinds of listener hear the same words.

A stranger takes the sentence as used. To the stranger it is a claim about sharing cars, and not an interesting one.

The family takes the sentence as a name, and swaps. Call “… is what Dad is telling us” T. It is a claim about the statement, whatever name the statement goes by.

T(w) @@ given: Dad said the sentence, and the sentence names a statement
w = S @@ given: the family's agreement
T(S) @@ swap axiom (0c1)

What Dad is telling us is that we need to leave, because it isn't safe. The family gets their coats. Nobody claimed anything about cars.

All three abilities are at work here together. A statement was given a name (this footing). The name could be anything, even another sentence (0crypt2). And the family did not stop to ask “why carpooling?”; the sameness let the sentence pass (0crypt1).

A name is not correct or incorrect. It only names. The name of S is the mention ““We should start carpooling””: the sentence between its quote marks (0a9).

The cost. Among the family, the sentence now has a job as a name, so it can no longer be used plainly. If Mom one day really does want to share rides and says so, everyone reaches for a coat. A good cover sentence sounds ordinary to a stranger, and is one the family would almost never need to say and mean.

The order. The agreement comes first. Said before the family has the key, “We should start carpooling” is a sentence about cars and nothing else.

0crypt5Two Questions at the Gate

The guard of 0crypt1 asks one question: is the word the visitor says the same as the word on the card? Anyone who overhears the word passes. A second guard can ask a second, different question: is the token in the visitor’s hand the same as the one on the guard’s list? The gate opens only to a visitor who passes both.

v = g @@ the word: something the visitor knows
t = h @@ the token: something the visitor holds
\text{overheard word, no token: the second guard stops them} @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{\text{stolen token, no word: the first guard stops them}} @@ two different thefts needed

Two-question technique: ask two questions of different kinds, one about something known and one about something held, and open only to a visitor who passes both; a thief must then steal two different things. Shown at a door: a visitor with the right word but no token is turned away, a visitor with the token but the wrong word is turned away, and only the visitor with both walks in.

Accepted names

Cryptography is the accepted name for the study of keeping messages secret. A password is a word checked for sameness and nothing else. A codename is a name whose sameness is held by some people only; “code word” is also said. A sentence used as a name for another statement is a code phrase. The sameness that is kept back is the key.

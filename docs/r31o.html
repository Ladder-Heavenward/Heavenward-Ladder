31o · technique

Principal components

A naturalist measures a hundred Dungeness crabs on the beach at Long Beach, Washington: the width of the shell, its length, the size of the claw, the weight. Four numbers for each crab, and a cloud of a hundred points in a space with four directions. Most of the cloud runs along one direction, big crabs against small ones, with a little spread around it. Which direction is that, and how much of the spread does it carry? The spread of the cloud along any direction is a quadratic form, read off one symmetric grid made of the co-spreads. Its axes are the answer. The first axis carries the most spread, the next carries the most of what is left, at right angles, and so on. Keep the first few, and the flat piece they build is the one the whole cloud lies closest to. The axes are found by the techniques of the rung, and nothing about them says what they mean.

The spread of a cloud of data along a direction d of size 1 is dᵀCd, with C the grid of co-spreads. Its axes carry the most spread, in falling order, each at right angles to the ones before, and the stretches are the spreads they carry. The flat piece built by the first r axes is the one the cloud lies closest to.

31o1Spread along a direction

Take n points x₁, …, xₙ in lists of p numbers, with their average already taken away, so that the points add to 0. Stack them as the rows of a grid X. From here on, the spread grid is C = XᵀX/n: its place (j, k) is the average of the jth number times the kth, the co-spread of 30g, and its middle line holds the spreads. It is symmetric.

Spread-along technique: the spread of the points along a direction d of size 1, the average of (xᵢ · d)², is dᵀCd. The shadow of xᵢ on the line through d is (xᵢ · d)d (31b1), the readings xᵢ · d average to 0, and Σᵢ(xᵢ · d)² = |Xd|² = dᵀXᵀXd.

31o2The directions of most spread

Most-spread technique: the direction of most spread is the axis e₁ of C’s largest stretch λ₁, and the spread along it is λ₁; among directions at right angles to e₁, the most spread is λ₂, along e₂; and so on. The spreads along all the axes add to the middle sum of C, the total spread.

From here on, the readings xᵢ · eₖ are the kth components of the points. Different components never co-spread: the average of (xᵢ · e₁)(xᵢ · e₂) is e₁ᵀCe₂ = λ₂ e₁ · e₂ = 0.

31o3The flat piece the cloud lies nearest

Closest-piece technique: among all flat pieces of dimension r through the average, the one built by e₁, …, eᵣ makes the total squared distance from the points least, and that least total is n(λᵣ₊₁ + ⋯ + λₚ).

\textstyle\sum_i |x_i - Px_i|^2 = \sum_i |x_i|^2 - \sum_i |Px_i|^2 = n\,\text{tr}\,C - n\sum_{k \le r} d_k^{\mathsf T}Cd_k @@ the square-sum technique; |Px|² = Σ(x · dₖ)² (31b2); tr C, the middle sum
\textstyle\sum_{k \le r} d_k^{\mathsf T}Cd_k = \sum_j \lambda_j\,w_j,\ \ w_j = \sum_{k \le r}(d_k\cdot e_j)^2 @@ the axes-form technique, for each dₖ
0 \le w_j \le 1,\ \ \textstyle\sum_j w_j = r @@ the energy-bound technique (31h2): eⱼ read along the list dₖ; each dₖ has size 1
\textstyle\sum_j \lambda_jw_j \le \lambda_1 + \cdots + \lambda_r @@ weights of at most 1 each, r in all, do best on the r largest
\htmlClass{new}{\textstyle\sum_i |x_i - Px_i|^2 \ge n(\lambda_{r+1} + \cdots + \lambda_p)} \quad \blacksquare @@ with equality when dₖ = eₖ

For the four points, the line along e₁ leaves a total squared distance of 4 × 0.17 = 0.69, against 4 × 1 = 4 for the line along the first axis of the page. In practice, points with many numbers each are kept as a few components, and the rest of the spread is reported as lost.

Which units the numbers are in decides C, so it decides the axes. Measure claw size in millimetres and weight in kilograms, and the first axis is almost all claw. That is why each number is often first divided by its own spread width, which uses the grid of correlations instead. Neither choice is the true one; the report must say which was made.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

The spread grid is the sample covariance matrix (often with n − 1 in place of n), and with each number divided by its spread width it is the correlation matrix. Its axes are the principal axes, the readings along them the principal component scores, and the method is principal component analysis (PCA). The share λ₁/(λ₁ + ⋯ + λₚ) is the proportion of variance explained. The bound with the weights wⱼ is Ky Fan’s maximum principle. Finding the axes from the SVD of X is how machines do it.

Who said it first

Karl Pearson in 1901 found the lines and planes of closest fit to systems of points in space. Harold Hotelling in 1933 found the components of a set of test scores, and gave them their name. Ky Fan proved the bound on sums of the largest stretches in 1949.

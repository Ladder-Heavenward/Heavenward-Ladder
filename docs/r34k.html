34k · technique

Holes That Do Not Depend on the Cutting

The chain count of 34j used a cutting into triangles. A different cutting, or a space of the same shape (34e), must give the same holes, or the count measures the knife, not the space. It does not depend on the cutting: chains can be built from every continuous map of a triangle, with no cutting at all. And in dimension 1 the count is the loop group of 34i with its letters allowed to change places. This step carries holes along maps, reads H₁ off loops, and counts the holes of every surface.

A continuous map f carries chains to chains and keeps edges, so it carries Hₖ to Hₖ; maps that slide into each other carry it the same way. So spaces of the same shape have the same holes. H₁ is the loop group with letters allowed to change places.

34k1Holes Carried Along Maps

f(\partial c) = \partial f(c) @@ the edge of the image is the image of the edge
\partial c = 0 \Rightarrow \partial f(c) = 0;\ \ c = \partial b \Rightarrow f(c) = \partial f(b) @@ holes go to holes, edges to edges
f \text{ slides into } g \Rightarrow f, g \text{ carry } H_k \text{ the same} @@ the slide sweeps out a chain between them
\htmlClass{new}{X, Y \text{ the same shape} \Rightarrow H_k(X) = H_k(Y)} @@ whatever the cutting

Carried-holes technique: every continuous map carries holes to holes; maps that slide into one another carry them alike; so spaces of the same shape, cut any way, have the same Hₖ.

34k2One-Holes Are Loops, Turned About

\text{a loop is a chain with no edge} @@ its end is its start
\text{loop } \alpha\beta \mapsto \alpha + \beta @@ following one then the other: adding
\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1} \mapsto 0 @@ in H₁, the order is lost
\htmlClass{new}{H_1 = \text{the loop group with its letters allowed to change places}} @@ the turn-about count (34i3)

Loop-hole technique: for a space in one piece, H₁ is its loop group with every pair of letters allowed to change places. So the figure eight, whose loop group does not turn about (34i), has H₁ = ℤ²: the order of its two loops is lost.

34k3Every Surface Counted

H_0 = \mathbb Z,\ H_1 = \mathbb Z^{2g},\ H_2 = \mathbb Z @@ the turn-about count of 34i3
V - E + F = 1 - 2g + 1 = 2 - 2g @@ the alternating count (34j3)
k \text{ cross-caps: } H_1 = \mathbb Z^{k-1} \times \{0, 1\},\ H_2 = 0 @@ one-sided: no surface without an edge as a chain
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the holes of a closed surface say which surface it is}} @@ the classification (34f), read off Hₖ

The holes of a closed surface name it: H₂ says whether it is two-sided, and H₁ how many tori or cross-caps it is made of.

The cellar · there if you want it

Accepted names

Chains from every map of a triangle are singular chains; carried holes are induced maps, and their sameness under slides is homotopy invariance. H₁ as the turned-about loop group is the Hurewicz theorem in dimension 1; letters allowed to change places is abelianization.

Who said it first

Solomon Lefschetz and James Alexander built singular chains between 1922 and 1944, Samuel Eilenberg in final form in 1944; Witold Hurewicz proved the loop link in 1935.

Where It Keys In

The rung spent six rungs of mathematics on a sound and on what an ear keeps of it. This step says, one footing per rung, what each of 31 to 36 gave and what sound gave back. Then it ends with the rung’s last technique, the one its name is about: a sound that has lost part of itself on the way still tells part of what was sent, exactly, and sometimes all of it.

One footing for each of rungs 31 to 36: what each gave, and what sound gave back. Then the rung’s last technique: a signal partly lost can still be partly told.

37j1Rung 31

31 gave functions lengths and angles. A stretch of sound is a function of time, so it is an arrow with a length, its energy, and an angle with every other stretch. Turnings at different frequencies are at right angles, which is why intensities add (37c1). A filter keeps the arrow’s shadow on the turnings it passes (37h2), and the best canceller is 31b2’s closest point (37h4). Sound gave back the projection that is heard every day: every phone call is a shadow of a voice on the turnings from 300 to 3400 Hz.

37j2Rung 32

32 gave the sum of turnings and the transform. Every repeating sound is such a sum (37b1), its recipe is the list of coefficients (37d1), the sound picture is the transform of short stretches with 32f’s price (37f1), and 32g’s samples are why a sound can be written down as a list of numbers. Sound gave back the place where 32’s idea was first heard before it was written: the ear sorts a sound into bands (37d3), a rough transform made of flesh.

37j3Rung 33

33 gave rates in space and time. The air obeys 33c’s wave equation (37a2), a tube’s formants are 33c3’s held shapes with one end open (37f3), and the share the air takes each metre is the heat rule’s forgetting, laid on top of the wave’s keeping (37e1). 33g3’s round drum has tones that are not whole multiples, so by 37b1 its sound does not repeat exactly, and its pitch is weak. Sound gave back the wave equation in the one medium everyone lives in.

37j4Rung 34

34 gave nearness without distance. Moving a tune to another key slides every note the same distance along the pitch line (37b3), a map that keeps nearness and can be undone, so the tune keeps its shape. And laying each octave of the pitch line on the next, as 37g3’s notes do, closes the line into a loop: the twelve notes sit round a circle as the hues sat round 36d’s. Sound gave back a circle a student can sing round.

37j5Rung 35

35 gave infinities their sizes. A vowel holds a copy of an interval of reals, so it cannot be listed (37g2). And a sound with a top frequency is fixed by its samples (32g), a list of reals, and by 35e’s arithmetic there are no more such lists than points of the line. So among sounds with a top frequency, a vowel is exactly the size of the line. Sound gave back a case where the uncountable is named by one short word.

37j6Rung 36

36 gave colour as a blindness: the eye keeps three numbers of a spectrum and drops the rest. The ear is blind the other way round. It keeps the spectrum, in bands across about ten octaves (37b2, 37d3), which the eye collapses to three; and it drops the turns (37d4) and whatever a louder neighbour hides (37c4), which the eye has no use for. Two lights that look the same may sound, as chords, quite different; two notes that sound the same may look, on a screen, quite different. Sound gave back the other half of the blindness.

37j7A Signal Partly Lost Can Still Be Partly Told

The rung’s last technique, and what its name means. On the way from a mouth to an ear, a sound loses part of itself: the air cuts its top (37e2), a phone line cuts both ends (37h3), a limiter flattens its peaks (37h5), noise is added. What arrives is not what was sent. The question is what can still be told about what was sent from what arrived.

Let s be the sound sent, an arrow of 31. Let K be the flat piece of the turnings a channel keeps, and let P send each arrow to its closest point in K, its shadow (31b2, 31h4). Let what arrives be r = Ps + n, where n is noise with energy at most ε².

Partly-told technique: partial loss of signal can still be analyzed for partial induction of initial transmission. In marks: the shadow Pr is within ε of Ps, so the kept part of what was sent is told to within the noise; the lost part s − Ps is at right angles to K, and nothing of it is told; and if s is known to come from a family whose kept parts lie more than 2ε apart, s is told in full.

Pr = P(Ps) + Pn = Ps + Pn @@ the shadow keeps adding; the shadow of a shadow is itself (31b2)
\|Pr - Ps\| = \|Pn\| \le \|n\| \le \varepsilon @@ a shadow is never longer than its arrow (31a4, right angles)
s' - s \perp K \Rightarrow Ps' = Ps @@ any sound differing from s at right angles to K arrives the same
v \ne s \text{ in the family}:\ \|Pr - Pv\| \ge \|Pv - Ps\| - \|Pr - Ps\| > 2\varepsilon - \varepsilon @@ 31a4, the triangle
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the member nearest } Pr \text{ in its kept part is } s} \quad \blacksquare @@ told in full, when the family is known

The case everyone hears. An old phone line keeps about 300 to 3400 Hz. A voice at 120 Hz loses its lowest turning, at 120, and the next, at 240; it keeps 360, 480, …, 3360. By the repeat-rate technique (37b1) pitch is the repeat, not the lowest turning, and the kept turnings repeat every 1/120 s: the spacing of the kept harmonics is 120 Hz. Take the family to be voices whose harmonics all have some amount. Two voices of different pitch in that family keep different sets of frequencies, so their kept parts are told apart, and the pitch is told in full. The listener hears a man’s voice at its own pitch, through a line that carries none of it.

The rung’s name is this technique said as a promise. What is lost is lost: the turns the ear drops, the band the line cuts, the peaks the limiter flattens, all at right angles to what is kept, and no cleverness brings them back from r alone. What is kept is kept exactly, to within the noise. And what is known about where the sound came from, its family, can carry the telling across the gap.

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

Hearing the pitch of a voice whose lowest turnings are cut is the missing fundamental, or residue pitch. The shadow P is an orthogonal projection, and telling a member of a known family from its shadow is decoding; the condition that kept parts lie more than 2ε apart is a minimum-distance condition, the same that 43·comp’s codes for damaged messages rest on.

Who said it first

August Seebeck heard the missing fundamental with a siren of holes in 1841 and argued with Georg Ohm over it; Jan Schouten named the residue pitch in 1940. That a phone line carries a voice’s pitch without its lowest turnings was known to the engineers who chose its band.

A proof that is not allowed

“The line keeps nothing below 300 Hz, so the 120 Hz voice arrives with no information about 120 Hz, so its pitch is lost.” Not allowed: the claim takes pitch to be the lowest turning; by 37b1 it is the repeat, and the kept harmonics repeat every 1/120 s, so the pitch is a fact about the kept part.

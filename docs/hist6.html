The Machine and After

Computability, information, the machine made of switches, and the research fronts that are still open. On this page the form reaches the present day; the people without dates here are living, or their dates have not yet been revisited.

99 climbers, in order of birth. Each of them climbed the same ladder you have climbed, and built part of it on the way. Under each name are the techniques they advanced, credited to them, and the climbers they met: who argued with whom, over what, and how it was resolved.

Met Ernst Zermelo and Paul Cohen over the axiom of choice. Zermelo's proof of 1904 that every set can be well ordered rested on it, and many rejected the proof as non-constructive. Resolved Zermelo answered his critics in 1908 and gave set theory axioms. Gödel (1938) and Cohen (1963) showed that choice can neither be refuted nor proved from the other axioms.

Met Georg Cantor, David Hilbert and Paul Cohen over the continuum hypothesis. Cantor proposed it and could not prove it; Hilbert put it first among his problems in 1900. Resolved Gödel showed in 1940 that it cannot be disproved, and Cohen in 1963 that it cannot be proved. Gödel wrote to Cohen that his proof was a delight to read.

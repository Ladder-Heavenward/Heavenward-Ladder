45e · technique

Shapes That Do Not Split

xy = 0 is two lines; x² + y² = 1 is one circle that cannot be split into two smaller shapes. A shape that is not the union of two smaller shapes is whole, and whole shapes match prime ideals as whole numbers match primes. Every shape splits into finitely many whole shapes, in one way, as every number factors into primes. This step matches whole shapes with prime ideals and splits a shape into its pieces.

X is whole when X = Y ∪ Z with Y, Z shapes forces Y = X or Z = X. X is whole exactly when I(X) is prime. Falling chains of shapes stop, since rising chains of ideals do (45a, 45b); so every shape is a union of finitely many whole shapes, none inside another, in one way.

45e1Whole Shapes Are Prime

fg \in I(X):\ X = (X \cap V(f)) \cup (X \cap V(g)) @@ split X by f and g
X \text{ whole} \Rightarrow X \subseteq V(f) \text{ or } X \subseteq V(g) \Rightarrow f \text{ or } g \in I(X) @@ so I(X) is prime (44b)
X = Y \cup Z \text{ smaller}:\ f \in I(Y) \setminus I(X),\ g \in I(Z) \setminus I(X),\ fg \in I(X) @@ not prime
\htmlClass{new}{X \text{ whole} \iff I(X) \text{ prime}} @@ the circle: x² + y² − 1 is unsplittable

Whole-prime technique: a shape is whole exactly when the ideal of polynomials vanishing on it is prime; over ℂ, whole shapes match the prime ideals one to one (45d3).

45e2Falling Chains of Shapes Stop

I(X_1) \subseteq I(X_2) \subseteq \cdots @@ smaller shape, bigger ideal
\text{the ideals stop rising: } I(X_N) = I(X_{N+1}) = \cdots @@ finitely made (45a, 45b)
X_k = V(I(X_k)) @@ each shape comes back from its ideal
\htmlClass{new}{X_N = X_{N+1} = \cdots} @@ the shapes stop falling

Every falling chain of shapes in n-space stops after finitely many steps.

45e3Splitting Into Pieces

\text{a shape with no finite split would split for ever} @@ a falling chain that never stops
X = X_1 \cup \cdots \cup X_r,\ \text{each whole, none inside another} @@ the pieces
V(xz, yz) = \{z = 0\} \cup \{x = y = 0\} @@ a plane and a line through it
\htmlClass{new}{\text{the pieces are unique, as prime factors are}} @@ a whole piece lies in one of the others’ pieces

Piece technique: every shape is the union of finitely many whole shapes, none inside another, and the pieces are fixed by the shape. Over ℂ, V(f) has one piece for each unsplittable factor of f (44d).

Accepted names

A whole shape is irreducible, a shape that does not split, sometimes the only kind called a variety; the pieces are its irreducible components. Falling chains stopping makes the space Noetherian.

Who said it first

Emanuel Lasker (1905) and Emmy Noether (1921) proved the splitting for ideals; Lasker was the world chess champion from 1894 to 1921.

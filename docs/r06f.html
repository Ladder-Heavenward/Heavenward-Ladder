6f · support

Three Things Choice Does

Rung 6 learned to choose. This step names what a choice does to guesswork: it can collapse a process into an answer, make a process give up more, or become a process itself.

Choose to get an answer, choose to learn more, or make the choosing a tool.

6f1Collapse It Into an Answer

A choice can end a process with an answer. A set with a member lets you pick one and name it (6b). The open question “is there one?” collapses to “here is one”, and the proof goes on with it.

6f2Make It Say More

A choice made well gives more than any choice. Pick the least member, as 6b’s rule for least members does, and you know something about your pick: nothing smaller is in the set.

The process is the same. The way you choose makes it tell you more.

6f3Make a Process

A choice can be a process you use again. A function that picks one member from every set in a collection (6b) is a tool. Give it any set from the collection, and it hands back a member.

The choosing is no longer a step in one proof. It is a machine that later proofs can run.

Anexact, yet rigorous · the pick that guides the guess

A choice steers the guess without changing what it is about. It adds no fact about the sets, and it decides what you get to know.

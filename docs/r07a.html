7a · proof

Deduction Revisited

Each rung of this floor has opened by going back to its language: 0a the name and the statement, 1a the line, 2a rows, 3a boxes, 4a the proof, 5a the equation. This step goes back to deduction. A proof does more than settle one claim: once it is written, it hands you a move, and a set of moves is an operator that does something.

Prove it once, then use it as a move. A mark, its condition and its moves make an operator.

7a1A Proof Hands You a Move

A proved if is a move you may make from then on. 4a built a proof as a list of lines, each one licensed. When the last line is an if, “if P then Q”, the proof never has to be written again. Wherever you have P, you may write Q, and cite the proof by name.

That is how every technique on the ladder was made. The swap of 0b, the turn-about of an and in 1c, the strays technique of 4b: each was proved once, and each is now one line of work.

7a2A Mark, Its Condition, Its Moves

An operator is a mark, the condition that says when a line with it holds, and the moves that keep that condition. 5a read each operator this way: = holds when its two sides name one number, and its moves are the ones that keep them naming one number.

The definition gives the mark its condition. The proofs give it its moves. Until the proofs are written, the mark means something but does nothing.

7a3Adding, From Definition to Operator

4f defined adding by repeating the next one. That told you what 1 + 3 names, and nothing more. Then it proved that zero on the left changes nothing, that the order does not matter, and that the brackets do not matter. Each proof is a move.

Now + does something. To see that 0 + (3 + 1) = 1 + 3, you do not unpack either side down to next ones. Zero on the left gives 3 + 1, the order turns it to 1 + 3, and you cite the two proofs. The work of the proofs is done once, and every later line borrows it.

7a4What This Rung Adds

This rung adds one principle, the either-way principle of 7b: every statement holds or its denial does. It is not a new mark. It is a new licence, and with it the old marks get moves they were refused. Not-not out, proof by cases on any statement, and the turned-back techniques of 7e are moves that rung 1’s operators could not make before.

So the rung works the way the floor has worked: a principle is laid down, proofs are written from it, and each proof becomes a move.

Accepted names

A proved if used as a move is a lemma or a derived rule of inference. Building operators from definitions and proved laws is how algebra is set up from axioms.

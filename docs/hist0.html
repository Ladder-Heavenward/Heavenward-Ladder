Mathematics as a Form

Mathematics is a form before it is a subject. A definition is laid down before it is used. An axiom is stated and then never argued with. A proof cites only what came before it, and a technique is named by what it does, not by who found it. The ladder keeps that form from its first rung to its last: every step stands on the steps below, and nothing is taken on authority.

That is why the lessons carry no names. A lesson teaches a move, and a name beside the move would make it a fact about a person rather than a thing you can do. The names were kept to the cellars, under the floor of each step, for whoever wanted them.

This step gives them back. Here, in order of birth, are the people the cellars name: the logicians, the mathematicians, the physicists and the chemists and the economists whose work the ladder teaches without saying so. Each has a line on what they did that the ladder uses, and the techniques they advanced, each credited to them by name.

Every one of them was a climber. They climbed the same ladder, a step at a time, from what the climbers before them had left; where a step was missing they built it, and where two of them disagreed about a step they argued until the step held or fell. Some were wrong for years. Some were right and were not believed. The steps you stood on were built that way, by climbers who were further up a ladder with no top.

There is nothing to answer on these pages. They are the view from the top.

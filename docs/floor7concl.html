F7·∎ · floor conclusion

What the Advanced floor shows

A thing is known by its arrows, and the field’s open questions can now be asked in marks you built.

The floor conclusion · its principles, in the words and marks it built

This page opens once every step of the Advanced floor is ticked. Its principles are proved on the rungs below, so it waits for them.

What the floor set out to show

The Mathematics floor turned the ladder’s tools on themselves. The Advanced floor sends them out along several roads at once, each to the edge of what is known: a machine built from switches and the questions no machine can answer, counting that says how things connect, plus and times on a clock and on their own, the old question of formulas for roots, and at the top, arrows in place of things. It promised to take each road as far as the field has gone, and to prove what is known along the way.

Here are the floor’s seven principles again, one to a rung, each in the words and the marks the floor built. Each links to the rung conclusion that proves it.

The principles, rung by rung

39 · a machine made of switches

True and false are 1 and 0 on a wire. One gate that restores its level builds every table; a ring of nots is a clock when it is odd and a memory when it is even; and a machine that keeps its list among its numbers follows any list, in a count of steps that can be read off the page.

40 · some questions no machine can answer

A tape machine reaches as far as any machine; one machine runs them all; and no machine decides which machines stop, so no machine decides any property of what machines compute.

41 · counting alone says how things connect

A tree on n dots has n − 1 links; a side can be matched exactly when every part of it has enough neighbours; the largest flow equals the smallest cut; and past a size, disorder is impossible.

42 · arithmetic works on a clock

On a clock of n hours plus and times keep every rule; an hour can be undone exactly when it shares no factor with n; powers cycle; and the primes are endless and thin.

43 · the rules of plus and times stand on their own

Plus and times, kept with their rules alone, make a ring: what a map forgets is an ideal, a quotient by a largest ideal is a field, and factoring is unique wherever division with remainder works.

44 · past degree four, no formula

The roots of an equation have a group of symmetries whose part-groups match the fields in between; a formula by roots exists exactly when that group comes apart into turn-about pieces, and past degree four it need not.

45 · arrows matter more than things

A thing is known by its arrows: whatever a universal property names is unique up to exactly one matching, and two things with the same arrows are the same.

What the floor shows

The floor began with a wire. Rung 39 read true and false as 1 and 0, and from one gate that restores its level built a clock, a memory and a machine that follows a list, with a count of how long it takes. Rung 40 made that machine exact, and found that it cannot decide which machines stop. Rung 41 counted dots and links and found that counting alone decides how they connect. Rung 42 put arithmetic on a clock, rung 43 let plus and times stand on their own, and rung 44 used the symmetries of the roots to show that past degree four no formula exists.

On the Advanced floor, each road the ladder built ran to the edge of what is known, and the edges turned out to be the same kind of thing: a structure, and the arrows that keep it.

Rung 45 stepped back and looked at all of it at once. A machine, a graph, a clock, a ring and a group of symmetries are each a world of things and the arrows between them, and what matters is the arrows. The questions the field is still asking are on the research fronts of every rung of this floor, and some of them are now yours.

So the floor demonstrates that the field’s open questions can be stated, exactly, with tools you built yourself. Past it there is no top. The next floor has no measuring stick and no title, only the next guess.

The floor’s measuring stick

Beside this page on the ladder is the floor’s measuring stick: one question a rung, about an hour, of fresh questions. Sit it under a proctor and it becomes a green check. Sit it on your own and it becomes a gold star. It gives no new title. Passing it opens the Endless floor, where the titles stop because the ladder has no top.

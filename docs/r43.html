Past degree four, no formula

Rung eight solved every equation of the second degree with one formula. Equations of the third and fourth degree were solved the same way in the sixteenth century, with cube roots and square roots, and for nearly three hundred years after that the fifth degree resisted everyone. This rung says why, and the answer is not about cleverness. The roots of an equation make a field, and the field has symmetries: ways of moving the roots about that keep every relation of adding and multiplying among them. Those symmetries form a group, the group of the equation. Fields between the rationals and the roots match the smaller groups inside it, one for one. A formula by roots is a tower of fields, each floor adding one root of something below, and each floor turns out to be a step of the group that is a simple clock. So a formula exists exactly when the group comes apart into clocks. The shuffles of five things contain a group of sixty that cannot be cut at all, and an equation of degree five whose group is all the shuffles has no formula. On the way, the rung counts what a group’s size forces, finally proves that the old problems of building with compass and straightedge cannot be solved, builds the seventeen-sided figure, and works it all by hand.

The roots of an equation make a field, and its symmetries make a group. Middle fields match smaller groups. A formula by roots is a tower of root steps, and exists exactly when the group comes apart into clocks of prime size. The even shuffles of five things cannot be cut, so the shuffles of five do not come apart, and an equation of degree five with all of them as its group has no formula. Compass and straightedge reach exactly the towers of square roots.

What it means to say it

To say that past degree four there is no formula is to say this. For each degree up to four, one expression in the coefficients, built by the four operations and roots, gives every answer of every equation of that degree. For degree five and every degree above, no such expression exists, and particular equations, like x⁵ − 4x + 2 = 0, have answers that no expression by roots of rational numbers reaches at all.

An equation has a formula by roots exactly when the group of symmetries of its roots comes apart, step by step, into clocks of prime size; the shuffles of five or more roots do not, so past degree four there is no formula. The rung proves it, and two facts are held in trust where it says so.

Where it comes from

The cubic was solved in Italy around 1515 and 1535 and printed in 1545, with the quartic. In 1770 and 1771 the solutions were studied by how expressions in the roots change when the roots are shuffled, which is where the groups first show. In 1799 a proof that the quintic has no formula was published and not believed, and in 1824 a complete one was given. In 1831 a memoir that attached a group to every equation and gave the exact test was sent back by a referee as unclear; it was printed in 1846. The seventeen-sided figure was built in 1796, the impossibility of the old problems proved in 1837, the part-groups of prime-power size found in 1872, and the pieces of a group shown fixed in 1869 and 1889. The names are in the basements of the steps.

The principles, in plain words

The rung proves six things.

First, the equations of degree three and four have formulas by roots, and when a cubic has three different real answers, its formula must pass through the plane of numbers of rung 25.

Second, anything built from the roots by adding and multiplying that every shuffle of the roots leaves alone is built from the coefficients.

Third, the roots of an equation make a field, its symmetries are shuffles of the roots, there are exactly as many as the degree of the field, and they form the group of the equation.

Fourth, the fields between the rationals and the roots match the part-groups of that group one for one, the larger group with the smaller field, and the middle fields that are themselves fields of roots match the normal part-groups.

Fifth, every finite group comes apart into simple pieces, always the same ones; the even shuffles of five things are simple and not a clock; and an equation has a formula by roots exactly when the pieces of its group are all prime clocks. So x⁵ − 4x + 2, whose group is all the shuffles of five, has none.

Sixth, a group has part-groups of every prime-power size dividing its size, and a field whose group has a power of two members comes down in square roots. So compass and straightedge build exactly the towers of square roots: not the doubled cube, not the third of sixty degrees, not the seven-sided figure, and the seventeen-sided figure, yes.

The annexes ask what particles are built from, where the same pieces sort the heavy particles and every symmetry of the laws keeps an amount; what the building blocks of all finite symmetry are; and whether every group comes from an equation.

What you can do because you know it

You can solve any cubic or quartic, find the group of an equation of low degree, and list the fields between the rationals and its roots. You can say exactly why the quintic has no formula, and prove that a given equation has none. You can decide whether a length or a regular figure can be built with compass and straightedge, and build the ones that can. You can read off from the size of a group a great deal of its shape.

And you can say what the result does not say: not that the roots of the quintic cannot be found, since they can be computed to any accuracy, but that no formula by roots reaches them.

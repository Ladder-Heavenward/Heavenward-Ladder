The floor conclusion · its principles, in the words and marks it built

This page opens once every step of the Analysis floor is ticked. Its principles are proved on the rungs below, so it waits for them.

What the floor set out to show

The Analysis floor asked what a point on a line is. The fractions seemed to fill the line, and yet they left holes. It promised that the line, the function and the limit would become things you can prove, not pictures you trust.

Here are the floor’s four principles again, one to a rung, each in the words and the marks the floor built. Each links to the rung conclusion that proves it.

The principles, rung by rung

8 · drawing lines

Anything with a member and something above it all has a least thing above it all, so the line has no holes.

9 · making functions

A function is nothing but its pairs, one pair for each input.

10 · pushing the limit as close as you can imagine

Getting as close as you like is a statement of every and some.

11 · drawing without lifting the pen

A function is continuous where it lands where it was headed.

What the floor shows

Each rung handed the next its material. The line had to be whole before a limit had somewhere to land. A function had to be exact before its outputs could be chased. The limit had to be a statement before continuity could be defined by it.

On the Analysis floor, “as close as you like” stopped being a picture and became a statement of every and some, which the Logic floor’s techs can prove or refute.

Nothing new was laid down to do it. The line came from cuts of fractions, the function from sets of pairs, the limit from every and some. The game of a challenger’s ε and your δ is the Logic floor’s turn A and turn E, played with numbers.

So the floor demonstrates that the continuous can be reasoned about one line at a time. Everything that comes after it, slopes, areas and the rest, stands on these four rungs. You have only started to see what they can carry.

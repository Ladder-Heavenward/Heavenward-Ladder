36e · proof

The cylinder

The hue-chord technique sent mixes inside the circle. This step names the inside: distance from the centre is saturation, and the disc of all saturations at every hue is the circle filled in. Then brightness is added as a height, and the disc sweeps out a cylinder: the ring fully saturated, the axis grey, black at one end and white at the other. On the axis the hue has no answer, and the rung meets 5e’s forbidden division as a place in a solid.

A colour is an angle, a radius in [0, 1], and a height in [0, 1]. The radius is saturation; the height is brightness, called alpha on this rung. The axis is the greys, where the angle is every angle and none. The ends are black and white, where the radius is 0 as well.

36e1Saturation

From here on, a point of the plane inside or on the unit circle is written in polar form (r, θ), 21r1’s turn and reach: r its distance from the centre, θ its direction’s angle (21a3), so that the point is r·h(θ) = (r cos θ, r sin θ). From here on, the saturation of such a point is r, in [0, 1]. On the unit circle r = 1: the hues of 36d are the fully saturated colours. The hue-chord technique says a mix of two different hues has r < 1; mixing desaturates.

The point with r = 0 is the centre, and there θ is not fixed by the point: (0, θ) is the centre for every θ. That is 36e3’s business.

Polar technique: for r > 0, the pair (r, θ) and the point r·h(θ) determine each other; for r = 0 the point is the centre whatever θ is.

36e2Brightness

Brightness is 36b4’s second weighting, scaled into [0, 1]. From here on, the alpha of a colour, written z, is its brightness in [0, 1], and a colour in the cylinder is a triple (r, θ, z) with r and z in [0, 1] and θ an angle. The set of all such triples is 2g8’s product of the disc with the interval [0, 1], and 34’s word for that product is a cylinder: the disc swept along a line.

On this rung the cylinder is drawn with z = 0 at the top and z = 1 at the bottom, so that black is at the top and white at the bottom; the choice is the author’s and the ladder keeps it. The ring r = 1 at any height is the fully saturated colours at that brightness; moving along the ring is a hue shift, moving inward is desaturating, moving up and down is dimming and brightening.

Cylinder technique: the colours in the cylinder are exactly the points (x, y, z) of ℝ³ with x² + y² ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ z ≤ 1. Proof: 36e1’s polar form for (x, y), and a as the third coordinate (18a7, points in space).

36e3The axis

The axis of the cylinder is r = 0: the greys, one at each brightness. At a grey, θ has no value. Try to find it: the angle of a point is the direction of the arrow from the centre to it (21a), and the arrow to the centre is (0, 0), which points nowhere. In coordinates the angle is read from y/x, and at the centre that is 0/0: 5e5, no number times zero is one, and 5e6, no patch works.

So the hue of a grey is not a hue the ladder has failed to find; it is a question with every answer, and 2c12’s care applies: the set of all the answers is not one of them. From here on, a grey has no hue, and (0, θ, z) names the same grey for every θ.

Grey-axis technique: two colours in the cylinder are the same colour exactly when their r and z agree and, if r > 0, their θ differ by a whole number of turns. Proof: polar technique, with 2g5’s equal pairs.

This is the second time the rung has met a place with no answer (36c3 found a colour with no wavelength); the two are different kinds. The purples are a value the band lacks. The axis is a coordinate that a value lacks.

36e4The ends

At z = 0 there is no light, so the three numbers of 36b3 are (0, 0, 0) and the saturation is 0 as well: the top of the cylinder is one point, black, and the ring at the top is not a ring of colours but the single colour black written in every hue and saturation. At z = 1 the eye is full: the ring at the bottom closes to white for the same reason from the other side (36·phys says why a full eye cannot also be saturated). From here on, black is (0, θ, 0) and white is (0, θ, 1), each for every θ.

So the cylinder, as a set of colours, is not quite a cylinder: its two ends are pinched to points, and the shape with pinched ends is 34’s closed surface of another kind. The ladder keeps the word cylinder for the coordinates and says the pinch when it matters.

Ends technique: every colour with z = 0 is black, and every colour with z = 1 is white. Proof: 36b3 with no light gives (0, 0, 0), from which r = 0; the z = 1 case is laid down with 36b3’s curves.

36e5Two charts of one space

The triples (l, m, s) of 36b3 and the triples (r, θ, z) of the cylinder are two ways of writing one set of colours, as 21r’s turn-and-reach and 18a’s across-and-up are two ways of writing one plane, and as 29h1’s pages of an atlas are two ways of writing one surface. Going from one to the other is a function each way, and the two compose to the identity off the axis.

The function from (l, m, s) to (r, θ, z) does not keep adding and stretching (27c): z is a weighted sum of l and m, which is linear, but r and θ are a distance and an angle, 18a2 and 21a, which are not. The ladder gives the function a name and not a formula: from here on, cyl takes a triple of 36b3 to its cylinder coordinates, and cart takes cylinder coordinates back; cart(cyl(c)) = c for every c, and cyl(cart(p)) = p for every p off the axis.

Chart technique: a statement about colours is true in one chart exactly when it is true in the other, since the charts are related by a bijection off the axis (9d3) and the axis is handled by the grey-axis technique; this is 29h3, every page tells the same story. Each chart makes some functions easy: mixing is addition in (l, m, s) (36c2) and a chord in the cylinder (36d4); a hue shift is a turn in the cylinder (36d3) and a grid in (l, m, s) (27d, when the shift is small; the slides show it).

The basement · there if you want it

The accepted names

The cylinder of (r, θ, z) is a hue, saturation and value space, HSV, or with the ends pinched as here, close to HSL; brightness on this rung is the value or lightness. The axis is the grey axis, or achromatic axis. The two charts are a cylindrical and a Cartesian coordinate system on one space. That a hue has no value at r = 0 is the coordinate singularity of polar coordinates at the origin.

Who said it first

Philipp Otto Runge built a colour sphere in 1810 with white and black at the poles and the pure hues round the middle, the pinched ends of this step. Albert Munsell built a solid of hue, value and chroma from matching by eye, published from 1905. Alvy Ray Smith gave the hue, saturation and value cylinder for computer pictures in 1978.

A proof that is not allowed

“The grey (0, 30°, ½) has hue 30°, and the grey (0, 200°, ½) has hue 200°. Their hues differ, so they are different colours.” Not allowed: the grey-axis technique compares θ only when r > 0. At r = 0 both name one grey.

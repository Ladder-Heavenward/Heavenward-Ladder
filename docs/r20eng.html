Which Weaves Look the Same Turned Over?

Which weaves look the same turned over? Woven cloth is two sets of threads at right angles: the warp, held tight on the loom, and the weft, passed over and under it. Where each weft thread goes over or under each warp thread decides everything: how the cloth looks, how it hangs, and whether its back matches its front. This page writes a weave as a grid of overs and unders, finds the block each weave repeats, asks which of 20c’s slides, flips and turns keep a weave the same, sees what turning the cloth over does, and sorts weaves by their moves, as 20h sorted geometries.

Mark each crossing 1 if the warp is on top and 0 if the weft is. A weave is a grid of 1s and 0s that repeats. A move keeps the weave if the grid lands on itself. Turning the cloth over swaps every 1 and 0 and mirrors the picture; the weave looks the same from behind only if some move undoes that.

Joy is designing cloth for a weaving mill. Each crossing of a warp thread (running up the cloth) and a weft thread (running across) is one square of a grid. Mark it 1 where the warp is on top, 0 where the weft is.

20eng2Plain, Twill and Satin: the Repeat

A weave’s repeat is the smallest block of the grid that fills the whole cloth by slides alone. Three families cover most cloth.

weaverule for a 1 at row r, column crepeatwhat you see
plainr + c is even2 by 2a fine checker
2/2 twillc − r leaves 0 or 1 on division by 44 by 4diagonal ribs, as on jeans
5-end satinevery square except where c − 2r leaves 0 on division by 55 by 5long runs of warp, broken by scattered single weft crossings: a smooth sheen

In the twill each row is the row above slid one place to the right, so the overs line up along diagonals. In the satin each row slides two places, so no two of the scattered weft crossings touch and no lines show; the long runs of warp on top give the smooth shine of satin cloth.

20eng3Moves That Keep a Weave

A move of the plane (20c: slide, flip, turn) keeps a weave when every 1 lands on a 1 and every 0 on a 0. Slides by the repeat always keep it. Which other moves do?

\text{slide one row down, one column right: } c - r \text{ unchanged} @@ kept: the diagonal slide
\text{half turn: } (r, c) \to (a - r, b - c) @@ 
c - r \to -(c - r) + (b - a):\ \{0, 1\} \to \{0, 1\} \text{ when } b - a = 1 @@ kept, about the right centre
\text{flip across an upright line: } c \to b - c @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{c - r \to b - (c + r):\ \text{the ribs now run the other way}} @@ not kept: a mirror twill

Weave-move technique: a weave is kept by a move when the move sends its rule to itself; find the moves by applying each to the rule, not by redrawing the cloth. The 2/2 twill is kept by slides along its diagonal and by half turns, but an upright flip turns it into the mirror twill, ribs running the other way. Weavers call the two directions Z and S.

20eng4Turning the Cloth Over Swaps Over and Under

Seen from the back, every crossing that showed warp on top now shows weft: 1s and 0s swap. The picture is also mirrored, left to right, because you are looking from the other side.

\text{plain: swap} = \text{slide one column};\ \text{mirror keeps it} @@ the back matches the front
\text{2/2 twill: swap: } \{0, 1\} \to \{2, 3\} = \text{slide two columns} @@ the swap is a slide
\text{2/2 twill: mirror: ribs run the other way} @@ the back is the mirror twill
\htmlClass{new}{\text{5-end satin: swap leaves one 1 per row in five}} @@ weft on top: the dull face

A weave looks the same from behind exactly when swapping over and under, followed by the mirror, can be undone by a move that keeps the weave. Plain weave passes; a 2/2 twill’s back is the mirror twill; a satin’s back is a different-looking cloth, mostly weft, which is why satin has a shiny face and a dull back. A cloth inspector checks the back of each bolt against what the design predicts.

20eng5Sorting Weaves by Their Moves

Rung 20 sorted geometries by the moves they keep (20h). Weaves can be sorted the same way: list the moves that keep each, and weaves with the same list belong together, whatever their size.

Weaves that keep the same moves share their look and their back-to-front behaviour, so the list of moves sorts them more usefully than their sizes. A textile engineer choosing a cloth for a reversible garment looks for weaves whose back is kept, and for a sheen looks for a satin, which no short slide keeps.

Accepted names

The grid of overs and unders is a weave draft or point paper design; a weave where every thread does the same thing in turn is isonemal. 2/2 twill is a balanced twill; 3/1 a warp-faced twill, and its back a weft-faced one. Z and S twill name the rib direction. The step of a satin is its move number.

Who said it first

Branko Grünbaum and Geoffrey Shephard set out the geometry of weaves by their symmetries in 1980, in “Satins and twills: an introduction to the geometry of fabrics”.

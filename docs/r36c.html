36c · proof

The Purple That Is Not in the Band

col sends endlessly many spectra to three numbers, so it cannot tell most of them apart. This step says exactly which it cannot, and then finds the one thing it does that the band does not: the mix of a short-wavelength spike and a long-wavelength spike is three numbers that no single wavelength has. Those are the purples, and they are what makes the mathematics of colour the eye’s and not the world’s.

Two spectra are metamers when col sends them to one triple. Mixing lights adds triples. The triples of the single wavelengths trace an open curve, and the mixes of its two ends fill in a straight edge that no wavelength touches: the purples.

36c1Metamers

From here on, two spectra S and T are metamers, written S ≈ T, when col(S) = col(T). ≈ is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, since = on triples is (0b), so ≈ is 2h14’s sameness relation on spectra, and a colour is 2h15’s class: the set of every spectrum with that triple.

Metamer technique: every colour that is the colour of some spectrum is the colour of endlessly many.

The D is light past the window’s ends: ultraviolet below 380 nm, infrared past 700 nm. All three curves give it 0, so it changes the spectrum and leaves the colour alone. From here on, a spectrum may be read on a band wider than W, as 36b1 allows, whenever such light is in play.

Anexact, yet rigorous · the cloud

A colour is a crowd of spectra, and the crowd is exact in number, uncountable, and unseen. Everything true of the crowd that col keeps is true of the colour; everything it drops is still true of each spectrum (36j7) and invisible from the triple. The rule: be rigorous about the triple, and remember that it stands for a crowd.

36c2Mixing Lights

Two lights together have spectrum S + T (36a3) and so colour col(S) + col(T) (36b3). From here on, the mix of colours c and d is c + d, their triples added; it is the colour of the lights shone together. Mixing is addition of triples: commutative, associative, with (0, 0, 0) as the colour of no light. Scaling a colour by k ≥ 0 is making the light k times as strong.

The mix of a red light and a green light is a triple with l and m both large and s small, which is also the triple of a single wavelength near 580: that is why red and green light together look yellow, and 36c1 says the two are metamers. The mix of all the spikes of W in equal amounts is a triple with all three numbers large, and the accepted name for that colour is white; the ladder keeps the word.

Mix technique: mixing is 27a1’s adding of arrows on triples, and every rule of 27a1 holds for mixes. Proof: triples of reals are 27a2’s second space, arrows of three parts.

36c3The Purples

Run a spike along W from 380 to 700 nm and take its triple at each wavelength. The triples trace a curve in the space of triples, 18i1’s point that moves, from a short-wavelength violet to a long-wavelength red, and it is connected in 34’s sense, since col is continuous in λ (36·phys). The curve is open: its two ends are different triples, and no wavelength’s triple lies on the segment between them except the ends.

Now mix the two ends. By 36c2, the mixes k·col(380) + (1 − k)·col(700) for k in [0, 1] fill 18a5’s segment between the two end triples. Every point of that segment is a colour, the colour of a violet light and a red light together. From here on, the purples are the colours on that segment, other than its ends.

Purple technique: no purple is the colour of any spike.

The last line’s “laid down” is honest: that the curve of spikes bows away from its chord is a fact about the measured curves, not a technique, and the rung takes it with them. What is proved is the shape of the argument: given that bow, the purples are mixes and nothing else.

So the eye makes a colour the band does not have. The purples are the first colours on the rung that are the eye’s own, and they are the reason the next step can close the line into a circle: the open curve of spikes and the straight segment of purples together make one closed loop.

The cellar · there if you want it

The accepted names

Metamers keep their accepted name; a pair is a metameric match, and a spectrum the eye does not see is a metameric black. Mixing by adding triples is additive colour mixture, and its laws are Grassmann’s laws. The curve of spikes is the spectral locus, and the segment of purples is the line of purples. Magenta is a non-spectral colour.

Who said it first

Hermann Grassmann set out the laws of adding lights as arrows in 1853. Isaac Newton had already joined the two ends of the prism’s band to draw a closed circle of colour, and the purples on it are his joining. The word metamerism was taken into colour science from chemistry in the twentieth century.

A proof that is not allowed

“Purple is made of red and violet light. Red and violet are both in the band. So purple is in the band.” Not allowed: the band holds each spike, and a purple is the colour of two spikes together. The purple technique shows no single spike has that colour.

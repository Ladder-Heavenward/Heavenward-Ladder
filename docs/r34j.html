34j · technique

Holes Counted by Chains

Loops count holes (34g), but their group can be hard to compute, and it sees only holes a loop can go round. A hollow ball has a hole no loop catches. Cut a space into triangles and count instead. A chain is a sum of pieces with signs; its edge is the sum of their edges (29·cb28); and since an edge has no edge, the chains with no edge contain the edges. What is left over is a count of holes in each dimension. This step counts the holes of a hollow triangle, a hollow ball and a ring-shaped surface by chains.

Cₖ are the sums of k-dimensional pieces with whole-number signs; ∂ takes each to its edge, and ∂∂ = 0. A k-hole is a chain with no edge that is the edge of nothing: Hₖ = (chains with ∂c = 0) counted up to edges. The hollow ball has H₂ of one whole number, the ring surface H₁ of two.

34j1Chains and Their Edges

\partial[ab] = b - a @@ an edge’s edge: end less start
\partial\big([ab] + [bc] + [ca]\big) = (b - a) + (c - b) + (a - c) = 0 @@ a loop of edges has no edge
\text{no face } [abc]:\ \text{the loop is the edge of nothing} @@ so it is a hole
\htmlClass{new}{\text{fill the face: } \partial[abc] = [ab] + [bc] + [ca],\ \text{the hole is gone}} @@ edge-of-an-edge (29·cb28)

Hole-count technique: with Cₖ the chains of k-dimensional pieces and ∂ the edge, Hₖ is the chains with no edge, counted as the same when they differ by an edge; it is a group because ∂∂ = 0. H₀ counts the pieces the space is in (34c).

34j2A Hollow Ball

4 \text{ corners},\ 6 \text{ edges},\ 4 \text{ faces} @@ the shape of a sphere (34e)
\text{every loop of edges is the edge of some faces} @@ H₁ = 0, as no loop catches a hole
\partial\big(\text{all four faces, turned outward}\big) = 0 @@ every edge comes twice, opposite signs
\htmlClass{new}{H_2 = \mathbb Z:\ \text{the whole surface, the edge of no solid}} @@ the hole no loop catches

The hollow ball has no 1-holes and one 2-hole: the surface itself, which has no edge and bounds nothing, because the solid inside is not part of the space.

34j3Counting Corners, Edges and Faces

V - E + F = r_0 - r_1 + r_2 @@ r_k: whole-number directions in H_k
\text{sphere: } 4 - 6 + 4 = 2 = 1 - 0 + 1 @@ the tetrahedron
\text{ring surface: } H_0 = \mathbb Z,\ H_1 = \mathbb Z^2,\ H_2 = \mathbb Z @@ round the ring, and round the tube
\htmlClass{new}{1 - 2 + 1 = 0 = V - E + F \text{ for every cutting of the ring}} @@ the count of 34f

Alternating-count technique: for any cutting into triangles, corners less edges plus faces equals r₀ − r₁ + r₂; the dimension count of 27b, done at each level, gives it. So 34f’s count is a count of holes.

Accepted names

These are simplicial chains, the edge is the boundary operator, chains with no edge are cycles, edges are boundaries, and Hₖ is the k-th homology group. rₖ is the k-th Betti number; V − E + F is the Euler characteristic.

Who said it first

Henri Poincaré counted holes this way in Analysis Situs (1895); Enrico Betti had counted them in 1871. Emmy Noether saw around 1925 that the counts are groups.

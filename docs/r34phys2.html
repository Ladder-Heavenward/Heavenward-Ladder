Is a Whirlpool a Thing or a Motion?

Is a whirlpool a thing or a motion? A whirlpool in a draining bath keeps its shape for minutes, though not one drop of water stays in it for more than a second. A smoke ring crosses a room as if it were solid. A wire sings in the wind with a steady note. This page sees a whirlpool as a process, as 4m saw processes without things, finds that spin is carried with the flow using 28·phys’s frictionless streams and 28l’s circulation, watches a smoke ring carry itself, hears the street of swirls behind a post and its beat, as 31·eng heard a building sway, and finds that swirl lines can neither end nor cross, with 34’s linking.

In a fluid without friction, the circulation round any loop carried along with the fluid never changes. So swirl moves with the water, and a stretched swirl spins faster. Behind a post, swirls shed alternately at a steady beat, f = 0.2 V/d. Swirl lines can only close into loops or end on walls, and loops once linked stay linked.

34phys21A Whirlpool Keeps Its Shape While Its Water Passes Through

Mike studies flows in a water tank. In a draining tank, a whirlpool forms over the drain and stays put. Dye dropped in shows each bit of water spiralling in and down and out through the drain in seconds; the whirlpool stays for as long as the tank drains.

From here on, on this page, a swirl is a pattern of spinning flow, and its strength is its circulation Γ: the speed along a loop round it, added up all the way round (28l). Like 4m’s processes without things, a swirl is kept by what the water does, not by which water does it.

34phys22Spin Is Carried With the Flow

Follow a loop of water as it moves. With no friction, nothing can push the water along the loop on balance (28·phys), so the circulation round the moving loop stays the same.

\Gamma = 2\pi r\,v \text{ round a circle of radius } r @@ speed v all round
\Gamma \text{ kept}:\ \ v = \dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r} @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{r: 5 \text{ cm} \to 1 \text{ cm}:\ v \text{ five times faster}} @@ a swirl pulled thin spins up

Kept-spin technique: in a fluid without friction, the circulation round any loop carried with the fluid never changes; so swirl travels with the water, and a swirl stretched thinner spins faster. A slow stir in a wide bath becomes a fast whirl at the narrow drain, and a tornado spins fast because rising air stretches a slow, wide swirl into a thin one.

34phys23A Smoke Ring Carries Itself

A smoke ring is a swirl bent into a circle and closed. Each part of the ring is pushed along by the flow the rest of the ring makes, and all those pushes point the same way, through the ring’s middle. So the ring travels, carrying its smoke.

U \approx \dfrac{\Gamma}{4\pi R}\left(\ln\dfrac{8R}{a} - \dfrac14\right) @@ the accepted speed of a thin ring
\htmlClass{new}{U \approx \dfrac{0.02}{0.628}\,(4.38 - 0.25) \approx 0.13 \text{ m/s}} @@ a slow drift across the room

Mike fires rings from a box with a hole in a tank of still water; their measured speeds match this count to within the uncertainty of their size. A bigger ring of the same strength moves more slowly.

34phys24Behind a Post: a Street of Swirls and Its Beat

Wind past a round post or wire sheds swirls from alternate sides, first one side, then the other: a double row of swirls, turning opposite ways, trailing behind. Each swirl shed pushes the post sideways, so the post feels a push that swings back and forth at the shedding beat.

f \approx 0.2\,\dfrac{V}{d} @@ measured, over a wide range of speeds
\text{wire, } d = 2 \text{ mm},\ V = 10 \text{ m/s}:\ f \approx 1\,000 \text{ beats a second} @@ a singing wire
\htmlClass{new}{\text{chimney, } d = 2 \text{ m},\ V = 10 \text{ m/s}:\ f \approx 1 \text{ a second}} @@ near a tall chimney’s own sway (31·eng)

Behind a post, swirls shed at about 0.2 V/d beats a second; if that matches the post’s own sway, it rocks harder and harder. That is why tall chimneys wear a spiral fin, which breaks the shedding into a jumble with no steady beat.

34phys25Swirl Lines Can’t End or Cross

Draw lines along the axis of spin everywhere in the flow. The circulation round a tube of such lines is the same all along it, so a tube cannot just stop in the open water: its swirl would have nowhere to go. Swirl lines close into loops, like a smoke ring, or end on a wall or the surface, like a whirlpool.

\text{a tube of swirl lines: the same } \Gamma \text{ all along} @@ 
\text{so: closed loops, or ends on a boundary} @@ 
\htmlClass{new}{\text{lines carried with the flow: two linked loops stay linked}} @@ their linking count is kept (34o, 34p)

Since swirl is carried with the fluid and never ends inside it, swirl loops that are linked or knotted keep that linking as long as friction can be ignored. Researchers have made knotted swirl loops in water with specially shaped wings, and watched them hold their knot until friction lets them reconnect.

Accepted names

A swirl is a vortex; that circulation round a moving loop is kept is Kelvin’s circulation theorem, and that swirl tubes keep their strength and cannot end are Helmholtz’s vortex theorems. The double row behind a post is a Kármán vortex street, 0.2 is its Strouhal number, a singing wire gives an Aeolian tone, and the chimney fin is a helical strake.

Who said it first

Hermann von Helmholtz published his swirl theorems in 1858 and William Thomson, later Lord Kelvin, the circulation theorem in 1869; knotted vortices were made in water in 2013 (not yet revisited for the dates).
